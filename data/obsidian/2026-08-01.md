# 2026-08-01

**SIB-CRT for LARC: 9-yr OS 74.3% vs 48.9% (HR 0.43, P=0.008) off a 56-60 Gy boost, with pCR flat and no toxicity cost.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Lower-GI carries the day, and it is a dose question: a 106-pt randomized phase 2 missed its pCR primary endpoint yet separated on every 9-yr survival endpoint, LC 87.1% vs 70.1% (HR 0.40). MFS moving as hard as LC (HR 0.48) is the part that does not follow from a local boost.

## 🌀 GI Lower

Single trial, and it argues the pCR surrogate is the wrong readout for a rectal boost.

### SIB-CRT vs Standard CRT for LARC (NCT02195141) · [[NCT02195141]]

> 9-yr OS 74.3% vs 48.9% (HR 0.43, P=0.008) with SIB dose escalation to 56-60 Gy, despite identical pCR.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The boost was modest and deliverable on standard IMRT, 56 Gy to PGTV and 60 Gy to involved lateral nodes on a 50 Gy/25 fx pelvis, with acute G3 toxicity no higher (14.5% vs 19.6%). It moves the dose-escalation question in chemo-ineligible pts, where the DFS benefit concentrated (HR 0.343, P=0.014).

- 🔍 Randomized phase 2, N=106 (55 SIB-CRT / 51 CRT), stage II/III rectal adenocarcinoma, accrued Aug 2013 to Feb 2015
- 🔍 RT: both arms 50 Gy/25 fx to pelvis; SIB arm added 56 Gy to PGTV and 60 Gy to lateral metastatic nodes when present
- 🔍 Radical surgery planned 6-8 wks post-CRT; median f/u 116.6 mo
- 📊 1° EP pCR was NEGATIVE: 15.2% vs 18.4%, P=0.695. Every survival endpoint is secondary
- 📊 9-year ITT outcomes, SIB-CRT vs CRT

  | Endpoint | SIB-CRT | CRT | Effect size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | DFS | 70.8% | 47.4% | HR 0.46, P=0.013 |
  | OS | 74.3% | 48.9% | HR 0.43, P=0.008 |
  | MFS | 70.8% | 47.2% | HR 0.48, P=0.017 |
  | LC | 87.1% | 70.1% | HR 0.40, P=0.038 |
  | CSS | 77.4% | 57.2% | P=0.027 |

- 📊 Acute G3 toxicity 14.5% SIB-CRT vs 19.6% CRT, mostly radiation dermatitis. No toxicity cost to the boost
- 📊 Curative treatment (radical surgery or W&W) reached in 89.1% (49/55) vs 78.4% (40/51), P=0.135
- ⚠️ MFS moved as much as LC (HR 0.48 vs 0.40), which a local dose boost should not do. Distant control is the harder claim
- ⚠️ Subgroup: benefit confined to pts who got NO perioperative chemo (9-yr DFS 70.8%, HR 0.343, P=0.014); no added benefit with chemo. Exploratory, hypothesis-generating
- ⚠️ N=106 phase 2 with a negative 1° EP; 9-yr survival separations of 23-25 absolute points off ~50 pts/arm are fragile to a handful of events

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 42456783]] **Dose-escalated versus Standard Neoadjuvant Chemoradiotherapy for Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer: 9-year Results of a Randomized Phase 2 Trial.** — Li; Wang; Xu et al.. *International journal of radiation oncology, biology, physics* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.07.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.07.004)
  > PURPOSE: To compare the safety and efficacy of preoperative simultaneous integrated boost chemoradiotherapy (SIB-CRT) versus standard chemoradiotherapy (CRT) for locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC).
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: This prospective, randomized phase II trial ([[NCT02195141]]) enrolled patients with stage II/III rectal adenocarcinoma. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to CRT (50 Gy/25 fx to pelvis) or SIB-CRT (50 Gy/25 fx to the pelvis with SIB of 56 Gy to PGTV and 60 Gy to lateral metastatic nodes if present). Radical surgery was planned 6-8 weeks after chemoradiotherapy. The primary endpoint was pathological complete response (pCR) rate; secondary endpoints were disease-free survival (DFS), overall survival (OS), metastasis-free survival (MFS), local control (LC), cancer-specific survival (CSS) and toxicity.
  > RESULTS: From August 2013 to February 2015, 106 patients were enrolled: 55 in the SIB-CRT group and 51 in the CRT group. Acute grade 3 toxicity occurred in 14.5% (SIB-CRT) and 19.6% (CRT) of patients, primarily manifested as radiation dermatitis. Curative treatment (radical surgery or watch-and-wait) was achieved in 89.1% (49/55) of SIB-CRT patients and 78.4% (40/51) of CRT patients (P=0.135). After a median follow-up of 116.6 months, the SIB-CRT group showed superior 9-year outcomes in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population: DFS (70.8% vs 47.4%; HR 0.46, P=0.013), OS (74.3% vs 48.9%; HR 0.43, P=0.008), MFS (70.8% vs 47.2%; HR 0.48, P=0.017), LC (87.1% vs 70.1%; HR 0.40, P=0.038) and CSS (77.4% vs 57.2%; P=0.027). Similar benefits were observed in the curative treatment cohort. The pCR rates were comparable (15.2% vs 18.4%; P=0.695). Exploratory subgroup analysis showed that the survival benefit of SIB-CRT was statistically significant in patients who did not receive perioperative chemotherapy (9-year DFS: 70.8%; HR=0.343, P&#x202f;=&#x202f;.014), whereas no additional benefit was observed in those receiving chemotherapy.
  > CONCLUSIONS: Dose escalation through SIB during neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy translates into superior long-term survival outcomes. Despite comparable pCR rates, the intensified control of both local disease and micrometastases by SIB-CRT, coupled with its significant superiority within the chemotherapy-naive subgroup, likely contributed to these robust results. These findings establish dose escalation as a potent strategy for optimizing long-term cure in the modern management of LARC, particularly in chemotherapy-ineligible patients.

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does dose escalation improve DFS in a phase 3 powered on survival?
> - Late toxicity after simultaneous integrated boost not reported in source

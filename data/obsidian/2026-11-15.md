# 2026-11-15

**Salvage RT + ADT: RTOG 9601 bicalutamide OS HR 1.13 at pre-SRT PSA <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5; author argues against routine ADT with early SRT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> No new trial readouts today, only reviews and planning guides. Prostate leads: pre-SRT PSA predicts ADT benefit with salvage RT, and 60 Gy/20 fx is the preferred moderate hypofx schedule, backed by 5-yr non-inferiority in CHHiP, PROFIT and NRG Oncology 0415. Also: target volumes for H&N PNI and seminoma, and a case of hemidiaphragm paralysis after lung SABR (phrenic-area Dmax 57.7 Gy).

## ♂️ Germ Cell / Other GU

Seminoma planning review: para-aortic 20 Gy/10 fx for stage I, superior border lowered to the top of T12.

### Radiotherapy treatment planning for testicular seminoma

> Review recommends para-aortic 20 Gy/10 fx (stage I), modified dog-leg plus 10 Gy or 16 Gy boost (IIA/IIB), superior border top of T12.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The parameters that transfer: superior border at top of T12 (no positive nodes above L1 among 69 mapped stage II pts), dog-leg inferior border at top of acetabulum (no pelvic relapses in 87 pts), and a vascular CTV of IVC + 1.2 cm and aorta + 1.9 cm. Each trims kidney, stomach or bowel dose.

- 🔍 Evidence-based narrative review (Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2012); no new outcome data
- 🔍 85% of seminoma pts present clinical stage I, 11% clinical stage II
- 🔍 Stage I: surveillance preferred if compliant; adjuvant RT reserved for the minority choosing treatment
- 💊 Recommended management by clinical stage

  | Setting | RT option | Systemic option |
  |---|---|---|
  | Stage IA/IB/IS, no prior pelvic/scrotal surgery | Para-aortic 20 Gy/10 fx over 2 wk | Carboplatin AUC=7 x 1-2 cycles |
  | Stage IA/IB/IS, prior pelvic/scrotal surgery | Classic dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx | Carboplatin AUC=7 x 1-2 cycles (preferred) |
  | Stage IIA | Modified dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx + 10 Gy/5 fx boost | BEP x 3 or EP x 4 |
  | Stage IIB | Modified dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx + 16 Gy/8 fx boost | BEP x 3 or EP x 4 |

- 🔍 Field border changes and their evidence base
  - Superior border: top of T11 → top of T12 (all fields)
  - Bruns: no relapses in the cranially reduced volume, 80 pts, median f/u 7.1 yrs
  - Paly nodal mapping, 69 stage II pts: no positive nodes above L1
  - Dog-leg inferior border: obturator foramen → top of acetabulum
  - German cohort, 87 pts: no pelvic relapses with acetabular inferior border
- 🔍 Vascular-based CTV/PTV construction (3D-CRT)
  - CTV1: IVC + 1.2 cm, aorta + 1.9 cm (lateroaortic nodes), excluding bone and bowel
  - Stage II: ipsilateral iliac vessels + 1.2 cm added to CTV1, down to top of acetabulum
  - CTV2: GTV + 0.8 cm; PTV = CTV + 0.5 cm
  - Block edge: PTV + 0.7 cm for penumbra
  - 2D alternative: 2-cm margin on adenopathy to block edge
- 📐 Kidney dose constraints

  | Phase | Each kidney D50% | Combined kidney Dmean |
  |---|---|---|
  | Modified dog-leg | ≤8 Gy | ≤9 Gy |
  | Boost | ≤2 Gy | ≤3 Gy |

- 🔗 TE18: 20 Gy/10 fx not inferior to 30 Gy/15 fx; less lethargy, faster return to work
- 🔗 TE10: para-aortic vs dog-leg fields → less acute toxicity, faster sperm count recovery
- ⚠️ AP-PA favored over 7-field IMRT: lower kidney, liver, bowel Dmean despite worse marrow sparing
- ⚠️ 45% second-cancer reduction from field shrinkage is a linear dose-response model prediction, not observed

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Radiotherapy Treatment Planning for Testicular Seminoma** — Wilder, Richard B.; Buyyounouski, Mark K.; Efstathiou, Jason A. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2012-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2012.01.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2012.01.044)
  - 📎 [[papers/gu-other/2012-wilder-radiotherapy-treatment-planning-testicular-seminoma.pdf|Radiotherapy treatment planning for testicular seminoma (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does vascular-based 3D-CRT CTV lower late toxicity vs bony-anatomy fields?
> - Proton cost-effectiveness vs photons or BEP in stage II seminoma
> - Does the T12 superior border hold in larger prospective cohorts?

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Rare late effect after central lung SABR: phrenic injury 2 yr out; authors suggest contouring the phrenic nerve as an OAR.

### Unilateral diaphragmatic paralysis after lung SABR (case report)

> Left hemidiaphragm paralysis 2y after 50 Gy/4fx lung SABR; phrenic-area Dmax 57.7 Gy (BED3 335.14 Gy), conservative mgmt, no local recurrence.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable detail is the authors' mediastinal constraint set, not the 57.7 Gy point: mediastinum outside PTV held to prescription dose, PTV/mediastinum overlap ≤110%, V>105% minimized. Injury occurred despite trimming the PTV to 3 mm into mediastinum, so this moves contouring and hotspot placement, not prescription.

- 🔍 Single case report: woman in her 50s, metastatic sinonasal adenoid cystic carcinoma, 2 lung mets
- 🔍 SABR 50 Gy/4 fx via VMAT, 4DCT sim, amplitude-based expiratory gating, daily CBCT
- 🔍 PTV 5 mm from GTV, limited to 3 mm into mediastinum for the LUL mass
- 📐 Phrenic-area Dmax 57.7 Gy (point dose to approximate location), BED3 335.14 Gy
- 📊 Toxicity and disease course
  - L hemidiaphragm elevation first visible on serial CT at 23 mo
  - Dyspnea + cough ~2 mo later, at 2 years; initially attributed to asthma
  - Sniff test confirmed L hemidiaphragmatic paralysis
  - Breathing exercises + ambulation → symptom improvement within 3 mo, no surgery
  - Paralysis persists on imaging; asymptomatic, no local recurrence at 38 mo
- 🔗 Prospective central SABR trials cited by authors: no neuropathy reported, phrenic never contoured

  | Trial | N | Dose | Neuropathy | Phrenic contoured |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | RTOG 0813 (phase 1/2) | 120 | up to 60 Gy/5 fx | None reported | No |
  | Indiana University (phase 2) | 70 | Not stated in source | None noted | No |
  | Stanford dose-adapted (phase 2) | 74 central tumors | up to 50 Gy/4 fx | None observed | No |

- ⚠️ Phrenic nerve not visible on CT; dose reconstructed retrospectively to an approximate location
- ⚠️ n=1; authors note other pts received higher doses without complications
- ⚠️ Prior L neck dissection + H&N RT; L vocal cord paresis attributed to vagus injury from that course
- 🔍 Authors' planning mitigations for tumors abutting mediastinum
  - Consider phrenic nerve as OAR along its course
  - Mediastinum outside PTV: max dose ≤ prescription dose
  - PTV/mediastinum overlap: ≤110% of prescription, V>105% kept small, GTV dose not compromised
  - No further dose reduction or coverage compromise advocated
  - Add phrenic injury to consent as a rare late effect

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Unilateral Diaphragmatic Paralysis After Stereotactic Ablative Radiation Therapy to a Lung Tumor Abutting the Course of the Phrenic Nerve** — Eke, Iris; Guo, H. Henry; Loo, Billy W. et al.. *Practical Radiation Oncology* (2023-09). [doi:10.1016/j.prro.2023.04.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2023.04.010)
  - 📎 [[papers/thoracic/2023-eke-unilateral-diaphragmatic-paralysis-stereotactic-ablative.pdf|Unilateral diaphragmatic paralysis after lung SABR (case report) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Phrenic nerve dose-response threshold for SABR near the mediastinum
> - True incidence of SABR-induced phrenic injury, likely underreported
> - Optimal management: conservative care vs diaphragm plication

## 👄 Head & Neck

Dose and coverage framework for the tumor bed and elective cranial nerve pathways in PNI and PNTS, from 60 Gy adjuvant to 70 Gy gross PNTS.

### Perineural invasion and perineural tumor spread in head and neck cancer

> 60 Gy bed, 64-66 Gy close/positive margin, 50-60 Gy elective CN pathways, 70 Gy gross PNTS: expert target-volume framework for H&N PNI.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The decision this moves is how far up the nerve to treat, not whether to irradiate: tumor bed alone for microscopic focal intratumoral PNI, retrograde CN pathway to the skull base at 50-60 Gy for ACC/SDC, extensive PNI, a named or >0.1 mm nerve, or a nerve margin. Interconnections (V2/VII via vidian and GSPN) are added only for PNTS.

- 🔍 Narrative review with author recommendations, built on 6 representative PNI/PNTS cases
- 🔍 Cases span salivary (submandibular, parotid, palate), forehead cSCC, NPC, oral cavity SCC
- 🔍 Authors recommend adjuvant RT for: cSCC with extensive PNI or large-caliber nerve; ACC/SDC with microscopic PNI; mucosal SCC with extensive PNI; any clinical/radiographic PNTS
- 📐 Author dose recommendations by scenario

  | Scenario | Dose |
  |---|---|
  | Tumor bed, microscopic PNI (adjuvant) | 60 Gy |
  | Tumor bed, close or positive margin | 64-66 Gy |
  | Elective CN pathways | 50-60 Gy |
  | Elective CN near skull base, cochlea/brainstem hard to spare | De-escalate toward 50 Gy equivalent |
  | Elective nerve, intraop extensive PNI or limited PNTS in bed | Up to 66 Gy |
  | Gross PNTS, definitive or incompletely resected | 70 Gy |

- 🔍 Extent of coverage ladder (author Table 2)

  | Coverage level | Author indications |
  |---|---|
  | Tumor bed only | Microscopic focal intratumoral PNI |
  | Bed + retrograde elective CN pathway to skull base | ACC or SDC; extensive PNI; named or >0.1 mm nerve; close or positive nerve margin; PNTS |
  | Add communicating interconnections | Clinical or radiographic PNTS; consider for ACC or extensive PNI |
  | Anterograde coverage | Only if symptoms, op report or imaging suggest it; cover in PNTS |

- 🔍 Site-specific elective nerve pathways

  | Primary site | Elective pathway | Boundary |
  |---|---|---|
  | Submandibular, high-grade with extensive PNI or ACC | V3 lingual nerve to foramen ovale | XII to hypoglossal canal only if involved; VII not routinely covered |
  | Parotid ACC/SDC/high-grade, extensive PNI or PNTS on VII | VII from stylomastoid foramen to geniculate ganglion and labyrinthine segment | Focal microscopic PNI: stylomastoid + mastoid VII only, spare cochlea |
  | Hard palate, microscopic PNI | Palatine foramina + PPF to foramen rotundum | PNTS/extensive PNI: add Meckel cave, cavernous sinus, vidian nerve, GSPN |
  | Forehead skin SCC with PNTS | V1 pathway, elective cisternal segment of V1 | Gross PNTS to superior orbital fissure and cavernous sinus in CTV |
  | NPC T3-4 | At least 5 mm into posterior maxillary sinus/choanae; bilateral PPF, rotundum, ovale, lacerum; cavernous sinus; one third of clivus | Spare uninvolved jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal (late lower CN palsy) |
  | Oral cavity SCC | No routine CN pathway to skull base | Only with gross named nerve or CN involvement |

- 📊 Disease control with RT reported in cited retrospective series
  - Skin cancer with gross PNTS, definitive RT: 50% to 57%
  - Unresectable/subtotally resected ACC, photon or particle: 36% to 93%
  - cSCC extensive PNI: 2y DSS 73% with RT vs DFS 40% unirradiated
- ⚠️ cSCC comparison sets DSS against DFS: mismatched endpoints, not a like-for-like benefit estimate
- ⚠️ PNI definitions not standardized across oral cavity series; local-failure association contested
- 💊 Microscopic PNI alone in resected mucosal SCC is not an indication for concurrent systemic therapy
- 🔍 MRI with fat-sat post-gad T1 is the detection standard; PET limited for small-volume and skull-base PNTS

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.12.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.12.009)

> - Independent prognostic weight of PNI histologic features

## 🌰 Prostate

Two reviews: PSA-stratified ADT with salvage RT, and a planning guide for 60 Gy/20 fx moderate hypofx in localised disease.

### Risk stratification to guide hormone therapy with salvage RT

> Pre-SRT PSA predicts ADT benefit: RTOG 9601 OS HR 1.13 at PSA <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5; author argues against routine ADT with early SRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT argument is biological: at low pre-SRT PSA, recurrence is likely confined to bed or pelvis, where 64 to 72 Gy treats microscopic disease, so ADT adds little; above PSA 0.7, micrometastatic spread makes systemic therapy necessary. RTOG 9601's bicalutamide OS HR moved from 1.13 below 0.7 to 0.45 above 1.5.

- 📊 Simplified ADT-with-salvage-RT grid (Table 3) by pre-RT PSA (ng/mL); * discuss ADT if ≥12-yr life expectancy and multiple high-risk features

  | Grade group (Gleason) | PSA 0.1-0.5 | PSA 0.6-1.0 | PSA 1.0-1.5 | PSA >1.5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 (6) | RT | RT | RT + STADT | RT + STADT |
  | 2, 3 (7) | RT | RT ± STADT* | RT + STADT | RT + LTADT |
  | 4, 5 (8-10) | RT* | RT + STADT | RT + LTADT | RT + LTADT |

- 📊 RTOG 9601: bicalutamide OS effect by pre-SRT PSA (interaction test positive)

  | Pre-SRT PSA (ng/mL) | Absolute OS change | HR |
  |---|---|---|
  | <0.7 | decreased 4.1% | 1.13 |
  | >1.5 | improved 24.6% | 0.45 |

- 📊 GETUG-AFU-16: crude biochemical control gain from 6 mo GnRH agonist 12% at PSA <0.5 vs 36% at >0.5
- 🔍 RTOG 9601 was mostly late SRT: only ~10% had PSA ≤0.3, PSA <0.5 excluded for much of accrual
- 🔗 RTOG 9408: 4 mo HT improved BCR ~10% even in low-risk intact disease, near GETUG's early-SRT gain
- ⚠️ Biochemical control uninterpretable under GnRH agonist: testosterone stays subnormal 6-12 mo after the course
- 🔍 ICECaP (>28,000 men): DM a strong survival surrogate (correlation >0.9), BCR is not
- 🔍 Trials the author routes pts to
  - NRG GU-006 ([[NCT03371719]]): SRT ± apalutamide, PSA <0.7; excludes PSA >1.0; biomarker-stratified
  - FORMULA-509 ([[NCT03141671]]) / RTOG 3506: PSA ≥0.7, pN+, or pT3b-T4 / pattern 5
  - NRG GU-002 ([[NCT03070886]]): persistent postoperative PSA ≥0.2, docetaxel
- ⚠️ PSA gradient rests on one RTOG 9601 subgroup interaction; prespecification of cut points not stated in source
- ⚠️ Cross-trial overlays (Figs 1-2) mix populations, agents, durations and time zeros

  - 📎 [[papers/prostate/2018-spratt-evidence-based-risk-stratification-guide-hormone-therapy.pdf|Risk stratification to guide hormone therapy with salvage RT (full text)]]

### Moderate hypofractionation for prostate cancer: a user's guide

> 60 Gy/20 fx preferred: PROFIT, CHHiP and NRG Oncology 0415 show 5-yr non-inferiority for disease control and late toxicity in localised prostate cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The practical payload is the planning envelope: CTV-PTV ~7 mm with daily IGRT, urethra/bladder neck Dmax ≤ 61 Gy, and the 57 to 30 Gy gradient within ≤10 mm at the rectal interface. Prior TURP and large glands were never trial exclusions, so they don't by themselves argue for conventional fractionation if constraints are met.

- 📊 Three large RCTs: moderate hypofx non-inferior for disease control, late toxicity and PROMs at 5 yr
- 📊 Core non-inferiority trials (hypofx vs conventional)

  | Trial | N / risk | Regimens | ADT | Median f/u | Efficacy HR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | CHHiP | 3,216, predominantly intermediate | 60 Gy/20 or 57 Gy/19 vs 74 Gy/37 | 3-6 mo | 5.2 yr | 0.84 (90% CI 0.68-1.03), pNI = 0.0018 |
  | PROFIT | 1,206, intermediate | 60 Gy/20 vs 78 Gy/39 | Not permitted | 6 yr | 0.96 (90% CI 0.77-1.2) |
  | NRG Oncology 0415 | 1,115, low | 70 Gy/28 vs 73.8 Gy/41 | Not stated | 5.8 yr | DFS 0.85 (95% CI 0.64-1.14) |

- 📊 5-yr biochemical-clinical FFS in the order of 85% to 90% across all three trials
- 📊 Late G≥3 toxicity 1%-4% across the three trials, regardless of schedule
- 📊 PMH phase 2, 60 Gy/20 fx, >10 yr f/u
  - Any G3: 2%
  - G≥2 GI: 4% at 5 yr, unchanged at 8 yr
  - G≥2 GU: 9% at 5 yr, 12% at 8 yr; many events transient
- ⚠️ NRG 0415 hypofx arm carried higher BED (128 vs 118 Gy, α/β 3) → small rise in late G2 GI/GU
- ⚠️ HYPRO 64.6 Gy/19 fx (EQD2 90 Gy prostate, 83 Gy normal tissue) → unfavourable toxicity
- 🔍 Elective nodal RT not evaluated in any large hypofx trial
- 🔍 CHHiP excluded upper-end high risk (Gleason ≥9, Gleason 8 with T3 or PSA >10, among others)
- 🔍 Relative contraindications: authors call none firm
  - Baseline IPSS >12: worse GU function in FCCC post hoc subgroup only, not confirmed in larger trials
  - Prior TURP: not excluded in any large trial; 8% of CHHiP
  - Gland size: no exclusion if dose-volume constraints can be met
  - Anticoagulation: excluded in CHHiP only
- 🔍 IMRT/VMAT + daily IGRT; CTV-PTV ~7 mm, 3 to 5 mm with intrafraction monitoring
- 📐 Planning aims by trial

  | Structure | PROFIT | CHHiP |
  |---|---|---|
  | CTV | D99 ≥ 60 Gy | n/a |
  | PTV | D99 ≥ 57 Gy, D1cc ≤ 63 Gy | D99 ≥ 57 Gy, D1 ≤ 63 Gy, D50 = 60 Gy ±1% |
  | Rectum | V46 ≤ 30%, V37 ≤ 50% (wall) | V57 ≤ 15%, V40 ≤ 60% (whole organ) |
  | Bladder | V46 ≤ 30%, V37 ≤ 50% (wall) | V60 ≤ 5%, V48 ≤ 25%, V40 ≤ 50% (whole organ) |
  | Penile bulb | n/a | V40 ≤ 50% |
  | Femoral head | V43 ≤ 5% | V40 ≤ 50% |

- 📐 Authors' modified ideal aims (whole-organ OARs, 7 mm PTV)
  - Rectum: V60 ≤ 1%, V57 ≤ 10%, V30 ≤ 35%
  - Bladder: V60 ≤ 1%, V50 ≤ 20%, V30 ≤ 50%
  - Bladder neck/urethra: Dmax ≤ 61 Gy
  - PTV/rectum gradient: 57 to 30 Gy in ≤10 mm (acceptable ≤15 mm)
- 🔗 Arcangeli 62 Gy/20 vs 80 Gy/40 fx, 168 high-risk pts: 10-yr FFBF 72% vs 65% (p = 0.148)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Moderate hypofractionation for prostate cancer: A user's guide** — Pryor, David I; Turner, Sandra L; Tai, Keen Hun et al.. *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Oncology* (2018-01). [doi:10.1111/1754-9485.12703](https://doi.org/10.1111/1754-9485.12703)
  > Summary Three large randomised controlled trials have been published in the last year demonstrating the non‐inferiority of moderate hypofractionation compared to conventional fractionation for localised prostate cancer with respect to both disease control and late toxicity at 5 years. Furthermore, no clinically significant differences in patient‐reported outcomes have emerged. More mature follow‐up data are now also available from phase 2 studies confirming that moderate hypofractionation is associated with low rates of significant toxicity at 10 years. Moving forward it is likely that appropriate patient selection, integration of androgen deprivation and attention to optimising technique will play a more important role than modest differences in dose‐fractionation schedules. Here we briefly review the evidence, discuss issues of patient selection and provide an approach to implementing moderately hypofractionated radiation therapy for prostate cancer in clinical practice.

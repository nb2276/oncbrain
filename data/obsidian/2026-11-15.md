# 2026-11-15

**Salvage RT ± ADT: RTOG 9601 OS HR 1.13 at pre-SRT PSA <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5 ng/mL, arguing against routine ADT with early SRT (2018 commentary).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Planning-focused day, no new trial readouts. Prostate carries it: pre-SRT PSA gates ADT benefit (RTOG 9601), and 60 Gy/20 fx moderate hypofx holds non-inferior across CHHiP, PROFIT and NRG 0415. H&N PNI framework sets 60 Gy bed, 50-60 Gy elective CN pathways; a case report flags phrenic nerve palsy ~2 yr after 50 Gy/4 fx lung SABR.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

### Unilateral diaphragmatic paralysis after lung SABR abutting phrenic nerve

> Max point dose 57.7 Gy (BED 335.14 Gy, α/β=3) near the left phrenic nerve after 50 Gy/4 fx SABR, followed by hemidiaphragm paralysis at ~2 yr.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> This is about planning and surveillance, not a tolerance: the authors contour the mediastinum as a surrogate, cap dose outside the PTV at prescription and the overlap at 110%. CT elevation came ~2 mo before dyspnea, so reviewing the hemidiaphragm on routine surveillance scans is the transferable step. The case also supports adding phrenic injury to consent.

- 🔍 Single case report: woman in her 50s, metastatic sinonasal adenoid cystic carcinoma, 2 lung mets
- 🔍 SABR 50 Gy in 4 fractions, VMAT, 4D-CT sim, amplitude-based expiratory gating
- 🔍 Uniform 5 mm PTV, limited to 3 mm into the mediastinum for the LUL mass
- 📊 Max point dose to the approximate phrenic nerve region 57.7 Gy (BED 335.14 Gy, α/β=3)
- 📊 L hemidiaphragm elevation first visible on CT at 23 mo, ~2 mo before dyspnea
- 📊 Sniff test confirmed L hemidiaphragmatic paralysis. Dyspnea first attributed to known asthma
- 📊 Conservative mgmt (breathing exercises, ambulation) improved symptoms within 3 mo. No plication
- 📊 No local recurrence at 38 mo. Paralysis persists on imaging
- 🔗 Central-tumor SABR trials cited in source: no neuropathy, phrenic nerve never contoured

  | Trial | N | Dose | Neuropathy |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | RTOG 0813 (phase 1/2) | 120 | Up to 60 Gy in 5 fx | None reported |
  | Indiana University (phase 2) | 70 | Not stated in source | None noted |
  | Stanford dose-adapted (phase 2) | 74 central tumors | Up to 50 Gy in 4 fx | None observed |

- 🔍 Authors' institutional mediastinal planning constraints
  - Mediastinum outside PTV: max dose capped at prescription
  - PTV/mediastinum overlap: capped at 110% of prescription
  - Volume >105% kept as small as possible, GTV dose not compromised
  - Authors do NOT advocate reducing target coverage for mediastinal-abutting tumors
- ⚠️ Phrenic nerve not visible on CT; 57.7 Gy is a dose to an approximated location
- ⚠️ Radiation nerve injury is a diagnosis of exclusion; no confirmatory test exists
- ⚠️ Concurrent L vocal cord paresis attributed to H&N RT 14 yr earlier, a second prior-RT exposure

## ♂️ Germ Cell / Other GU

### Radiotherapy treatment planning for testicular seminoma

> 20 Gy/10 fx AP-PA fields with T12 superior and acetabular inferior borders; stage II boost 10 Gy/5 fx (IIA) or 16 Gy/8 fx (IIB).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The T12 superior border rests on mapping of 69 stage II pts with no positive nodes above L1, plus 80 pts without relapse in the reduced volume at median 7.1 years. The vascular CTV recipe (IVC +1.2 cm, aorta +1.9 cm, PTV 0.5 cm, block edge 0.7 cm) ports directly to CT-based planning, though large trials still used T11.

- 🔍 Evidence-based planning review, clinical stage IA-IIB seminoma post radical inguinal orchiectomy
- 🔍 Surveillance preferred for compliant stage I pts; adjuvant RT reserved for the minority choosing treatment
- 💊 Management options by setting (per review pathways)

  | Setting | RT option | Systemic option |
  |---|---|---|
  | Stage I, no prior pelvic/scrotal surgery | Para-aortic 20 Gy/10 fx | Carboplatin AUC=7 x 1-2 cycles |
  | Stage I, prior pelvic/scrotal surgery | Classic dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx | Carboplatin AUC=7 x 1-2 cycles (preferred) |
  | Stage IIA | Modified dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx + 10 Gy/5 fx boost | BEP x3 or EP x4 |
  | Stage IIB | Modified dog-leg 20 Gy/10 fx + 16 Gy/8 fx boost | BEP x3 or EP x4 |

- 📐 Vascular-anatomy target margins (3D-CRT)

  | Volume | Expansion |
  |---|---|
  | IVC → nodal CTV1 | 1.2 cm |
  | Aorta → CTV1 (lateroaortic nodes) | 1.9 cm |
  | Ipsilateral iliac vessels → CTV1 (stage II) | 1.2 cm |
  | GTV → CTV2 (boost) | 0.8 cm |
  | CTV → PTV | 0.5 cm |
  | PTV → block edge | 0.7 cm |

- 📐 Kidney constraints, stage II

  | Phase | Each kidney D50% | Combined mean |
  |---|---|---|
  | Modified dog-leg | ≤8 Gy | ≤9 Gy |
  | Boost | ≤2 Gy | ≤3 Gy |

- 📊 Evidence behind the border changes
  - Superior T12: Paly mapping, 69 stage II pts, no positive nodes above L1
  - Superior T12: Bruns, 80 pts, no relapses in reduced volume, median f/u 7.1 years
  - Inferior acetabulum: German cohort, 87 pts, no pelvic relapses
- 🔗 TE18: 20 Gy/10 fx not inferior to 30 Gy/15 fx; less lethargy, faster return to work
- 🔗 TE10: para-aortic vs dog-leg fields → less acute toxicity, faster sperm count recovery
- ⚠️ IMRT lowers marrow/bowel V75%/V100%, but AP-PA keeps kidney, liver, bowel Dmean lower
- ⚠️ Large trials used a T11 superior border; T12 rests on mapping plus cohort data

## 👄 Head & Neck

### Perineural invasion and perineural tumor spread in head and neck cancer

> Graded RT framework for PNI/PNTS: tumor bed 60 Gy, elective cranial nerve pathways 50-60 Gy retrograde to skull base, gross PNTS 70 Gy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The underused rule is interconnections: anastomotic pathways such as V to VII should always be covered with PNTS or a positive margin along an adjacent named nerve, retrograde to the skull base. Oral cavity SCC is the stated exception, with no skull-base CN coverage absent gross named-nerve or CN involvement.

- 🔍 Narrative review with author recommendations; dose data described as limited and mostly empirically derived
- 🔍 Adjuvant RT recommended by the authors in:
  - Cutaneous H&N SCC with extensive microscopic PNI or large-caliber nerve involvement
  - Aggressive salivary cancers (ACC, SDC) with microscopic PNI
  - Mucosal SCC with extensive microscopic PNI
  - Any primary with clinical or radiographic PNTS
- 📐 Dose by setting (author recommendations)

  | Setting | Dose |
  |---|---|
  | Tumor bed, microscopic PNI | 60 Gy |
  | Tumor bed, close or positive margin | 64 to 66 Gy |
  | Elective CN pathways | 50-60 Gy |
  | Elective CN pathways near skull base (cochlea, brain stem hard to spare) | De-escalate toward 50 Gy |
  | Extensive PNI or limited PNTS in resection bed, proximate nerve pathways | Up to 66 Gy |
  | Gross PNTS, definitive or incomplete resection | 70 Gy |

- 🔍 Extent and direction of coverage (Table 2)

  | Coverage | Indication |
  |---|---|
  | Tumor bed only | Microscopic focal intratumoral PNI |
  | Tumor bed + elective CN pathways | ACC or SDC; extensive PNI; large-caliber (>0.1 mm) or named nerve; close/positive margin along nerve; PNTS |
  | Retrograde (toward skull base) | Standard |
  | Anterograde (away from skull base) | Only if symptoms, operative report or imaging suggest it; cover in clinical/radiographic PNTS |
  | Communicating interconnections (e.g. V to VII) | Clinical or radiographic PNTS; consider in ACC or extensive PNI |

- 🔍 Elective nerve targets by primary site

  | Site | Elective nerve target | Trigger |
  |---|---|---|
  | Submandibular gland | V3 (lingual) to foramen ovale; XII to hypoglossal canal if involved; VII not elective | High-grade with extensive PNI, or ACC |
  | Parotid gland | Stylomastoid foramen + VII to geniculate ganglion/labyrinthine segment; ATN + V3 to foramen ovale if concern | ACC, SDC or high-grade with extensive PNI or PNTS along VII |
  | Parotid gland | Stylomastoid foramen + mastoid segment of VII; consider sparing cochlea | Focal microscopic PNI |
  | Hard palate | Palatine foramina + PPF to foramen rotundum | ACC or SCC with microscopic PNI |
  | Hard palate | Add Meckel cave, cavernous sinus, vidian nerve, GSPN to anterior genu | PNTS or extensive PNI |
  | Nasopharynx | ≥5 mm into posterior maxillary sinuses/choanae; bilateral PPF, rotundum, ovale, lacerum, parapharyngeal spaces; cavernous sinus; sphenoid; one third of clivus | T3-4 |
  | Oral cavity | Not routinely to skull base | Only with gross named-nerve or CN involvement |

- 📊 Definitive RT for gross PNTS in skin cancer: disease control 50% to 57%
- 📊 Unresectable or subtotally resected salivary (mainly ACC): definitive RT control 36% to 93%, photon or particle
- ⚠️ cSCC extensive PNI: RT 2-yr DSS 73% vs unirradiated DFS 40%; different endpoints, retrospective
- ⚠️ Oral cavity: PNI link to local failure contested; adjuvant RT for early-stage PNI-only disease unsettled
- 💊 Microscopic PNI alone in resected mucosal SCC: not an indication for concurrent systemic therapy
- 🔍 MRI with post-gadolinium fat-sat T1 is the gold standard for PNTS; PET limited at skull base

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.12.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.12.009)

## 🌰 Prostate

Two 2018 pieces: PSA-gated ADT with SRT and a practical guide to 60 Gy/20 fx; both predate SPPORT/RADICALS-HD and mature PACE data.

### Risk stratification to guide hormone therapy with salvage RT

> Pre-SRT PSA predicts ADT benefit: RTOG 9601 OS HR 1.13 at PSA <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5 ng/mL; argues against routine ADT with early SRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Pre-SRT PSA, not Gleason, is the variable that predicted ADT benefit in RTOG 9601: OS HR 1.13 at PSA <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5 ng/mL. The GETUG-AFU-16 biochemical gain at PSA <0.5 (12%) sits near the 10% RTOG 9408 gave low-risk intact pts, so it reads as PSA suppression, not disease control.

- 🔍 Single-author Q&A commentary (Red Journal 2018); no new patient data
- 📊 ADT benefit by pre-SRT PSA stratum, as cited in source

  | Trial · measure | Lower PSA stratum | Higher PSA stratum |
  |---|---|---|
  | RTOG 9601 · OS absolute change (PSA <0.7 vs >1.5) | 4.1% decrease | 24.6% improvement |
  | RTOG 9601 · OS HR (PSA <0.7 vs >1.5) | 1.13 | 0.45 |
  | GETUG-AFU-16 · crude biochemical control benefit (PSA <0.5 vs >0.5) | 12% | 36% |

- 📐 RTOG 9601: interaction test by pre-SRT PSA reported significant
- 📐 Higher Gleason score did not predict who benefits from added HT
- ⚠️ RTOG 9601 excluded PSA <0.5 for much of accrual; only ~10% had PSA ≤0.3
- ⚠️ GETUG-AFU-16 biochemical gain confounded: GnRH agonist keeps testosterone subnormal an additional 6-12 mo
- 🔗 RTOG 9408: 4 mo HT improved BCR by 10% even in low-risk intact prostate, near GETUG at PSA <0.5
- 🔗 ICECaP (>28,000 men on RCTs): BCR not a survival surrogate; DM correlation over 0.9
- 🔍 RT: 64 to 72 Gy judged sufficient for microscopic bed/pelvic disease at early SRT
- 📊 Simplified ADT-with-SRT framework (source Table 3). *Discuss HT if minimal comorbidity, 12-yr life expectancy, multiple high-risk features

  | ISUP GG (Gleason) | PSA 0.1-0.5 | PSA 0.6-1.0 | PSA 1.0-1.5 | PSA >1.5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 (6) | RT | RT | RT + STADT | RT + STADT |
  | 2, 3 (7) | RT | RT ± STADT* | RT + STADT | RT + LTADT |
  | 4, 5 (8-10) | RT* | RT + STADT | RT + LTADT | RT + LTADT |

- 💊 Author's trial routing
  - PSA <0.7: NRG GU-006 (SRT ± apalutamide); excludes PSA >1.0
  - PSA ≥0.7, pN+, or pT3b-T4 / pattern 5: FORMULA-509 or RTOG 3506 (ADT both arms + second-gen HT)
  - Persistent PSA ≥0.2: NRG GU-002 (docetaxel question)
- ⚠️ 2018 opinion; predates later salvage-ADT RCTs (SPPORT, RADICALS-HD) and PSMA PET restaging

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Evidence-based Risk Stratification to Guide Hormone Therapy Use With Salvage Radiation Therapy for Prostate Cancer** — Spratt, Daniel E.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2018-11). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.06.037](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.06.037)

### Moderate hypofractionation for prostate cancer: a user's guide

> 2018 practical review: CHHiP, PROFIT and NRG 0415 all non-inferior at 5-6 yr; 60 Gy/20 fx preferred, with planning aims and patient-selection caveats.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Most relative contraindications fall away on this evidence: the IPSS >12 GU signal is a post hoc FCCC subgroup unconfirmed in larger trials, 8% of CHHiP pts had prior TURP, and no trial excluded on gland size. The planning layer transfers directly: ~7 mm CTV-PTV margin with daily IGRT and whole-organ OAR contours.

- 📊 The three large non-inferiority RCTs, as summarized in review text

  | Trial | N / risk | Hypo vs conventional | ADT | Median f/u | Efficacy HR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | CHHiP | 3,216, predominantly int-risk | 60 Gy/20 or 57 Gy/19 vs 74 Gy/37 | 3–6 mo | 5.2 yr | 60 Gy arm: HR 0.84 (90% CI 0.68–1.03), pNI = 0.0018 |
  | PROFIT | 1,206, int-risk | 60 Gy/20 vs 78 Gy/39 | Not permitted | 6 yr | HR 0.96 (90% CI 0.77–1.2) |
  | NRG Oncology 0415 | 1,115, low-risk | 70 Gy/28 vs 73.8 Gy/41 | Not stated in text | 5.8 yr | DFS HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.64–1.14) |

- 📊 5-yr biochemical-clinical failure-free survival in the order of 85% to 90% across all three trials
- 📊 Late G≥3 toxicity 1%–4% across the three trials, regardless of schedule
- ⚠️ Late toxicity diverged between trials
  - PROFIT: lower late ≥G2 GI in the 4-week arm
  - NRG 0415: small increase in late G2 GI and GU with hypofractionation
  - NRG 0415 hypo arm carried higher BED (128 vs 118 Gy, α/β 3)
  - CHHiP: no difference in late effects on clinician- or patient-reported measures
- 🔗 HYPRO 64.6 Gy/19 was dose-escalated (EQD2 90 Gy at α/β 1.5), likely explaining its unfavourable toxicity
- 📊 Princess Margaret phase 2, 60 Gy/20, >10 yr f/u: any G3 toxicity 2%
  - Late ≥G2 GI: 4% at 5 yr, unchanged at 8 yr
  - Late ≥G2 GU: 9% at 5 yr, 12% at 8 yr
- 🔍 Relative contraindications: authors consider none firm
  - Baseline IPSS >12 GU signal is a post hoc FCCC subgroup, unconfirmed in larger trials
  - Prior TURP not an exclusion; 8% of CHHiP pts had one
  - No gland-size exclusion; PROFIT excluded only one pt for unmet constraints
  - Anticoagulation excluded in CHHiP only; Arcangeli G1 haematuria tracked bladder volume >40 Gy
- 🔍 Prescription aims, PROFIT vs CHHiP (PROFIT used 3 mm rectal/bladder walls; CHHiP whole organs)

  | Structure | PROFIT | CHHiP |
  |---|---|---|
  | CTV | D99 ≥ 60 Gy | Not listed |
  | PTV | D99 ≥ 57 Gy; D1cc ≤ 63 Gy | D99 ≥ 57 Gy; D1 ≤ 63 Gy; D50 = 60 Gy ±1% |
  | Rectum | V46 ≤ 30%; V37 ≤ 50% | V57 ≤ 15%; V40 ≤ 60% |
  | Bladder | V46 ≤ 30%; V37 ≤ 50% | V60 ≤ 5%; V48 ≤ 25%; V40 ≤ 50% |
  | Penile bulb | Not listed | V40 ≤ 50% |
  | Femoral head | V43 ≤ 5% | V40 ≤ 50% |

- 🔍 Technique recommendations
  - IMRT/VMAT with daily IGRT; CTV-PTV margin ~7 mm
  - Margins down to 3 to 5 mm with continuous intrafraction monitoring
  - SV: PROFIT proximal 1 cm if SV risk >15% (Partin); CHHiP proximal 2 cm if <15%, entire SV if >15% (Roach)
  - Conformity index <1.1; <1,000 MU per 3 Gy fraction generally achievable
- ⚠️ Elective nodal RT not evaluated in any large hypofractionation trial
- ⚠️ CHHiP high-risk 12% (almost 400 men) but excluded Gleason ≥9 and other very-high-risk features
- ⚠️ 2018 snapshot: HYPO-RT-PC cited as preliminary abstract only, PACE still ongoing at writing

- 📄 **Moderate hypofractionation for prostate cancer: A user's guide** — Pryor, David I; Turner, Sandra L; Tai, Keen Hun et al.. *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Oncology* (2018-01). [doi:10.1111/1754-9485.12703](https://doi.org/10.1111/1754-9485.12703)
  > Summary Three large randomised controlled trials have been published in the last year demonstrating the non‐inferiority of moderate hypofractionation compared to conventional fractionation for localised prostate cancer with respect to both disease control and late toxicity at 5 years. More mature follow‐up data are now also available from phase 2 studies confirming that moderate hypofractionation is associated with low rates of significant toxicity at 10 years. Here we briefly review the evidence, discuss issues of patient selection and provide an approach to implementing moderately hypofractionated radiation therapy for prostate cancer in clinical practice.

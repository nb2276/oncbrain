# 2026-09-28 — ASTRO Annual Meeting 2026

**SWOG S1827/MAVERICK: CFFS HR 0.60 (90% CI 0.46-0.78) favoring MRI surveillance alone over PCI 25 Gy/10 fx in LS/ES-SCLC; interim OS HR 0.90, final analysis pending.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> CNS-directed RT led the day: MRI surveillance beat PCI on CFFS in LS/ES-SCLC (HR 0.60, interim OS HR 0.90), and Cs-131 tiles cut SBR vs cavity SRT after brain met resection (HR 0.06). Prostate: PACE-A SBRT vs prostatectomy has only 13 BCF events; STOP SBRT + iADT in PSMA PET-oligomet HSPC/BCR cut 3-yr biochemical progression, 21% vs 60%.

## 🌰 Prostate

Two small randomised SBRT trials: PACE-A vs prostatectomy in localised low/intermediate-risk PCa (N=123, no NI margin in source) and STOP adding SBRT to iADT in PSMA PET-oligometastatic HSPC/BCR (n=60).

### STOP: iADT ± SBRT in PSMA PET-oligometastatic HSPC/BCR

> 3-yr biochemical progression 21% vs 60% (p=0.004) with SBRT added to iADT in PSMA PET+/CI-oligometastatic HSPC/BCR, n=60.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> ADT was fixed at 8-9 months in both arms, so the 21% vs 60% 3-yr biochemical progression gap isolates SBRT, delivered within 4 months of ADT. Dose and fractionation followed institutional protocols and are not reported in source, which limits transfer. ADT re-initiation trended lower (p=0.06).

### PACE-A · [[NCT01584258]]

> SBRT 36.25 Gy/5fx vs prostatectomy: 13 BCF events, HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.16-1.46), p=0.18; 5-yr BCF-free 94.8% vs 94.1% in localised low/intermediate-risk PCa.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Failure was scored at nadir+2 ng/ml after SBRT but >0.2 ng/ml after prostatectomy, so the BCF comparison is not symmetric; salvage is the cleaner read (RT in 5 prostatectomy pts, surgery in 1 SBRT pt). At 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions without ADT, this informs counselling surgical candidates on definitive SBRT.

- 📄 **Efficacy of Radical Prostatectomy Versus Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT) for Localised Prostate Cancer: Results from an international Phase III Randomised Controlled Trial (PACE - A)** — N. van As; J. Patel; A. Tree et al.. *ASTRO 2026 Annual Meeting* (2026-09). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.3129](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.3129). [article](https://amportal.astro.org/sessions/pl-01-22946/efficacy-of-radical-prostatectomy-versus-stereotactic-body-radiotherapy-sbrt-for-localised-pr-114308)
  > ASTRO 2026 abstract LBA 32 · PL 01 - Plenary Session · 2026-09-28 · presented by Nicholas van As, MD, MB
  > Purpose/Objective(s): Treatment selection for localised prostate cancer (LPCa) is often influenced by expected functional outcomes, assuming comparable cancer control between treatments. Randomised evidence comparing efficacy of radical prostatectomy and stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) is lacking.
  > Materials/Methods: PACE ([[NCT01584258]]) is phase III, open label, multicohort randomised controlled trial. In PACE-A, men with LPCa (cT1-T2, Gleason score =3+4, PSA =20 ng/mL) who were suitable candidates for prostatectomy were randomised (1:1) to SBRT or prostatectomy. SBRT was delivered to 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions over 1-2 weeks. Prostatectomy could be open, laparoscopic, or robot assisted. Androgen deprivation therapy was not permitted. Freedom from biochemical/clinical failure (BCF), based on PSA rises (nadir+2ng/ml or >0.2 ng/ml post radiotherapy or prostatectomy) or commencement of ADT or recurrence or metastases or prostate cancer death was analysed using Cox proportional hazards and log-rank test by intention to treat.
  > Results: 123 patients were randomised from 8 centres between August 2012 and February 2022 (60 prostatectomy, 63 SBRT). Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between groups. Median age was 65.5 years (IQR 60.5-68.6), median pre-treatment PSA was 7.9ng/mL (IQR 5.9-11.0), 5.7% and 94.3% of patients had low/intermediate-risk disease, respectively. With median follow-up of 8 years (IQR: 6.1 – 8.0), 13 BCF events had occurred (5 SBRT, 8 prostatectomy), unadjusted hazard ratio 0.48 (95% CI 0:16-1.46; p=0.18). The estimated absolute difference at 5 years (based on the HR) is 3.1% (95% CI -2.6, 5.0) with 5-year BCF-free rates 94.8% (84.6, 98.3) and 94.1% (82.6, 98.1) for SBRT and prostatectomy. 8-year rates were 91.2% (80.1, 96.2) and 83.7% (68.4, 92.0). Subsequent treatment for progression included surgery in 1 SBRT patient and radiotherapy in 5 prostatectomy patients. ADT was administered to 5 patients (3 prostatectomy, 2 SBRT). At 5 years, using Expanded Prostate Cancer Index Composite 26-item (EPIC-26) questionnaire, use of =1 urinary pad was reported by 3/36(8.3%) SBRT patients and 14/29(48%) prostatectomy patients. 1/28(3.6%) prostatectomy patient and 0 SBRT patients reported moderate/big bowel function problem. Clinician reported CTCAE grade =2 gastrointestinal side effects were reported by 1/54(2%) patient receiving SBRT and 0 prostatectomy patients. Grade =2 genitourinary side effects occurred in 2/53 (3.8%) receiving SBRT and1/40 (2.5%) patients undergoing prostatectomy.
  > Conclusion: With long-term follow-up, both prostatectomy and SBRT achieved excellent biochemical control in patients with localised prostate cancer. These findings support SBRT as an effective non-surgical treatment option with comparable long-term disease control to prostatectomy.

## 🧠 CNS

ROADS tests intraoperative Cs-131 tiles against post-op cavity SRT after brain met resection; 17.8% of the SRT arm never received SRT and median f/u is 12.9 mo.

### ROADS · [[NCT04365374]]

> Time-to-SBR HR 0.06 (0.01-0.46) for Cs-131 tiles vs post-op cavity SRT after brain met resection; 12-mo SBR 1.3% vs 15.4%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The control was not a weak arm: 92.2% got multi-fraction cavity SRT (27 Gy/3 or 30 Gy/5) and 12-mo SBR was 15.4%, in line with published fractionated series. The SBR HR stayed 0.06 in per-protocol while OS did not hold (HR 0.73, p=0.2156), so the cavity-control decision moves, the survival claim does not. TBRT prescription dose is not in the source text.

- 📊 R+TBRT vs R+SRT, mITT (103 vs 101)
- 🔍 Randomized, open-label, non-inferiority phase 3, 32 US centers, 230 randomized 1:1 pre-operatively
- 🔍 Eligibility: newly diagnosed, one surgical index brain metastasis 2.0-7.0 cm
- 🔍 Control cavity SRT: 17-20 Gy/1, 27 Gy/3 or 30 Gy/5, multi-fraction in 92.2%
- 🔍 Median surgery-to-SRT 27.0 days (range 6-47); non-resected mets got SRT in both arms
- 📐 NI margin HR 1.26; hierarchical testing NI → superiority → OS, 95.04% CIs
- ⚠️ 17.8% of R+SRT never received SRT (18 pts: 8 died, 5 withdrew, 3 hospice)
- ⚠️ OS lost in per-protocol: 42.5 vs 20.2 mo, HR 0.73 (0.44-1.20), p=0.2156
- ⚠️ SBR HR held in per-protocol: HR 0.06 (0.01-0.46), p=0.0069
- ⚠️ Median f/u 12.9 mo; seed MRI artifact may have unblinded central reviewers
- ⚠️ TBRT prescription dose not reported in source text
- 🔗 Control 12-mo SBR 15.4% matches fractionated cavity SRT series (12-mo freedom-from-SBR 84-87%)

- 📄 **Surgery and tile-based radiation therapy versus surgery and stereotactic radiation for newly diagnosed brain metastases (ROADS): a randomized, open-label, phase 3 trial** — Weinberg, Jeffrey S.; Imber, Brandon S.; DiNapoli, Vincent et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-09). [doi:10.1200/jco-26-01894](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-26-01894)
  > PURPOSE Post-operative stereotactic radiation (SRT) is the standard-of-care for resected brain metastases. Implantation of cesium-131 collagen tiles (tile-based radiation therapy, TBRT) initiates focal radiation immediately after resection, potentially offering therapeutic and logistical advantages. ROADS: a randomized, open-label, non-inferiority, phase 3 trial ( [[NCT04365374]] ) compared the safety and efficacy of resection with TBRT (R+TBRT) to resection with SRT (R+SRT) for patients with a newly diagnosed brain metastasis indicated for surgical resection. PATIENTS AND METHODS Across 32 United States centers, patients were pre-operatively randomized 1:1 to resection R+TBRT or R+SRT. Any non-resected brain metastases received SRT post-operatively. Co-primary outcomes were time-to-surgical bed recurrence (SBR) and surgical bed recurrence-free survival (SB-RFS). Outcomes were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards models with stratification factors as covariates. Multiplicity was controlled by hierarchical testing. Analyses used the pre-specified modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population (patients who underwent surgery, had pathologic confirmation of brain metastasis, and had follow-up information). RESULTS From April 2021 through August 2025, 230 patients were randomized (115 per arm); 204 of whom (103 R+TBRT, 101 R+SRT) comprised the mITT population. Median follow-up was 12.9 months. Median time-to-SBR was not reached (R+TBRT) versus 17.4 months (R+SRT) (hazard ratio [HR]: 0.06; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.01–0.46; p=0.0070). SB-RFS was improved with R+TBRT, with a median of not reached versus 10.9 months (R+SRT) (HR: 0.48; 95% CI: 0.30–0.76; p=0.0021). Overall adverse events did not appear to differ: 83 patients (79.0%, 95% CI: 70.0–86.4) (R+TBRT) versus 67 patients (80.7%, 95% CI:70.6–88.6) (R+SRT). CONCLUSION For patients with newly diagnosed brain metastases requiring resection, R+TBRT significantly improved SBR and SB-RFS, demonstrating both non-inferiority and superiority.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

PCI omission in SCLC with MRI surveillance: CFFS favors omission, OS non-inferiority (upper 90% CI bound 1.25) awaits the final analysis at 190 deaths.

### SWOG S1827/MAVERICK

> CFFS HR 0.60 (90% CI 0.46-0.78) favoring MRI surveillance alone over PCI 25 Gy/10 fx in LS/ES-SCLC; interim OS HR 0.90.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The HA-PCI escape route is closed: 77% of PCI was hippocampal-avoidant, and MRI-arm pts pre-declared for HA-PCI still did better than HA-PCI (HR 0.63, 90% CI 0.47-0.83). The omission decision now rests on OS, where the interim upper bound of 1.20 sits inside the 1.25 margin with 190 deaths still pending.

- 📄 **SWOG S1827/MAVERICK: A Randomized Phase III Trial of Brain MRI Surveillance with and without Prophylactic Cranial Irradiation (PCI) for Small-Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)** — C. G. Rusthoven; M. Redman; P. D. Brown et al.. *ASTRO 2026 Annual Meeting* (2026-09). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.2463](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.2463). [article](https://amportal.astro.org/sessions/ct-01-22948/swog-s1827-maverick-a-randomized-phase-iii-trial-of-brain-mri-surveillance-with-and-without-p-114268)
  > ASTRO 2026 abstract LBA 03 · CT 01 - Clinical Trials Session · 2026-09-27 · presented by Paul Brown, MD
  > Purpose/Objective(s): For SCLC, studies from the pre-MRI era demonstrated a reduction in brain metastases (BM) and improved overall survival (OS) with PCI. We hypothesized that brain MRI surveillance without PCI would result in improved cognitive failure free survival (CFFS) without a decline in OS.
  > Materials/Methods: This international phase III trial enrolled patients (pts) with limited (LS) and extensive-stage (ES) SCLC who had completed initial therapy, with no BM on MRI prior to enrollment. Pts were randomized to MRI surveillance +/- PCI (25 Gy in 10 Fx), stratified by stage (LS/ES), receipt of immunotherapy (IO), and PS (0-1 vs 2). MRIs were q3 months during year 1 and q6 months during year 2 in both arms. Cognitive testing was performed at the same time points using the Hopkins Verbal Learning Test-Revised, Controlled Oral Word Association, and Trail Making Test. The primary objective was to compare CFFS defined as the time to first cognitive failure (CF) on any test or death, using a 1-sided 0.05 level log-rank test. The key secondary objective was to evaluate if OS was not inferior with MRI alone, with a non-inferiority margin for the upper bound 90% CI of 1.25. The final OS analysis will take place after 190 deaths. Additional objectives included comparing cumulative incidence of CF by Fine-Gray, BM-free survival, and toxicities. Patient reported cognitive concerns (via the MDASI-BT cognitive factor score and the PROMIS CFSF4a) and overall quality of life (QOL) (single-item LASA) were analyzed and preliminary results are presented.
  > Results: From 01/2020 to 12/2025, 304 pts were randomly assigned; 68% had LS-SCLC. The median follow-up for living pts is 22 months. CFFS was improved with MRI alone (HR 0.60, 90% CI 0.46-0.78, p=0.001). Subgroup analyses of CFFS demonstrated no significant differences in the benefit of MRI alone by either stage (LS/ES, interaction-p=0.92) or receipt of IO (yes/no, interaction-p=0.25). Of pts receiving PCI, 77% had hippocampal-avoidant (HA)-PCI. CFFS was superior for pts in the MRI arm who would have received HA-PCI (declared pre-randomization) compared to HA-PCI (HR 0.63, 90% CI 0.47-0.83, p=0.006). Preliminary analysis of OS at 128 deaths suggests no difference in OS (HR for MRI alone vs MRI+PCI: 0.90, 90% CI 0.67-1.20). There was a decreased risk of CF with MRI alone (subdistribution HR 0.62, 90% CI 0.48-0.79, p=0.001). At the Day 180 primary endpoint assessment for patient reported outcomes, no statistically significant differences in PROMIS or MDASI cognitive concerns (p=0.07 and 0.69, respectively) were found; however, pre-specified longitudinal modeling demonstrated improvements with MRI alone in PROMIS cognitive concerns that were significant at all time points and improved overall QOL on LASA at 90 and 180 days (all p<0.05).
  > Conclusion: Compared to MRI+PCI, MRI alone is associated with superior CFFS, reduced cognitive failure, and improvements in longitudinal QOL outcomes. These results support MRI surveillance alone as the standard of care for pts with SCLC.

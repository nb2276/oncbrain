# 2026-05-17 — ESTRO Congress 2026

**PEACE-2: elective pelvic RT misses cPFS (HR 0.81, p=0.088), splitting from POP-RT (bPFS HR 0.50); whole-pelvis nodal benefit now contested.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate led with the elective pelvic-RT question splitting: PEACE-2 null on cPFS against POP-RT's positive biochemical signal. Breast brought long-horizon de-escalation reads (APBI, hypofx, boost/IM-MS omission). OLIGOMA delivered the first positive breast-oligomet MDT RCT (mPFS 35.8 vs 20.4, HR 0.48).

## 🌰 Prostate

Elective pelvic nodal RT is the meeting's contested question: PEACE-2 null on cPFS against POP-RT's HR 0.50, with PACE-NODES and PIVOTALboost reporting only acute bowel cost and neither efficacy endpoint mature.

### POP-RT vs PEACE-2

> WPRT bFFS HR 0.50 (POP-RT) vs bPFS HR 0.97 (PEACE-2); pelvic RT benefit vanishes with 3yr ADT, PSMA staging.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The reversal tracks four coupled changes, and the one an RT reader controls is target volume: with 36 months ADT, 78 Gy EQD2 and PSMA staging, WPRT bought nothing biochemically (HR 0.97, p=0.73) where it halved biochemical failure at 24 months ADT and 74-76 Gy (HR 0.50). This moves elective nodal coverage toward optional in PSMA-staged very-high-risk N0M0 on 3 years of ADT, not in conventionally staged pts.

![POP-RT vs PEACE-2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIkfsYnaYAAfa1-.jpg)

| Endpoint | POP-RT HR (95% CI), p | PEACE-2 HR (95% CI), p |
|---|---|---|
| bFFS / bPFS | 0.50 (0.42-0.61), p<0.001 | 0.97 (0.81-1.16), p=0.73 |
| cFFS / cPFS | 0.74 (0.61-0.90), p=0.002 | 0.81 (0.63-1.03), p=0.09 |
| MFS | 0.72 (0.58-0.89), p=0.002 | 0.93 (0.74-1.17), p=0.54 |

- 🔍 Curator-made comparison graphic, not a formal pooled analysis; both trials phase III open-label randomized WPRT vs prostate-only RT
- 📊 WPRT vs prostate-only RT across the two trials (HR <1 favors WPRT)

  | Endpoint | POP-RT | PEACE-2 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Biochemical (bFFS / bPFS) | HR 0.50 (0.42-0.61), p<0.001 | HR 0.97 (0.81-1.16), p=0.73 |
  | Clinical (cFFS / cPFS) | HR 0.74 (0.61-0.90), p=0.002 | HR 0.81 (0.63-1.03), p=0.09 |
  | MFS | HR 0.72 (0.58-0.89), p=0.002 | HR 0.93 (0.74-1.17), p=0.54 |

- 🔍 The four differences that plausibly explain the divergence
  - ADT: 24 months (POP-RT) vs 36 months (PEACE-2)
  - Staging: conventional CT/bone scan vs PSMA PET/CT widely used
  - Prostate dose: 74-76 Gy EQD2 vs 78 Gy EQD2
  - Population: high/very-high-risk N0M0 vs very-high-risk N0M0 only
- 📐 PEACE-2 is an interim analysis at ESTRO 2026, median f/u ~5.5 yr, vs POP-RT median 6.3-7.2 yr updated
- 🔍 Accrual eras barely overlap: 2011-2016 vs 2018-2023, so systemic and staging practice differ throughout
- ⚠️ PSMA PET staging in PEACE-2 removes occult N1 pts POP-RT would have called N0 and treated: the population that stands to gain from elective pelvic RT is partly staged out
- ⚠️ cPFS HR 0.81 p=0.09 is the only surviving PEACE-2 signal; a borderline secondary at interim, not evidence of benefit
- 🔗 Both trials report comparable Grade ≥2 late GU toxicity between arms; WPRT added no significant toxicity in either
- ⚠️ Cross-trial HR comparison is not a randomized comparison: no formal interaction test, and the ADT, dose, staging and era differences are confounded with each other

### PRIME · [[NCT03561961]]

> Interim: acute grade ≥2 GU ~5.4% vs ~4.0% (p=0.59) for 5-fx SBRT vs 25-fx, both with whole-pelvis RT; BFFS immature.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable parameter is the nodal dose: 25 Gy in 5 fractions to elective pelvis in both arms, with SIB to involved nodes permitted only in the SBRT arm. Late grade ≥2 GU ran ~10-12% vs ~9-11% at 1-2 yr, so the 5-fraction schedule carried no toxicity penalty over 25 fractions in a node-covered field.

### PIVOTALboost

> Adding pelvic nodal IMRT to a focal prostate boost in 20fx raised early bowel toxicity only; 2yr G2+ rates similar across arms.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 2-year cumulative G2+ rates run in the wrong direction for a toxicity argument against nodal RT: bowel 6.5% (4.5-9.5) and bladder 16.5% (13.1-20.6) in the nodes+boost arm, below both prostate-only arms. Whatever excess bowel toxicity nodal RT causes lives inside 18 weeks. That removes late toxicity as the reason to omit pelvic nodes in 20fx, and leaves the decision resting entirely on the unreported efficacy endpoint.

| Arm | Bowel G2+ (95% CI) | Bladder G2+ (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Prostate (n=281) | 8.2% (5.7-11.7) | 19.5% (15.5-24.4) |
| Prostate+Boost (n=345) | 8.7% (6.3-12.1) | 24.1% (20.2-28.7) |
| Prostate+Nodes+Boost (n=347) | 6.5% (4.5-9.5) | 16.5% (13.1-20.6) |

![N=1465 randomised, 39 UK centres. Prostate IMRT 388, Prostate+Boost 464, Prostate+Nodes+Boost 462.](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIiVWumXYAAAIXc.jpg)
*N=1465 randomised, 39 UK centres. Prostate IMRT 388, Prostate+Boost 464, Prostate+Nodes+Boost 462.*

- 🔍 Phase 3 RCT, N=1465 randomised across 39 UK centres; 1° EP is biochemical/clinical failure, not reported here
- 🔍 Three arms: prostate IMRT alone (n=388), prostate IMRT + focal boost (n=464), prostate + pelvic nodal IMRT + boost (n=462)
- 📐 20-fraction moderate hypofractionation throughout; dose per arm not stated in source slides
- 📊 Early (<18wk) bowel side effects increased with pelvic nodal RT; the slide reports no significant arm differences at 2 years
- 📊 Presenter's read: nodal RT bowel toxicity resolves by 18 weeks post-RT
- ⚠️ Toxicity is a SECONDARY endpoint; the efficacy question (does nodal RT or the boost improve biochemical/clinical failure) is unreported
- ⚠️ Late analysis covers 973/1314 (74%) with ≥2yr follow-up, so the 2-year cumulative rates are not the full randomised cohort
- ⚠️ Grade scale and reporting instrument (clinician vs patient-reported) not stated in the source slides
- 🔗 Sits against POP-RT (nodal RT benefit in bFFS) and PEACE-2 (null); PIVOTALboost is powered to arbitrate in the 20fx era but has not yet reported that endpoint

### PACE-NODES

> CTCAE G2+ GI toxicity 28% vs 21% with added pelvic nodal SBRT; no GU difference, symptoms resolved by 12wk.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Nodal SBRT at 25Gy/5f costs 7 points of acute G2+ GI (28% vs 21%) and nothing in GU, with the EPIC-26 bowel signal at 4 weeks resolving by 12. That makes acute tolerability a weak argument against 5f elective nodal coverage; the decision now waits on late effects and the immature failure endpoint.

![PACE-NODES](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhsLTUXoAAbiFs.jpg)

| Endpoint | PPN-SBRT | P-SBRT |
|---|---|---|
| CTCAE G2+ GI, 12wk | 28% | 21% |
| Did not receive allocation | 11% | 4% |

![Prostate 36.25Gy/5f both arms; nodes 25Gy/5f in PPN-SBRT. Target n=1128.](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhsLcDXAAAcNgO.jpg)
*Prostate 36.25Gy/5f both arms; nodes 25Gy/5f in PPN-SBRT. Target n=1128.*

- 🔍 Phase 3 randomised 1:1, high-risk localised prostate, target n=1128; 1166 randomised
- 🔍 Eligibility: T3a-T4 and/or Gleason 8-10 and/or PSA>20ng/ml, planned 12-36mo ADT
- 📐 P-SBRT arm: 36.25Gy/5f to prostate, alternate days
- 📐 PPN-SBRT arm: 36.25Gy/5f prostate + 25Gy/5f pelvic nodes, alternate days
- 📊 Acute toxicity co-primaries: CTCAE G2+ GI and G2+ GU up to 12 weeks
- 📊 G2+ GI over the 12-week period 28% PPN-SBRT vs 21% P-SBRT
- 📊 No difference in acute GU toxicity, clinician- or patient-reported
- 📊 EPIC-26 bowel domain showed the GI difference at 4 weeks; PRO collected at 4wk only
- ⚠️ 11% PPN-SBRT vs 4% P-SBRT did not receive allocated treatment, mostly unmet constraints
- ⚠️ Efficacy primary (time to biochemical or clinical failure) not yet mature; this is a toxicity-only readout
- 🔗 Nodal RT benefit to date rests on moderate hypofractionation (POP-RT); PACE-NODES tests whether 5f delivers it

### PEACE-2

> cPFS HR 0.81 (0.63-1.03), p=0.088: pelvic RT over prostate-only missed its primary endpoint in very-high-risk N0M0.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The target-volume decision is the whole trial: ADT × 3 years and the systemic backbone were fixed, so the 4.2-point 7yr cPFS gap (67.1% vs 62.9%, p=0.088) is what elective pelvic coverage buys in conventionally-staged N0M0 disease. No dose or fractionation appears in the source slides, and staging used conventional imaging or choline PET, not PSMA.

![PEACE-2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhXl7VWkAAwqHi.jpg)

| Arm | 7yr cPFS | HR (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pelvic RT | 67.1% [61.6; 72.2] | 0.81 [0.63; 1.03] | 0.088 |
| Prostate only RT | 62.9% [57.4; 68.1] | n/a | n/a |

![PEACE-2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhXl7XXMAADF2L.jpg)

![PEACE-2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhXl7ZWkAAoHej.jpg)

- 📊 1° EP cPFS negative: HR 0.81, 95%CI 0.63-1.03, p=0.088 (multivariable)
- 📊 7yr cPFS 67.1% (61.6-72.2) pelvic RT vs 62.9% (57.4-68.1) prostate-only RT
- 🔍 International multicenter randomized trial, 2x2 design: ADT+RT ± cabazitaxel × 4, prostate vs pelvic target volume
- 🔍 N at risk 381 pelvic RT vs 380 prostate-only RT at randomization
- 🔍 Eligibility: very-high-risk localized CaP, ≥2 of Gleason ≥8 / T3-T4 / PSA ≥20; N0M0 by conventional imaging or choline PET/CT
- 💊 ADT × 3 years in all arms; RT target volume is the randomized variable, systemic backbone held constant
- ⚠️ No RT dose or fractionation reported in source slides; transferability to a reader's technique unverifiable here
- ⚠️ Staging predates PSMA PET: pelvic nodal micrometastasis in the prostate-only arm is undetected, which biases toward the null if node-positive pts were enrolled as N0
- 🔗 Contrast POP-RT (single-center, N=224), which reported a bPFS and DFS benefit for whole-pelvis RT; PEACE-2's larger multicenter cohort does not reproduce a comparable cPFS signal
- 📊 Curator's read from the plenary conclusion slide: fewer than 1 in 10 men died of prostate cancer within the first decade, which questions the "very high-risk" label itself

## 🎀 Breast

Long-horizon randomised data pushing toward less: 20yr IM-MS OS flat, no case for boost escalation beyond 48Gy/15F, boost omission supported at 0-2 risk factors, but DBCG RT Natural shows omitting PBI outright fails its own 4% threshold.

### OLIGOMA · [[NCT04495309]]

> mPFS 35.8 vs 20.4mo, HR 0.48 (0.25-0.91) p=0.021 with ablative RT to all lesions in oligometastatic breast.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT question here is whether ALL lesions must be treated: eligibility required ablative RT to every metastasis, and pts needing palliative RT to all sites were excluded, so this is comprehensive ablation, not selective consolidation. With 2/3 bone lesions and >80% carrying 1-3 mets, the transferable case is the low-burden bone-dominant pt. No dose or fractionation reported in source.

| Experimental | 72.2 (67.2-77.2) | -2.1 (-9.2-5.1) |
| Control | 74.3 (69.3-79.3) | n/a |

- 📊 Co-1° EPs: PFS and EORTC QLQ-C30 at 12 weeks post-randomisation, both met
- 📐 PFS HR 0.48 (95%-CI 0.25-0.91), p=0.021; median 35.8mo (24.1-NR) vs 20.4mo (12.4-28.4)
- 🔍 Randomised, systemic therapy ± local ablative RT to ALL metastatic lesions; stratified by systemic regimen type and treatment line
- 🔍 Eligibility: metastatic breast, any treatment line, max 5 lesions; systemic regimen fixed at MDT board before randomisation
- 🔍 Palliative RT to symptomatic mets allowed, but pts needing palliative RT to all mets were excluded
- 📊 >80% had 1-3 metastases; 2/3 of lesions were bone
- ⚠️ Recruitment stopped early: <20% of the initial target, <40% of the amended target. 43 experimental / 44 control
- ⚠️ PFS CI is wide (0.25-0.91) on 87 pts; a 15-month median gap from an underaccrued trial will shrink on replication
- ⚠️ Bone-dominant, ER+/HER2- first-line population: the setting where systemic control is best and MDT's marginal contribution is hardest to isolate
- 🔗 Adds breast-specific randomised evidence to SABR-COMET (mixed histology) and the prostate MDT trials; 8 confirmatory trials still accruing (TAORMINA, STEREO-SEIN, LARA, CLEAR, COSMO, ARCHER, ISTMET, OLIGAMI)

### APBI-IMRT Florence · [[NCT02104895]]

> 15-yr IBTR 7.7% APBI vs 4.2% WBI, HR 1.57 (0.82-3.04) p=0.17; excess driven by new ipsilateral primaries, not true local relapse.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 5-fraction 30Gy IMRT schedule is what transfers: this is the longest follow-up for that specific PBI regimen, and the 15-yr excess sits in new ipsilateral primaries (5.9% vs 2.7%, p=0.09) rather than local relapse (2.1% vs 1.6%, p=0.75). That distinction is the whole case for keeping PBI in Florence-eligible pts, and it rests on adjudication, not on a powered endpoint.

![APBI-IMRT Florence](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhdbJaWEAAdjH2.jpg)

*IBTR: HR 1.57 (95% CI 0.82-3.04), p=0.17. 15-yr IBTR 20 (7.7%) APBI vs 11 (4.2%) WBI, p=0.14; local relapse 5 (2.1%) vs 4 (1.6%), p=0.75; new ipsilateral 15 (5.9%) vs 7 (2.7%), p=0.09.*

![Phase III, n=520, 1:1 (2005-2013). PBI IMRT 30Gy in 5#, n=260; WBI 50Gy in 25# + 10Gy in 5# TBB, n=260. 5-year estimated IBTR 3%, equivalence 5%, 80% power.](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HIhdbJcXQAAOErF.jpg)
*Phase III, n=520, 1:1 (2005-2013). PBI IMRT 30Gy in 5#, n=260; WBI 50Gy in 25# + 10Gy in 5# TBB, n=260. 5-year estimated IBTR 3%, equivalence 5%, 80% power.*

- 📊 15-yr IBTR 20 (7.7%) APBI vs 11 (4.2%) WBI, p=0.14; HR 1.57 (95% CI 0.82-3.04), p=0.17
- 🔍 Split of that IBTR signal: true local relapse 5 (2.1%) vs 4 (1.6%), p=0.75; new ipsilateral primary 15 (5.9%) vs 7 (2.7%), p=0.09
- 🔍 Phase III equivalence, N=520, 1:1, accrual 2005-2013; powered 80% on a 5-yr estimated IBTR of 3% with a 5% equivalence margin
- 🔍 Eligibility: BCS, pT <25 mm, final surgical margins ≥5 mm, age >40 yrs
- 📐 PBI arm 30Gy in 5# IMRT; WBI arm 50Gy in 25# plus 10Gy in 5# tumour-bed boost
- 📊 Locoregional recurrence 20 (7.2%) vs 13 (5.0%), p=0.28 at 15 yrs; no signal separating the arms
- 📊 Distant metastasis 7 (2.7%) vs 12 (4.6%), p=0.35; deaths 56 (21.5%) vs 51 (19.6%), p=0.66; breast-cancer deaths 6 (2.3%) vs 8 (3.1%), p=0.79
- ⚠️ Trial powered for 5-yr equivalence, not for a 15-yr IBTR difference; the HR 1.57 point estimate with a CI to 3.04 cannot exclude a real excess
- ⚠️ New-primary vs true-relapse adjudication is the load-bearing claim, and at p=0.09 the new-primary excess is the larger of the two components
- ⚠️ Toxicity and cosmesis were per-protocol (14 withdrew) while survival outcomes were ITT; no toxicity numbers in this source

### EORTC IM-MS (22922/10925)

> 20yr OS 61.0% vs 61.8%, HR 1.00 (0.90-1.10), P=0.967: the 15yr breast-cancer mortality benefit is erased by non-BCM.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 20yr null OS is a competing-risk cancellation, not absent efficacy: BCM 18.6% vs 22.4% (HR 0.82) offset by non-BCM 20.4% vs 15.8% (HR 1.26), with cardiac disease 15.2% vs 11.7% and lung fibrosis 6.3% vs 3.2%. Whether IM coverage survives depends entirely on your heart dose, and DBCG IMN2 ran 4-9x lower.

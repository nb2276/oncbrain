# 2026-07-26

**PACE-B 5-yr PROMs: leak-free 64% SBRT vs 69% CRT (p=0.19), no domain differing — 36.25 Gy/5 fx holds its late tolerability against conventional RT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carries the day, and it is a tolerability readout rather than an efficacy one: at 85-mo median f/u the 2-yr urinary excess after 5-fraction SBRT has converged, with pad-free 91% vs 90% and identical bowel and potency outcomes. EPIC-26 completion near 60% caps how firmly the 5-point leak-free gap can be read.

## 🌰 Prostate

Late PROMs from the only phase 3 randomising 5-fraction SBRT against conventional fractionation in low-/intermediate-risk disease.

### PACE-B

> 5-yr PROMs: leak-free 64% (164/258) SBRT vs 69% (172/249) CRT, p=0.19; no domain differed significantly.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transient 2-yr urinary leakage excess after SBRT converged by 5 yr, which is the number that settles the fractionation conversation: 36.25 Gy/5 fx carried no durable continence penalty against 78 Gy/39 fx or 62 Gy/20 fx. Note the irritative/obstructive domain was not collected, so the symptom cluster patients complain of most after SBRT is unmeasured here.

- 🔍 Phase 3 international RCT, 874 randomised, 844 analysed (SBRT=414, CRT=430); median f/u 85.7 vs 85.6 mo
- 🔍 SBRT 36.25 Gy/5 fx vs CRT 78 Gy/39 fx or 62 Gy/20 fx; open-label, stratified by centre + NCCN risk (low vs intermediate)
- 📊 Pad-free at 5 yr 91% (233/257) SBRT vs 90% (225/250) CRT; leak-free AND pad-free 88% both arms (160/181 vs 169/191)
- 📊 Moderate/big urinary leakage problems 6% (15/250) SBRT vs 4% (9/244) CRT, up from 3% both arms at baseline
- 📊 Intercourse-adequate erections fell 36% (133/374) to 17% (43/250) SBRT; 40% (157/391) to 20% (47/240) CRT
- 📊 Among men potent at baseline, 41% retained intercourse-adequate function at 5 yr in both arms (SBRT 34/82, CRT 38/92)
- 📊 Moderate/big bowel problems 5% both arms at 5 yr (SBRT 12/265, CRT 13/253), from 2% at baseline
- 🔗 vs TrueNTH robotic prostatectomy 1-yr PROMs: 42% leak- and pad-free, 6% of baseline-potent men retained intercourse-adequate erections
- 🔗 Authors contextualise against ProtecT active monitoring at 12 yr (pad use ~10%, intercourse-adequate erections 30%, weekly faecal leakage 6%) and CEASAR, which found no clinically meaningful EPIC-26 difference between EBRT and surveillance over 5 yr
- ⚠️ EPIC-26 completion fell to 62% (269/433) SBRT and 58% (256/441) CRT at 5 yr; differential attrition can move a 5-point leak-free gap
- ⚠️ The 2-yr excess in urinary leakage problems after SBRT (mirroring physician-reported GU events) had converged by 5 yr, so the reassurance is about the late timepoint, not the whole course
- ⚠️ EPIC-26 irritative/obstructive domain was not collected, and no analysis by image-guidance method; low-/intermediate-risk only
- ⚠️ Cross-modality comparison with TrueNTH is between separate cohorts at different timepoints (5 yr vs 1 yr), not a randomised RT-vs-surgery contrast

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 42476886]] **Patient-reported Outcomes After Prostate Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy at 5 yr: Results from the PACE-B Trial.** — Cooper; Patel; Moore et al.. *European urology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.eururo.2026.05.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2026.05.034)
  > Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) complement oncological endpoints by capturing what matters most to patients. The TrueNTH surgical collaboration presented 1-yr PROMs following robotic prostatectomy using accessible visual formats. We present 5-yr PROMs from the phase 3, international PACE-B trial, which randomised men with localised prostate cancer to stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) or conventionally fractionated radiotherapy (CRT). PROMs are reported from baseline to 5&#xa0;yr and presented using waffle charts to enable visual alignment with surgical outcomes. At 5&#xa0;yr, urinary incontinence outcomes were favourable and comparable between SBRT and CRT. Leak-free rates were 64% (164/258) for SBRT and 69% (172/249) for CRT, pad-free rates were 91% (233/257) for SBRT and 90% (225/250) for CRT, and moderate or big urinary leakage problems were reported by only 6% (15/250) for SBRT and 4% (9/244) for CRT. Intercourse-adequate erections declined in both groups from baseline to 5&#xa0;yr: from 35% (133/374) to 17% (43/250) for SBRT, and from 40% (157/391) to 20% (47/240) for CRT. Moderate or big sexual problems increased in both groups, from 24% (87/367) to 31% (74/242) for SBRT and from 22% (85/382) to 32% (77/238) for CRT. Bowel effects were low and comparable between groups, with moderate or big bowel problems reported by 2% (7/397) at baseline and 5% (12/265) for SBRT at 5&#xa0;yr, and 2% (9/415) at baseline and 5% (13/253) for CRT at 5&#xa0;yr. Stool incontinence as a moderate or big problem rose from <1% (1/374) to 2% (4/240) in the SBRT group and from <1% (1/395) to 3% (7/238) in the CRT group.

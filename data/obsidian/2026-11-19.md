# 2026-11-19

**HyTEC prostate SBRT TCP: EQD2 90 Gy (36.1 Gy/5fx) → 95% 5-yr TCP in low-intermediate risk; high risk needs EQD2 102 Gy (38.7 Gy/5fx).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> HyTEC/AAPM SBRT dose-response day across prostate, thoracic, liver and H&N, all pooled modeling of published series, no randomised data. Prostate led: 36.1 Gy/5fx → 95% 5-yr TCP in low-intermediate risk; urethra Dmax ≥47 Gy and rectal C39Gy ≥40% flag late toxicity. Stage I NSCLC LC plateaus at 50±1 Gy/5fx PTV periphery.

## 🌰 Prostate

Two HyTEC prostate SBRT reviews: modeled TCP dose thresholds by risk group plus dose-volume toxicity constraints, neither from randomised data.

### Heterogeneous-dosing Prostate SBRT, 5-year Outcomes

> Urethra Dmax ≥47 Gy → late ≥G2 GU OR 4.2; rectal C39Gy ≥40% → ≥G3 GI OR 8.7 (HyTEC review; Fuller 5-yr data absent).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The heterogeneity signal transfers: UCSF plans with larger hotspots carried more G2-3 urinary toxicity, and urethra Dmax ≥47 Gy (5-fx equivalent) tracked late ≥G2 urinary toxicity (OR 4.2). For CyberKnife plans prescribed to low isodose lines, prescription isodose and OAR Dmax may matter as much as nominal Rx dose.

- ⚠️ Source mismatch: title/DOI cite Fuller et al (Eur Urol Oncol 2018); full text is Wang et al HyTEC prostate SBRT toxicity review (IJROBP)
- ⚠️ No heterogeneous-dosing trial efficacy or 5-yr outcomes in source text; no effect size reported for that trial
- 🔍 AAPM working group review of prostate SBRT reports 2001-2018; SBRT defined as ≤5 fx
- 🔍 Excluded series <40 pts (except sexual outcomes), SBRT boost, proton, non-English, acute-only
- 📐 NTCP-derived dose thresholds (authors' refits of published patient data)

  | Endpoint (series) | Dose metric | OR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Late ≥G2 urinary (UCSF, 38 Gy/4 fx) | Urethra Dmax ≥47 Gy (5-fx equiv) | 4.2 (1.2-15.0) | 0.04 |
  | Late ≥G3 bowel (multicenter, 45-50 Gy/5 fx) | Rectal circumference C39Gy ≥40% | 8.7 (1.3-59.4) | 0.02 |
  | EPIC incontinence MID (UCLA, 40 Gy/5 fx) | Bladder mean ≥17 Gy | 7.7 (1.3-45.0) | 0.008 |

- 📊 Prescription dose vs late toxicity (Sunnybrook series)

  | Endpoint | 35 Gy | 40 Gy |
  |---|---|---|
  | RTOG ≥G2 late urinary | 5% | 48% |
  | CTCAE ≥G2 hematochezia | 8% | >20% |

- 📊 Heterogeneity: UCSF hotspot-heavy plans had more CTCAE G2-3 urinary toxicity; no effect size reported in source
- 📊 At 50 Gy/5 fx: 6 of 61 pts (10%) CTCAE G3-4 rectal toxicity, 5 needing colostomy
- 📊 Non-dosimetric risk factors
  - Prostate >60 cc: late G2-3 urinary 15% vs 8%
  - Prior TURP: late G2-3 hematuria 21% vs 2%
  - QD vs QOD (Stanford): late G1-2 urinary 56% vs 17%, bowel 44% vs 5%
  - Anticoagulants: ≥G2 late hematochezia 47% vs 18%
- 🔗 2018 ASTRO/ASCO/AUA guideline advised Rx ≤36.25 Gy; authors read ≥G3 risk as low up to 40 Gy
- ⚠️ QD vs QOD gap may reflect early-era learning curve, per authors
- ⚠️ Thin events (Kim: 6 events) give wide ORs; authors state firm constraints cannot be made

### Prostate SBRT TCP Modeling and Systematic Review

> Low-intermediate risk: EQD2 90 Gy (36.1 Gy/5fx) gives 95% 5-yr TCP; high risk needs EQD2 102 Gy (38.7 Gy/5fx) for 95%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 90% TCP anchor for low-intermediate risk (EQD2 71 Gy) lies below approximately 80 Gy, a range no reviewed cohort treated, so it is extrapolation. The data-backed read is 36.1 Gy/5fx at 95% TCP, which supports staying in the 35-40 Gy range rather than de-escalating below it.

- 🔍 AAPM HyTEC systematic review + Poisson TCP dose-response model; endpoint FFBR
- 🔍 25 cohorts, 4,821 pts; literature through March 2018
- 📊 Modeled 5-yr TCP (FFBR) by risk group, α/β=1.5 Gy

  | Risk group | TCP 90% | TCP 95% |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low-intermediate | EQD2 71 Gy (31.7 Gy/5fx) | EQD2 90 Gy (36.1 Gy/5fx) |
  | High | EQD2 97 Gy (37.6 Gy/5fx) | EQD2 102 Gy (38.7 Gy/5fx) |

- 📊 Risk mix: 2,235 low, 1,894 intermediate, 446 high risk
- 💊 Regimens 32-50 Gy in 4-5 fx; most prescriptions 35-40 Gy
- 🔍 All fiducial-guided; 16 cohorts Cyberknife, 7 linac
- ⚠️ Model fit only to cohorts with ≥5y f/u: 9 studies (1069 pts) low-intermediate, 3 studies (85 pts) high risk
- ⚠️ No cohort treated below EQD2 approximately 80 Gy, so the 71 Gy → 90% TCP point is extrapolated
- ⚠️ Dose modeled from prescription only, no DVH; target, margin and SV coverage varied widely
- ⚠️ Phoenix nadir+2 used in all but 3 cohorts; 2 used PSA decline trends
- 🔗 HYPO-RT-PC: 7-fx SBRT-like regimen matched conventional fractionation on control and toxicity at 5 yrs

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Tumor Control Probability Modeling and Systematic Review of the Literature of Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy for Prostate Cancer** — Royce, Trevor J.; Mavroidis, Panayiotis; Wang, Kyle et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.08.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.08.014)
  - 📎 [[papers/prostate/2021-royce-tumor-control-probability-modeling-systematic-review.pdf|Prostate SBRT TCP Modeling and Systematic Review (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Durability of FFBR beyond 5 years across risk groups
> - Efficacy and safety of SBRT as a boost to IMRT
> - Impact of MRI-based target delineation on control and toxicity

## 👄 Head & Neck

Reirradiation SBRT for locally recurrent previously irradiated H&N: dose-response supports 40-50 Gy/5fx, with carotid blowout up to 10% to 20%.

### HyTEC H&N SBRT Tumor Control (AAPM WG)

> 3y LC 40% at 44.4 vs 15% at 26.8 Gy 5-fx equivalent (P<.01); 40-50 Gy/5fx suggested for locally recurrent previously irradiated H&N SBRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Durability, not early response, is where dose matters: the 1y fit was flat (P=.096), while 2y and 3y LC tracked dose (P<.01), with 45 Gy/5fx modeled at 41% 3y LC. The WG ties escalation to volume, with a 45 Gy/5fx ceiling for <25 cm3 and 45-50 Gy for larger targets, weighed against carotid blowout in up to 10% to 20% of pts.

- 🔍 AAPM HyTEC working group systematic review with pooled logistic dose-response modeling
- 🔍 8 publications, >300 cases of locally recurrent previously irradiated H&N cancer
- 🔍 Doses converted to 5-fx equivalent via LQ (Withers), nominal α/β 10 Gy
- 🔍 Only SBRT >6 weeks after primary RT included; boosts and benign tumors excluded
- 📊 Model-estimated LC by dose (5-fx equivalent)

  | Endpoint | Lower dose | Higher dose | Dose-response P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1y LC | 50% at 25.6 Gy | 60% at 40.7 Gy | Model P=.096, median split P=.069 (ns) |
  | 3y LC | 15% at 26.8 Gy | 40% at 44.4 Gy | P<.01 (model and median split) |

- 📊 2y LC dose-response also reached P<.01 for model and median split
- 📊 2y OS at highest doses almost double the lowest; ns (model P=.060, median split P=.13)
- 📊 Modeled outcomes at 45 Gy/5fx
  - 2y LC 50%
  - 3y LC 41%
  - 2y OS 47%
- 🔍 Volume-adapted dose suggestion (not modeled; expert read from Rwigema data)
  - <25 cm3: maximum 45 Gy/5fx equivalent, flat dose-response above this
  - Larger tumors: 45-50 Gy/5fx equivalent, consider novel escalation strategies
- ⚠️ Carotid blowout reported in up to 10% to 20% even at lower doses
- ⚠️ Rwigema 2011 contributes one-third of cases, heavily shaping 2y and 3y fits
- ⚠️ Better-prognosis pts may have received higher dose; association, not causation
- ⚠️ HPV status, histology, concurrent cetuximab, QD vs QoD and tumor volume not modeled
- ⚠️ LC definitions mixed local and locoregional failure across series

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Head and Neck Tumor Control Probability: Radiation Dose–Volume Effects in Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy for Locally Recurrent Previously-Irradiated Head and Neck Cancer: Report of the AAPM Working Group** — Vargo, John A.; Moiseenko, Vitali; Grimm, Jimm et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.01.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.01.044)
  - 📎 [[papers/head-neck/2021-vargo-head-neck-tumor-control-probability-radiation-dose.pdf|HyTEC H&N SBRT Tumor Control (AAPM WG) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does concurrent systemic therapy shift the SBRT reRT dose-response?
> - Prospective validation of 40-50 Gy/5fx for recurrent H&N
> - Optimal dose for recurrent tumors >25 cm3

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Stage I NSCLC SBRT: modeled LC plateau doses by T stage and prescription point (PTV periphery vs isocenter).

### HyTEC Stage I NSCLC SBRT Local Control

> Modeled LC plateau at PTV periphery: 43±1 Gy/3 fx, 47±1 Gy/4 fx, 50±1 Gy/5 fx across 46 pooled SBRT series in stage I NSCLC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Prescription convention gates transfer: the modeled LC plateau is 43, 47 and 50 Gy (±1) to the PTV periphery in 3, 4 and 5 fx but 54, 59 and 63 Gy at isocenter. T2 needs 45, 50 and 53 Gy at the PTV vs 42, 46 and 48 Gy for T1, so one size-blind schedule sits closer to the margin for T2.

- 🔍 160 published SBRT series for stage I NSCLC screened (PubMed through May 2014)
- 🔍 46 studies met quality criteria; series with <10 pts, >T2, or oligometastatic disease excluded
- 🔍 Pooled cohort by dose per fraction
  - ≥6 Gy/fx: 3268 pts
  - 3-4 Gy/fx: 211 pts from 3 studies, included to anchor the curve shoulder
- 📊 1, 2, 3 and 5 yr LC fitted against BED with LQ, USC and regrowth models
- 📐 None of the models fit well by χ2/ndf; the regrowth model fit best
- 📐 Regrowth-fitted α/β ~25 Gy (T1), 19 Gy (T2), 21 Gy (T1+T2)
- 📊 Regrowth model: dose needed to reach max TCP, by T stage and prescription point

  | T stage / point | 3 fx | 4 fx | 5 fx |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | T1, PTV (80% isodose) | 42±1 Gy | 46±1 Gy | 48±1 Gy |
  | T2, PTV (80% isodose) | 45±1 Gy | 50±1 Gy | 53±1 Gy |
  | T1+T2, PTV (80% isodose) | 43±1 Gy | 47±1 Gy | 50±1 Gy |
  | T1, isocenter | 52±1 Gy | 57±1 Gy | 60±1 Gy |
  | T2, isocenter | 56±1 Gy | 62±1 Gy | 66±1 Gy |
  | T1+T2, isocenter | 54±1 Gy | 59±1 Gy | 63±1 Gy |

- 📊 LQ and USC models give near-identical T1+T2 PTV doses: 44±1, 47±1, 50±1 Gy for 3/4/5 fx
- 🔗 vs historic conventional RT (2-3 Gy/fx to ≈54-60 Gy): long-term LC ≈30-70%, OS ≈15-30%
- ⚠️ Measurement caveats on the pooled LC
  - Competing non-cancer deaths censored, so actuarial 1-3 yr LC may overestimate true LC
  - LC mostly radiographic; post-SBRT fibrosis and early FDG uptake confound recurrence calls
  - Most series report LC only to 3 yr; OS collected but not modeled
  - Surgical series count intralobar and nodal failures as local, so LC isn't comparable to resection

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Local Control After Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy for Stage I Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer** — Lee, Percy; Loo, Billy W.; Biswas, Tithi et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.03.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.03.045)
  - 📎 [[papers/thoracic/2021-lee-local-control-stereotactic-body-radiation-therapy-stage.pdf|HyTEC Stage I NSCLC SBRT Local Control (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does dose above the modeled plateau add LC for T2 tumors?
> - Dose-response for central and >5 cm tumors
> - Validation of plateau doses in patient-level prospective data

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

Liver SBRT: G≥3 GI risk <10% at mean Rx 50 Gy in 3 to 6 fx; QUANTEC MLD limits judged still reasonable.

### HyTEC Liver SBRT Dose-Volume Effects

> Grade ≥3 GI D50 87.1 Gy and D10 50.6 Gy (Rx dose); liver-enzyme fit not significant (P = .10); QUANTEC MLD limits judged still reasonable.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable planning number is GI, not liver: mean Rx 50 Gy in 3 to 6 fx kept modeled G≥3 GI risk <10%, and series using the ≥700 cm3 sparing guideline carried 9.3% G≥3 GI. Because the GI model runs on Rx dose, not duodenal or gastric DVH, it does not replace organ-level constraints for a central or hilar target.

- 🔍 Pooled study-level review of published liver SBRT series; PubMed search through May 2014
- 🔍 Probit NTCP fits by maximum likelihood; physical dose used, no BED conversion
- 🔍 HCC, IHC and liver mets pooled; mostly 3 to 6 fx, one single-fraction series
- 📊 Pooled dose-response parameters by endpoint (Table 3)

  | Endpoint | Events/pts | D50 (95% CI) | γ50 (95% CI) | D10 / D20 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | G≥3 liver enzymes (vs MLD) | 17/288 | 40.8 Gy (25.5-∞) | 0.95 (0.58-1.44) | not reported |
  | G≥2 general GI (vs Rx dose) | 99/278 | 48.0 Gy (43.6-65.9) | 0.83 (0.37-1.31) | 18.4 / 28.6 Gy |
  | G≥3 general GI (vs Rx dose) | 25/399 | 87.1 Gy (69.1-194.4) | 1.22 (0.78-1.68) | 50.6 / 63.1 Gy |

- 📐 Liver-enzyme model: no dose response established (P = .10), 5 studies
- 📊 Mean Rx 50 Gy in 3 to 6 fx → G≥3 general GI risk <10%
- 📊 Series using the ≥700 cm3 sparing guideline (15 or 17 Gy): G≥3 GI 9.3% (11 of 118), 68% CI 6.6% to 12.8%
- 🔗 vs QUANTEC MLD limits: G3 liver-enzyme risk <20% probability
  - Primary liver: 13 Gy (3 fx), 18 Gy (6 fx)
  - Liver mets: 15 Gy (3 fx), 20 Gy (6 fx)
- ⚠️ Rx dose stands in for duodenal, gastric and bowel dose; OAR DVHs not available
- ⚠️ General GI is a composite including fatigue and nausea, not organ-specific injury
- ⚠️ Liver failure not modeled (inconsistent reporting)
  - Bujold: 3 of 102 HCC pts died of liver failure without progression
  - Andolino: liver failure in 4 of 8 HCC pts with baseline CP ≥8

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Radiation Dose-Volume Effects for Liver SBRT** — Moyed Miften; Yevgeniy Vinogradskiy; Vitali Moiseenko et al.. *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys*
  - 📎 [[papers/hepatobiliary/miften-radiation-dose-volume-effects-liver-sbrt.pdf|HyTEC Liver SBRT Dose-Volume Effects (full text)]]

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

Low-evidence pieces: a hypofractionation position paper built on observational cohorts and a single-pt HDR mould case report.

### HDR Surface Mould Brachytherapy for Eyelid Carcinoma

> 52 Gy/13 fx HDR surface mould (4 Gy BID) after excision of eyelid sebaceous carcinoma: disease-free at 6 mo, mild dry eye only.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable content is the build: a wax-wrapped 5-mm lead shield under the lid, a wax dummy for CT, CTV = scar + 1 cm with no PTV, and 4 Gy BID 6 hours apart to 52 Gy. OAR doses appear only as 'within tolerable limits' (Fig 3D), so lens and lacrimal dose cannot be benchmarked from the source text.

- 🔍 Single-pt case report: 54-yo woman, sebaceous carcinoma of R upper eyelid near lateral canthus
- 🔍 Treated after excisional biopsy; margin status not reported in path
- 🔍 MRI face/neck: no residual disease, no significant lymphadenopathy
- 🔍 Brachytherapy technique
  - 52 Gy/13 fx, 4 Gy/fx BID, 6 hours apart, Ir-192 (Flexitron afterloader)
  - EQD2 60.67 Gy, matched by authors to 60 Gy LDR via LQ model
  - CTV = scar (copper wire on CT) + 1 cm margin, no PTV expansion
  - 5-mm lead eye shield under the lid, wax-wrapped to reduce backscatter
  - IPSA inverse planning (Oncentra Brachy 4.6.0) on 1 mm CT slices
- 📐 Plan evaluation
  - CTV 0.71 mL
  - D90 4 Gy, above 90% of per-fraction Rx
  - DNR (V100/V150) 0.26, limit < 0.36
- 📊 Disease-free at 6 mo post-treatment; mild dry eye the only reported effect
- 📊 No acute side effects across weekly review for 9 consecutive weeks
- ⚠️ OAR doses stated only as 'within tolerable limits' (Fig 3D); no lens or lacrimal numbers in text
- ⚠️ Adjuvant scar target with no gross disease: 6-mo disease-free status does not test tumor control
- ⚠️ α/β behind the EQD2 conversion not stated in source
- 🔗 Authors cite GEC-ESTRO preference for brachy over EBRT for small-volume skin targets

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Implementing high-dose rate surface mould brachytherapy for carcinoma of eyelid: a practical approach and weekly review** — Patro, Kanhu Charan; Avinash, Ajitesh; Banidutta, Arya et al.. *Radiation Oncology Journal* (2024-06). [doi:10.3857/roj.2023.00682](https://doi.org/10.3857/roj.2023.00682)
  > Surface mould brachytherapy is a conformal radiotherapy technique that can deliver high dose to the target while sparing nearby normal structures, Here, we aim to describe the procedurals details for high-dose rate (HDR) surface mould brachytherapy in sebaceous carcinoma of eyelid in a 54-year old lady. She was hesitant for surgery and any form of invasive intervention like interstitial brachytherapy. So, she was treated with surface mould HDR brachytherapy to a total dose of 52 Gy in 13 fractions at a dose of 4 Gy per fraction delivered twice daily using Iridium-192 isotope with no acute side effects. She was evaluated on a weekly basis for any radiation side effects and now she is disease-free for 6 months post-treatment with only mild dry eye. A detailed step-by-step procedure of surface mould technique, simulation procedure, dose prescription, planning, plan evaluation and treatment has been described in this paper. Surface mould HDR brachytherapy can be safely used as organ preserving modality of treatment for eyelid carcinoma.
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2024-patro-implementing-high-dose-rate-surface-mould-brachytherapy.pdf|HDR Surface Mould Brachytherapy for Eyelid Carcinoma (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions

### Hypofractionated RT for NMSC in the Post COVID-19 Era

> ≤15 fractions proposed for most NMSC RT and 1–4 fractions for pts over 80 or ECOG 2/3; hypofractionated LC cited at 90-100%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The bigger de-escalation is in the Table 1 footnote: observation rather than adjuvant RT for most BCCs and low-risk SCCs, framed as an omission question, not a fractionation one. The schedule menu goes beyond RCR's modest trim (50 Gy in 15 fx instead of 55 Gy in 20 fx), and the author keeps PNI fields and high-risk lower lip SCC as exceptions at 20 or 25 fractions.

- 📊 Definitive RT LC >90–95% regardless of technique, per author
- 📊 Hypofractionated RT LC 90-100% across the published literature cited
- 🔍 Cited systematic review of hypofractionation in NMSC
  - 40 publications, over 12 000 NMSC
  - Median lesion size 1–5 cm
  - Mean dose 38 Gy, 8 Gy per fraction, 3 times per week
  - Heterogeneous pts and technique (external beam vs BT)
- 💊 Author's fractionation tiers by age/PS (body text)
  - <70 y, good PS: 55–50 Gy in 2–2.5 Gy fractions, to limit late cutaneous effects
  - 70–80 y: 3–4 Gy fractions over 2–3 wk, e.g. 40–45 Gy in 10–15 fx
  - Over 80 and/or ECOG 2/3: 1–4 fractions posed as the default
- 🔗 RCR (UK) COVID-19 guidance: modest fraction reduction
  - 40 Gy in 8 fx instead of 10
  - 50 Gy in 15 fx instead of 55 Gy in 20 fx
- 📊 Table 1 schedules span 15–18 Gy single fraction to 45–50 Gy in 15–20 fx; some cells read 'no RT'
- 🔍 Table 1 footnote: consider observation over adjuvant RT in most BCCs and low-risk SCCs
- ⚠️ Carve-outs: PNI fields near neural structures, high-risk lower lip SCC (20 or 25 fx may be justified)
- ⚠️ Author concedes low-quality evidence: mostly observational cohorts, very few RCTs
- 🔍 Case (Figs 1-2): 88-yo, 28 Gy in 4 fx twice weekly orthovoltage, no residual SCC at 6 mo

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Hypofractionated radiotherapy in patients with non‐melanoma skin cancer in the post COVID‐19 era: Time to reconsider its role for most patients** — Veness, Michael J. *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Oncology* (2020-06). [doi:10.1111/1754-9485.13070](https://doi.org/10.1111/1754-9485.13070)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2020-veness-hypofractionated-radiotherapy-patients-non-melanoma-skin.pdf|Hypofractionated RT for NMSC in the Post COVID-19 Era (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Adjuvant RT vs observation after excision of BCC or low-risk SCC
> - Late cosmesis with hypofractionation in pts under 70

### Single Isocenter LINAC Radiosurgery of Multiple Brain Metastases

> Three-center 'how we do it' reference: 0-1 mm PTV, fractionate if lesion >2-3 cm or V12 >10 cm3, generally up to 15 lesions per session.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The summed-V12 question is the additive read: UAB does not total V12 across targets, arguing 10 1-cc targets may be less toxic than 1 10-cc target, while the fractionation trigger stays at V12 >10 cm3 or a >2 to 3 cm lesion. That gap in single-lesion necrosis models decides when a multimet plan gets hypofractionated.

- 🔍 Practice reference from 3 centers (UAB, OHCI, UCLA); authors state it is not a consensus guideline
- 🔍 Delivery platforms described in text

  | Platform | Delivery | Immobilization | IGRT |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Varian | VMAT (RapidArc or HyperArc) | QFix Encompass for HyperArc; other masks with manual VMAT | CBCT |
  | Brainlab | Enhanced dynamic conformal arc | Brainlab stereotactic mask | ExacTrac stereoscopic x-ray |

- 🔍 6DOF couch used at all 3 centers; cited as reducing the required PTV margin
- 📐 PTV margin historically 0 mm; typical frameless multimet margin 0 to 1 mm
- 📐 Fractionate when largest lesion >2 to 3 cm diameter or V12 >10 cm3
- 📐 Fractionated prescriptions listed: 27 Gy in 3 fractions and 30 Gy in 5 fractions
- ⚠️ UAB does not sum V12 across targets: 10 1-cc targets may carry less toxicity than 1 10-cc target
- ⚠️ Distant targets: rotational error and field-size limits push toward multiple isocenters, tighter rotational tolerance, or larger PTV
- ⚠️ No outcome data; dose, margin and constraint choices are institutional practice, not tested comparisons

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Practical Considerations for Single Isocenter LINAC Radiosurgery of Multiple Brain Metastases** — Meeks, Sanford L.; Mercado, Catherine E.; Popple, Richard A. et al.. *Practical Radiation Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1016/j.prro.2021.09.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2021.09.007)
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2022-meeks-practical-considerations-single-isocenter-linac.pdf|Single Isocenter LINAC Radiosurgery of Multiple Brain Metastases (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does summed V12 across targets predict radionecrosis in multimet plans?
> - Safe maximum isocenter-to-target distance for single-isocenter plans
> - 0 vs 1 mm PTV margin: local control vs necrosis trade-off

### MRI for Brain Stereotactic Radiotherapy

> MRI-to-SRS interval ≥14 days cut 6-month LC to 56% vs 95%; review sets <1 mm total distortion and vendor 3D correction as minimums.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The QA gap often sits outside the department: external MRIs are frequently only 2D-corrected, leaving distortions up to 2 mm, and uncorrected images produced geographic miss in 8 of 28 lesions. A ≤1 mm GTV-PTV margin is only defensible with vendor 3D correction, higher readout bandwidth, ≤1 mm3 3D T1 and a short imaging interval.

- 🔍 Narrative review (Erlangen group, Strahlenther Onkol 2020) of planning MRI for intracranial SRS/fSRT
- 📊 Imaging-to-treatment interval and outcome

  | Study | Interval | Finding |
  |---|---|---|
  | Seymour et al. | ≥14 days vs shorter | 6-month LC 56% vs 95% |
  | Salkeld et al. | ≤7 days | Management change in 41% |
  | Salkeld et al. | >7 days | Management change in 78% |

- 📊 Erlangen rule: MRI-to-treatment interval must not exceed 5 days; same-day imaging optimal
- 📊 Repeat MRI mid-fSRT (Hessen, 3-5 fx): PTV coverage loss up to 34.8% intact mets, up to 4.5% cavities
- 📐 Distortion magnitudes cited
  - Seibert, uncorrected images: median GTV shift 1.2 mm, max 3.9 mm
  - Seibert: geographic miss in 8 of 28 lesions if uncorrected images used
  - 2D vendor correction: residual distortions up to 2 mm in authors' example
  - Wang, 3 T MPRAGE: susceptibility distortion <0.5 mm in 86.9% of volume
  - Wang: 1.6 mm at sinus air-bone boundaries, 0.8 mm at 12 mm distance
- 📐 Consensus target: total MRI distortion <1 mm for brain stereotactic RT
- 🔍 3D isotropic sequences preferred; volumetric error >10% when GTV spans <5 slices
- 📊 Danieli et al.: T1 sequence comparison (16 brain mets, 38 gliomas)

  | Metric | T1-SPACE | T1-MPRAGE | T1-VIBE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Best conspicuity rating | All cases | 27.8% | 44.4% |
  | Median GTV | 1.78 cm3 | 1.36 cm3 | 1.62 cm3 |
  | Brain mets missed | None | 15.8% | NR |

- 📊 Contrast timing (Yuh): mets <5 mm seen in 40.6% at 10 min vs 75.0% at 20 min
- 🔗 Aligns with 2016 MRI-simulation consensus (Paulson et al.): vendor 3D correction minimum, higher readout bandwidth
- ⚠️ Only the imaging-interval data tie an MRI parameter to LC; distortion and sequence data are geometric or simulated

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Magnetic resonance imaging for brain stereotactic radiotherapy** — Putz, Florian; Mengling, Veit; Perrin, Rosalind et al.. *Strahlentherapie und Onkologie* (2020-03). [doi:10.1007/s00066-020-01604-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00066-020-01604-0)
  > Abstract Due to its superior soft tissue contrast, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is essential for many radiotherapy treatment indications. This is especially true for treatment planning in intracranial tumors, where MRI has a long-standing history for target delineation in clinical practice. Despite its routine use, care has to be taken when selecting and acquiring MRI studies for the purpose of radiotherapy treatment planning. Requirements on MRI are particularly demanding for intracranial stereotactic radiotherapy, where accurate imaging has a critical role in treatment success. However, MR images acquired for routine radiological assessment are frequently unsuitable for high-precision stereotactic radiotherapy as the requirements for imaging are significantly different for radiotherapy planning and diagnostic radiology. To assure that optimal imaging is used for treatment planning, the radiation oncologist needs proper knowledge of the most important requirements concerning the use of MRI in brain stereotactic radiotherapy. In the present review, we summarize and discuss the most relevant issues when using MR images for target volume delineation in intracranial stereotactic radiotherapy.
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2020-putz-magnetic-resonance-imaging-brain-stereotactic.pdf|MRI for Brain Stereotactic Radiotherapy (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Do distortion-correction and interval QA steps improve SRS local control?
> - Does mask-immobilized MR simulation improve registration under current imaging standards?
> - Does T1-SPACE contouring change outcomes, not just conspicuity?

Surgical de-escalation recap; Z11 excluded third-field nodal RT, so its RT read stays indirect.

### NCCN 2023 Breast Congress: Locoregional Management (surgical, ACOSOG Z11 10-yr)

> Surgery de-escalates in selected breast cancer: ALND omission after 1-2 positive SLN, technique-driven post-NAC nodal staging, and BCT for 2 or 3 foci.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The nodal-pCR cohort omitted ALND with nodal RT in 78% (SLNB) and 85% (TAD), so any axillary control it reports rests on regional RT, not surgery alone. Z11 likewise excluded third-field RT without reporting tangent axillary coverage: once dissection is dropped, field design carries the axilla.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Jessica S. Young, MD
> The speaker argued that locoregional surgery is de-escalating on three fronts: the axilla, nodal staging after NAC, and multiple ipsilateral breast cancer. For 1-2 positive SLN with lumpectomy and WBRT, ACOSOG Z11 at 10 yrs shows no OS, DFS or locoregional penalty from omitting ALND, and AMAROS offers axillary RT as an alternative to dissection. After NAC, staging accuracy depends on technique: dual tracer, 3 SLN and clipped-node retrieval via TAD. BCT for 2 or 3 foci is presented as acceptable on a 5-yr LR of 3.1%.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Z11 excluded third-field nodal RT, but the slides never show what axillary dose the whole-breast tangents delivered, so omitting ALND cannot be separated from the RT those pts received.
> - Post-NAC staging rests on false-negative rates against completion ALND, a surrogate for residual disease; the slides carry no axillary recurrence result for the 1144-pt nodal-pCR cohort.
> - The multicentric series is single-arm with 204 evaluable pts; close-margin and pN1-3 subgroups had 5-yr LR of 7.5% and 7.8%, with upper CI bounds of 26.5 and 22.2.
> - Z11 required lumpectomy and excluded mastectomy, so its result does not transfer to mastectomy pts; AMAROS covers them but compares axillary RT with dissection, not observation.

**Trials cited:** ACOSOG Z11 · AMAROS · SENTINA

- 🔍 ACOSOG Z11: phase 3 non-inferiority, 891 pts, cT1-2, 1-2 positive SLN, lumpectomy + WBRT planned
- 📊 ACOSOG Z11 at 10 yrs (median f/u 9.3 yrs)

  | Endpoint | ALND | SLNB |
  |---|---|---|
  | OS | 83.6% | 86.3% |
  | DFS | 78.2% | 80.2% |
  | Locoregional recurrence | 6.2% | 5.3% |

- 📊 27% of the ALND arm had additional positive nodes
- 📊 Only 1 regional recurrence in yrs 5-10 in the SLND arm, none in ALND
- ⚠️ Z11 excluded third-field nodal RT; slides do not report incidental axillary dose from tangents
- 📊 AMAROS: phase 3 randomized non-inferiority, 4806 enrolled, lumpectomy or mastectomy allowed, median f/u 6.1 yrs

  | Endpoint | AND | ART |

  | 5-yr DFS | 86.9% | 82.7% |
  | 5-yr OS | 93.3% | 92.5% |

- 📊 Nodal RT given in 78% after SLNB vs 85% after TAD
- 📊 VACB after NAC, single-arm phase 2, 50 pts: no ipsilateral recurrences at median 26.4 mo
- 📊 BCT for 2 or 3 foci: 6/204 LR, 3.1% at 5 yrs vs acceptability threshold <8%

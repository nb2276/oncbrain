# 2026-11-13

**APBI-IMRT-Florence VMAT update: 30 Gy/5 consecutive fx PBI in 50 pts, no grade >1 acute or late toxicity at median 4.5y, cosmesis excellent/good 98%.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Day is RT technique, not efficacy: two society guidelines set brain-met SRS floors (≤1 mm geometric tolerance, ≤7-day planning MRI; SRS for 1-15 lesions ≤3 cm, fractionate 2-4 cm). Breast adds consecutive-day VMAT PBI without grade >1 toxicity; ASTRO think tank says BRCA1/2, PALB2, CHEK2 and VUS shouldn't alter indicated RT, deleterious ATM excepted.

## 🧠 CNS

Two brain-met SRS guidelines (ISRS technical, Radiosurgery Society case-based); both lean mostly on retrospective and single-institution data.

### ISRS Technical Guidelines for SRS: Small Brain Metastases (≤1 cm)

> ≤1 mm end-to-end geometric tolerance, ≤7-day planning MRI, ≤1.5-mm slices, dosimetric accuracy within 5%: the ISRS technical floor for SRS to brain mets ≤1 cm.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The margin call is where most of the content sits: a 1-mm margin on a 0.8-cm sphere takes it from 0.268 to 0.524 cc, and randomized 1 vs 3 mm margins showed no LC difference but more radionecrosis at 3 mm. Planning-MRI age matters too: 12-month local FFP 95% vs 56% at <14 vs ≥14 days.

- 🔍 PRISMA review, [[PMID 2009]]-2020: 484 abstracts → 37 articles, plus 44 from references; 81 papers total
- ⚠️ 57 of 81 papers (70%) single-institution; evidence base is mostly technical reports and retrospective series
- 🔍 10-question survey of 14 ISRS guideline committee members, 100% completion
- ⚠️ Strong consensus (≥80%) on only 5 questions; no consensus on 3. Authors read this as high practice variability
- 🔍 ISRS recommendations by domain (Table 2)

  | Domain | Recommendation |
  |---|---|
  | Imaging | MRI ≤7 days from treatment; slice thickness ≤1.5 mm |
  | Imaging | Scan delay 10 to 15 min after contrast |
  | Imaging | CT slices equal to or thinner than MR slices |
  | Contouring | Contour all targets and OARs; weigh margins by SRS platform |
  | Treatment | Sub-millimeter geometric accuracy; lower energy beams may reduce normal-tissue dose |
  | Dosimetry | Dosimetric accuracy within 5%; small-field dosimetry per IAEA and AAPM |

- 📊 Margin evidence cited for small mets

  | Study | Design | Finding |
  |---|---|---|
  | LINAC SRS, 93 mets | Retrospective, with vs without 2-mm PTV margin | Severe parenchymal complications 7.1% and 19.6%; no effect on LC |
  | LINAC SRS, 80 mets in 49 pts | Randomized, 1 vs 3 mm PTV margin | No LC difference; more radionecrosis with 3 mm |
  | Sneed et al, 2200 GK treatments | Retrospective, lesions up to 1 cm, ≤20 Gy, no margin | 1-year adverse radiation effect probability 1% or less |

- 📐 1-mm margin on a 0.8-cm sphere: 0.268 cc → 0.524 cc, roughly doubling target volume
- 📊 Planning-MRI age and growth
  - 151 mets: 12-month local FFP 95% vs 56% with contouring MRI <14 vs ≥14 days from SRS
  - 531 lesions: median volume growth 20% over a median interscan interval of 8 days
- 📐 Mask positioning, dedicated SRS mask vs conventional thermoplastic (ExacTrac post-treatment imaging)

  | Mask | Median vector displacement | Max residual error |
  |---|---|---|
  | BrainLab SRS mask | 0.38 mm | 1 mm |
  | Conventional mask | 0.74 mm | 2 mm |

- 📐 Single-isocenter noncoplanar arcs + CBCT + 6-DoF couch: within 1 mm for targets within 4 cm of isocenter, 2 mm beyond
- 📐 6 MV FFF vs 10 MV FFF (93 lesions, 35 pts): normal-brain volume at 50% isodose 11% lower with 6 MV FFF
- ⚠️ MLC ≤5 mm is the only firm conclusion; 2.5 vs 5 mm superiority left unresolved
- ⚠️ Commissioning, QA program build, and platform-specific planning explicitly out of scope

[doi:10.1016/j.prro.2022.10.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2022.10.013)
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2023-grishchuk-isrs-technical-guidelines-stereotactic-radiosurgery.pdf|ISRS Technical Guidelines for SRS: Small Brain Metastases (≤1 cm) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions

### Stereotactic Radiosurgery in the Management of Brain Metastases: Case-Based Radiosurgery Society Practice Guideline

> Radiosurgery Society case-based guide: SRS suitable for 1-15 lesions ≤3 cm/≤14 cc, fractionate 2-4 cm lesions, surgery first above 4 cm.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Dosimetry, not diameter alone, drives the fractionation call: single-fraction V12 of 5, 10 and >15 cc tracks with ~10%, 15% and 20% symptomatic radionecrosis, and in 4-14 cc lesions multifraction SRS cut radionecrosis 23.1% to 7.3% (P = .003). Above 14 cc the gap narrowed, keeping resection first line.

- 🔍 Suitability for SRS (Table 1)

  | Category | Number of lesions | Metastasis size | Specific scenarios |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Suitable | 1-15 | Diameter ≤3 cm, volume ≤14 cc | n/a |
  | Cautionary | >15 | Diameter 3-6 cm, volume >14 cc (operative management preferred) | SCLC, nodular leptomeningeal disease |
  | Unsuitable | n/a | >6 cm | Classical leptomeningeal disease |

- 💊 Dose/fractionation by lesion diameter

  | Diameter | Recommendation |
  |---|---|
  | <2 cm | 20-24 Gy in 1 fraction (per ASTRO guideline) |
  | 2-4 cm | 27 Gy in 3 fx or 30 Gy in 5 fx; staged SRS an alternative |
  | >4 cm | Surgery recommended; if inoperable, multifraction over single-fraction SRS |

- 📊 Single- vs multifraction SRS, large mets (meta-analysis, 24 studies, 1887 lesions)

  | Group | 1-yr LC 1 fx vs multi-fx | Radionecrosis 1 fx vs multi-fx |
  |---|---|---|
  | A (4-14 cc) | 77.6% vs 92.9% | 23.1% vs 7.3% (P = .003) |
  | B (>14 cc, >3 cm) | 77.1% vs 79.2% (P = .76) | 11.7% vs 6.5% (P = .29) |

- 📐 Single-fraction V12 of 5, 10, >15 cc → symptomatic radionecrosis ~10%, 15%, 20%
- 🔗 HyTEC (56 studies), 2.1-4 cm lesions: 1-yr LC 69% to 75% single-fraction vs ~80% fractionated
- 🔍 Common SRS dose constraints (Table 2)

  | Organ | 1 fx | 3 fx | 5 fx |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Brain | V12 <5-10 cc; V10 <12 cc | V18 <26 cc; V21 <21 cc; V24 <16.8 cc | V25 <16 cc; V28.8 <7 cc; V30 <10.5-30 cc |
  | Brain stem (not medulla) | Dmax <15 Gy; V10 <0.5 cc | Dmax <23.1 Gy; V15.9 <0.5 cc | Dmax <31 Gy; V23 <0.5 cc |
  | Spinal cord and medulla | Dmax <12.4-14 Gy; V10 <0.35 cc | Dmax <20.3-22.5 Gy; V15.9 <0.35 cc | Dmax <25.3-28 Gy; V22 <0.35 cc |
  | Cochlea | Dmax <9 Gy | Dmax <14.4 Gy | Dmax <22 Gy |
  | Optic pathway | Dmax <10 Gy; V8 <0.2 cc | Dmax <17.4-20 Gy; V15.3 <0.2 cc | Dmax <25 Gy; V23 <0.2 cc |

- 🔍 Target volumes and margins
  - Intact mets: GTV on contrast T1, no CTV, PTV 0-2 mm by platform
  - Recommended PTV 1-2 mm, 1 mm whenever possible
  - Margin 0→1 mm doubles V5-V12; 0→2 mm triples (48 plans)
  - Postop CTV: cavity, up to 5 mm along meningeal margin/bone flap
  - Dural contact: up to 10 mm along bone flap; sinus contact: 1-5 mm
  - Postop PTV typically 2 mm; surgical tract coverage individualized
- 🔍 Postop SRS within 4 weeks of surgery, planning MRI ≤7 days before SRS
- 📊 Preop SRS: meningeal disease 5.8% vs 16% to 21% postop; doses ~20% to 25% lower
- ⚠️ Preop SRS risks irradiating a benign/primary CNS lesion in 2% to 11%
- 📊 Single- vs multi-isocenter LINAC SRS: 6-mo freedom from recurrence 96% vs 96% (P = .81)
- 💊 Concurrent systemic therapy + SRS
  - Most studies support concurrency with no washout
  - BRAF inhibitor concurrent with SRS: higher intratumoral bleeding
  - Erlotinib radionecrosis signal was in pts receiving WBRT plus SRS
  - 193 pts: concurrent vs SRS alone OS 41.6 vs 21.5 mo (P < .05)
- 🔍 Reirradiation goals
  - ≥5 months since prior course
  - Total brain EQD2 <120 Gy
  - Single fraction: max summed dose <40 Gy, V12 <9 cc
  - Cord cumulative EQD2 ≤70 Gy, reirradiation EQD2 ≤25 Gy
- ⚠️ 15-lesion ceiling rests on observational data plus an early-terminated, abstract-only MD Anderson trial
- ⚠️ SCLC SRS called acceptable, not default; nodular LMD SRS based on 16 pts

[doi:10.1016/j.adro.2023.101402](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adro.2023.101402)

## 🎀 Breast

DCIS natural-history review (recurrence 8.9% CS+RT vs 22.5% CS alone) alongside a consecutive-day VMAT PBI technique update.

### Natural History of DCIS of the Breast: A Review

> 14–53% of DCIS misdiagnosed as benign progressed to invasive cancer over 10+ yrs; CS+RT recurrence 8.9% vs 22.5% after CS alone.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Latency is the RT-relevant detail: low-grade DCIS recurs less often early but its recurrence keeps rising with long follow-up, and Page's untreated cases invaded at 23 and 31 yrs. Omission arguments for low-grade DCIS built on short-horizon ipsilateral event rates are reading an incomplete curve.

- 📊 DCIS misread as benign, biopsy only: 14–53% progressed to invasive cancer over 10 or more yrs
- 📊 Misdiagnosed-DCIS follow-up series (biopsy alone)
  - Page: 28 misdiagnosed DCIS; 7 invasive at 15 yrs, 2 more at 23 and 31 yrs
  - Eusebi: 80 cases; 11 invasive, avg f/u 17.5 yrs
  - Collins (Nurses' Health study): 13 misdiagnosed; 6 invasive, across low/intermediate/high grade
  - Betsill: 25 low-grade micropapillary; f/u on 10, 7 developed subsequent cancer, avg 9.7 yrs
  - Rosen: 30 micropapillary; f/u on 15, 8 invasive ipsilateral, avg f/u 9 yrs
- 📊 Boyages meta-analysis: recurrence after conservative surgery ± RT

  | Subgroup | CS alone | CS+RT |
  |---|---|---|
  | Overall | 22.5% (95% CI 16.9–28.2) | 8.9% (95% CI 6.8–11.0) |
  | Comedo type | 34.4% | 8.3% |
  | Necrosis present | 24.7% | 8.2% |
  | High nuclear grade | 23% | 9.5% |
  | Intermediate nuclear grade | 22.7% | 7.5% |

- 📊 Mastectomy recurrence 1.4% (95% CI 0.7–2.1) in the same meta-analysis
- 📊 About half of recurrences after local therapy for DCIS are invasive
- 📊 Autopsy: median DCIS prevalence 8.9% (range 0–14.7%) vs 1.3% undiagnosed invasive cancer
- 🔍 Kopans model: reservoir up to 20x detected DCIS expected even if all DCIS progresses
- 🔗 Low-grade DCIS recurs with longer latency; long-term series show rising low-grade recurrence over time
- ⚠️ Misdiagnosed series skew low grade; high-grade DCIS less likely misread as benign
- ⚠️ Rosen's 53% rests on f/u for 15 of 30; ascertainment may favor women who became invasive
- ⚠️ Autopsy prevalence may be inflated by ADH read as DCIS; criteria varied across studies

### APBI-IMRT-Florence Technique Update (IMRT to VMAT) · [[NCT02104895]]

> 30 Gy/5 consecutive-fraction VMAT PBI in 50 pts: no grade >1 acute or late toxicity at median 4.5y; cosmesis excellent/good 98%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable detail is the planning screen: ipsilateral breast V15Gy tracks the PTV/ipsilateral breast volume ratio (R2 0.911), so a high ratio flags a pt likely to fail the V15Gy constraint before optimization. Constraints now carry ideal and accepted tiers, and failing the accepted tier sends the pt to WBI.

- 🔍 Single-center series of 50 consecutive pts (39 left, 11 right), all planned and treated free breathing
- 🔍 Delivery moved from 5-field 6-MV step-and-shoot IMRT to patient-specific partial-arc VMAT
- 🔍 Schedule moved from every-other-day to consecutive daily 30 Gy/5 fx
- 🔍 Volume definitions updated (authors: formal change only, no actual change in treated volume)
  - Original: CTV = clips + 1 cm, PTV = CTV + 1 cm
  - Current: CTV = clips + 1.5 cm, PTV = CTV + 0.5 cm
  - PTV may extend ≤4 mm into ipsilateral lung, cropped 3 mm inside skin
- 🔍 Dose constraints, original protocol vs updated (Table 1); failing the accepted tier → WBI

  | Structure | Original | Updated ideal | Accepted deviation |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PTV | V28.5 Gy = 100%; Dmax ≤105%; Dmin ≥28 Gy | V28.5 Gy ≥98%; V31.5 Gy ≤5%; Dmax ≤110% | V28.5 Gy ≥95%; V31.5 Gy ≤10% |
  | Ipsilateral uninvolved breast | V15 Gy ≤50% | V15 Gy ≤50% | V15 Gy ≤60% |
  | Ipsilateral lung | V10 Gy ≤20% | V10 Gy ≤20% | - |
  | Contralateral lung | V5 Gy ≤10% | V5 Gy ≤10% | - |
  | Contralateral breast | Dmax ≤1 Gy | Dmax ≤1 Gy | Dmax ≤2 Gy |
  | Heart | V3 Gy ≤10% | V3 Gy ≤10% | - |

- 📐 Ipsilateral breast V15Gy correlates with PTV/ipsilateral breast volume ratio, R2 0.911
- 📊 Acute G1 any: 32% (n=16); no grade >1
  - Dermatitis 28%
  - Fatigue 4%
  - Edema 2%, breast pain 2%
- 📊 Late G1 any: 16% (n=8); no grade >1
  - Fibrosis 14%
  - Telangiectasia 2%
- 📊 Physician-assessed cosmesis (Harvard 4-point): excellent 84%, good 14%, fair 2%
- 🔗 Any-grade toxicity: consecutive-day series vs parent trial (every other day)

  | Toxicity (any grade) | Consecutive-day VMAT series | APBI-IMRT-Florence trial |
  |---|---|---|
  | Acute | 32% | 21.1% |
  | Early late | 16% | 4.5% |

- 🔗 Parent phase 3: no significant IBTR or survival difference vs WBI at 10y; toxicity and cosmesis favored PBI
- ⚠️ No IBTR or local control reported for the consecutive-day series
- ⚠️ Follow-up range 0.6-5y (mean 3.4), short for late fibrosis and cosmesis
- ⚠️ Requires inverse planning; authors flag access gaps and US insurance authorization barriers for IMRT/VMAT

[doi:10.1016/j.prro.2022.05.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2022.05.010)

## 📊 Cross-cutting

ASTRO think tank on germline variants and RT: treat VUS as normal; deleterious heterozygous ATM is the one that argues against optional RT.

### Implications of Genetic Testing on RT Decisions: A Guide for Radiation Oncologists

> ASTRO think tank: heterozygous germline variants (BRCA1/2, PALB2, CHEK2, VUS) should not alter indicated RT; deleterious ATM in young pts is the exception.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The ATM carve-out rests on small absolute numbers: a carrier's 5-yr contralateral breast cancer risk moves from 2.9% to 3.8% (EAR 0.9%), and toxicity data are evenly split. Somatic ATM truncation points the other way, with better local control after palliative RT (P < .001), which is the case for genomically adjusted dose rather than omission.

- 🔍 ASTRO-convened think tank (February 2019): genetics, genomics, radiosensitivity, risk-estimate and clinical oncology experts
- 🔍 Consensus RT read by variant class

  | Variant / syndrome | Consensus RT read |
  |---|---|
  | VUS | Consider normal for RT decision-making |
  | BRCA1/2 | No increased RT toxicity or RT-induced second cancer with standard adjuvant RT |
  | PALB2, CHEK2, RAD50/51 | Should not affect RT decisions (extended from BRCA data) |
  | Deleterious heterozygous ATM | Avoid optional RT, esp. young pts; close CBC screening after indicated RT |
  | RB1, NF1, TP53, PTCH1 syndromes | No known excess acute/late toxicity; in-field second malignancy risk thought elevated |
  | NF1 neurofibromas | Avoid RT; RT for MPNST considered if margin-negative resection not achievable |

- 📊 ATM missense/truncating carriers: 5-yr CBC risk 2.9% → 3.8% (RR 1.3, EAR 0.9%), worked example
- ⚠️ ATM toxicity data evenly split: as much evidence of no excess risk as of modest excess risk
- 📐 ATM rs1801516 SNP (~24% of 5456 breast/prostate pts): acute toxicity OR 1.5, late OR 1.2; not deemed actionable
- 📊 Hereditary retinoblastoma: EBRT → 30-yr second cancer incidence 35% vs 6% without RT
- 📊 Somatic ATM truncation: better radiographic local control after palliative RT vs ATM VUS/wildtype (P < .001); OS no different
- 🔍 Second-cancer baseline, 647,672 adult 5-yr survivors
  - 9% developed a second solid malignancy at mean 12-yr f/u
  - ~8% of second cancers attributed to RT
  - EAR of RT-attributable second solid malignancy 0.5% by 15 yr
- ⚠️ Many cited series lack a no-RT control, so predictive vs prognostic effects can't be separated

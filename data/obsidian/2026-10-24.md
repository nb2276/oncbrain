# 2026-10-24

**Single-fraction SBRT (12 or 16 Gy) vs 30 Gy/10fx for nonspine bone mets: ITT 3-mo pain response 38% vs 21% (P=.05), 24-mo local failure 0% vs 9.7%.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Ablative RT carried the day: a randomized phase 2 favors single-fraction SBRT over 30 Gy/10fx for nonspine bone mets on pain and local control, while a 9-expert survey shows wide practice spread with 35 Gy/5 most chosen. Ultra-central lung SBRT meta-analysis pools 2-yr LC 89% at G5 toxicity 4%; 4 Gy LD-IFRT gives ORR 92% in recurrent indolent lymphoma.

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

Nonspine bone SBRT: randomized single-fraction vs 30 Gy/10fx data next to an expert survey of how dose is actually chosen.

### Nonspine Bone Metastases SBRT International Practice Patterns

> 35 Gy/5 most chosen (29 of 99 responses) and 40 Gy/5 most accepted across 11 NSBM cases; all 9 experts prescribed BED ≤100 Gy10.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dose ceiling is the RT read: every expert stayed at BED ≤100 Gy10, below the >100 Gy10 threshold used for lung and liver SBRT, and site mattered more than histology. Single-fraction 20 Gy/1 drew majority rejection in humerus, acetabulum and femur, and 50 Gy/5 in humerus and acetabulum, which frames fractionation choice for weight-bearing long bones and joints.

- 🔍 Survey of 9 international radiation oncologists, 11 nonsurgical NSBM cases, 100% completion
- 🔍 Cases covered long (femur, humerus), pelvic and thoracic bones; RCC, breast, NSCLC, prostate histologies
- 🔍 GTV pre-contoured and sent with planning CT + MRI; descriptive analysis only
- 📊 Schedules spanned 18-24 Gy/1, 24 Gy/2, 28-30 Gy/3, 30-50 Gy/5, 42-50 Gy/10
- 📐 BED range 48 to 100 Gy10; 58% of schedules had BED 60 Gy10
- 📊 35 Gy/5 chosen in 29 of 99 responses (29%); 5 experts used it in ≥1 case
- 📊 40 Gy/5 judged acceptable unanimously (n=9) or near-unanimously (n=8) wherever listed
- 📊 SIB used by 3 experts; 30 Gy/3 + 15-24 Gy/3 most common (12 of 24, 50%)
- 🔍 MRI fusion routine for 5 experts (56%); 2 CT-only; 2 fuse MRI selectively
- 📊 Schedules a majority of experts did NOT recommend, by site

  | Case | Not recommended by majority |
  |---|---|
  | Humerus (RCC) | 20 Gy/1, 50 Gy/5 |
  | Acetabulum (breast) | 20 Gy/1, 50 Gy/5, 8 Gy/1 + 18 Gy/1 SIB |
  | Femur (NSCLC) | 20 Gy/1 |

- 📊 Factors changing dose selection (n experts)

  | Factor | Direction | Experts |
  |---|---|---|
  | Prior SBRT to same target >3 mo ago | De-escalate | All |
  | Prior cRT to same target >3 mo ago | De-escalate | 7 |
  | Weight-bearing, moderate-severe cortical erosion | De-escalate | 7 |
  | Weight-bearing bone in general | De-escalate | 5 |
  | Radioresistant histology | Escalate | 3 |
  | Bulky tumor | Escalate | 1 |
  | Extraosseous disease | Escalate | 1 |

- 📊 Unanimous: no dose change for mild cortical erosion or non-weight-bearing bone with moderate-severe erosion
- 🔗 vs Erler et al: 30-35 Gy/5 gave LC 95% at 6 mo, 87% at 24 mo (106 NSBM)
- 🔗 Same series: fracture 8.5%, median time to fracture 2.5 mo after 30-35 Gy/5
- ⚠️ No formal consensus process; expert opinion only, no outcomes measured
- ⚠️ BED computed at α/β 10, which overstates equivalence for breast and prostate histologies

[doi:10.1016/j.prro.2020.02.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prro.2020.02.011)

### Single-Fraction SBRT vs Multifraction RT for Nonspine Bone Metastases · [[NCT02163226]]

> Single-fraction SBRT (12 or 16 Gy) vs 30 Gy/10fx: ITT 3-mo pain response 38% vs 21% (P=.05); local failure 0% vs 9.7% at 24 mo.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dose split matters most: at 3 mo, 16 Gy (lesions ≤4 cm) gave 62% CR+PR vs 30% for 12 Gy and 21% for 30 Gy/10fx, and 42.9% vs 13.3% vs 15.2% at 9 mo. Lesion size set the dose, so 12 Gy for larger lesions adds little over the long course, and the trial never tested 8 Gy x1.

- 🔍 Single-institution (MD Anderson) randomized phase 2 noninferiority trial, nonblinded, N=160 (81 SBRT, 79 MFRT)
- 🔍 SBRT 12 Gy (>4 cm) or 16 Gy (≤4 cm) x1 vs 30 Gy/10fx
- 🔍 PTV = GTV + 5mm, no CTV; prophylactic 7-day low-dose dexamethasone recommended for SBRT
- 📐 1° EP pain response (CR+PR) per international consensus criteria; NI margin 10%, 1-sided α 0.20, 90% power
- 📊 Pain response (CR+PR) by population and timepoint

  | Population · timepoint | SBRT | MFRT | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ITT · 1 mo | 36 of 81 (44%) | 24 of 79 (30%) | .18 |
  | ITT · 3 mo | 31 of 81 (38%) | 17 of 79 (21%) | .05 |
  | Per-protocol · 2 wk | 34 of 55 (62%) | 19 of 52 (36%) | .01 |
  | Per-protocol · 3 mo | 31 of 43 (72%) | 17 of 35 (49%) | .03 |
  | Per-protocol · 9 mo | 17 of 22 (77%) | 12 of 26 (46%) | .04 |

- 📊 Pain response by dose, SBRT ITT subset (dose assigned by lesion size, not randomized)

  | Timepoint | 16 Gy x1 | 12 Gy x1 | 30 Gy/10fx | P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 3 mo | 62% (13 of 21) | 30% (18 of 60) | 21% (17 of 79) | .003 |
  | 9 mo | 42.9% (9 of 21) | 13.3% (8 of 60) | 15.2% (12 of 79) | .005 |

- 📊 Local control, reirradiation and OS

  | Endpoint | SBRT | MFRT | Stat |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Local PFS 1y | 100% | 90.5% | P=.01 |
  | Local PFS 2y | 100% | 75.6% | P=.01 |
  | Cum. incidence local failure 24 mo | 0% | 9.7% | P=.02 |
  | Reirradiation 2y | 0% | 5.3% | P=.10 |
  | Median OS | 6.7 mo | 6.7 mo | no difference |

- 📐 ITT hazard ratios at 3 mo, SBRT vs MFRT
  - Composite pain failure (PP + IR): HR 0.73 (95% CI 0.16-3.27)
  - ≥50% MED increase: HR 0.26 (95% CI 0.07-0.95)
  - ≥2-point pain score increase: HR 1.22 (95% CI 0.82-1.81)
  - Local failure: HR 0.18 (95% CI 0.02-1.47)
- 📊 No toxicity differences between arms
  - G3 nausea 1.2% SBRT vs 5.0% MFRT (P=.21)
  - G3 fatigue 9.9% vs 5.1% (P=.37)
  - Fracture 1.2% vs 0% (P=.99)
- ⚠️ Superiority signal comes from the per-protocol analysis; ITT 1-mo difference P=.18
- ⚠️ Comparator was 30 Gy/10fx, not 8 Gy x1
- ⚠️ 5 SBRT pts not treated due to insurance denial; no equivalent loss in the control arm
- ⚠️ Size cut-point for 12 vs 16 Gy stated inconsistently (≥4 cm vs >4 cm for 12 Gy)

[doi:10.1001/jamaoncol.2019.0192](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2019.0192)
  - 📎 [[papers/oligo-mets/2019-nguyen-single-fraction-stereotactic-conventional-multifraction.pdf|Single-Fraction SBRT vs Multifraction RT for Nonspine Bone Metastases (full text)]]

## 🌐 Lymphoma

### Low-Dose Involved Field RT in Indolent Lymphomas

> 4 Gy involved-field RT: ORR 92%, CR 61%, median TP 14 mo and TLP 25 mo in symptomatic recurrent indolent lymphoma, toxicity virtually absent.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Response depth sets how long 4 Gy controls the site: PR pts relapsed in-field first (median TLP 10 mo) while CR pts held local control for a median 42 mo. Re-treatment of selected responders still gave RR 98%, so one low-dose course does not rule out another at relapse.

- 🔍 Phase II, 109 assessable of 111 treated, 304 symptomatic sites, Nov 1997 to Mar 2002
- 🔍 Symptomatic recurrent B-cell indolent lymphoma: FL 98 (G1 43, G2 55), MALT 9, lymphoplasmacytoid 2
- 🔍 Median 2 prior regimens; 50% refractory to last regimen; bulky ≥5cm in 52%
- 🔍 RT: 4 Gy as 2×2 Gy 48h apart (n=80) or 1×4 Gy (n=29); whole nodal area or node + 1.5cm margin
- 📊 Time to progression by response to first LD-IF-RT

  | Group | N | Median TP (mo) | Median TLP (mo) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All | 109 | 14 | 25 |
  | CR | 67 | 25 | 42 |
  | PR | 34 | 9 | 10 |
  | Nonresponders | 8 | 2 | 2 |

- 📊 First course vs LD-IF-RT re-treatment (selected responders)

  | Course | N | RR | CR | PR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | First LD-IF-RT | 109 | 92% | 61% | 31% |
  | Re-treatment | 41 | 98% | 71% | 27% |

- 📊 Toxicity virtually absent: 1 transient alopecia, 1 transient hyperpigmentation, no tumor lysis
- 📐 RR vs prior regimens: better than 2nd (P=.04) and last (P=.03); no difference vs 1st (P=.08)
- ⚠️ Median f/u for pts still in remission only 7 mo
- 🔗 vs Girinsky (Paris) series: similar RR and CR, but 2y local PFS in PR pts 46% vs 10% here

**Sources:**
- 📄 **High Response Rates and Lasting Remissions After Low-Dose Involved Field Radiotherapy in Indolent Lymphomas** — Haas, R.L.M.; Poortmans, Ph.; de Jong, D. et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2003-07). [doi:10.1200/jco.2003.09.542](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2003.09.542)
  > Purpose: To study the response rates and duration of response after low-dose (4 Gy) involved field radiotherapy (LD-IF-RT) in patients with recurrent indolent lymphoma. Patients and Methods: A total of 109 assessable patients (304 symptomatic sites) were irradiated (53 males and 56 females; median age, 62 years; range, 35 to 93), including 98 patients with follicular lymphoma (43 grade 1 and 55 grade 2), nine extranodal marginal zone lymphomas of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue-type and two patients with lymphoplasmacytoid lymphoma. Bulky disease (≥5 cm) was present in 52% of all patients. A median of two prior regimens (range, 0 to 11) preceded LD-IF-RT. The median time since diagnosis was 41 months (range, 2 to 358 months). Time to (local) progression was calculated according to the Kaplan-Meier method. Differences in response rates between treatments within the same patient were compared using the McNemar test. Results: The overall response rate was 92%; complete response was reached in 67 patients (61%), partial response in 34 patients (31%), stable disease in six patients (6%), and progressive disease in two patients (2%). The median time to progression was 14 months. The median time to local progression was 25 months. The 67 patients with complete response showed a median time to progression of 25 months and a median time to local progression of 42 months. None of the factors studied (age, sex, follicular lymphoma grade, radiotherapy regimen, number of previous regimens and previous history, number of positive sites or largest lymphoma diameter) were found to be related to response rate. Conclusion: LD-IF-RT is a valuable asset in the management of patients with follicular lymphoma and should be considered in patients with recurrent disease.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

### SBRT for Ultra-Central Lung Tumors: Meta-Analysis and ISRS Guidelines

> Pooled 1-yr LC 92% and 2-yr LC 89%, with G5 toxicity 4%, across 1183 pts with ultra-central lung tumors; ISRS sets PBT Dmax and fractionation limits.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable output is the PBT constraint: Dmax <133-150 Gy BED3 (EQD2 80-90 Gy), prescribed to an 80-85% IDL, dropping to 60 Gy/15 with Dmax <100 Gy BED3 for endobronchial disease. Pooled meta-regression could not test PBT Dmax, so the threshold rests on HILUS, Tekatli, Mihai and Wang dosimetry.

- 🔍 SR/MA of 27 studies (2010-2022), 1183 pts, random-effects model
- 🔍 All retrospective except HILUS (prospective phase 2 observational); none randomized
- 🔍 Ultra-central definition varied: PTV-PBT overlap in all studies, GTV overlap in only 16 (59%)
- 📊 Pooled estimates

  | Endpoint | Pooled | 95% CI | I² |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1-yr LC | 92% | 86-96 | 93.2% |
  | 2-yr LC | 89% | 82-94 | 94.3% |
  | Any G3-4 toxicity | 6% | 3-10 | 71% |
  | Any G5 toxicity | 4% | 2-6 | 68% |
  | G5 hemoptysis | 1% | 0-3 | 65% |
  | G3-4 pneumonitis | 1% | 0-2 | 5% |

- 📊 NSCLC-only subset: 1- and 2-yr LC both 95%
- 📐 Higher BED linked to 1-yr LC (HR 2.14, p=0.03); no 2-yr association (p=0.66)
- 📊 Hemoptysis caused most G5 events (42 events, 58%); G5 range across studies 0-22%
- 🔍 ISRS recommendations by category

  | Category | Recommendation |
  |---|---|
  | Dose / delivery | 60 Gy/8 or 60 Gy/15; global Dmax far <150% of Rx; prescribe to 80-85% IDL |
  | PBT constraint | Dmax <133-150 Gy BED3 (EQD2 α/β=3 <80-90 Gy); lower if risk factors or near trachea/main bronchi |
  | Antiplatelet / anticoagulation | Discontinue when pausing is low risk; else de-escalate PBT Dmax or use non-SBRT schedule |
  | Targeted therapy / IO | Avoid concomitant use; ≥2-3 days for targeted, 3-4 wk for VEGF inhibitors, ≥2 days for IO |
  | Endobronchial tumor | Non-SBRT regimen, e.g. 60 Gy/15; PBT Dmax <100 Gy BED3 (EQD2 <60 Gy) |
  | ILD | Lung V20 <10% strict cutoff; treat on trial |

- ⚠️ Fatal-toxicity risk modifiers from included series
  - VEGF inhibitor within 30 days of SBRT: fatal hemoptysis HR 16.9, p<0.001 (88 pts)
  - Endobronchial tumor in 8 fatal cases
  - Antiplatelet and/or anticoagulant use in 17 G5 hemoptysis cases
  - ILD: 5 G5 events (2 pneumonitis, 3 hemoptysis)
- 🔗 vs HILUS (56 Gy/8): G5 15%, prescribed to 67% IDL, PTV margin up to 15 mm
- 🔗 HILUS G5 hemoptysis 4% if >1 cm from main bronchi/trachea vs 18% if ≤1 cm
- ⚠️ PBT Dmax showed no association with toxicity on meta-regression; too few studies reported dosimetry
- ⚠️ 60% of studies had median f/u <2 yr; late hemoptysis likely under-captured
- ⚠️ Publication bias detected across all LC and toxicity endpoints

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Stereotactic body radiotherapy for Ultra-Central lung Tumors: A systematic review and Meta-Analysis and International Stereotactic Radiosurgery Society practice guidelines** — Yan, Michael; Louie, Alexander V.; Kotecha, Rupesh et al.. *Lung Cancer* (2023-08). [doi:10.1016/j.lungcan.2023.107281](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lungcan.2023.107281)
  - 📎 [[papers/thoracic/2023-yan-stereotactic-body-radiotherapy-ultra-central-lung-tumors.pdf|SBRT for Ultra-Central Lung Tumors: Meta-Analysis and ISRS Guidelines (full text)]]

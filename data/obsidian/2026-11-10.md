### Modeling clinical outcomes in radiotherapy: NTCP, TCP and the TECs

> Personal review of TCP/NTCP outcome modeling and the AAPM/ASTRO TEC projects (QUANTEC, HyTEC, PENTEC); no effect size reported in source.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> This is a field history of where the dose-volume constraints in daily planning come from: the QUANTEC, HyTEC and PENTEC efforts, by a contributor to them. Useful as background on the limits of TCP/NTCP modeling. The abstract carries no specific constraint or model value, so nothing here changes a planning decision directly.

- 🔍 Narrative review by a single author (Medical Physics, 2023), not a outcomes report
- 🔍 Frames RT planning as balancing high TCP against low NTCP
- 🔍 Core gap named: dosimetric and clinical correlates of desired outcomes rarely unified
- 🔗 TEC projects discussed (AAPM + ASTRO)
  - QUANTEC: normal tissue effects in the clinic
  - HyTEC: hypofractionated treatment effects
  - PENTEC: pediatric normal tissue effects
- 🔍 Draws on in-house institutional data analysis and the author's TEC participation
- ⚠️ Abstract only in source; no dose constraints, model parameters or NTCP/TCP values reported
- ⚠️ Perspective piece by a TEC participant; no systematic search or pooled analysis described

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Modeling clinical outcomes in radiotherapy: NTCP, TCP and the “TECs”** — Yorke, Ellen. *Medical Physics* (2023-02). [doi:10.1002/mp.16274](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.16274)
  > Abstract Radiation therapy is a double‐edged sword which damages both tumor and normal cells. I am fortunate to have participated in some of these struggles, including analyzing and using in‐house data and being involved in the AAPM and ASTRO “TEC” projects—QUANTEC (Quantitatie Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic), HyTEC (Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic) and PENTEC (Pediatric Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic).

### Biological principles of SBRT/SRS: indirect cell death

> Narrative review: doses above ~10 Gy/fraction cause tumor vascular injury and secondary cell death, plus enhanced antitumor immunity, beyond direct cell kill.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The ~10 Gy/fraction figure is the RT-relevant detail: above it, the review attributes extra tumor kill to vascular injury and immune effects that direct-kill models do not capture. The same review flags the normal tissue mechanism at these doses as unresolved, so the biology supports ablative tumor dosing more firmly than it informs OAR tolerance.

- 🔍 Narrative review of prior reports and recent observations; no systematic search or pooled analysis described in source
- 🔍 Scope: high-dose RT effects on tumor cell survival, tumor vasculature, and antitumor immunity
- 📊 Above ~10 Gy/fraction: vascular injury in tumors → secondary (indirect) tumor cell death
- 📊 High-dose irradiation reported to increase antitumor immunity; approaches to further boost it under investigation
- ⚠️ Mechanism of normal tissue damage after high-dose RT stated as unresolved, needs further study
- ⚠️ No effect size reported in source; mechanistic claims rest on preclinical and observational data per abstract
- ⚠️ ~10 Gy/fraction is an approximate threshold, not a validated clinical cutoff

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Biological Principles of Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT) and Stereotactic Radiation Surgery (SRS): Indirect Cell Death** — Song, Chang W.; Glatstein, Eli; Marks, Lawrence B. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.02.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.02.047)
  > Purpose To review the radiobiological mechanisms of stereotactic body radiation therapy stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and stereotactic radiation surgery (SRS). Methods and materials We reviewed previous reports and recent observations on the effects of high-dose irradiation on tumor cell survival, tumor vasculature, and antitumor immunity. We then assessed the potential implications of these biological changes associated with SBRT and SRS. Results Irradiation with doses higher than approximately 10 Gy/fraction causes significant vascular injury in tumors, leading to secondary tumor cell death. Irradiation of tumors with high doses has also been reported to increase the antitumor immunity, and various approaches are being investigated to further elevate antitumor immunity. The mechanism of normal tissue damage by high-dose irradiation needs to be further investigated. Conclusions In addition to directly killing tumor cells, high-dose irradiation used in SBRT and SRS induces indirect tumor cell death via vascular damage and antitumor immunity. Further studies are warranted to better understand the biological mechanisms underlying the high efficacy of clinical SBRT and SRS and to further improve the efficacy of SBRT and SRS.

> [!question] Open questions

> - Best approach to amplify antitumor immunity after SBRT/SRS

### Late effects of RT in children vs adults: QUANTEC, HyTEC, PENTEC

> No effect size reported in source; pediatric NTCP similar to adults for brain necrosis, optic, parotid, liver, greater for neurocognition, cerebrovascular, lactation, lower for ovary.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Adult QUANTEC/HyTEC limits are not a safe proxy in children for neurocognition, cerebrovascular injury, or breast lactation, where pediatric risk is greater; for brain necrosis, optic apparatus, parotid, and liver the risk appears similar. Within pediatrics, younger age raises hearing and neurocognitive risk but lowers ovarian and thyroid risk, so age gates which constraint set to trust.

- 🔍 Review: compares PENTEC (pediatric) with adult QUANTEC and HyTEC on content, oversight, support, scope, literature-review method
- 📊 Pediatric vs adult late-effect risk by organ/endpoint (direction only, no NTCP values in source)

  | Child vs adult risk | Organ / endpoint |
  |---|---|
  | Similar | Brain (necrosis), optic apparatus, parotid, liver |
  | Greater | Brain (neurocognition), cerebrovascular, breast (lactation) |
  | Perhaps slightly less | Lung |
  | Less | Ovary |
  | Relevant only in children | Musculoskeletal maldevelopment, teeth, breast development, reproductive organs |

- 📊 Within the pediatric range (incl. adolescence), younger age shifts risk
  - Younger = greater risk: hearing, brain (neurocognition)
  - Younger = lesser risk: ovary, thyroid
- ⚠️ Child vs adult NTCP confounded by treatment paradigm: cancer types, prescribed dose, fields, chemo agents
- ⚠️ Unresolved whether age should be modeled continuously or by grouping (infant through older adult)
- ⚠️ Abstract only: no dose-volume constraints or NTCP estimates reported in source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Comparison of Risks of Late Effects From Radiation Therapy in Children Versus Adults: Insights From the QUANTEC, HyTEC, and PENTEC Efforts** — Milano, Michael T.; Marks, Lawrence B.; Olch, Arthur J. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2024-06). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2023.08.066](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2023.08.066)
  > Pediatric Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic (PENTEC) seeks to refine quantitative radiation dose-volume relationships for normal-tissue complication probabilities (NTCPs) in survivors of pediatric cancer. This article summarizes the evolution of PENTEC and compares it with similar adult-focused efforts (eg, Quantitative Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic [QUANTEC] and Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic [HyTEC]) with respect to content, oversight, support, scope, and methodology of literature review. It then summarizes key organ-specific findings from PENTEC in an attempt to compare NTCP estimates in children versus adults. In brief, select normal-tissue risks within developing organs and tissues (eg, maldevelopment of musculoskeletal tissue, teeth, breasts, and reproductive organs) are primarily relevant only in children. For some organs and tissues, children appear to have similar (eg, brain for necrosis, optic apparatus, parotid gland, liver), greater (eg, brain for neurocognition, cerebrovascular, breast for lactation), less (ovary), or perhaps slightly less (eg, lung) risks of toxicity versus adults. Similarly, even within the broad pediatric age range (including adolescence), for some endpoints, younger children have greater (eg, hearing and brain for neurocognition) or lesser (eg, ovary, thyroid) risks of radiation-associated toxicities. NTCP comparisons in adults versus children are often confounded by marked differences in treatment paradigms that expose normal tissues to radiation (ie, cancer types, prescribed radiation therapy dose and fields, and chemotherapy agents used). To add to the complexity, it is unclear if age is best analyzed as a continuous variable versus with age groupings (eg, infants, young children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, older adults). Further work is needed to better understand the complex manner in which age and developmental status affect risk.

### HyTEC: spinal cord dose tolerance to SBRT

> De novo cord Dmax 12.4-14.0 Gy/1fx, 17.0/2, 20.3/3, 23.0/4, 25.3 Gy/5fx carries an estimated 1-5% radiation myelopathy risk.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The limits depend on the contouring structure. Most refer to the thecal sac (effectively a cord PRV), but the 14 Gy single-fraction figure came from a CT-myelogram-defined cord using the MSKCC technique, so it transfers only with comparable contouring. Both RM cases in the 228-pt Katsoulakis series sat below the 13.85 Gy median Dmax.

- 📊 De novo spinal cord Dmax (Gy) by fraction number, estimated 1-5% RM risk

  | Fractions | Sahgal 2013 model (thecal sac) | LQ conversion of 14 Gy (α/β 2) | Rounded median limit |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 12.4 | 14 | 14 |
  | 2 | 17 | 19.3 | 18 |
  | 3 | 20.3 | 23.1 | 22 |
  | 4 | 23 | 26.2 | 26 |
  | 5 | 25.3 | 28.8 | 28 |

- ⚠️ LQ conversions of 14 Gy to 2-5 fx are not clinical data; authors say they cannot be recommended
- 🔍 Reirradiation SBRT (1-5 fx), factors linked to lower RM risk per Sahgal 2012
  - Cumulative thecal sac EQD2 Dmax ≤70 Gy
  - SBRT thecal sac EQD2 Dmax ≤25 Gy
  - SBRT-to-cumulative EQD2 Dmax ratio ≤0.5
  - Interval to reirradiation ≥5 mo
- 🔍 Pooled 7 de novo + 5 reirradiation series from 40 screened (2005-2018)
- ⚠️ Dose-reporting structure varied: 7 thecal sac, 3 true cord, 2 cord PRV (+1.5 mm)
- 📐 Garg: mean Dmax 12 Gy to cord vs 14.5 Gy to 1.5-mm PRV
- 📊 Katsoulakis (228 pts, single-fx 18-24 Gy): RM by cord V7Gy

  | Cord V7Gy | RM cases | Rate |
  |---|---|---|
  | >5.8 cm3 | 1 of 13 | 7.7% |
  | <5.8 cm3 | 1 of 246 | 0.4% |

- ⚠️ Both Katsoulakis RM cases (Dmax 13.4, 13.6 Gy) fell below the 13.85 Gy series median
- 🔗 At 14.6 Gy x1: Sahgal 2013, QUANTEC, Gibbs models predict ~17%, ~8%, ~3% RM
- 📊 Median RM latency after SBRT: 12 mo de novo, 6 mo after reirradiation

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Spinal Cord Dose Tolerance to Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy** — Sahgal, Arjun; Chang, Joe H.; Ma, Lijun et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.09.038](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.09.038)
  > Spinal cord tolerance data for stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) were extracted from published reports, reviewed, and modelled. For de novo SBRT delivered in 1 to 5 fractions, the following spinal cord point maximum doses (D max ) are estimated to be associated with a 1% to 5% risk of radiation myelopathy (RM): 12.4 to 14.0 Gy in 1 fraction, 17.0 Gy in 2 fractions, 20.3 Gy in 3 fractions, 23.0 Gy in 4 fractions, and 25.3 Gy in 5 fractions. For reirradiation SBRT delivered in 1 to 5 fractions, reported factors associated with a lower risk of RM include cumulative thecal sac equivalent dose in 2 Gy fractions with an alpha/beta of 2 (EQD2 2 ) D max ≤70 Gy; SBRT thecal sac EQD2 2 D max ≤25 Gy, thecal sac SBRT EQD2 2 D max to cumulative EQD2 2 D max ratio ≤0.5, and a minimum time interval to reirradiation of ≥5 months. Larger studies containing complete institutional cohorts with dosimetric data of patients treated with spine SBRT, with and without RM, are required to refine RM risk estimates.

### HyTEC: SBRT for pancreatic cancer, TCP models

> Pooled TCP model: 1-yr LC ≈79% to 86% at 30-36 Gy/3 fx equivalents, <70% below 24 Gy; 33 Gy/5 fx maps to 77%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 33 Gy/5 fx used in Alliance A021501 converts to 28.2 Gy in 3 fx, which the pooled model puts at 77% 1-yr LC without surgery, on the rising part of the curve below the ≈79% to 86% seen at 30-36 Gy equivalents. That frames dose escalation beyond 33/5 as a testable LC question when GI organ-at-risk constraints allow it.

- 🔍 HyTEC review: PubMed search of published SBRT series in 1-5 fx for localized pancreatic cancer
- 🔍 Settings pooled: preoperative (neoadjuvant), borderline resectable, locally advanced
- 📐 LQ model with α/β=10 Gy; all regimens converted to 3-fx equivalent doses
- 📐 Logistic TCP models fit by maximum likelihood
- 📊 Modeled 1-yr LC by 3-fx equivalent dose

  | 3-fx equivalent dose | Setting | 1-yr LC |
  |---|---|---|
  | <24 Gy | Without surgery | <70% |
  | 28.2 Gy (33 Gy/5 fx, Alliance A021501) | Without surgery | 77% |
  | 30-36 Gy | Without surgery | ≈79% to 86% |
  | >28 Gy | Margin-negative resection | >90% |

- 📊 Dose response seen across the 25 to 36 Gy 3-fx equivalent range
- ⚠️ Literature-level pooling, not individual pt data; case mix and systemic therapy not adjusted
- ⚠️ α/β=10 LQ conversion at ablative doses per fraction is an assumption, not a measured parameter
- ⚠️ R0-resection subgroup is selected; >90% LC reflects who reached surgery, not dose alone
- ⚠️ 1-yr LC only; no OS or toxicity data in the source abstract
- ⚠️ Authors state findings should be viewed with caution

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Maximizing Tumor Control and Limiting Complications With Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy for Pancreatic Cancer** — Mahadevan, Anand; Moningi, Shalini; Grimm, Jimm et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.11.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.11.017)
  > Purpose Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and stereotactic ablative body radiation therapy is being increasingly used for pancreatic cancer (PCa), particularly in patients with locally advanced and borderline resectable disease. A wide variety of dose fractionation schemes have been reported in the literature. This HyTEC review uses tumor control probability models to evaluate the comparative effectiveness of the various SBRT treatment regimens used in the treatment of patients with localized PCa. Methods and materials A PubMed search was performed to review the published literature on the use of hypofractionated SBRT (usually in 1-5 fractions) for PCa in various clinical scenarios (eg, preoperative [neoadjuvant], borderline resectable, and locally advanced PCa). The linear quadratic model with α/β= 10 Gy was used to address differences in fractionation. Logistic tumor control probability models were generated using maximum likelihood parameter fitting. Results After converting to 3-fraction equivalent doses, the pooled reported data and associated models suggests that 1-year local control (LC) without surgery is ≈79% to 86% after the equivalent of 30 to 36 Gy in 3 fractions, showing a dose response in the range of 25 to 36 Gy, and decreasing to less than 70% 1-year LC at doses below 24 Gy in 3 fractions. The 33 Gy in 5 fraction regimen (Alliance A021501) corresponds to 28.2 Gy in 3 fractions, for which the HyTEC pooled model had 77% 1-year LC without surgery. Above an equivalent dose of 28 Gy in 3 fractions, with margin-negative resection the 1-year LC exceeded 90%. Conclusions Pooled analyses of reported tumor control probabilities for commonly used SBRT dose-fractionation schedules for PCa suggests a dose response. These findings should be viewed with caution given the challenges and limitations of this review. Additional data are needed to better understand the dose or fractionation-response of SBRT for PCa.

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does escalation beyond 33 Gy/5 fx improve LC without excess GI toxicity?
> - Does improved 1-yr LC translate into OS benefit in LAPC?
> - Does the dose response hold with patient-level data and harmonized LC definitions?

### HyTEC: carotid blowout and bleeding in H&N SBRT reirradiation

> Pooled 5-fx major-vessel Dmax TD50 45.7 Gy (95% CI 39.6-64.9); Dmax <20-30 Gy associated with low bleeding risk in H&N SBRT reirradiation.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Small-volume dose above 20 Gy is the planning lever: with alternating-day SBRT plus risk-reduction strategies, modeled G3-5 CBS was 2.2% when 1 cm3 exceeded 20 Gy in 5 fx and <1% at 0.1 cm3. The pooled Dmax TD50 of 45.7 Gy sits above RTOG 3507's carotid Dmax ≤42 Gy, so that limit reads conservative, not validated.

- 🔍 HyTEC pooled modeling: 238 major vessel Dmax values from 6 articles (PubMed, April 2018)
- 🔍 2 later dose-volume datasets (Mavroidis/Yazici; Gebhardt/Ling) modeled separately for comparison
- 📊 Pooled 5-fx Dmax model: TD50 45.7 Gy (95% CI 39.6-64.9), γ50 1.1817 (0.65-1.8)
- 📊 Only 1 bleeding event (BE) occurred at carotid Dmax <30 Gy
- 📐 Dmax <20 to 30 Gy appears associated with low risk (5 fx, SBRT course only)
- 📊 Gebhardt series, risk-reduction strategies applied: 150 carotids in 75 pts, median 44 Gy (40-50) in 5 fx every other day
  - CBS 3/67 after 1 SBRT course vs 1/8 after multiple courses (P = .4)
  - Modeled G3-5 CBS 2.2% when 1 cm3 exceeded 20 Gy in 5 fx
  - Modeled G3-5 CBS <1% when 0.1 cm3 exceeded 20 Gy in 5 fx
  - Model rests on 5 scored events in 4 pts
- 🔍 RTOG 3507 carotid constraints for 5-fx SBRT (nonvalidated, cited as conservative guide)

  | Metric | Limit | Acceptable deviation |
  |---|---|---|
  | Carotid Dmax | ≤42 Gy | >42 but <44 Gy |
  | Carotid D50% | ≤32 Gy | >32 but <34 Gy |

- 🔍 Lower-BE factors: nonconsecutive-day delivery, less circumferential tumor involvement, no surgical manipulation around SBRT
- ⚠️ Prior RT dose not reported per pt; nominal 70 Gy assumed and excluded from all models
- ⚠️ All but 1 dataset mixes possibly tumor-driven bleeding into the BE endpoint
- ⚠️ Median f/u <2 yr in modeled series; Gebhardt 8 mo overall, 37 mo in survivors

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Initial Data Pooling for Radiation Dose-Volume Tolerance for Carotid Artery Blowout and Other Bleeding Events in Hypofractionated Head and Neck Retreatments** — Grimm, Jimm; Vargo, John A.; Mavroidis, Panayiotis et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.12.037](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.12.037)
  > Purpose Dose-volume data for injury to carotid artery and other major vessels in stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT)/SABR head and neck reirradiation were reviewed, modeled, and summarized. Methods and materials A PubMed search of the English-language literature (stereotactic and carotid and radiation) in April 2018 found 238 major vessel maximum point doses in 6 articles that were pooled for logistic modeling. Two subsequent studies with dose-volume major vessel data were modeled separately for comparison. Attempts were made to separate carotid blowout syndrome from other bleeding events (BE) in the analysis, but we acknowledge that all except 1 data set has some element of BE interspersed. Results Prior radiation therapy (RT) dose was not uniformly reported per patient in the studies included, but a course on the order of conventionally fractionated 70 Gy was considered for the purposes of the analysis (with an approximately ≥6-month estimated interval between prior and subsequent treatment in most cases). More recent experiences with alternating fractionation schedules and additional risk-reduction strategies are also presented. Complications data for the most critical structures such as spinal cord and carotid artery are so limited that they cannot be viewed as strong conclusions of probability of risk, but rather, as a general guideline for consideration. There is a great need for better reporting standards as noted in the High Dose per Fraction, Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic introductory paper.

### Contouring guidelines for the trigeminal nerve in H&N cancers

> CTV-P1 = GTV or R1 margin + 5 mm along the nerve; CTV-P2 covers the at-risk V1/V2/V3 path ≥30 mm, often to skull base.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> MRI is the reference standard yet misdefined PNI extent in 5 of 30 and 4 of 36 operated cases, and imaging skip lesions understate pathologically continuous spread. That is the basis for a ≥30 mm nerve-path CTV-P2 rather than enhancement plus margin alone, moving the elective nerve-path coverage decision.

- 🔍 Target volumes by PNI scenario (Table 2 + text)

  | Scenario | High-risk CTV (CTV-P1) | Low-risk CTV (CTV-P2) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Primary RT, non-resected macroscopic PNI | GTV (post-contrast enhancement along nerve) + 5 mm along nerve path | Nerve path ≥30 mm; up to skull base discussed, often recommended; to brainstem if skull base involved |
  | Post-op RT, macroscopic PNI with R1 margin | Positive margin + 5 mm along nerve path; PNI in entire tumor bed | Per histology/location risk |
  | ACC or SCC with multiple/extensive microscopic PNI | PNI in tumor bed | Nerve path ≥30 mm; up to skull base discussed, often recommended |
  | SCC with focal microscopic PNI | PNI in tumor bed | Nerve paths in GTV/tumor bed |

- 🔍 5 mm CTV-P1 margin can be increased when MRI extent is in doubt
- 🔍 Nerve-path CTV-P2 triggers: ACC, SCC with extensive PNI, PPF or masticator space invasion, NPC, maxillary sinus
- 🔍 Non-lateralized tumors: consider bilateral nerve paths for at least 20–30 mm
- ⚠️ Imaging underestimates PNI extent
  - MRI sensitivity 95–100% for macroscopic PNI
  - Gandhi 2011: MRI misdefined extent in 5 of 30 operated cases (17%)
  - Baulch 2015: MRI missed 2 of 38 (5%); extent wrong in 4 of 36 (11%)
  - Radiologic skip lesions; PNI usually continuous on pathology
  - 18FDG-PET-CT often inferior to MRI for macroscopic PNI
- 🔍 Check V-VII junctions: PPF/Vidian nerve, chorda tympani to lingual, auriculotemporal in parotid
- 🔍 MRI in treatment position recommended when possible; rigid MRI-CT co-registration near skull base
- 🔗 Zukauskaite: 1576 pts, primary RT for H&N SCC, most recurrences in GTV; excluded ACC, NPC, sinus, skin
- 🔗 EORTC 22931 enrolled PNI pts; concurrent cisplatin improved locoregional control vs PORT alone
- ⚠️ Focal microscopic PNI alone: only a relative adjuvant RT indication, case-by-case
- ⚠️ Skull base coverage hits brainstem, brain, optic pathway; undercoverage vs toxicity left to case-by-case judgment

### Neck node level delineation: 2013 update

> Cooperative-group consensus CT boundaries for neck node levels Ia through Xb, extending Robbins nomenclature to retropharyngeal, retro-styloid, parotid, bucco-facial and posterior skull groups.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The boundary changes that alter contours: IVa ends 2 cm above the sternal manubrium, with IVb and Vc carved out below it, and level V now starts at the hyoid. VIb starts at the thyroid cartilage unless the primary is anterior FOM, tongue tip or lower lip. Level selection and dose sit outside this document by design.

- 🔍 Consensus endorsed by DAHANCA, EORTC, HKNPCSG, NCIC CTG, NCRI, RTOG and TROG
- 🔍 Panel: radiation oncologists plus a head and neck surgeon and an anatomist
- 🔍 Boundaries defined on axial CT for a pt in neutral position
- 📐 Cranial and caudal limits by level (Tables 2-9)

  | Level | Cranial limit | Caudal limit |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ia (submental) | Mylo-hyoid m. | Platysma m. (caudal edge of anterior belly of digastric mm) |
  | Ib (submandibular) | Cranial edge of submandibular gland; anteriorly, mylo-hyoid m. | Plane through caudal edge of hyoid bone and caudal edge of mandible |
  | II (upper jugular) | Caudal edge of lateral process of C1 | Caudal edge of the body of the hyoid bone |
  | III (middle jugular) | Caudal edge of the body of the hyoid bone | Caudal edge of cricoid cartilage |
  | IVa (lower jugular) | Caudal edge of cricoid cartilage | 2 cm cranial to sternal manubrium |
  | IVb (medial supraclavicular) | Caudal border of level IVa | Cranial edge of sternal manubrium |
  | V (posterior triangle) | Cranial edge of the body of hyoid bone | Plane just below transverse cervical vessels |
  | Vc (lateral supraclavicular) | Plane just below transverse cervical vessels | 2 cm cranial to sternal manubrium |
  | VIa (anterior jugular) | Caudal edge of hyoid or submandibular gland, whichever is more caudal | Cranial edge of the sternal manubrium |
  | VIb (prelaryngeal, pretracheal, paratracheal) | Caudal edge of thyroid cartilage (hyoid body for anterior FOM, tongue tip, lower lip) | Cranial edge of the sternal manubrium |

### Target volume selection and delineation (T and N), oral cavity to larynx SCC

> N0-N1 neck: levels I-III (oral cavity) or II-IVa (pharynx/larynx); N2a-N3: Ib-V; primary CTV = GTV + 5 and 10 mm, anatomically edited.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The trade is explicit: N1 hypopharynx is pulled out of the selective II-IVa template because of its nodal propensity, and oral cavity N2a+ is flagged for contralateral coverage even when lateralized. On the primary, a Danish 3-center series found local relapse unrelated to margin across 0 to 10 mm, which is the evidence behind trusting a GTV + 5 mm high-dose CTV.

- 🔍 Narrative review of international consensus guidelines, Oral Oncology 2018; primary (non-postop) RT for oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx SCC
- 🔍 Nodal staging per AJCC/UICC 8th ed; T1N0 glottic excluded from nodal recommendations
- 📊 Elective nodal level selection by N category (from review text)

  | Nodal setting | Oral cavity | Oropharynx / hypopharynx / larynx |
  |---|---|---|
  | N0 | Levels I-III | Levels II-IVa |
  | N1 | Levels I-III | Levels II-IVa; NOT hypopharynx (high nodal propensity) |
  | N2a, N2b, N3 | Ib (Ia for anterior oral cavity) to V | Ib to V; RP nodes for oropharynx + hypopharynx |
  | N2c | Select each side by its own N category | Select each side by its own N category |

- 🔍 Conditional add-on levels
  - VIIa (RP nodes): posterior pharyngeal wall primaries
  - VI: piriform apex, post-cricoid or esophageal extension; transglottic or subglottic larynx
  - IVb + Vc: when IVa or Vb infiltrated
  - VIIb: upper level II infiltration
  - N3: non-lymphatic structures (skin, muscle, parotid, bone) may need inclusion
- 🔍 Unilateral neck RT advisable for well-lateralized primaries
  - Upper/lower gum not approaching midline; lateral FOM; cheek; RMT
  - Lateral mobile tongue not within 1 cm of midline
  - Tonsillar fossa without BOT, soft palate or posterior pillar extension
  - Lateral wall of piriform sinus
  - Exception: contralateral neck may be advisable for oral cavity N2a or more
- 🔍 Nodal CTV: 10 mm isotropic expansion into abutting structures from nodal GTV, excluding bone and airway (expert opinion)
- 🔍 Therapeutic nodal CTV = nodal GTV + 5 mm, kept inside the prophylactic nodal CTV
- 💊 SIB dose schema cited
  - N0 neck: 54-56 Gy in 35 fx of 1.64-1.6 Gy over 6.5-7 wk
  - N+ neck prophylactic: 54-56 Gy in 33-35 fx
  - N+ neck therapeutic: 70 Gy in 33-35 fx of 2.12-2.0 Gy over 7 wk
- 🔍 Primary CTV: CTV-P2 = GTV + 10 mm, then edited for air, uninvolved bone and barriers to spread
- 🔍 Planning: IV contrast CT, 2 mm axial sections; GTV from endoscopy under GA + CT/MRI, ± FDG-PET for larger tumors
- 🔗 Anatomic school (Eisbruch, Grégoire/Daisne, Lapeyre): GTV + 3 to 10 mm high-dose CTV, whole compartment low-dose, 2-3 cm where no barrier
- 🔗 DAHANCA geometric: GTV + 5 mm high-risk CTV, + further 5 mm lower-risk; improved inter-center delineation consistency
- 📊 Danish 3-center study (margins 0 to 10 mm): recurrences mainly within GTV, no relation between local relapse and margin
- ⚠️ Out-of-CTV recurrences fell within the 95% isodose, so authors attribute them to biology rather than delineation
- ⚠️ Margin rationale rests on pathology series (spread typically within first 5 mm) and retrospective recurrence data; DAHANCA marginal-miss analysis still ongoing
- ⚠️ No data supporting different nodal level selection for p16-positive OPC; recommendation is extrapolated, not tested
- ⚠️ Not valid for postop setting or adenoid cystic carcinoma; post-induction chemo cases should use pre-chemo extent

### CTV-P delineation consensus guidelines, H&N SCC

> CTV-P1 = GTV-P + 5 mm (high dose), CTV-P2 = GTV-P + 10 mm (lower dose), anatomically edited, by subsite and T stage.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The hypopharynx is the exception to 5 + 5: submucosal spread up to 20-30 mm cranio-caudal supports a 15 mm CC margin to CTV-P2 for T2-T4. At the other end, T1 glottic gets no CTV-P2 at all, and anatomic barriers (cartilage, bone, fascia) are edited out unless invaded.

- 🔍 International multi-society consensus (AIRO, DAHANCA, EORTC, GORTEC, NRG Oncology, TROG, others) for primary-tumour CTV
- 📐 '5 + 5 mm' concept: CTV-P1 = GTV-P + 5 mm; CTV-P2 = GTV-P + 10 mm; CTV-P1 always within CTV-P2
- 📊 CTV-P margins by subsite and T stage (as extracted; supraglottic T2-T3 and oral cavity text truncated in source)

  | Subsite / T | CTV-P1 | CTV-P2 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Glottic T1 | GTV-P + 5 mm | Not recommended |
  | Glottic T2 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm (may omit if small/superficial, not from anterior commissure) |
  | Glottic T3-T4 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm |
  | Supraglottic T1, T4 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm |
  | Subglottic T1-T4 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm (T1: may omit) |
  | Hypopharynx T1 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm (may omit if small/superficial) |
  | Hypopharynx T2-T4 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm; consider 15 mm cranio-caudal |
  | Oropharynx T1 | GTV-P + 5 mm | GTV-P + 10 mm, excl air, bone, mobile tongue |

- 💊 Dose levels tied to volumes (EQD2-style, 2 Gy per fraction)
  - CTV-P1: typically 70 Gy over 7 weeks
  - 2-level centres: CTV-P2 typically 50 Gy over 5 weeks
  - 3-level centres: CTV-P2 typically 60 Gy over 6 weeks, CTV-P3 50 Gy
- 🔍 Expansion always edited for air cavities, abutting subsites, and barriers (mandible, maxilla, hyoid, spine, fascia) unless invaded
- 🔗 Laser surgery T1-T2 glottic/supraglottic: 2-3 mm mucosal margin, LC 85-95% without PORT
- 🔗 Transoral tonsil T1-T2: margin of at least 2-3 mm, LC above 90% without PORT
- ⚠️ Hypopharynx: submucosal extension up to 20-30 mm cranio-caudal reported, hence the 15 mm option
- ⚠️ Surgical series highly selected, mostly HPV-positive; HPV effect on required margin unknown
- ⚠️ Primary setting only: not for post-operative or recurrent disease; PTV margin left to each centre

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

Spine SBRT: dose for target LC by fractionation, with epidural undercoverage next to the cord named as the predominant failure site.

### HyTEC: SBRT for spinal metastases, TCP analyses

> Pooled 2y local control model: 82% at 18 Gy, 90% at 20 Gy, 96% at 24 Gy single fraction; 82% at 24 Gy/2 fx, 78% at 27 Gy/3 fx.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Target coverage likely drives control more than the prescription: at a fixed 24 Gy/1 fx, 2y control was 98% with PTV D95 >22.4 Gy vs 85% with <16.5 Gy. Most failures are epidural, next to the cord, so the dose reaching the epidural margin decides whether these TCP estimates hold in a given plan.

- 🔍 HyTEC pooled TCP modeling: 24 articles, 2619 spinal metastases, published 2006-2018
- 🔍 Inclusion required prescription dose, fractionation and actuarial 2y local control; 30 dose/fractionation data points
- 📐 α/β estimated at 6 Gy by matching isoeffect levels across fractionations; computed values ranged 3 to 12 Gy

  - 811 lesions at 24 Gy/1 fx: 2y control 98% if PTV D95 >22.4 Gy vs 85% if <16.5 Gy

  > Purpose We sought to investigate the tumor control probability (TCP) of spinal metastases treated with stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) in 1 to 5 fractions. Methods and materials PubMed-indexed articles from 1995 to 2018 were eligible for data extraction if they contained SBRT dosimetric details correlated with actuarial 2-year local tumor control rates. Logistic dose-response models of collected data were compared in terms of physical dose and 3-fraction equivalent dose. Results Data were extracted from 24 articles with 2619 spinal metastases. ## 🧠 CNS

Brain mets SRS: lesion size drives the dose needed for LC; 21-40 mm lesions reach 80% 1-yr LC only with 27-35 Gy in 3-5 fx.

### HyTEC: TCP of SRS and fSRS for brain metastases

> Mets ≤20 mm: 18 Gy and 24 Gy single-fraction gave >85% and 95% 1-yr LC; 21-40 mm reached 80% with 27-35 Gy in 3-5 fx.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The fractionation advantage concentrates in large lesions at 2 years: single-fraction data never cleared 60% 2-yr LC, while 7 of 8 fractionated points did (P = .0014). At α/β 20 Gy, 30 Gy/3 and 35 Gy/5 carry 8% and 11.5% more tumor SFED than 20 Gy/1, backing fSRS over the RTOG 90-05 MTD for lesions >20 mm.

- 🔍 AAPM HyTEC pooled dose-response model: 56 of 2951 screened manuscripts, 13,929 brain mets
- 🔍 PubMed studies Jan 1995 to Sep 2017; each stratum ≥10 pts with ≥1-yr actuarial LC
- 📊 Modeled 1-yr LC by lesion size and regimen (intact mets, SRS alone)

  | Lesion size | Regimen | 1-yr LC |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤20 mm | 18 Gy x1 | >85% |
  | ≤20 mm | 24 Gy x1 | 95% |
  | 21-30 mm | 18 Gy x1 | 75% |
  | 31-40 mm | 15 Gy x1 | ~69% |
  | 21-40 mm | 27-35 Gy in 3-5 fx | 80% |
  | Melanoma, small | 18 Gy x1 | 19.5% |
  | Melanoma, small | 24.6 Gy x1 | 95% |

- 📊 2-yr LC, large tumors: single-fx data never exceeded 60%; 7 of 8 fractionated points did (P = .0014)
- 📐 18 Gy/1 tracked 24 Gy/3, implying α/β ≈ 22 Gy; α/β 20 Gy used for all SFED modeling
- 📐 Fractionation vs 20 Gy/1 (Table 2 logic, α/β 20 Gy tumor, 2 Gy normal brain)
  - 30 Gy/3 and 35 Gy/5: 8% and 11.5% higher tumor SFED
  - Normal-brain SFED 10% and 16% below tumor SFED for 3 and 5 fx
  - 80% 1-yr LC for medium and large tumors at SFED 19.6 and 21.4 Gy
- 🔗 vs RTOG 90-05: size-based MTDs 24/18/15 Gy set by toxicity in re-irradiated pts; in-field control ≈50%
- ⚠️ LC mostly MRI-defined; pseudoprogression can inflate failure, censoring at death can overstate control
- ⚠️ Prior WBRT (median 44% of pts in ~half of studies) recorded as binary; cumulative dose not modeled
- ⚠️ 1-yr OS >50% in 9/35 single-fx vs 8/13 fractionated points (P = .039); OS mostly extracranially driven

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

ESTRO ACROP LA-NSCLC volumes: FDG-PET-CT mandatory, 5-8 mm CTV, elective nodal CTV beyond the hilum and adjacent stations discouraged.

### ESTRO ACROP target volume guidelines, locally advanced NSCLC

> Consensus requires contrast CT, FDG-PET-CT and a 5-8 mm CTV, and discourages elective nodal CTV beyond the hilum and adjacent stations in LA-NSCLC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Its nodal rules have the most effect on volumes. A PET+/EBUS-negative node stays in the GTV, and after induction chemo every pre-chemo involved node stays in, even if it is no longer visible. Elective nodal CTV beyond the hilum and adjacent stations is discouraged. Whether to build the nodal CTV by whole station or by a 5-8 mm geometric expansion is left to the clinician.

- 🔍 ESTRO ACROP committee consensus: structured questionnaire, then consensus discussion and voting
- 🔍 Recommendations graded Mandatory (M), Recommended (R), Optional (O), Discouraged (D)
- 🔍 Key recommendations by planning step

  | Step | Recommendation | Grade |
  |---|---|---|
  | Diagnostic imaging | IV contrast CT + whole-body FDG-PET-CT | M |
  | PET-CT timing | Within 3 weeks before start of treatment | R |
  | Sequential chemo → RT | PET-CT before induction chemo; repeat contrast chest CT before RT | M |
  | Planning CT | Treatment position, 2-3 mm slices, cricoid to mid abdomen | M |
  | Respiratory motion | 4D-CT assessment | R |
  | Planning PET-CT | Preferably in planning position | R |
  | Nodal GTV after induction | All pre-chemo involved nodes, even if no longer visible on imaging | M |
  | Primary CTV | GTV + e.g. 5-8 mm, edited to anatomic barriers | M |
  | Elective nodal CTV beyond hilum/neighbouring stations | Not recommended | D |
  | Manual PTV editing | Should not be performed | D |
  | Adaptive re-planning | Individual basis for baseline shift or anatomic change | O |
  | Final contour approval | Radiation oncologist | M |

- 🔍 Nodal CTV: two accepted options (M)
  - Option 1: whole involved nodal station, at least a 5-8 mm margin around the GTV
  - Option 2: geometric expansion of the nodal GTV, 5-8 mm
- 🔍 FDG-PET+ but EBUS/EUS-negative nodes go into the GTV because EBUS/EUS false-negative rates are high
- 🔍 When an FDG+ node is uncertain, the guideline favours including it in the GTV
- 🔍 PORT CTV: resected involved mediastinal regions, bronchial stump, ipsilateral hilum, stations 4 and 7
- 🔍 Mandatory OARs for conventional or moderately hypofractionated RT
  - Lungs, separately and together, excluding GTV
  - Whole heart (cardiac substructures optional)
  - Oesophagus, cricoid to GE junction
  - Spinal canal as PRV surrogate for cord
- ⚠️ Motion management left to the department: ITV, mid-ventilation/mid-position, or gating/tracking
- ⚠️ The source excerpt reports no per-item vote margins or panel agreement

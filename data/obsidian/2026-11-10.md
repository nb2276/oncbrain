### NTCP, TCP and the TECs (QUANTEC, HyTEC, PENTEC)

> Narrative review of TCP/NTCP outcome modeling through the QUANTEC, HyTEC and PENTEC normal-tissue projects; no effect sizes reported in source.

- 🔍 Single-author first-person review in Medical Physics, not a systematic review or new dataset
- 🔍 Frames RT as a tradeoff: high TCP vs low NTCP
- ⚠️ Author's premise: dosimetric and clinical correlates of outcome rarely form a unified picture
- 🔗 AAPM/ASTRO TEC projects covered
  - QUANTEC: normal tissue effects, conventional fractionation
  - HyTEC: hypofractionated treatment effects
  - PENTEC: pediatric normal tissue effects
- 🔍 Draws on in-house institutional outcome data alongside the TEC work
- 📊 No effect size, dose-volume constraint or model parameter reported in source abstract
- ⚠️ Written by a TEC participant; not an independent appraisal of those constraints

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Modeling clinical outcomes in radiotherapy: NTCP, TCP and the “TECs”** — Yorke, Ellen. *Medical Physics* (2023-02). [doi:10.1002/mp.16274](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.16274)
  > Abstract Radiation therapy is a double‐edged sword which damages both tumor and normal cells. I am fortunate to have participated in some of these struggles, including analyzing and using in‐house data and being involved in the AAPM and ASTRO “TEC” projects—QUANTEC (Quantitatie Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic), HyTEC (Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic) and PENTEC (Pediatric Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic).

### Biological principles of SBRT/SRS: indirect cell death

> Above ~10 Gy/fraction, SBRT/SRS adds indirect tumor cell death via vascular injury and antitumor immunity on top of direct cell kill.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The parameter the review keys on is dose per fraction: above ~10 Gy/fraction, vascular injury and immune effects add tumor kill beyond direct cytotoxicity. That frames ablative few-fraction schedules as biologically distinct from moderate hypofractionation, though the authors flag the normal tissue mechanism at these doses as unresolved.

- 🔍 Narrative radiobiology review (IJROBP 2021); no systematic search methods stated in abstract
- 📊 Doses above ~10 Gy/fraction cause tumor vascular injury → secondary tumor cell death
- 📊 High-dose irradiation reported to increase antitumor immunity
- 💊 Approaches to further raise antitumor immunity under investigation; specific agents not named in source
- ⚠️ Normal tissue damage mechanism after high-dose RT still uncharacterized, per authors
- ⚠️ No effect size reported in source; mechanistic synthesis, no clinical outcome data

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Biological Principles of Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT) and Stereotactic Radiation Surgery (SRS): Indirect Cell Death** — Song, Chang W.; Glatstein, Eli; Marks, Lawrence B. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.02.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2019.02.047)
  > Purpose To review the radiobiological mechanisms of stereotactic body radiation therapy stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and stereotactic radiation surgery (SRS). Methods and materials We reviewed previous reports and recent observations on the effects of high-dose irradiation on tumor cell survival, tumor vasculature, and antitumor immunity. We then assessed the potential implications of these biological changes associated with SBRT and SRS. Results Irradiation with doses higher than approximately 10 Gy/fraction causes significant vascular injury in tumors, leading to secondary tumor cell death. Irradiation of tumors with high doses has also been reported to increase the antitumor immunity, and various approaches are being investigated to further elevate antitumor immunity. The mechanism of normal tissue damage by high-dose irradiation needs to be further investigated. Conclusions In addition to directly killing tumor cells, high-dose irradiation used in SBRT and SRS induces indirect tumor cell death via vascular damage and antitumor immunity. Further studies are warranted to better understand the biological mechanisms underlying the high efficacy of clinical SBRT and SRS and to further improve the efficacy of SBRT and SRS.

### PENTEC vs QUANTEC/HyTEC: late effects in children vs adults

> No effect size reported in source; organ-by-organ synthesis finds pediatric late-effect risk similar, greater, or less than adult, depending on tissue and age.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Adult QUANTEC/HyTEC constraints do not transfer uniformly to children: neurocognition, cerebrovascular and breast-lactation risks run greater in children, ovary less, lung perhaps slightly less. Within the pediatric range, younger children carry greater hearing and neurocognitive risk, so age at RT should modify how a constraint is read when planning brain or posterior fossa fields.

- 🔍 Review comparing pediatric PENTEC with adult QUANTEC and HyTEC: content, oversight, support, scope, literature-review method
- 🔍 PENTEC aims to refine dose-volume NTCP relationships in pediatric cancer survivors
- 📊 Late RT toxicity risk: children vs adults, and younger vs older children (direction only, per source)

  | Organ / endpoint | Children vs adults | Younger vs older children |
  |---|---|---|
  | Brain, necrosis | Similar | Not stated |
  | Optic apparatus | Similar | Not stated |
  | Parotid | Similar | Not stated |
  | Liver | Similar | Not stated |
  | Brain, neurocognition | Greater | Greater in younger |
  | Cerebrovascular | Greater | Not stated |
  | Breast, lactation | Greater | Not stated |
  | Lung | Perhaps slightly less | Not stated |
  | Ovary | Less | Lesser in younger |
  | Hearing | Not stated | Greater in younger |
  | Thyroid | Not stated | Lesser in younger |
  | Musculoskeletal, teeth, breast, reproductive maldevelopment | Relevant primarily only in children | Not stated |

- ⚠️ No dose-volume thresholds or NTCP estimates given in source abstract; directions only
- ⚠️ Child vs adult comparisons confounded by treatment paradigm: cancer types, RT dose and fields, chemo agents
- ⚠️ Unresolved whether age is best modeled as continuous or by groupings (infant through older adult)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Comparison of Risks of Late Effects From Radiation Therapy in Children Versus Adults: Insights From the QUANTEC, HyTEC, and PENTEC Efforts** — Milano, Michael T.; Marks, Lawrence B.; Olch, Arthur J. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2024-06). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2023.08.066](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2023.08.066)
  > Pediatric Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic (PENTEC) seeks to refine quantitative radiation dose-volume relationships for normal-tissue complication probabilities (NTCPs) in survivors of pediatric cancer. This article summarizes the evolution of PENTEC and compares it with similar adult-focused efforts (eg, Quantitative Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic [QUANTEC] and Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic [HyTEC]) with respect to content, oversight, support, scope, and methodology of literature review. It then summarizes key organ-specific findings from PENTEC in an attempt to compare NTCP estimates in children versus adults. In brief, select normal-tissue risks within developing organs and tissues (eg, maldevelopment of musculoskeletal tissue, teeth, breasts, and reproductive organs) are primarily relevant only in children. For some organs and tissues, children appear to have similar (eg, brain for necrosis, optic apparatus, parotid gland, liver), greater (eg, brain for neurocognition, cerebrovascular, breast for lactation), less (ovary), or perhaps slightly less (eg, lung) risks of toxicity versus adults. To add to the complexity, it is unclear if age is best analyzed as a continuous variable versus with age groupings (eg, infants, young children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, older adults). Further work is needed to better understand the complex manner in which age and developmental status affect risk.

### HyTEC carotid blowout and bleeding events in H&N SBRT reirradiation

> Pooled 5-fx Dmax model TD50 45.7 Gy for major vessel bleeding after H&N SBRT reRT; carotid Dmax <20 to 30 Gy appears low risk.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For 5-fx reRT, the usable planning targets are carotid Dmax <20 to 30 Gy (low observed risk), D0.5cc <20 Gy and every-other-day delivery; RTOG 3507's Dmax ≤42 Gy and D50% ≤32 Gy are conservative, nonvalidated guides. Circumferential encasement or pre-existing skin necrosis tilts the choice toward conventional or hyperfractionated reRT.

- 🔍 Pooled 238 major vessel Dmax values from 6 articles (PubMed, April 2018) into a logistic NTCP model
- 🔍 Two later series modeled separately: Mavroidis 2018 (Yazici 2013 recalculation) and Gebhardt 2018/Ling 2019
- 📊 Pooled Dmax model (5 fx): TD50 45.7 Gy (95% CI 39.6-64.9)
- 📊 Only 1 BE at carotid Dmax <30 Gy; median carotid SBRT Dmax 27 Gy across cases
- 📊 Gebhardt series (every-other-day 5 fx, risk-reduction applied), G3-5 CBS
  - 1 cm3 >20 Gy in 5 fx: est. risk 2.2%
  - 0.1 cm3 >20 Gy in 5 fx: est. risk <1%
  - CBS 3/67 after 1 SBRT course vs 1/8 after multiple courses, P=.4
  - Median SBRT dose 44 Gy (range 40-50) in 5 fx; 150 carotids in 75 pts
- 🔍 Factors likely lowering BE risk
  - Nonconsecutive-day delivery
  - Major vessel D0.5cc <20 Gy; minimize volume >20 to 30 Gy
  - Less circumferential tumor involvement around the vessel
  - No surgical manipulation before or after SBRT
- 📐 RTOG 3507 carotid constraints (nonvalidated, 5-fx SBRT reRT)

  | Metric | Limit | Acceptable deviation |
  |---|---|---|
  | Carotid Dmax | ≤42 Gy | >42 but <44 Gy |
  | Carotid D50% | ≤32 Gy | >32 but <34 Gy |

- ⚠️ BE endpoint mixes tumor bleeding with radiation injury in all but 1 data set
- ⚠️ Prior RT assumed a nominal 70 Gy; dose and interval not reported per pt
- ⚠️ CBS-only Gebhardt model rests on 5 events in 4 pts

  > Purpose Dose-volume data for injury to carotid artery and other major vessels in stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT)/SABR head and neck reirradiation were reviewed, modeled, and summarized. Methods and materials A PubMed search of the English-language literature (stereotactic and carotid and radiation) in April 2018 found 238 major vessel maximum point doses in 6 articles that were pooled for logistic modeling. Two subsequent studies with dose-volume major vessel data were modeled separately for comparison. Attempts were made to separate carotid blowout syndrome from other bleeding events (BE) in the analysis, but we acknowledge that all except 1 data set has some element of BE interspersed. Results Prior radiation therapy (RT) dose was not uniformly reported per patient in the studies included, but a course on the order of conventionally fractionated 70 Gy was considered for the purposes of the analysis (with an approximately ≥6-month estimated interval between prior and subsequent treatment in most cases). ### Trigeminal nerve contouring guidelines in H&N cancers

> Unresected macroscopic PNI: CTV-P1 = enhancing nerve + 5 mm; CTV-P2 extends ≥30 mm along nerve, skull base often recommended, brainstem if intracranial.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> MRI misdefined macroscopic PNI extent in 17% and 11% of operated series, and imaging skip lesions understate pathologically continuous spread. That is the rationale for running CTV-P2 ≥30 mm along the nerve and often to skull base rather than stopping at visible enhancement, and for MRI in treatment position.

- 🔍 Practical contouring guideline for PNI along V1/V2/V3, built on radio-anatomy and imaging review
- ⚠️ Consensus methodology (panel, Delphi, grading) not described in source
- 📊 Table 2 target volumes for PNI (CTV-P1 high risk, CTV-P2 low risk)

  | Scenario | CTV-P1 | CTV-P2 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Primary RT, unresected macroscopic PNI | GTV (entire post-contrast nerve enhancement) + 5 mm along nerve path | ≥30 mm along nerve path, skull base discussed (often recommended); to brainstem if skull base involved |
  | Post-op RT, resected macroscopic PNI, R1 | Close margin + 5 mm along nerve path, plus PNI in entire tumor bed | Per histology/location risk below |
  | High-risk histology (SCC with extensive PNI, ACC) or location (PPF, masticator space, nasopharynx, maxillary sinus) | Per primary or post-op rule above | Nerve path ≥30 mm by primary site, skull base often recommended; bilateral if non-lateralized |

- 📐 5 mm CTV-P1 margin can be increased when MRI extent is in doubt
- 📐 Non-lateralized tumors: bilateral nerve path coverage for at least 20-30 mm considered
- 🔍 Coverage is retrograde toward skull base by default; antegrade if symptoms, op report or imaging suggest
- 🔍 Indications for adjuvant RT by PNI extent (SCC)
  - Focal microscopic PNI, no other high-risk features: relative indication, case-by-case
  - Extensive PNI: more systematic indication
- 🔍 Nerve divisions, skull base exit, tumors at risk

  | Division | Exit / path | Tumors at risk |
  |---|---|---|
  | V1 | Superior orbital fissure → orbit roof | Upper-third face skin, frontal sinus, lacrimal gland |
  | V2 | Foramen rotundum → PPF | Middle-third face skin, maxillary sinus, nasopharynx, oropharynx, hard palate |
  | V3 | Foramen ovale → masticator space | Lower-third face skin, mandible, floor of mouth, tongue, submandibular gland, parotid |

- 🔍 PPF is the hub linking V2 branches, V1 (via orbit) and VII (Vidian nerve)
- ⚠️ Macroscopic V PNI warrants checking VII: chorda tympani-lingual and auriculotemporal-parotid connections
- 📊 MRI performance for macroscopic PNI
  - Sensitivity 95-100% for detection
  - Gandhi 2011: extent misdefined in 5 of 30 operated cases (17%)
  - Baulch 2015: missed 2 of 38 (5%); extent misdefined in 4 of 36 detected (11%)
  - Imaging skip lesions vs pathologically continuous spread → imaging underestimates extent
- 🔍 MRI in treatment position recommended whenever possible; rigid co-registration with planning CT
- ⚠️ FDG-PET-CT often inferior to MRI for macroscopic PNI (small volume, limited resolution)
- 💊 Particle RT may be considered for inoperable or macroscopically residual ACC, without randomized evidence
- ⚠️ Target volumes may overlap brainstem, brain, optic pathway; PTV undercoverage vs toxicity left case-by-case

### CTV-P delineation consensus, laryngeal/hypopharyngeal/oropharyngeal/oral cavity SCC

> GTV-P+5 mm → CTV-P1 (high dose), GTV-P+10 mm → CTV-P2 (lower dose), both anatomically edited; hypopharynx may take 15 mm cranio-caudal.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The high-dose volume shrinks to GTV-P+5 mm, and T1 glottic primaries get no CTV-P2 at all. The main exception is hypopharynx: submucosal spread up to 20-30 mm in surgical series supports a 15 mm cranio-caudal CTV-P2, so a uniform 10 mm expansion under-covers it.

- 🔍 Multi-society consensus (AIRO, DAHANCA, EORTC, GORTEC, NCIC CTG, NRG Oncology, TROG, others) for primary-tumour CTV in H&N SCC
- 🔍 Nomenclature: CTV-P1 = GTV-P + 5 mm (high dose); CTV-P2 = GTV-P + 10 mm (prophylactic or intermediate dose)
- 📐 Dose levels attached to each CTV-P (2 Gy-fraction equivalents)

  | Volume | 2 dose-level centres | 3 dose-level centres |
  |---|---|---|
  | CTV-P1 | 70 Gy over 7 wk | 70 Gy over 7 wk |
  | CTV-P2 | 50 Gy over 5 wk (prophylactic) | 60 Gy over 6 wk (intermediate) |
  | CTV-P3 | not delineated | 50 Gy over 5 wk (prophylactic) |

- 📊 CTV-P margins by subsite and T stage (from GTV-P, then edited)

  | Subsite / T | CTV-P1 | CTV-P2 | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Glottic T1 | 5 mm | not recommended | CTV-P1 includes paraglottic space, excludes thyroid cartilage |
  | Glottic T2 | 5 mm | 10 mm | CTV-P2 optional if small/superficial, not from anterior commissure |
  | Glottic T3-T4 | 5 mm | 10 mm | CTV-P2 includes part of thyroid and cricoid cartilage |
  | Supraglottic T1-T4 | 5 mm | 10 mm | Includes pre-epiglottic space; stops at strap muscles unless invaded |
  | Subglottic T1 | 5 mm | 10 mm (optional) | Margin e.g. 2 mm into cricoid cartilage |
  | Subglottic T2-T4 | 5 mm | 10 mm | Edited off posterior pharyngeal wall, cervical oesophagus (T3) |
  | Hypopharynx T1 | 5 mm | 10 mm (optional if small/superficial) | No retropharyngeal space in CTV-P1 |
  | Hypopharynx T2-T4 | 5 mm | 10 mm; consider 15 mm cranio-caudal | Submucosal spread |
  | Oropharynx T1 | 5 mm | 10 mm | Excludes air, bone, mobile tongue; tonsil CTV-P2 stops at parapharyngeal space |

- 🔍 Four rules for editing the geometric expansion
  - Air cavities: always edited out
  - Complex subsite anatomy: e.g. soft-palate GTV-P edited at mobile tongue
  - Barriers (mandible, maxilla, spine, hyoid, fascia): edited unless likely infiltrated
  - Surgical experience: small T1 glottic/tonsil may justify smaller margins
- 🔗 Margin rationale: transoral laser/TORS series with 2-3 mm margins report LC 85-95% (larynx) and >90% (T1-T2 tonsil) without PORT
- 🔗 Zanoni et al.: surgical margin of 2.2 mm optimal for loco-regional recurrence-free survival
- ⚠️ Hypopharynx exception: submucosal extension up to 20-30 mm cranio-caudal in surgical series
- ⚠️ Valid only for the primary treatment setting; not post-op or recurrent disease
- ⚠️ PTV margins left to each centre; a PTV should be built on both CTV-P1 and CTV-P2

### Neck node level delineation: 2013 update consensus

> Consensus CT boundaries for neck levels I-X, adding retropharyngeal, retro-styloid, parotid, bucco-facial and occipital groups; level selection and dose out of scope.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Three edits change the contour itself. Level V now begins at the hyoid, IVa ends 2 cm above the sternal manubrium with IVb/Vc covering the supraclavicular fossa below it, and VIIa is limited to the lateral retropharyngeal nodes. Level selection and dose are explicitly out of scope, so the guideline standardizes the contour, not the CTV decision.

- 🔍 Consensus endorsed by DAHANCA, EORTC, HKNPCSG, NCIC CTG, NCRI, RTOG and TROG
- 🔍 Panel: radiation oncologists, a H&N surgeon, an anatomist; European, Asian, Australian/NZ, North American members
- 📐 Cranial and caudal limits by level (axial CT, neutral position)

  | Level | Cranial | Caudal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ia (submental) | Mylo-hyoid m. | Platysma m. (caudal edge of anterior belly of digastric mm) |
  | Ib (submandibular) | Cranial edge of submandibular gland; anteriorly mylo-hyoid m. | Caudal edge of hyoid and mandible, or of submandibular gland if more caudal |
  | II (upper jugular) | Caudal edge of lateral process of C1 | Caudal edge of body of hyoid bone |
  | III (middle jugular) | Caudal edge of body of hyoid bone | Caudal edge of cricoid cartilage |
  | IVa (lower jugular) | Caudal edge of cricoid cartilage | 2 cm cranial to sternal manubrium |
  | IVb (medial supraclavicular) | Caudal border of IVa | Cranial edge of sternal manubrium |
  | V (posterior triangle) | Cranial edge of body of hyoid bone | Plane just below transverse cervical vessels |
  | Vc (lateral supraclavicular) | Plane just below transverse cervical vessels | 2 cm cranial to sternal manubrium |
  | VIa (anterior jugular) | Caudal edge of hyoid or submandibular gland, whichever more caudal | Cranial edge of sternal manubrium |
  | VIb (prelaryngeal, pretracheal, paratracheal) | Caudal edge of thyroid cartilage | Cranial edge of sternal manubrium |
  | VIIa (retropharyngeal) | Upper edge of body of C1/hard palate | Cranial edge of body of hyoid bone |
  | VIIb (retro-styloid) | Base of skull (jugular foramen) | Caudal edge of lateral process of C1 |
  | VIII (parotid) | Zygomatic arch, external auditory canal | Angle of the mandible |
  | IX (bucco-facial) | Caudal edge of the orbit | Caudal edge of the mandible |
  | Xa (retroauricular) | Cranial edge of external auditory canal | Tip of the mastoid |

  - VIIa covers lateral retropharyngeal nodes only; inconsistent medial group of 1-2 nodes excluded
  - VIb starts at the hyoid body for anterior FOM, tongue tip and lower lip primaries
  - Level II splits into IIa/IIb at the posterior edge of the IJV

### Target volume selection and delineation (T and N), oral cavity/oropharynx/hypopharynx/larynx SCC

> Consensus nodal levels keyed to N category, plus 5 and 10 mm GTV expansions edited to anatomy, for primary RT of oral cavity and pharyngo-laryngeal SCC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The margin case rests on a three-center Danish analysis: with GTV-to-CTV margins from 0 to 10 mm, recurrences sat mainly within the GTV and local relapse did not track margin. That supports 5 and 10 mm edited expansions over whole-compartment CTVs, but only with 2 mm contrast planning CT, endoscopy under GA and MRI defining the GTV.

- 🔍 Narrative review of the international consensus guidelines (Oral Oncology 2018); no new outcome data
- 📊 cN0 neck by imaging: up to 30% microscopic nodal infiltration reported, the basis for elective nodal RT
- 🔍 Elective nodal level selection by N category (from review text)

  | Nodal category | Oral cavity | Oropharynx / hypopharynx / larynx |
  |---|---|---|
  | N0 | Levels I to III | II to IVa; + VIIa for posterior pharyngeal wall; + VI for piriform apex, post-cricoid, esophageal, transglottic or subglottic extension |
  | N1 (single small node) | As N0 | As N0, except hypopharynx (high propensity for lymphatic infiltration) |
  | N2a, N2b, N3 | Ib (Ia for anterior oral cavity) to V | Ib to V; retropharyngeal nodes for oropharynx and hypopharynx |
  | N2c (bilateral) | Each side by its own N category | Each side by its own N category |

- 🔍 Add-on levels in the node-positive neck
  - IVb and Vc if IVa or Vb infiltrated
  - VIIb (retro-styloid) if upper level II infiltrated

- 🔍 Primary-tumor CTV: three schools compared

### HyTEC spinal metastases SBRT TCP

> Pooled model, 2619 spinal metastases: estimated 2y LC 82% at 18 Gy, 90% at 20 Gy, 96% at 24 Gy single fraction (α/β 6 Gy).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Coverage moves control more than prescription: in one 811-lesion series at 24 Gy/1, 2y control was 98% with PTV D95 >22.4 Gy vs 85% below 16.5 Gy, and epidural failure next to the cord predominates. A higher prescription gains little if the cord constraint carves out the epidural PTV. The authors point to separation surgery or higher cord tolerance as the way to raise TCP.

- 🔍 Maximum-likelihood logistic TCP fit of actuarial 2y LC against prescription dose
- 📐 Estimated α/β = 6 Gy from cross-fractionation isoeffect comparisons (computed range 3-12 Gy), used to pool data in 3-fraction equivalent dose

  | 4 | 32 | 36 (extrapolated) | 40 (extrapolated) |
  | 5 | 33 | 40 (extrapolated) | 45 (extrapolated) |

- 📄 **Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy for Spinal Metastases: Tumor Control Probability Analyses and Recommended Reporting Standards** — Soltys, Scott G.; Grimm, Jimm; Milano, Michael T. et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05).   > Purpose We sought to investigate the tumor control probability (TCP) of spinal metastases treated with stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) in 1 to 5 fractions. Methods and materials PubMed-indexed articles from 1995 to 2018 were eligible for data extraction if they contained SBRT dosimetric details correlated with actuarial 2-year local tumor control rates. ### HyTEC pancreatic SBRT tumor control probability

> Pooled TCP model: 1-yr LC ≈79% to 86% at 30 to 36 Gy/3fx equivalent, <70% below 24 Gy; 33 Gy/5fx maps to 77%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> A021501's 33 Gy/5fx converts to 28.2 Gy/3fx and sits on the dose-response slope (77% 1-yr LC without surgery), below the 30 to 36 Gy band modeled at ≈79% to 86%.   |---|---|---|

  | Below 24 Gy | Without surgery | <70% |

- 📊 Dose response seen across 25 to 36 Gy (3-fx equivalent)

  > Purpose Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and stereotactic ablative body radiation therapy is being increasingly used for pancreatic cancer (PCa), particularly in patients with locally advanced and borderline resectable disease. ### ESTRO ACROP target volume guidelines, locally advanced NSCLC

> Consensus M/R/O/D rules for LA-NSCLC volumes: FDG-PET-CT mandatory, all pre-induction involved nodes in GTV, CTV GTV + 5-8 mm, elective nodal CTV discouraged.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology

  |---|---|---|
  | Staging imaging | Diagnostic contrast CT + whole-body FDG-PET-CT | M |

  | Sequential chemo then RT | PET-CT before induction; repeat contrast chest CT before RT | M |
  | Planning CT | Treatment position, 2-3 mm slices, cricoid to mid abdomen | M |
  | Motion | Respiratory-correlated 4D-CT | R |
  | Planning PET-CT | Preferably in planning position | R |

  | Further elective nodal CTV | Not recommended | D |
  | PTV | Individualised anisotropic margins (primary vs nodes) | R |
  | PTV manual editing | Should not be performed | D |
  | Adaptive re-planning | Individual basis on baseline shift / anatomic change | O |

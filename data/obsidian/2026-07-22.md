# 2026-07-22

**ESTRO Delphi locks prostate SBRT at 36.25 Gy/5 fx and votes 100% against elective pelvic nodal RT outside trial.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day from two directions. ESTRO's Delphi gives radiation oncologists an operational floor for SBRT (dose, SV coverage, motion management, no elective nodal RT, no spacer), while a ProtecT secondary analysis finds cribriform-negative pts (87% of reviewed slides) got no significant 15-yr metastasis reduction from early RP or EBRT + ADT, which is a selection question upstream of technique.

## 🌰 Prostate

### ProtecT (cribriform morphology secondary analysis)

> Secondary analysis: cribriform-negative disease (87% of reviewed cohort) got no significant 15-yr metastasis reduction from early radical treatment vs monitoring.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For the RT reader this is a selection question, not a technique one: it asks whether cribriform status identifies the ProtecT subgroup that actually earned the metastasis reduction from radical treatment. RT here was EBRT with 3-6mo neoadjuvant ADT, a dated backbone. No subgroup HRs, CIs or event counts appear in the source excerpt.

- 🔍 Secondary analysis of ProtecT (N=1,643 randomized to active monitoring, RP, or EBRT + 3-6mo neoadjuvant ADT)
- 🔍 Central pathology review of 712 pts with retrievable biopsy slides; regrading applied 2019 ISUP criteria over the trial's original 2005 grading
- 📊 Cribriform-positive on biopsy in roughly 13% of the reviewed cohort; 87% cribriform-negative
- 📊 1° outcome: metastases at 15-yr median follow-up
- 📊 In cribriform-negative pts, early radical treatment gave no statistically significant reduction in 15-yr metastases vs active monitoring, in BOTH ITT and per-protocol analyses
- 📊 On multivariable Cox (ITT), cribriform-negative grade group 2 carried the same long-term metastasis risk as grade group 1 / Gleason 6
- ⚠️ Cribriform status was not a randomization stratification factor; the presenter notes a chance imbalance toward higher cribriform prevalence in the RT + ADT arm
- ⚠️ Slides were retrievable for 712 of 1,643 pts (centralization still ongoing); randomization characteristics (age, PSA, revised Gleason) reported as not significantly different across arms in this subset
- ⚠️ No effect sizes (HRs, CIs, p-values, event counts) reported in the source excerpt, which is a video-interview transcript that cuts off mid-analysis
- 🔗 Context is ProtecT's 15-yr paradox: PCa-specific mortality under 3% and equal across all three arms, while early radical treatment did reduce metastases; this analysis asks who carried that metastasis risk
- 🔗 EAU already advises against active surveillance with any cribriform disease at diagnostic biopsy; ISUP and GUPS both recommend binary cribriform reporting on all needle and surgical specimens. Those positions were not built on prospective surveillance trials

### ESTRO Prostate SBRT Consensus Recommendations

> ESTRO Delphi: 36.25 Gy/5 fx standard, 100% vote against elective pelvic nodal RT with prostate SBRT outside trial.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Two operational lines move practice: elective pelvic nodal RT alongside prostate SBRT is rejected 100% (12 votes) outside a trial, and intra-fraction tracking is only carried by 71% (10 votes) once PTV margin drops below 5 mm, no consensus. Prior BPH surgery is permitted with a median 6-month wait (range 2-12).

- 📊 Controversy questions and where the panel landed

  | Question | Vote | Level |
  |---|---|---|
  | Elective pelvic nodal RT with prostate SBRT (Q6) | No 100% (12 votes) | Strong consensus against |
  | Bladder/bowel prep protocol (Q8) | Yes 100% (14 votes) | Strong consensus |
  | ADT per existing guidelines, fractionation-independent (Q7) | Yes 100% (13 votes) | Strong consensus |
  | Rectal spacer (Q9) | No 85% (11 votes) | Consensus against |
  | Max IPSS cut-off (Q3) | Yes 85% (11 votes) | Consensus; median 17, range 10-20 |
  | Max prostate volume cut-off (Q2) | Yes 62% (8 votes) | No consensus; median 90 cc, range 70-150 |
  | Prophylactic meds (α1-blockers etc, Q4) | No 46% (6 votes) | No consensus |
  | SBRT after BPH surgery (Q5) | Selected pts with waiting period 100% (13 votes) | Strong consensus; median wait 6 mo, range 2-12 |

- 📐 Motion management by PTV margin (Q10a/Q10b)
  - Margin ≥5 mm: CBCT soft-tissue or fiducial matching 79% (11 votes); + intra-fraction tracking 21% (3 votes)
  - Margin <5 mm: pre-treatment matching + intra-fraction tracking 71% (10 votes); matching alone 21% (3 votes)
  - Consensus for CBCT/fiducial matching at ≥5 mm; no consensus on tracking below 5 mm
- 🔍 Dose: 36.25 Gy/5 fx (7.25 Gy) to 95% PTV with 40 Gy to 95% of CTV, per PACE-B
- 🔍 Alternative: 42.7 Gy in 7 fx of 6.1 Gy, the HYPO-RT-PC schedule
- 🔍 SV handling: omit in low risk; proximal 1 cm included in PACE for all, proximal 2 cm to 30 Gy/5 fx in Gleason 4+3 or NCCN high risk
- 🔍 Panel = 11 radiation oncologists, 3 medical physicists, 1 RTT from 9 European countries; 2 Delphi rounds
- 🔍 Consensus ≥75% agreement, strong consensus ≥90%, thresholds taken from APCCC
- 💊 ADT unchanged by fractionation: no evidence SBRT modifies the established ADT benefit
- 🔗 NRG-GU005 (ASTRO 2025 preliminary): 36.25 Gy/5 fx gave lower side effects but possibly higher 3y biochemical relapse
- 🔗 SHARP consortium showed inferior bRFS in high-risk pts when ADT was withheld; Jackson et al meta-regression found no ADT association
- 🔗 PARTIQoL phase III: no difference in outcomes or QoL between proton and IMRT, pencil beam scanning in only 48% of cases
- ⚠️ High-risk evidence is thin: HYPO-RT-PC is the only phase III with oncologic outcomes there, and high-risk pts were 11% of participants
- ⚠️ PACE-C has published toxicity but not yet oncological outcomes
- ⚠️ Post hoc PACE-B analysis found no significant association between urinary substructure dose and late urinary toxicity, so the urethra-sparing rationale is not settled
- ⚠️ Table 2 flags the PACE-B urethra PRV V42 <50% constraint as possibly too permissive
- ⚠️ Small panel (13-14 voters per question), European only, so a 100% vote reflects narrow agreement not breadth
- 📊 Late urinary symptom flare 6 to 18 months post-treatment in about 10% to 20% of pts, self-limiting

# 2026-10-18

**Luo T1-2N1 nomogram: PMRT cut 5y LRR 2.2% vs 14.9% (HR 0.164) only in high-risk pts (score >18); no LRR association in low/intermediate risk.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> An all-breast, all-retrospective day on PMRT selection and salvage of post-mastectomy recurrence. Luo and Katz nomograms aim to target nodal and chest wall RT in T1-2N1 / SLN+ disease. They are internally or externally validated, not prospective. Halverson favors entire-chest-wall salvage fields (5-yr CW control 75% vs 36%), but field size is confounded with treatment era.

## 🎀 Breast

All five are single-institution retrospective series, several from the pre-taxane, pre-HER2 era. Read the absolute rates as hypothesis-generating for modern risk-adapted PMRT.

### Close or positive margin after mastectomy and chest wall irradiation (Freedman)

> 8-yr chest wall failure 28% if aged ≤50 vs 0% if >50 (p=0.04) after close/positive margin mastectomy without PMRT, T1-T2, 0-3 nodes.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The margin itself did not separate risk (24% ≤2 mm vs 7% 2.1-6 mm, p=0.36); age did, with 0 of 12 failures above 50. Chemo did not prevent it: 4 of 5 failures followed chemo ± tamoxifen. That puts age, not margin width, at the center of the chest wall RT decision in otherwise low-risk pts.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review, Fox Chase, 789 mastectomy pts, 1984-1993
- 🔍 136 (17%) had tumor within 1 cm of the deep margin; 34 met inclusion criteria
- 🔍 Inclusion and baseline
  - Clinical tumor ≤5 cm, 0-3 positive nodes, microscopic margin ≤1 cm, no PMRT
  - Median age 43 (range 29-76)
  - Deep margin positive in 2 pts, ≤2 mm in 17, 2.1-4 mm in 7, 4.1-6 mm in 8
  - 65% node-negative, 35% with 1-3 positive nodes; MRM in all
- 📊 5 chest wall failures; 5- and 8-yr cumulative incidence 9% and 18%
- 📊 8-yr cumulative incidence of chest wall failure by factor

  | Factor | Higher-risk group | Comparison group | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Age ≤50 vs >50 | 28% | 0% | 0.04 |
  | Margin ≤2 mm vs 2.1-6 mm | 24% | 7% | 0.36 |
  | Clinical T2 vs T1 | 24% | 7% | 0.98 |

- 📊 Crude chest wall failure by age

  | Age | Failures | Crude rate |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤40 | 3 of 11 | 27% |
  | 41-50 | 2 of 11 | 18% |
  | 51+ | 0 of 12 | 0 |

- 📊 Only 1 isolated chest wall first failure; 3 were associated with distant mets
- 💊 4 of 5 chest wall failures occurred after adjuvant chemo ± tamoxifen (CMF, CAF, AC era)
- ⚠️ Age confounded with chemo: 91% of ≤50 got chemo vs 25% of >50
- ⚠️ 19 of 34 margins never centrally reviewed
- ⚠️ Age denominators inconsistent within paper: 21/13 in Methods vs 22/12 in Table 1
- ⚠️ Margin p=0.36 rests on 5 events; underpowered, not evidence that margin is irrelevant

**Sources:**
- 📄 **A CLOSE OR POSITIVE MARGIN AFTER MASTECTOMY IS NOT AN INDICATION FOR CHEST WALL IRRADIATION EXCEPT IN WOMEN AGED FIFTY OR YOUNGER** — Gary M. Freedman; Barbara L. Fowble; Alexandra L. Hanlon et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics* (1998)
  - 📎 [[papers/breast/1998-freedman-close-positive-margin-mastectomy-not-indication-chest.pdf|Close or positive margin after mastectomy and chest wall irradiation (Freedman) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does young age predict chest wall failure independent of tumor biology?
> - Does the margin effect emerge in larger modern cohorts?
> - Is omission safe in close-margin pts >50 with modern systemic therapy?

### Isolated local-regional recurrence after mastectomy: radiotherapeutic management (Halverson)

> 5-yr freedom from chest wall re-recurrence 75% vs 36% with large vs small field RT (p=0.0001) for isolated post-mastectomy LRR, no prior PMRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The pattern-of-failure detail matters most: two-thirds of small-field failures landed outside the field but inside the chest wall, so the whole chest wall is the at-risk volume. Dose is graded by burden: ≥6000 cGy took <3 cm recurrences to 100% control, while bulky disease plateaued near 50% even at 7000 cGy. Elective axillary RT added arm edema (11% vs 1%) with rare axillary re-recurrence.

- 🔍 Retrospective single-institution series, Mallinckrodt 1964-1986, N=224
- 🔍 First isolated LRR after mastectomy; prior PMRT, distant mets, and outside-salvage failures excluded
- 🔍 Median f/u 46 mo (range 24-241); actuarial life-table method, timed from recurrence
- 📊 Whole cohort: 5-/10-yr OS 43%/26%, DFS 25%/15%; 5-yr LRC 57%
- 📊 Outcomes by site of initial recurrence (5-yr)

  | Recurrence site | OS | RFS | LRC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Chest wall alone | 50% | 35% | 63% |
  | Nodal alone | 36% | 15% | 45% |
  | Chest wall + nodal | 19% | 5% | 27% |
  | p | 0.0002 | 0.0001 | 0.0084 |

- 📊 Solitary chest wall recurrence: freedom from chest wall re-recurrence by field size

  | Timepoint | Large field (entire CW) | Small field | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 5-yr | 75% | 36% | 0.0001 |
  | 10-yr | 63% | 18% | 0.0001 |

- ⚠️ Small fields used in only 14 pts, mostly the early era; field size confounded with treatment period
- 📊 Small-field failures: 33% in-field or at margin, 67% outside field but within chest wall
- 📊 Elective RT to uninvolved sites

  | Endpoint | Elective RT | No elective RT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | SCV re-recurrence | 6% | 16% | 0.0489 |
  | 5-yr OS, elective SCV RT | 53% | 35% | 0.03 |
  | CW re-recurrence, elective CW RT | 17% | 27% | .32 |
  | CW re-recurrence, ≥5000 cGy CW | 12% | 27% | .20 |

- 📐 Dose: <3 cm recurrences 100% controlled at ≥6000 cGy vs 76% at lower doses
- 📐 Excised recurrences controlled across 4500-7000 cGy; larger lesions about 50% even at 7000 cGy
- ⚠️ The SCV OS benefit is an unadjusted retrospective comparison; selection and era effects can explain it
- ⚠️ Late sequelae: G2 9%, G3 1%, no fatal toxicity
  - Arm edema 8/75 (11%) with elective axillary RT vs 1/109 (1%) without
  - Pneumonitis 2% tangents alone vs 8% (3/37) tangents + parasternal photons
  - Bone necrosis in 4 pts, all at doses >5400 cGy
- 🔗 vs Toonkel and Deutsch series cited in source: conflicting survival direction with elective nodal RT, neither reported failure patterns

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Isolated local-regional recurrence of breast cancer following mastectomy: radiotherapeutic management** — Karen J. Halverson; Carlos A. Perez; Delia M. Garcia et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics* (1990-10)
  - 📎 [[papers/breast/1990-halverson-isolated-local-regional-recurrence-breast-cancer.pdf|Isolated local-regional recurrence after mastectomy: radiotherapeutic management (Halverson) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Contribution of modern systemic therapy to RT for isolated LRR
> - Does elective SCV RT improve survival after adjustment for era?
> - Optimal strategy for bulky or diffuse chest wall recurrence

### Nomogram for ≥4 involved nodes in SLN-positive breast cancer (Katz)

> Seven-factor nomogram predicts ≥4 involved nodes after 1-3 positive SLNs; AUC 0.83 derivation, 0.81 external validation (N=206).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT read is the 5% cutoff: 34% and 37% of SLN+ pts fell below it, the group the authors hypothesize could get high tangents instead of a SCV/apex field. The field-related lymphedema numbers the source quotes (2% to 8% rising to roughly 9% to 24%) put a toxicity cost on that nodal coverage decision. Regional control after fields are reduced was never measured.

- 🔍 Retrospective, 2 academic centers (MGH n=248, BWH n=154), N=402 with 1-3 positive SLNs + completion ALND
- 🔍 Clinically node-negative, no neoadjuvant systemic therapy; ≥6 nodes examined required for inclusion
- 🔍 External validation: 206 SLN+ pts, Sibley Memorial community hospital
- 📐 Nomogram score components (Fig 2)
  - Primary tumor size (cm) x 2.6
  - Number of positive SLNs x 9.2
  - +11 each: LVSI, lobular histology, extranodal extension
  - +16 if largest SLN met is macrometastasis (>2 mm)
  - -8.6 if ≥1 SLN negative; constant -58
- 📊 Model performance, derivation vs validation

  | Metric | MGH/BWH | Sibley (validation) |
  |---|---|---|
  | N analyzed | 344 | 206 |
  | AUC | 0.83 | 0.81 |
  | Pearson / Hosmer-Lemeshow p | .9 / .4 | .6 / .5 |
  | Could be spared SCV/apex RT at <5% cutoff | 34% | 37% |

- 📊 At 5% cutoff: 113 of 118 low-risk pts had ≤3 nodes, NPV 94.7%
- ⚠️ Model somewhat underestimated observed ≥4-node rate in validation set
- ⚠️ Event count inconsistent: abstract states 87 with ≥4 nodes, Results text states 81
- ⚠️ Sensitivity reported 97.5%, but stated counts (54 of 57 classified positive) don't reconcile
- ⚠️ Derived only in dissected pts; the target population (no ALND) was excluded, incl. 73 trial pts given nodal RT
- 🔗 Builds on MD Anderson model (LVSI, >1 positive SLN, no drainage) and Chagpar model (1,133 SLN+ pts), adds LVSI + met size

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Nomogram for the Prediction of Having Four or More Involved Nodes for Sentinel Lymph Node–Positive Breast Cancer** — Katz, Angela; Smith, Barbara L.; Golshan, Mehra et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2008-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.2007.11.9479](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2007.11.9479)
  > Purpose The standard of care for patients with a positive (+) sentinel lymph node (SLN) is axillary dissection; however, for various reasons, some SLN+ patients do not undergo dissection. The purpose of this study was to define possible predictors of having four or more involved nodes to provide information for clinicians and patients making decisions about adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation. Patients and Methods We reviewed the records of 402 patients with invasive breast cancer and one to three involved SLNs who underwent completion axillary dissection at two academic cancer centers. None of these patients received neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Factors associated with having four or more involved axillary nodes (SLNs and non-SLNs) were evaluated by univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis. Results Eighty-seven patients had four or more positive nodes. On multivariate analysis, having four or more SLNs was associated with tumor histology, primary tumor size, lymphovascular space invasion, extranodal extension, the number of involved SLNs, the number of uninvolved SLNs, and the size of the largest SLN metastasis. A nomogram to predict the probability of having four or more nodes based on patients’ pathologic data was developed from the multivariate logistic regression model. A separate previously published data set of 206 SLN+ patients treated at a community hospital in another city was used to validate this model. Conclusion Patients with a low probability of having four or more nodes can be identified from known pathologic features. The nomogram developed will be helpful to clinicians making adjuvant treatment recommendations.
  - 📎 [[papers/breast/2008-katz-nomogram-prediction-having-four-more-involved-nodes.pdf|Nomogram for ≥4 involved nodes in SLN-positive breast cancer (Katz) (full text)]]

### Nomogram for locoregional recurrence to guide PMRT in T1-2N1 breast cancer (Luo)

> High-risk nomogram group (score >18): PMRT 5y LRR 2.2% vs 14.9%, HR 0.164; no LRR association in low- or intermediate-risk T1-2N1.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The low-risk unirradiated group (score ≤10) ran 5y LRR 0.4%, the strongest omission signal here, while intermediate-risk pts sat at 6.1% with a non-significant PMRT association on few events. Fields covered chest wall plus supraclavicular/infraclavicular nodes at 46-50 Gy, so any benefit is for comprehensive PMRT, not chest wall alone.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective cohort, N=1141 T1-2N1 post-mastectomy, 2001-2012; 518 (45.4%) got PMRT
- 🔍 Nomogram built in 623 unirradiated pts via Fine-Gray competing-risks regression
- 🔍 Nomogram inputs (independent LRR risk factors on MVA)
  - Tumor size T2 vs T1
  - 3 vs 1-2 positive ALN
  - ER status
  - Histologic grade III vs I-II
  - LVI
- 📐 Bootstrap-validated C index 0.73 (95% CI 0.68-0.78); internal validation only
- 🔍 Decision-tree cutoffs at score 10 and 18 → low (n=412), intermediate (n=385), high (n=344) risk
- 📊 Unirradiated 5y LRR: 0.4% low-risk, 6.1% intermediate-risk, 14.9% high-risk
- 📊 High-risk group: PMRT vs no PMRT, 5y cumulative incidence

  | Endpoint | PMRT | No PMRT | HR (95% CI) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | LRR | 2.2% | 14.9% | 0.164 (0.067-0.403) | <.001 |
  | DR | 8.4% | 17.6% | 0.491 (0.288-0.837) | .009 |
  | BCM | 6.3% | 13.3% | 0.371 (0.191-0.719) | .003 |

- 📊 PMRT association with LRR by risk group

  | Risk group | LRR P value |
  |---|---|
  | Low | .930 |
  | Intermediate | .240 |
  | High | <.001 |

- 📊 Whole cohort: 5y LRR 1.6% vs 6.0% with PMRT, HR 0.248 (0.121-0.509); BCM P=.130
- 💊 RT: 46-50 Gy/23-25 fx, CT-planned, chest wall + infraclavicular + supraclavicular
- 💊 IMN only for central tumors; axilla only with ECE
- ⚠️ Cutoffs derived and tested in same cohort; no external validation
- ⚠️ Low/intermediate null results rest on few LRR events; underpowered, not proof of no benefit
- ⚠️ Trastuzumab in only 26.2% of HER2+ pts; systemic era predates current standards
- ⚠️ Overall DR reported as 16.6 vs 17.8% beside HR 0.608; absolute figures read inconsistent with HR in source

### Chest wall recurrence outcomes by initial stage and PMRT implications (Chagpar)

> After CWR without prior PMRT, 5-yr OS 79.9% (T1-T2N0) vs 41.9% (T1-T2, 1-3 LN+) vs 29.1% (T3-T4/>4 LN+); 1-3 LN+ salvage no better than advanced.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT signal sits in two places: salvage after CWR in T1-T2 with 1-3 positive nodes was as poor as in T3-T4/>4 LN+ (Group 2 vs 3 OR 1.544, p=0.330), and omitting XRT for the recurrence itself carried OR 4.524 for death. Prevention of CWR, not reliance on salvage, is the argument for PMRT in 1-3 LN+ disease.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review (MDACC), CWR evaluated 1988-1998, N=96
- 🔍 All pts had mastectomy without PMRT; no distant mets at time of CWR
- 🔍 Groups by initial stage
  - Group 1: T1-T2N0, n=41
  - Group 2: T1-T2 with 1-3 positive LN, n=20
  - Group 3: T3-T4 or >4 positive LN, n=35
- 📊 5-yr outcomes after CWR by initial stage group

  | Endpoint | Group 1 | Group 2 | Group 3 | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5-yr OS | 79.9% | 41.9% | 29.1% | 0.0001 |
  | 5-yr DMFS | 75.2% | 33.6% | 25.9% | 0.0007 |

- 📐 Cox MVA for OS, pairwise group comparisons

  | Comparison | OR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|
  | Group 1 vs 2 | 4.871 (1.756-13.510) | 0.002 |
  | Group 1 vs 3 | 7.521 (2.761-20.482) | <0.001 |
  | Group 2 vs 3 | 1.544 (0.645-3.699) | 0.330 |

- 📊 No XRT for CWR independently predicted worse OS: OR 4.524 (95% CI 2.064-9.918), p<0.001
- 📊 No XRT for CWR also predicted worse DMFS: OR 4.024 (95% CI 1.825-8.874), p=0.001
- 📊 Interval to CWR ≤24 mo predicted worse OS: OR 4.636 (95% CI 2.106-10.206), p<0.001
- ⚠️ Group 2 n=20; the Group 2 vs 3 null comparison is underpowered, not evidence of equivalence
- ⚠️ XRT-for-CWR benefit confounded: some pts were not irradiated because of progression on systemic therapy
- ⚠️ Internal inconsistencies: DMFS pairwise ORs in text repeat the OS ORs; Group 2 vs 3 DMFS p given as 0.250 and 0.492
- ⚠️ Group 3 pts did not get PMRT despite it being standard (refusal, physician bias, often unknown): selected cohort
- 🔗 Pre-taxane, pre-HER2-targeted era; salvage rates may not transfer to modern systemic therapy

# 2026-10-23

**Gomez LCT, oligometastatic NSCLC: mOS 41.2 vs 17.0 mo (P = .017) with RT/surgery to all sites after first-line therapy; randomized phase II stopped early at N=49.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Oligomets and gyn carried the day. In NSCLC with ≤3 mets and no progression on first-line therapy, LCT more than doubled mOS (41.2 vs 17.0 mo), though the trial stopped early for benefit. In the PORTEC-3 final analysis, adding chemo to pelvic RT improved 5y FFS (HR 0.71) but not OS (HR 0.76, ns), with G3+ AEs at 60% vs 12%.

## 🌷 Gynecologic

Two brachy planning studies (eBT OAR sparing vs replanned Ir-192, cylinder tip-space dosimetry) sit beside PORTEC-3; neither reports late clinical toxicity.

### PORTEC-3 · [[NCT00411138]]

> Adding chemo to pelvic RT: 5y FFS 75.5% vs 68.6% (HR 0.71, p=0.022); 5y OS 81.8% vs 76.7%, HR 0.76, ns.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Pelvic RT alone already held pelvic control at 91%, so the chemo gain is a distant-failure question, not a local one. The FFS gain concentrates in stage III (69.3% vs 58.0%, HR 0.66), while stage I-II FFS HR 0.85 was ns, supporting RT alone below stage III.

- 🔍 Open-label randomised phase 3, 103 centres, GCIG intergroup
- 🔍 686 enrolled, 660 eligible pts in final ITT analysis (330 per arm)
- 🔍 Median f/u 60.2 mo (IQR 48.1-73.1)
- 🔍 Eligibility, high-risk EC (FIGO 2009)
  - Stage IA endometrioid grade 3 with LVSI
  - Stage IB endometrioid grade 3
  - Endometrioid stage II or III
  - Serous or clear cell, stage IA (with invasion) to III
- 💊 RT both arms: pelvic EBRT 48.6 Gy in 1.8 Gy fractions
- 💊 CRT arm: cisplatin 50 mg/m² x2 during RT, then carboplatin AUC5 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m² x4
- 🔍 Vaginal brachy boost in 47% (46% CRT vs 48% RT), mostly for cervical invasion
- 📊 5y outcomes, CRT vs RT alone (prose-reported values)

  | Endpoint | CRT | RT alone | HR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | OS, all pts | 81.8% | 76.7% | 0.76 (0.54-1.06) | 0.109 |
  | FFS, all pts | 75.5% | 68.6% | 0.71 (0.53-0.95) | 0.022 |
  | OS, stage III | 78.7% | 69.8% | 0.71 (0.45-1.11) | 0.13 |
  | FFS, stage III | 69.3% | 58.0% | 0.66 (0.45-0.97) | 0.031 |
  | FFS, stage I-II | 80.8% | 76.6% | 0.85 (0.54-1.33) | 0.47 |

- 📊 Pelvic control 91% with RT alone
- ⚠️ G3+ AEs during treatment 60% CRT vs 12% RT (p<0.0001)
- ⚠️ G2+ neuropathy persisting at 3y: 8% vs 1% (p<0.0001)
- ⚠️ OS underpowered: 136 OS events, 69% of required; time-based final analysis
- ⚠️ Stage III FFS gain is a subgroup read, hypothesis-generating
- 🔗 Consistent with pooled NSGO-EC-9501/EORTC-55991 + MaNGO Iliade (PFS 78% vs 69%, p=0.01)

### Axxent electronic brachytherapy, postoperative endometrial cancer

> eBT cut bladder V35% 15.2% vs 28.2% and rectal V35% 16.7% vs 32% vs replanned Ir-192, with 1 acute G2 event in 94 pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The OAR savings are TG-43 physical dose with no RBE correction, while PTV V150/V200 rose with eBT, most with the smallest cylinder. Against a cited 50 kV RBE of 1.29 to 1.85 vs Ir-192 at 5 mm, the biologic mucosal dose is unknown, which matters most for 2.5 cm cylinders and the 25 Gy/5 fx exclusive schedule.

- 🔍 Single-centre series, N=94, Sept 2015 to Oct 2017, Axxent 50 kVp eBT via vaginal cylinder
- 🔍 RT regimens
  - eBT exclusive: 37 pts, 25 Gy in 5 sessions (stage IA plus 7 IB1)
  - EBRT + eBT: 57 pts, whole pelvis 23 × 2 Gy, then eBT 15 Gy in 3 sessions
  - Prescribed at 5 mm from applicator; PTV = active length + 5 mm
  - Cylinders 2.5 to 3.5 cm diameter; eBT twice weekly
- 🔍 Each eBT plan replanned a posteriori for Ir-192 and Co-60: 282 plans, TG-43, no heterogeneity correction
- 📐 Mean OAR dose, eBT vs replanned Ir-192 HDR

  | OAR metric | Axxent eBT | Ir-192 HDR |
  |---|---|---|
  | Bladder D2cc (% prescribed) | 63.8% | 70.1% |
  | Bladder V50% | 7.2% | 12.7% |
  | Bladder V35% | 15.2% | 28.2% |
  | Rectum D2cc (% prescribed) | 61.2% | 68.4% |
  | Rectum V50% | 7.9% | 14.3% |
  | Rectum V35% | 16.7% | 32% |

- 📊 Co-60 plan OAR doses similar to Ir-192
- 📊 PTV V150/V200 higher with eBT; gap narrows as cylinder size increases
- 📊 Acute toxicity: only 1 event above G1 (G2, RTOG) across 94 pts
- 📊 Toxicity at 1 month (RTOG), all 94 pts

  | Organ | G0 | G1 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Vaginal | 94.7% | 5.3% |
  | Rectal | 98.9% | 1.1% |
  | Urinary | 97.9% | 2.1% |

- 📊 No recurrence in 31 pts past 12 mo (median f/u 19 mo, range 12-25)
- ⚠️ No RBE correction applied; cited 50 kV RBE vs Ir-192 is 1.29 to 1.85 at 5 mm
- ⚠️ Comparator plans recalculated, never delivered; no clinical HDR control cohort
- ⚠️ Late vaginal toxicity (stenosis) not reported; f/u 14 mo (4-25)
- ⚠️ Internal inconsistencies: 30 vs 31 pts with 12-mo f/u; average energy 26 vs 29.6 keV

- 📄 **Postoperative endometrial cancer treatments with electronic brachytherapy source** — Lozares-Cordero, Sergio; Font-Gómez, Jose Antonio; Gandía-Martínez, Almudena et al.. *Journal of Radiotherapy in Practice* (2018-09). [doi:10.1017/s1460396918000353](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1460396918000353)
  > Abstract Purpose This study is a dosimetric and acute toxicity comparison of endometrial cancer patients treated with either Axxent (Xoft, Inc., San José, CA, USA) electronic and interstitial brachytherapy versus interstitial high dose rate brachytherapy (HDRBT). Materials and Methods Between 2015 and 2017, 94 patients with postoperative endometrial cancer were treated in our centre with the Axxent electronic brachytherapy (eBT) system. The V 150 and V 200 are evaluated prospectively for each plan. The mean age of patients was 65.9 years (age range 33–84 years), with different tumour staging. Of the 94 patients, 37 received exclusive adjuvant brachytherapy (25 Gy in five sessions); the remaining patients received external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) with a regimen of 23 sessions of 2 Gy each to the entire pelvis, followed by eBT (15 Gy in three sessions). Additionally, the absorbed doses received by the organs at risk (OAR), urinary bladder, rectum and sigmoid colon were compared with HDRBT plans, evaluating D 2cc , V 50% and V 35% . Median follow-up was done for each of the 94 patients to assess the toxicity of the treatment: vaginal mucosa toxicity, rectal and urinary toxicity; and results are presented for acute toxicity, toxicity at 1 month after the end of treatment and follow-up after 12 months for a portion of patients according to the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) toxicity criteria. Results The doses in OAR for eBT plans were lower than that for HDRBT plans, both Ir-192 and Co-60 plans, whose doses were similar. The dose in bladder with eBT was 63.8% of the prescribed dose for D 2cc versus 70.1% for HDRBT Ir-192, for V 50% was 7.2% versus 12.7% and for V 35% was 15.2% versus 28.2%. In rectum the D 2cc was 61.2% versus 68.4%, for V 50% was 7.9% versus 14.3% and for V 35% was 16.7% versus 32%. Results demonstrated lower doses to OAR in all eBT plans. Acute toxicity in eBT was very low in cases of mucositis, with only one case of toxicity greater than grade 1, rectal toxicity and urinary toxicity; results at 1 month are equally good, toxicity symptoms disappeared and no relapses have occurred to date. Conclusions The results of treatment with the Axxent eBT unit for 94 patients are very good, as no recurrence has been observed and the toxicity of the treatment is very low. The increase in V 150 and V 200 has not produced an increase in vaginal mucosa toxicity, and the doses in the OAR are lower than in the plans implemented for HDRBT with Ir-192 or Co-60. eBT is a good alternative to treat endometrial cancer in centres without conventional HDR availability. To date, there are limited published studies reporting on outcomes from patients treated with eBT.

### HDR vaginal brachytherapy cylinder dosimetry (depth, size, length, tip space)

> Planning study: dropping optimization points at the cylinder dome gave hot (>150%) and cold (<60%) spots, and shorter tip space raised dosimetric uncertainty >30%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dome is where plans fail: omitting optimization points at the curved end swung given dose about 10% in either direction depending on tip space, with apex spots of 50-65% or 140-170%. The M formula (M ≈ 1.00 + 0.64/L + 1.23/S) gives a geometry-specific surface dose matching a 0.5-cm depth Rx, informing the surface-vs-depth prescription choice.

- 🔍 Dosimetric planning study (Plato BPS 14.2.4), no patient outcomes; single institution (Henry Ford)
- 🔍 Cylinders 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5 and 4.0 cm diameter; activated lengths 3 to 8 cm
- 🔍 Compared surface (P0) vs 0.5-cm depth (P5) prescription, each with and without dome optimization points (NEP)
- 🔍 Tip space: radiographic thin-metal-tube cylinder ~0.5 cm; CT/MR plastic-tube cylinder 0.6 to 0.8 cm on radiograph
- 📊 Surface vs 0.5-cm depth prescription, surface SD: 6% vs 18% (2-cm cylinder, 0.8-cm tip space)
- 📊 No-end-point (NEP) plans, 2-cm cylinder, by tip space

  | Tip space | Given dose (MD) | Apex |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.8 cm (P0NEP and P5NEP) | Decreased by about 10% | Cold spots 50-65% of Rx |
  | 0.5 cm (P0NEP) | Increased by about 10% | Hot spots 140-170% of Rx at surface |
  | 0.5 cm (P5NEP) | Desired MD at depth | Surface SD 25% |

- 📐 Surface conversion factor: M ≈ 1.00 + 0.64 (cm)/L + 1.23 (cm)/S, regression agreement within 2%
- 📐 Worked example: 7 Gy at 0.5 cm, 5-cm length, 3-cm cylinder → M = 1.54 → 10.8 Gy/fx at surface
- ⚠️ M regression built from only two treatment lengths × two cylinder sizes
- ⚠️ Toxicity benefit of surface Rx inferred from dose, not measured; no clinical endpoints
- 🔗 Authors frame surface-dose reduction against Sorbe randomized dose-fractionation trial (mucosal atrophy, bleeding, shortening)

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

### Gomez LCT vs maintenance/observation, oligometastatic NSCLC (long-term OS)

> mOS 41.2 vs 17.0 mo (P = .017) and mPFS 14.2 vs 4.4 mo (P = .022) favoring LCT in oligometastatic NSCLC without progression after first-line therapy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Survival after progression ran 37.6 vs 9.4 mo, and only 15 of 39 progressors (41%) received LCT at progression. Holding RT for progression therefore loses a large share of pts who can no longer get consolidation, often from polymetastatic progression (n=7 in MT/O). This argues for consolidating at the post-induction window rather than deferring.

- 🔍 Randomized phase II, 3 institutions, 1:1, not masked. 49 randomized of 74 enrolled
- 🔍 DSMB closed the trial early for PFS benefit. It was powered for 94 randomized pts
- 🔍 Stage IV NSCLC, ≤3 mets, no progression ≥3 mo after front-line systemic therapy
- 💊 Front-line therapy: ≥4 cycles platinum doublet, or ≥3 mo EGFR TKI / crizotinib for EGFR/ALK
- 🔍 LCT: RT or surgery to all active sites, chosen by MDT. Concurrent chemoRT allowed for primary/nodes
- 📊 Efficacy, LCT vs MT/O (median f/u 38.8 mo)

  | Endpoint | LCT (n=25) | MT/O (n=24) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | mPFS (1° EP) | 14.2 mo (7.4-23.1) | 4.4 mo (2.2-8.3) | .022 |
  | mOS | 41.2 mo (18.9-NR) | 17.0 mo (10.1-39.8) | .017 |
  | Survival after progression | 37.6 mo | 9.4 mo | .034 |
  | Median time to new lesions | 14.2 mo (5.7-24.3) | 6.0 mo (4.4-8.3) | 0.11 |
  | Deaths | 11 of 25 | 18 of 24 | n/a |

- 📐 Multivariable OS HR for LCT 0.46 (95% CI 0.21-0.99), P = .048, adjusted for met number + EGFR/ALK
- 📊 No additional G≥3 toxicity beyond what was previously reported
- 📊 OS benefit held despite crossover: 9 of 20 MT/O progressors received LCT to all lesions
- ⚠️ Significance threshold P<.10 (one-sided 10% type I error), so the bar is looser than a phase III
- ⚠️ Stopped early for benefit at N=49, so the effect size is likely inflated
- ⚠️ Opened 2012: no immunotherapy. Maintenance regimens were heterogeneous
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation and the RT vs surgery split are not reported in the source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Local Consolidative Therapy Vs. Maintenance Therapy or Observation for Patients With Oligometastatic Non–Small-Cell Lung Cancer: Long-Term Results of a Multi-Institutional, Phase II, Randomized Study** — Gomez, Daniel R.; Tang, Chad; Zhang, Jianjun et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2019-06). [doi:10.1200/jco.19.00201](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.19.00201)
  > PURPOSE Our previously published findings reported that local consolidative therapy (LCT) with radiotherapy or surgery improved progression-free survival (PFS) and delayed new disease in patients with oligometastatic non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that did not progress after front-line systemic therapy. Herein, we present the longer-term overall survival (OS) results accompanied by additional secondary end points. PATIENTS AND METHODS This multicenter, randomized, phase II trial enrolled patients with stage IV NSCLC, three or fewer metastases, and no progression at 3 or more months after front-line systemic therapy. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to maintenance therapy or observation (MT/O) or to LCT to all active disease sites. The primary end point was PFS; secondary end points were OS, toxicity, and the appearance of new lesions. All analyses were two sided, and P values less than .10 were deemed significant. RESULTS The Data Safety and Monitoring Board recommended early trial closure after 49 patients were randomly assigned because of a significant PFS benefit in the LCT arm. With an updated median follow-up time of 38.8 months (range, 28.3 to 61.4 months), the PFS benefit was durable (median, 14.2 months [95% CI, 7.4 to 23.1 months] with LCT v 4.4 months [95% CI, 2.2 to 8.3 months] with MT/O; P = .022). We also found an OS benefit in the LCT arm (median, 41.2 months [95% CI, 18.9 months to not reached] with LCT v 17.0 months [95% CI, 10.1 to 39.8 months] with MT/O; P = .017). No additional grade 3 or greater toxicities were observed. Survival after progression was longer in the LCT group (37.6 months with LCT v 9.4 months with MT/O; P = .034). Of the 20 patients who experienced progression in the MT/O arm, nine received LCT to all lesions after progression, and the median OS was 17 months (95% CI, 7.8 months to not reached). CONCLUSION In patients with oligometastatic NSCLC that did not progress after front-line systemic therapy, LCT prolonged PFS and OS relative to MT/O.

## 👄 Head & Neck

In resected HPV+ OPSCC (NCDB), margins and nodal size/laterality lose prognostic weight, and those features are the classic triggers for adjuvant RT/CRT.

### Composite pathologic risk stratification, resected HPV+ oropharyngeal cancer

> Composite point score raised C-index from 0.58 to 0.67 in resected HPV+ OPSCC; low-risk share rose from 24.5% to 75.3% (NCDB, n=2302).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The score demotes the classic CRT triggers: margin status lost prognostic weight in HPV+ disease on multivariable analysis, and microscopic ENE earns one point, so a T1-2 tumour with microscopic ENE can still sit low-risk. That points at adjuvant RT/CRT de-escalation, but those HRs were measured after the adjuvant therapy the features prompted.

- 🔍 Retrospective NCDB cohort, dx 2010-2013, primary surgery plus neck dissection with LN yield ≥15
- 🔍 2302 HPV+ OPSCC; 13 022 HPV-unassociated HNSCC as the contrast cohort
- 📊 Traditional (NCCN/ASCO/ECOG 3311/PATHOS-derived) risk C-index 0.58 in HPV+ OPSCC vs 0.68 in HPV-unassociated HNSCC
- 🔍 Composite score: adverse points per pathologic feature (low 0-3, intermediate 4-5, high ≥6 points)

  | Adverse points | Features |
  |---|---|
  | 0 | T1-2 · tonsil or BOT · 0-1 metastatic LNs · any LN size · level IV or V LNs · bilateral LN · differentiation |
  | 1 | LVI · microscopically positive margins · microscopic ENE |
  | 2 | T3 · 2-4 metastatic LNs · grossly positive margins · macroscopic ENE |
  | 3 | T4 · ≥5 metastatic LNs |

- 📊 Composite C-index 0.67 with 3 risk groups, 0.73 using individual points
- 📊 Reclassification in HPV+ OPSCC, traditional → composite
  - Low risk: 24.5% (564/2302) → 75.3% (1733/2302)
  - High risk: 45.1% (1038/2302) → 6.6% (151/2302)
- 📊 HPV+ multivariable: only T stage, LVI, number of metastatic LNs and ENE tracked with mortality
- 📊 Not prognostic in HPV+: surgical margins, LN size, level IV-V nodes, bilateral/contralateral nodes
- ⚠️ Cox models adjusted for adjuvant RT/CRT, which margins and ENE themselves triggered; treated risk can look benign
- ⚠️ OS is the only endpoint; NCDB carries no locoregional recurrence, PNI, close margins or smoking
- ⚠️ Score derived and scored in the same cohort; no external validation reported in source
- ⚠️ High-risk traditional share reported as 46.1% (n=1061) in Results but 45.1% (n=1038) in Discussion

# 2026-10-23

**Gomez LCT randomized phase II: mOS 41.2 vs 17.0 mo (P=.017), mPFS 14.2 vs 4.4 mo with RT/surgery to all sites in ≤3-met NSCLC after front-line therapy.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> LCT to all sites in oligometastatic NSCLC more than doubled mOS (41.2 vs 17.0 mo), though accrual stopped at 49 of 94 planned. In high-risk endometrial, PORTEC-3 CRT raised 5y FFS (HR 0.71) but not OS over pelvic RT alone, which held pelvic control at 91%. Brachy dosimetry and an HPV+ OPSCC risk model round it out.

## 🌷 Gynecologic

Adjuvant endometrial: PORTEC-3 defines what chemo adds to pelvic RT, plus two dosimetry papers on vaginal cuff brachy.

### Postoperative endometrial cancer with electronic brachytherapy (Axxent)

> Axxent eBT vs replanned Ir-192 HDR: bladder D2cc 63.8% vs 70.1%, rectum 61.2% vs 68.4% of prescription; one G2 acute toxicity in 94 pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The prescription copied Ir-192 doses onto a 26 keV source with no RBE adjustment, so the OAR %PD reductions are physical-dose figures and the higher V150/V200 sits at the mucosa. Low acute mucositis at 25 Gy/5 fx and 15 Gy/3 fx is reassuring, but vaginal stenosis and late effects are untested here.

- 🔍 Single-centre series, N=94 postop endometrial pts treated with Axxent 50 kVp eBT, Sept 2015 to Oct 2017
- 🔍 Same 94 CTs replanned a posteriori for Ir-192 and Co-60 (282 plans); dosimetric comparison, not a clinical comparator arm
- 💊 RT regimens
  - eBT alone: 25 Gy in 5 fx, n=37 (stage IA plus seven IB1)
  - Pelvic EBRT 23 × 2 Gy then eBT 15 Gy in 3 fx, n=57
  - Prescribed at 5 mm from cylinder; PTV = active length + 5 mm
  - Cylinders 2.5 to 3.5 cm diameter; 33 pts (35%) had prior chemo
- 📊 Mean OAR dose, eBT vs Ir-192 HDR plans

  | OAR parameter | Axxent eBT | Ir-192 HDR |
  |---|---|---|
  | Bladder D2cc (%PD) | 63.8% | 70.1% |
  | Bladder V50% | 7.2% | 12.7% |
  | Bladder V35% | 15.2% | 28.2% |
  | Rectum D2cc (%PD) | 61.2% | 68.4% |
  | Rectum V50% | 7.9% | 14.3% |
  | Rectum V35% | 16.7% | 32% |

- 📊 Co-60 plans similar to Ir-192 per authors
- 📊 V150/V200 higher with eBT; gap narrows as cylinder diameter increases
- 📊 Toxicity (RTOG) at 1 month, all 94 pts
  - Vaginal: G0 94.7%, G1 5.3%, no G2
  - Rectal: G0 98.9%, G1 1.1%
  - Urinary: G0 97.9%, G1 2.1%
  - Acute phase: only one G2 event across all pts
- 📊 31 pts with ≥12 mo follow-up (median 19 mo): no recurrence
- ⚠️ Prescription not RBE-corrected: mean energy 26 vs 355 keV for Ir-192; cited RBE 1.29 to 1.85 at 5 mm
- ⚠️ OAR reductions are physical dose under TG-43, no heterogeneity correction
- ⚠️ Mean f/u 14 mo (4 to 25); late vaginal, GI, GU toxicity and cuff recurrence not assessable

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Postoperative endometrial cancer treatments with electronic brachytherapy source** — Lozares-Cordero, Sergio; Font-Gómez, Jose Antonio; Gandía-Martínez, Almudena et al.. *Journal of Radiotherapy in Practice* (2018-09). [doi:10.1017/s1460396918000353](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1460396918000353)
  > Abstract Purpose This study is a dosimetric and acute toxicity comparison of endometrial cancer patients treated with either Axxent (Xoft, Inc., San José, CA, USA) electronic and interstitial brachytherapy versus interstitial high dose rate brachytherapy (HDRBT). Materials and Methods Between 2015 and 2017, 94 patients with postoperative endometrial cancer were treated in our centre with the Axxent electronic brachytherapy (eBT) system. The V 150 and V 200 are evaluated prospectively for each plan. The mean age of patients was 65.9 years (age range 33–84 years), with different tumour staging. Of the 94 patients, 37 received exclusive adjuvant brachytherapy (25 Gy in five sessions); the remaining patients received external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) with a regimen of 23 sessions of 2 Gy each to the entire pelvis, followed by eBT (15 Gy in three sessions). Additionally, the absorbed doses received by the organs at risk (OAR), urinary bladder, rectum and sigmoid colon were compared with HDRBT plans, evaluating D 2cc , V 50% and V 35% . Median follow-up was done for each of the 94 patients to assess the toxicity of the treatment: vaginal mucosa toxicity, rectal and urinary toxicity; and results are presented for acute toxicity, toxicity at 1 month after the end of treatment and follow-up after 12 months for a portion of patients according to the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) toxicity criteria. Results The doses in OAR for eBT plans were lower than that for HDRBT plans, both Ir-192 and Co-60 plans, whose doses were similar. The dose in bladder with eBT was 63.8% of the prescribed dose for D 2cc versus 70.1% for HDRBT Ir-192, for V 50% was 7.2% versus 12.7% and for V 35% was 15.2% versus 28.2%. In rectum the D 2cc was 61.2% versus 68.4%, for V 50% was 7.9% versus 14.3% and for V 35% was 16.7% versus 32%. Results demonstrated lower doses to OAR in all eBT plans. Acute toxicity in eBT was very low in cases of mucositis, with only one case of toxicity greater than grade 1, rectal toxicity and urinary toxicity; results at 1 month are equally good, toxicity symptoms disappeared and no relapses have occurred to date. Conclusions The results of treatment with the Axxent eBT unit for 94 patients are very good, as no recurrence has been observed and the toxicity of the treatment is very low. The increase in V 150 and V 200 has not produced an increase in vaginal mucosa toxicity, and the doses in the OAR are lower than in the plans implemented for HDRBT with Ir-192 or Co-60. eBT is a good alternative to treat endometrial cancer in centres without conventional HDR availability. To date, there are limited published studies reporting on outcomes from patients treated with eBT.

### PORTEC-3 · [[NCT00411138]]

> 5y FFS 75.5% vs 68.6% (HR 0.71, p=0.022) with CRT over pelvic RT alone; co-primary 5y OS 81.8% vs 76.7% not met (HR 0.76, p=0.11).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Pelvic control was 91% with RT alone, so chemotherapy is not buying locoregional control here; 48.6 Gy pelvic EBRT (plus brachy boost in 47%, balanced by arm) stays the backbone in both arms. The RT question left open is whether pelvic RT can be dropped once chemo is given, which GOG-258 tests and its abstract suggests costs vaginal and nodal control.

- 🔍 Open-label randomised phase 3, 103 centres, GCIG; N=660 analysed (330 vs 330), median f/u 60.2 mo
- 🔍 High-risk endometrial cancer, FIGO 2009 stage I-III: EEC grade 3 with deep MI and/or LVSI, EEC stage II-III, or serous/clear cell
- 💊 RT alone: 48.6 Gy in 1.8 Gy fractions, 5 days/wk
- 💊 CRT: cisplatin 50 mg/m² x2 during RT, then 4 cycles carboplatin AUC5 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m²
- 🔍 Vaginal brachy boost in 47% (46% CRT vs 48% RT), mainly for cervical involvement; balanced by arm
- 📊 Co-primary endpoints, stage subgroups, toxicity (CRT vs RT alone)

  | Endpoint | CRT | RT alone | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 5y OS | 81.8% (77.5–86.2) | 76.7% (72.1–81.6) | HR 0.76 (0.54–1.06), p=0.11 |
  | 5y FFS | 75.5% (70.3–79.9) | 68.6% (63.1–73.4) | HR 0.71 (0.53–0.95), p=0.022 |
  | Stage III 5y OS | 78.7% | 69.8% | HR 0.71 (0.45–1.11), adjusted p=0.074 |
  | Stage III 5y FFS | 69.3% | 58.0% | HR 0.66 (0.45–0.97), adjusted p=0.014 |
  | Stage I–II 5y FFS | 80.8% | 76.6% | HR 0.85 (0.54–1.33), p=0.47 |
  | G3+ AEs during treatment | 198 (60%) | 41 (12%) | p<0.0001 |
  | G2+ neuropathy at 3y | 20 (8%) | 1 (1%) | p<0.0001 |

- 📊 Pelvic control 91% with RT alone; chemo not needed for locoregional control
- 📊 Stage III: 11% absolute FFS gain, exceeding the 10% design target
- ⚠️ OS underpowered: 136 events, 69% of required; time-based final analysis
- ⚠️ Chemo completion: all 4 cycles carboplatin 79%, paclitaxel 71%; discontinued in 18%
- 🔗 vs NSGO-EC-9501/EORTC-55991 + ManGO Iliade pooled (n=534): PFS 78% vs 69%, OS trend 82% vs 75%
- 🔗 vs GOG-258 (abstract): chemo alone vs CRT, no OS/RFS difference but more vaginal and pelvic/para-aortic recurrence without RT

### HDR vaginal brachytherapy cylinder dosimetry

> Surface SD 6% vs 18% with surface vs 0.5-cm depth prescription (2-cm cylinder). Omitting dome optimization points gave hot spots >150% and cold spots <60%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The actionable physics: the dome needs optimization points. Without them the apex runs cold (50-65%) or hot (140-170%) depending on tip space, and the 0.5-cm depth prescription hides surface SD up to 18%. If you switch to a surface prescription, M = 1.00 + 0.64/L + 1.23/S gives the surface dose that keeps the 0.5-cm dose unchanged.

- 🔍 Treatment-planning physics study (Henry Ford, Plato BPS 14.2.4); no patients, no clinical endpoints
- 🔍 Single-channel CT/MR and radiographic cylinders, 2.0-4.0 cm diameter, active length 3-8 cm
- 🔍 Compared surface (P0) vs 0.5-cm depth (P5) prescription, each with and without curved-end optimization points (NEP)
- 📊 Surface SD 6% (P0) vs 18% (P5), 2-cm cylinder, 0.8-cm tip space
- 📊 Without dome points: extreme hot (>150%) and cold (<60%) spots
- 📊 NEP failure modes by tip space (2-cm cylinder)
  - 0.8-cm tip: MD at depth down ~10%, apex cold spots 50-65%
  - 0.5-cm tip, surface Rx: MD up ~10%, apex surface hot spots 140-170%
  - 0.5-cm tip, P5NEP: target MD on depth, but surface SD 25%
- 📊 Shorter tip space raises dosimetric uncertainty >30%
- 📐 Surface-dose magnification M ≈ 1.00 + 0.64/L + 1.23/S (cm); matched plans within 2%
- 📐 Worked example: 7 Gy at 0.5 cm, 3-cm cylinder, 5-cm length → 10.8 Gy to surface (M = 1.54)
- 🔗 Context: ABS survey cited, >90% of users prescribe to 0.5 cm; >one tenth omit dome points
- ⚠️ The conclusion that surface Rx is optimal is a dosimetric argument; no toxicity or vaginal-control data were collected
- ⚠️ Single vendor TPS and applicators, 2007-era point-based optimization; transfer to modern 3D planning not tested
- ⚠️ The regression for M comes from 2 lengths × 2 diameters; extrapolation beyond that grid is unvalidated

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Effects of prescription depth, cylinder size, treatment length, tip space, and curved end on doses in high-dose-rate vaginal brachytherapy** — Li, Shidong; Aref, Ibrahim; Walker, Eleanor et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2007-03). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2006.10.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2006.10.041)
  - 📎 [[papers/gyn/2007-li-effects-prescription-depth-cylinder-size-treatment.pdf|HDR vaginal brachytherapy cylinder dosimetry (full text)]]

### Composite pathologic risk stratification, resected HPV+ oropharyngeal cancer

> Traditional pathologic risk tiers discriminate poorly in resected HPV+ OPSCC (C-index 0.58); a points-based composite reaches 0.67 and moves high-risk share from 45.1% to 6.6%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The composite down-weights margins and ENE against T4 and ≥5 nodes, so most traditional high-risk pts become low risk (high-risk share 45.1% to 6.6%). That is the adjuvant CRT escalation trigger. NCDB has OS but no locoregional failure, so it cannot say whether reclassified microscopic-ENE or margin-positive pts keep in-field control with less RT or no chemo.

- 🔍 NCDB retrospective cohort, dx 2010-2013, upfront surgery + neck dissection with LN yield ≥15
- 🔍 HPV+ OPSCC n=2302; HPV-unassociated HNSCC n=13 022 as comparison cohort
- 📊 Traditional pathologic risk tiers, 5-y OS by cohort

  | Traditional tier | HPV+ OPSCC | HPV-unassociated HNSCC |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low | 93.2% | 76.2% |
  | Intermediate | 88.9% | 54.5% |
  | High | 83.7% | 40.9% |
  | C-index | 0.58 | 0.68 |

- 📊 HPV+ OPSCC reclassification, traditional vs composite

  | Risk group | Traditional | Composite |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low risk | 24.5% (564/2302) | 75.3% (1733/2302) |
  | High risk | 45.1% (1038/2302) | 6.6% (151/2302) |

- 📐 Composite C-index 0.67 with 3 tiers, 0.73 using individual risk points
- 🔍 Composite point assignment (HPV+ OPSCC)
  - 1 point: LVI, microscopically positive margins, microscopic ENE
  - 2 points: T3, 2-4 metastatic LNs, grossly positive margins, macroscopic ENE
  - 3 points: T4, ≥5 metastatic LNs
  - Tiers: low 0-3, intermediate 4-5, high ≥6 points
- 📊 HPV+ multivariate: only T stage, LVI, number of LN mets and ENE associated with mortality
- 📊 HPV+: LN size, level IV-V LNs, bilateral LNs and margins not associated with mortality
- ⚠️ OS is the only endpoint; NCDB carries no locoregional recurrence or patterns of failure
- ⚠️ PNI, close margins and smoking not captured in NCDB, so absent from the model
- ⚠️ Model derived and scored in the same cohort; no external validation described in source
- ⚠️ Margins still earn points despite no multivariate mortality association in HPV+
- ⚠️ Source gives traditional high-risk share two ways: 46.1% (1061/2302) and 45.1% (1038/2302)
- 🔗 ICON-S staging built on definitive CRT; only 2% surgical, so no pathologic features tested
- ⚠️ Prognostic, not predictive: OS models adjusted for adjuvant RT/CRT, so no test of RT omission

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

### Gomez LCT oligometastatic NSCLC (long-term results)

> mOS 41.2 vs 17.0 mo (P=.017) and mPFS 14.2 vs 4.4 mo favoring LCT over maintenance/observation in oligometastatic NSCLC after front-line therapy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The OS gap tracks survival after progression, 37.6 vs 9.4 mo: only 15 of 39 progressors (41%) got LCT at progression, and in the MT/O arm 7 were blocked by polymetastatic progression. That argues for consolidating every site while disease is still oligometastatic, not holding RT for progression. Dose and fractionation are not in source text.

- 🔍 Randomized phase II, 3 institutions, 1:1 LCT vs maintenance therapy or observation (MT/O), unmasked
- 🔍 Stage IV NSCLC, ≤3 metastases, no progression at ≥3 mo after front-line systemic therapy
- 🔍 LCT = RT, surgery or both to all active sites; concurrent chemoRT allowed to primary and nodes
- 📊 Outcomes by arm (ITT, median f/u 38.8 mo)

  | Endpoint | LCT (n=25) | MT/O (n=24) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Median PFS (1° EP) | 14.2 mo (7.4-23.1) | 4.4 mo (2.2-8.3) | .022 |
  | Median OS | 41.2 mo (18.9-NR) | 17.0 mo (10.1-39.8) | .017 |
  | Survival after progression | 37.6 mo | 9.4 mo | .034 |
  | Time to new lesions | 14.2 mo (5.7-24.3) | 6.0 mo (4.4-8.3) | 0.11 |
  | Deaths | 11 of 25 | 18 of 24 | n/a |

- 📐 Multivariable Cox (arm, met number, EGFR/ALK): LCT OS HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.21-0.99), P=.048
- 📊 Crossover: 9 of 20 MT/O progressors got LCT to all lesions; their mOS 17 mo (7.8-NR)
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation not reported in source text (Appendix Table A1)
- ⚠️ DSMB closed accrual at 49 of 94 planned; early stops for benefit tend to overstate effect
- ⚠️ Significance threshold P<.10; OS was a secondary endpoint
- ⚠️ Pre-IO era (opened 2012); front-line and maintenance regimens heterogeneous

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Local Consolidative Therapy Vs. Maintenance Therapy or Observation for Patients With Oligometastatic Non–Small-Cell Lung Cancer: Long-Term Results of a Multi-Institutional, Phase II, Randomized Study** — Gomez, Daniel R.; Tang, Chad; Zhang, Jianjun et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2019-06). [doi:10.1200/jco.19.00201](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.19.00201)
  > PURPOSE Our previously published findings reported that local consolidative therapy (LCT) with radiotherapy or surgery improved progression-free survival (PFS) and delayed new disease in patients with oligometastatic non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that did not progress after front-line systemic therapy. Herein, we present the longer-term overall survival (OS) results accompanied by additional secondary end points. PATIENTS AND METHODS This multicenter, randomized, phase II trial enrolled patients with stage IV NSCLC, three or fewer metastases, and no progression at 3 or more months after front-line systemic therapy. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to maintenance therapy or observation (MT/O) or to LCT to all active disease sites. The primary end point was PFS; secondary end points were OS, toxicity, and the appearance of new lesions. All analyses were two sided, and P values less than .10 were deemed significant. RESULTS The Data Safety and Monitoring Board recommended early trial closure after 49 patients were randomly assigned because of a significant PFS benefit in the LCT arm. With an updated median follow-up time of 38.8 months (range, 28.3 to 61.4 months), the PFS benefit was durable (median, 14.2 months [95% CI, 7.4 to 23.1 months] with LCT v 4.4 months [95% CI, 2.2 to 8.3 months] with MT/O; P = .022). We also found an OS benefit in the LCT arm (median, 41.2 months [95% CI, 18.9 months to not reached] with LCT v 17.0 months [95% CI, 10.1 to 39.8 months] with MT/O; P = .017). No additional grade 3 or greater toxicities were observed. Survival after progression was longer in the LCT group (37.6 months with LCT v 9.4 months with MT/O; P = .034). Of the 20 patients who experienced progression in the MT/O arm, nine received LCT to all lesions after progression, and the median OS was 17 months (95% CI, 7.8 months to not reached). CONCLUSION In patients with oligometastatic NSCLC that did not progress after front-line systemic therapy, LCT prolonged PFS and OS relative to MT/O.

# 2026-11-18

**HyTEC brain SRS: 1fx V12 (incl. target) 5/10/>15 cc → ~10%/15%/20% symptomatic RN; 3fx V18 <30 cc and V23 <7 cc → <10%.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> HyTEC carries the day: AAPM pooled SRS/SBRT data add tumor control to QUANTEC-style toxicity limits, with V12-anchored brain RN risk and <1% RION at 10 Gy/1 fx, 20 Gy/3 fx, 25 Gy/5 fx in pts without prior RT. In prostate, Decipher and PAM50 are proposed for choosing ADT intensity, now tested prospectively in NRG GU009 and GU006.

## 📊 Cross-cutting

### HyTEC overview: high dose per fraction hypofractionated treatment effects

> 7 NTCP and 9 TCP papers across 6 anatomic sites: AAPM pools SRS/SBRT dose-volume-outcome data, adding tumor control to QUANTEC-style toxicity estimates.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable RT detail sits in the footnotes: spine control may track GTV D95 >18.3 Gy/1 fx or Dmin >14–15 Gy better than nominal Rx, and prostate constraints come from heterogeneous plans (mean Rx isodose 85%). Constraints mostly assume an unirradiated field, so reirradiation and pediatric cases need the organ-specific papers.

- 🔍 AAPM WGSBRT literature-pooling initiative; reports published in the Red Journal by AAPM/ASTRO agreement
- 🔍 6 anatomic sites: cranial, head and neck, thoracic, abdominal, pelvic, spinal
- 🔗 HyTEC vs prior dose-volume guidance

  | Characteristic | Emami et al | QUANTEC | HyTEC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Scope | 26 normal tissues/organs | 16 normal organs | 7 NTCP organ papers; 9 TCP disease-site papers |
  | 3D data available | Minimal | More/moderate (studies span ≈ 18 years) | Moderate but rapidly increasing |
  | Format | Uniform risk levels (TD 5/5, 50/5); uniform 1/3, 2/3, 3/3 organ irradiation | Nonuniform risk across organs; range of dose-volume metrics | Nonuniform risk across organs; range of dose-volume metrics |

- 📐 Clinical data favored over model-based estimates when both were available
- 📐 Planning details carried in the summary-table footnotes
  - Prostate SBRT: reviewed Rx 35–40 Gy in 4–5 fx; many series treated every other day
  - Prostate: heterogeneous planning common; mean Rx isodose line 85%, most 70%–90%
  - Spine: Dmin >14–15 Gy single-fraction equivalent or GTV D95 >18.3 Gy/1 fx may track control better than Rx
  - Brain: GTV→PTV expansion 0–3 mm, median 1 mm in reviewed studies
  - HCC: sparing ≥800 cm3 to ≤18 Gy in 3 fx suggested (Son et al)
- ⚠️ NTCP entries largely assume no prior RT; reirradiation scenarios flagged separately
- ⚠️ Prior whole-brain RT appears to raise brainstem risk with subsequent SRS/fSRS
- ⚠️ Actuarial LC censored at death may overstate control in metastatic pts
- ⚠️ Short survival after SBRT may understate long-term NTCP
- ⚠️ Adult data almost exclusively; pediatric dose-volume effects deferred to PENTEC

**Sources:**
- 📄 **High Dose per Fraction, Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic (HyTEC): An Overview** — Grimm, Jimm; Marks, Lawrence B.; Jackson, Andrew et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.10.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.10.039)
  - 📎 [[papers/multi-site/2021-grimm-high-dose-per-fraction-hypofractionated-treatment.pdf|HyTEC overview: high dose per fraction hypofractionated treatment effects (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Do pooled SBRT constraints hold for reirradiation?

## 🌷 Gynecologic

Teaching talk, not a readout; the case of IMRT over 3D for pelvic EBRT rests on TIME-C patient-reported bowel outcomes.

### Early stage endometrial cancer (Mayo Clinic Arizona talk)

> Stage- and grade-driven adjuvant algorithm for early endometrial cancer: observation, vaginal cuff brachy, or pelvic EBRT, with IMRT favored over 3D per TIME-C.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The framework's stage IB grade 3 cell, the case shown, sits outside PORTEC-2, which excluded grade 3 with >1/2 invasion, so the talk shows no randomized brachy-vs-EBRT comparison there. When pelvic fields are used, TIME-C's patient-reported bowel data (diarrhea at 5 weeks 33.7% vs 51.9%) support IMRT over 3D.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Justin Anderson, MD
> The speaker walks a stage IB grade 3 endometrioid case through workup, surgical staging and adjuvant selection. The framework assigns adjuvant therapy by FIGO 2009 stage, grade and histology: observation for low-grade stage IA without LVSI, vaginal cuff brachytherapy for intermediate cells, pelvic EBRT for stage IB grade 3, and EBRT plus brachy boost for stage II. High-intermediate risk is defined through the competing GOG99, PORTEC-1, PORTEC-2 and GOG249 criteria. For pelvic fields the talk argues IMRT over 3D, resting on RTOG 1203 (TIME-C) patient-reported bowel outcomes.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - The algorithm runs on FIGO 2009 stage, grade and Type I/II histology; molecular class (POLE, MMRd, TP53-abnormal) does not enter, though it now shifts adjuvant intensity in exactly the stage I grade 3 cell the case occupies.
> - Stage IB grade 3 is assigned pelvic EBRT, but the PORTEC-2 definition on the slide excludes grade 3 with >1/2 invasion, so the talk offers no randomized brachy-vs-EBRT data for its own case.
> - GOG249 appears only as an HIR definition; its comparison of pelvic RT against vaginal brachy plus chemo, the data closest to this case, is not presented, and the four HIR definitions shown disagree on who qualifies.
> - The TIME-C slide lists diarrhea at 1 yr (5.8% vs 15.1%, p=0.4) among IMRT reductions; as printed that difference is nonsignificant, so either the slide carries a typo or the claim overreaches.

**Trials cited:** GOG99 · PORTEC-1 · PORTEC-2 · GOG249 · RTOG 1203 · TIME-C

- 🔍 Case: FIGO stage IB (pT1bN0M0) endometrioid, grade 3, 72% myometrial invasion, LVSI-, 0/3 LN
- 🔍 Staged by robot-assisted TAH/BSO/SLNB; margins negative, no cervical stromal involvement
- 📊 Adjuvant framework by FIGO 2009 stage and grade (endometrioid)

  | Stage / grade | Adjuvant rec |
  |---|---|
  | IA, grade I-II, no LVSI | Consider observation |
  | IA grade III, or IB grade I-II | Consider vaginal cuff brachy |
  | IB, grade III | Pelvic EBRT |
  | II | Pelvic EBRT + vaginal cuff brachy boost |
  | III-IV | ChemoRT, chemo alone, or RT alone +/- brachy |

- 🔍 High-intermediate risk (HIR) definitions differ by trial

  | Trial | HIR definition |
  |---|---|
  | GOG99 | Age ≥70 + 1 factor, ≥50 + 2, or any age + 3; factors grade 2-3, LVI, IC |
  | PORTEC-1 | 2/3 of: age >60, invasion >50%, grade 3 |
  | PORTEC-2 | Age >60 and IC grade 1-2, or IB grade 3; IIA any age; grade 3 with >1/2 invasion excluded |
  | GOG249 | Age ≥70 + 1, ≥50 + 2, ≥18 + 3 factors; grade 2-3, LVSI, >50% myometrium |

- 📊 RTOG 1203 (TIME-C), patient-reported bowel events, IMRT vs 3D conformal

  | Endpoint | IMRT | 3D conformal | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Diarrhea, 5 weeks | 33.7% | 51.9% | p=.01 |
  | Fecal incontinence, 5 weeks | 1.1% | 9.3% | p=.04 |
  | Diarrhea, 1 yr | 5.8% | 15.1% | p=0.4 |
  | Antidiarrheal meds, 1 yr | 4.6% | 13% | p=.03 |

- ⚠️ Slide prints 1 yr diarrhea as p=0.4 yet lists it as an IMRT decrease
- ⚠️ PORTEC-2 excluded grade 3 with >1/2 invasion, the case's own cell
- 🔍 Surgery: TAH/BSO for apparent stage I; SLNB slowly becoming standard of care
- 🔍 Late RT effects and follow-up
  - Late: vaginal stenosis, vaginal dryness
  - Rare: cystitis, proctitis, sacral insufficiency fracture, bowel obstruction, fistula
  - Dilator: consider 1-3 x week for 10 minutes
  - Exam every 3-6 months for 2-3 yrs, then every 6 months to 5 years

## 🧠 CNS

Two HyTEC SRS tolerance papers; optic limits hold only without prior RT, and no NTCP model was possible after prior RT.

### HyTEC: SRS dose/volume tolerances of the brain

> Brain mets: 1fx V12 (incl. target) 5/10/>15 cc → ~10%/15%/20% symptomatic RN; 3fx V18 <30 cc and V23 <7 cc → <10%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The thresholds use V12 including target, and definitions diverge: for a 2.5 cm target, brain V12 7.4 cc vs tissue V12 15.6 cc in the authors' example. A brain-minus-GTV readout run against these numbers understates exposure, and on the steep part of the curve about 1 cc of volume uncertainty moves risk by about 5%.

- 📊 1fx SRS for brain mets: symptomatic RN by V12 (including target volume)

  | V12 (incl. target), 1fx | Approx. symptomatic RN risk |
  |---|---|
  | 5 cc | ~10% |
  | 10 cc | ~15% |
  | >15 cc | ~20% |

- 📊 3fx fSRS for brain mets: normal-brain V18 <30 cc and V23 <7 cc → <10% RN risk
- 📊 AVM SRS: modestly lower symptomatic RN than brain mets at equivalent V12
- 📐 Pooled NTCP model parameters (single-fraction equivalent, α/β=2 Gy)

  | Endpoint / pooled data | Metric | Vx,50 (95% CI) | γ50 (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Edema or necrosis, 5 studies | V12 incl. GTV | 63.2 (49.2–97.0) | 0.87 (0.74–1.03) | <0.001 |
  | G1–3 edema or necrosis, 3 studies | V14 | 45.8 (33.0–106.2) | 0.88 (0.68–1.11) | 0.003 |
  | G3 path-confirmed necrosis, 3 studies | V14 | 42.6 (33.8–75.5) | 1.58 (1.17–2.09) | <0.001 |

- 🔍 AAPM HyTEC pooled review: 51 reports, 9,828 pts, 13,913 targets (~7,814 unique pts)
- 🔍 NTCP modelling limited to 5 studies with per-target dose, volume and outcome
- 🔍 Excluded cohorts made up solely of glioma, meningioma, vestibular schwannoma or brainstem targets
- 🔗 RTOG 90-05 (recurrent, previously irradiated mets + gliomas): crude G≥3 neurotoxicity by diameter

  | Max diameter | Peripheral dose | Crude G≥3 neurotoxicity |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤2.0 cm | 24 Gy | 10% |
  | 2.1–3.0 cm | 18 Gy | 20% |
  | 3.1–4.0 cm | 15 Gy | 14% |

- 🔗 Cleveland Clinic (896 pts, 3,034 mets ≤2 cm): >1 cm → 2.1-fold necrosis, 4.8-fold symptomatic necrosis
- ⚠️ Crude, not actuarial, risks; median RN onset roughly a year after SRS/fSRS
- ⚠️ RN vs progression: imaging sensitivity ~60–80%; most diagnoses imaging-only

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Single- and Multifraction Stereotactic Radiosurgery Dose/Volume Tolerances of the Brain** — Milano, Michael T.; Grimm, Jimm; Niemierko, Andrzej et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2021-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.08.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2020.08.013)
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2021-milano-single-multifraction-stereotactic-radiosurgery-dose.pdf|HyTEC: SRS dose/volume tolerances of the brain (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Do V12 thresholds hold for resection-cavity SRS?

### HyTEC: SRS dose tolerances of the optic pathways

> <1% RION at optic apparatus max point dose 10 Gy/1 fx, 20 Gy/3 fx, 25 Gy/5 fx in pts without prior RT; 34 studies, 1,578 pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The single-fraction-only fit is the conservative read: 1% RION at 10.0 Gy and 5% at 14.0 Gy max point dose, against 12.1 Gy for 1% once fSRS series are pooled in. The 3- and 5-fraction limits (20 and 25 Gy) rest on 265 fSRS pts with 1 event, converted at α/β 1.6, so they gate peri-optic fSRS planning less firmly than the single-fraction number.

- 📊 <1% RION at max point dose 10 Gy/1 fx, 20 Gy/3 fx, 25 Gy/5 fx (no prior RT)
- 📊 Calculated NTCP tolerance doses, optic apparatus max point dose, no prior RT (Table 3)

  | Model · NTCP risk | EQD2 α/β 1.6 (Gy) | 1 fx SRS (Gy) | 3 fx fSRS (Gy) | 5 fx fSRS (Gy) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | All studies · 1% | 46.0 | 12.1 | 20.0 | 25.1 |
  | All studies · 2% | 59.1 | 13.8 | 23.0 | 28.9 |
  | All studies · 5% | 79.0 | 16.1 | 26.9 | 33.9 |
  | Single-fraction SRS only · 1% | 32.2 | 10.0 | n/a | n/a |
  | Single-fraction SRS only · 2% | 39.2 | 11.1 | n/a | n/a |
  | Single-fraction SRS only · 5% | 60.7 | 14.0 | n/a | n/a |

- 🔍 Pooled analysis of published reports: 34 studies, 1,578 pts, [[PMID 1990]] to Jun 2015 (AAPM WGSBRT)
- 📊 Crude RION rates by era and prior RT
  - 1997+ studies, no prior RT: <1% (9 of 1224)
  - 1997+ SRS 9 of 959 vs fSRS 1 of 265, p=0.7
  - 1997+ studies, prior RT: 11% (7 of 61), p<0.00001 vs no prior RT
  - Pre-1997 studies, no prior RT: 8.5%; 3.6% excluding the VEP-endpoint study (p=0.002 vs 1997+)
- 📐 KM stratified at 12 Gy single-fraction (EQD2 45.3 Gy, α/β 1.6): lower RION risk below 12 Gy
- 🔗 vs QUANTEC: RION negligible below 8 Gy single-fraction max dose, rising to 10% at 12-15 Gy
- ⚠️ Non-event doses mostly reported only as medians or quartiles; likelihood fit may overweight events
- ⚠️ RION definitions varied; Leber's VEP-inclusive endpoint gave 23% at median max dose ~15 Gy
- ⚠️ No NTCP model possible after prior RT: too few pts, events, and details

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.01.053](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2018.01.053)
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2021-milano-single-multi-fraction-stereotactic-radiosurgery-dose.pdf|HyTEC: SRS dose tolerances of the optic pathways (full text)]]

## 🌰 Prostate

Talk, not a readout: classifier prognostic data are strong, but predictive value for ADT with RT awaits GU009 and GU006.

### Genomic testing for therapy selection in prostate cancer (Feng)

> Decipher and PAM50 subtyping may select who needs ADT with salvage RT or gains more from apalutamide; prospective NRG trials now test this.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Two RT decisions are in play: ADT duration with definitive RT, where PREDICT-RT tests intermediate-term ADT for low or intermediate Decipher, and whether to add ADT to salvage RT, where RTOG 9601 suggests smaller absolute benefit at low Decipher. Neither is settled until those trials and NRG GU006 read out.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Felix Feng
> The speaker argued that genomic classifiers, mainly Decipher, carry prognostic information beyond clinical variables across RTOG definitive-RT and salvage-RT trials and in SPARTAN, and that this should shape ADT and ARSI decisions. In RTOG 9601, low Decipher scores tracked with a smaller absolute benefit from hormone therapy with salvage RT. The framework then moves to predictive markers: PAM50 Luminal B subtype, borrowed from breast cancer, showed the greatest ADT response. The talk closed on prospective validation, with PREDICT-RT randomizing ADT intensity by Decipher score and NRG GU006 testing apalutamide with salvage RT stratified by Luminal B status.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Smaller absolute ADT benefit in low-Decipher pts is what any prognostic marker produces at lower baseline risk; the slides show no treatment-by-biomarker interaction, so predictive value for ADT is not shown.
> - In SPARTAN, high and low Decipher pts both benefited from apalutamide, so the 233-pt analysis ranks expected benefit rather than identifying anyone to spare.
> - The RTOG 9601 hormone arm was bicalutamide, not GnRH-based ADT, so carrying the Decipher-guided ADT decision to modern salvage regimens is an assumption.
> - All validation cohorts were conventionally staged archived tissue; the talk does not address how genomic risk groups behave once PSMA PET restages pts.

**Trials cited:** RTOG 92-02 · RTOG 94-13 · RTOG 99-02 · PREDICT-RT · NRG GU009 · NRG/RTOG 9601 · SPARTAN · NRG GU006

- 🔍 Framework: genomic classifiers flag poor-prognosis disease and are now built into phase III trials, newly diagnosed and recurrent
- 📊 Pooled RTOG 92-02 / 94-13 / 99-02 (RT ± ADT): GC score independently prognostic
  - DM HR 1.24 (1.12 - 1.38) per 0.1 unit GC, p<0.001
  - Independent for DM, BF and OS when baseline clinical variables were accounted for
- 🔍 PREDICT-RT (NRG GU009): previously untreated high-risk (NCCN), Primary Endpoint: MFS, sample size 2478
- 🔍 PREDICT-RT randomizes ADT intensity within Decipher strata

  | Decipher score | Arm 1 | Arm 2 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low or Intermed | RT + intermed term ADT | RT + LTADT |
  | High | RT + LTADT | RT + LTADT + abi/apalutamide |

- 🔗 RTOG 9601 parent: salvage RT + bicalutamide (2 years) vs placebo, n=760, median f/u 13 years, OS HR 0.77, p=0.04
- 📊 RTOG 9601 Decipher MVA (JAMA Oncology 2021): GC and treatment effect by endpoint

  | Endpoint | GC per 0.1 unit HR (95% CI) | p | Treatment vs placebo HR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | DM | 1.17 (1.05 - 1.32) | 0.006 | 0.62 (0.39 - 0.97) | 0.037 |
  | PCSM | 1.39 (1.20 - 1.63) | <0.001 | 0.53 (0.30 - 0.92) | 0.024 |
  | OS | 1.17 (1.06 - 1.29) | 0.002 | 0.82 (0.57 - 1.19) | 0.293 |

- 📊 Low Decipher risk groups: smaller absolute benefit from hormone therapy with salvage RT; per-group 12-year values not in source text
- 📊 SPARTAN nmCRPC: 233 pts with archived samples profiled; high and low Decipher both benefited from apalutamide, high benefited more
- ⚠️ SPARTAN biopsy preceded randomization by median ~ 6 years
- 🔗 PAM50 Luminal B prostate cancers showed the greatest ADT response in matched cohorts (Zhao, JAMA Oncology 2017)
- 🔍 NRG GU006: tests Luminal B as a predictive marker for apalutamide with salvage RT
  - Post-RP PSA ≥0.1 and ≤1.0 ng/mL plus ≥1 risk feature
  - Risk features: Gleason 4+3 or greater, persistent PSA after RP, pT3
  - Stratified Luminal B vs non-Luminal B
  - Salvage RT + 6 months placebo vs salvage RT + 6 months apalutamide
  - Accrued faster than expected
- ⚠️ Slides show prognostic HRs; no biomarker-by-treatment interaction test for ADT benefit in source text

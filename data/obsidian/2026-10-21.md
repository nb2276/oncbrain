# 2026-10-21

**HYPO-RT-PC: 5yr FFS 84% vs 84%, adj HR 1.002 (95% CI 0.758-1.325), 42.7 Gy/7 fx non-inferior to 78 Gy/39 fx in intermediate-to-high-risk prostate, no ADT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate RT led the day: HYPO-RT-PC gives randomised support for 7-fx UHF, though the upper CI bound sits just under the NI margin; 70 Gy/28 fx IMRT holds 10-yr late G≥3 GU 2%, GI 1%; RTOG 0415 modeling sets rectal D5% ≤62 Gy for 10% late ≥G2 GI in low-risk pts. Breast: crude LRR 4.7% without PMRT in high-risk T1-T2N0.

## 🌰 Prostate

A fractionation and dose-constraint day: UHF non-inferiority, 10-yr moderate-hypo durability, rectal D5% targets, and a Decipher-integrated risk system (C-index 0.77 vs 0.68 in training).

### NRG/RTOG 0415 late toxicity tolerance doses

> Rectal D5% (EQD2) ≤62 Gy predicted halving late ≥G2 GI toxicity from 20% to 10% after 70 Gy at 2.5 Gy/fx in low-risk prostate cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The constraint that matters is a small-volume hotspot, not the high-dose V74Gy rule, which 16% of plans missed anyway. Pts with acute GI toxicity have a lower modeled tolerance (D5% ≤55 Gy for a 10% late GI rate), so planning targets may need to differ by acute course. Bladder dose explained nothing for GU, pointing elsewhere for mitigation.

- 🔍 Secondary dose-volume analysis of the HRT arm of NRG/RTOG 0415, low-risk prostate cancer, N=521
- 💊 RT: 70 Gy at 2.5 Gy/fraction, 3DCRT/IMRT N=108/413
- 🔍 Median f/u 5.9 yr; crude late ≥G2 GI 19%, GU 29%
- 🔍 Random 70%/30% training/validation split; logistic NTCP models on bootstrapping, EQD2 with rectum α/β 3 Gy, bladder 6 Gy
- 📐 Two generalizable GI models (D5% ± acute GI tox): AUCvalidation 0.64 and 0.65, p=0.01 and 0.03, pHL 0.45-0.56
- 📊 Rectal D5%[Gy] (EQD2) thresholds by target late ≥G2 GI rate

  | Late GI rate | D5% (Model IV) | D5% if acute GI tox (Model II) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 20% (observed) | 65 Gy | ≤59 Gy |
  | 15% | ≤64 Gy | ≤58 Gy |
  | 10% | ≤62 Gy | ≤55 Gy |

- 📊 Proctitis: observed 14% at D5% 64 Gy; ≤63 Gy for 10%; 62 Gy limit modeled to 7%
- 📊 GU: no dose-response for bladder; acute GU tox only a trend (AUCtraining 0.57, p=0.07)
- ⚠️ Rectal V74Gy[%] ≤15 constraint adherence only 84% overall, 77% among pts with late GI tox
- ⚠️ Discrimination modest (AUC 0.64-0.65); proctitis model only approached significance in validation (p=0.07)
- ⚠️ Single-arm subset of one trial, IMRT-heavy cohort; technique effect could not be resolved
- 🔗 Parent trial: HRT vs CFRT late ≥G2 GI 22% vs 20% (p=0.005), GU 26% vs 21% (p=0.009)

### Moderate hypofractionation 70 Gy/28 fx, 10-year outcomes

> 10-yr bRFS 71%, cRFS 86%, PCSM 6.8% after 70 Gy/28 fx IMRT for localized prostate cancer; late G≥3 GU 2%, GI 1%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Failure after 70 Gy/28 fx was almost entirely distant: 117 of 125 clinical failures were metastatic, 8 local-only. The HR 10-yr bRFS of 42% came with only 5/244 HR pts receiving elective nodal RT, which the authors attribute to short ADT and rare nodal coverage rather than inadequate prostate dose.

- 🔍 Single-institution series, 854 consecutive pts treated 1998-2012, prospectively maintained registry
- 🔍 Median f/u 11.3 yr (max 19 yr)
- 🔍 Risk mix: 266 LR, 238 FIR, 106 UIR, 244 HR (NCCN high risk 29%)
- 🔍 RT technique
  - 70 Gy/28 fx at 2.5 Gy/fx, IMRT in all pts
  - Authors cite equivalence to 80 Gy in 2 Gy/fx at α/β 1.5
  - CTV: prostate alone for LR; prostate + proximal SV (≥1 cm) for IR/HR
  - PTV margin 4 to 10 mm
  - Daily IGRT, BAT ultrasound in 94.8%
- 💊 ADT in 61%, mostly 1-6 mo; 18% received >6 mo
- 📊 10-yr outcomes by risk group (abstract values)

  | Endpoint | LR | FIR | UIR | HR | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | bRFS | 88% | 78% | 71% | 42% | <.0001 |
  | cRFS | 95% | 91% | 85% | 72% | <.0001 |
  | PCSM | 2% | 5% | 5% | 15% | n/a |

- 📊 Whole cohort 10-yr OS 69% (95% CI 66%-73%)
- 📊 Clinical failures 125 (15%): 117 distant metastases, 8 local-only
- 📊 Late G≥3 toxicity, 10-yr cumulative incidence
  - GU 2% (95% CI 0.8%-3.0%), mostly urethral stricture needing dilatation
  - GI 1% (95% CI 0.5%-2.0%), mostly rectal bleeding
  - Grade 5: 1 GU, 1 GI (rectovesicular fistula, septic death)
  - Fig 3 read: G≥3 GU 3.7% at 15 yr
- ⚠️ HR bRFS 42% at 10 yr; only 5/244 HR pts received elective pelvic nodal RT
- ⚠️ Toxicity captured by retrospective chart review, physician-reported, no validated instruments
- ⚠️ Ultrasound-era IGRT and 4-10 mm margins; may not transfer to fiducial/CBCT practice
- 🔗 vs RTOG 0415 (same 70 Gy/28 fx): authors cite G3 toxicity 3% to 4% at 5 yr, IMRT not mandatory

> - HR outcomes with 70 Gy/28 fx plus ≥2 yr ADT and pelvic nodal RT
> - Toxicity reproducibility under looser multi-institutional dose constraints

### Integrated clinical-genomic risk groups (Decipher + NCCN)

> 10y DM 3.5%/29.4%/54.6% for clinical-genomic low/int/high vs NCCN 7.3%/9.2%/38.0%/39.5% in localized prostate cancer; 30% reclassified.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The pre-RT setting is the biopsy validation cohort (n=235), where 10y DM ran 0%/25.9%/55.2% across clinical-genomic tiers. The reclassification hits the NCCN favorable/unfavorable intermediate boundary hardest (56.8% and 36.0% moved in prospective cohort I), which is the line that gates adding ADT to RT.

- 🔍 Retrospective development: training n=756 (RP specimens, 1997-2004), validation n=235 (biopsy specimens, 1995-2005)
- 🔍 Reclassification-only prospective cohorts: n=4,960 (RP) and n=977 (biopsy); no outcome data in these
- 🔍 Point system: NCCN 0-3 + Decipher 0-2 → total 0-5
  - Decipher <0.45 = 0, 0.45-0.6 = 1, >0.6 = 2
  - 3-tier: low 0-1, int 2-3, high 4-5
  - 6-tier: very-low (0) to very-high (5)
- 📊 10y distant metastasis and HR for DM by risk system

  | Risk group | 10y DM, training | 10y DM, validation | HR DM, training (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | NCCN low | 7.3% | n/a | Ref |
  | NCCN fav-int | 9.2% | n/a | 1.2 (0.5-3.0) |
  | NCCN unfav-int | 38.0% | n/a | 5.4 (2.8-12.0) |
  | NCCN high | 39.5% | n/a | 6.0 (3.2-13.0) |
  | CG low | 3.5% | 0% | Ref |
  | CG int | 29.4% | 25.9% | 9.3 (4.8-21.5) |
  | CG high | 54.6% | 55.2% | 21.9 (11.1-50.4) |

- 📊 NCCN did not separate low from fav-int, or unfav-int from high, for DM in the RP cohort
- 📐 C-index for 10y DM

  | System | Cohort | C-index (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|
  | CG 6-tier | Training | 0.77 (0.72-0.81) |
  | NCCN 4-tier | Training | 0.68 (0.64-0.72) |
  | NCCN 3-tier | Training | 0.65 (0.60-0.69) |
  | CAPRA score | Training | 0.68 (0.62-0.74) |
  | CG 6-tier | Validation | 0.84 (0.61-0.93) |

- 📊 10y PCSM, clinical-genomic groups (training)
  - Low 2.0%, int 10.7%, high 27.3%
  - HR vs low: int 6.5 (2.5-14.0), high 18.9 (7.2-69.3)
- 📊 Reclassification vs NCCN in prospective cohorts
  - Cohort I (RP): 33.4% (1,655 of 4,960) reclassified by 3-tier
  - Cohort I: 56.8% of fav-int and 36.0% of unfav-int reclassified
  - Cohort II (biopsy): 29.9% (292 of 977) reclassified
  - 6-tier vs NCCN 6-tier: 66.6% reclassified
- ⚠️ Abstract's c-index 0.84 is the 235-pt validation estimate (CI 0.61-0.93); like-for-like training contrast is 0.77 vs 0.68
- ⚠️ Treatment-adjusted HRs shrink: CG int 3.5 (1.5-8.3), high 6.1 (2.6-14.4) after adjusting for ADT and RT
- ⚠️ Validation HRs 21.3 (2.8 to 2,727.6) and 62.5 (8.5 to 7,969.6): sparse events, 0% low-risk DM
- ⚠️ Prognostic only; no test of whether treatment changes on reclassification alter outcomes
- ⚠️ Many authors employed by GenomeDx, the Decipher manufacturer

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Development and Validation of a Novel Integrated Clinical-Genomic Risk Group Classification for Localized Prostate Cancer** — Spratt, Daniel E.; Zhang, Jingbin; Santiago-Jiménez, María et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2018-02). [doi:10.1200/jco.2017.74.2940](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2017.74.2940)
  > Purpose It is clinically challenging to integrate genomic-classifier results that report a numeric risk of recurrence into treatment recommendations for localized prostate cancer, which are founded in the framework of risk groups. We aimed to develop a novel clinical-genomic risk grouping system that can readily be incorporated into treatment guidelines for localized prostate cancer. Materials and Methods Two multicenter cohorts (n = 991) were used for training and validation of the clinical-genomic risk groups, and two additional cohorts (n = 5,937) were used for reclassification analyses. Competing risks analysis was used to estimate the risk of distant metastasis. Time-dependent c-indices were constructed to compare clinicopathologic risk models with the clinical-genomic risk groups. Results With a median follow-up of 8 years for patients in the training cohort, 10-year distant metastasis rates for National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) low, favorable-intermediate, unfavorable-intermediate, and high-risk were 7.3%, 9.2%, 38.0%, and 39.5%, respectively. In contrast, the three-tier clinical-genomic risk groups had 10-year distant metastasis rates of 3.5%, 29.4%, and 54.6%, for low-, intermediate-, and high-risk, respectively, which were consistent in the validation cohort (0%, 25.9%, and 55.2%, respectively). C-indices for the clinical-genomic risk grouping system (0.84; 95% CI, 0.61 to 0.93) were improved over NCCN (0.73; 95% CI, 0.60 to 0.86) and Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (0.74; 95% CI, 0.65 to 0.84), and 30% of patients using NCCN low/intermediate/high would be reclassified by the new three-tier system and 67% of patients would be reclassified from NCCN six-tier (very-low– to very-high–risk) by the new six-tier system. Conclusion A commercially available genomic classifier in combination with standard clinicopathologic variables can generate a simple-to-use clinical-genomic risk grouping that more accurately identifies patients at low, intermediate, and high risk for metastasis and can be easily incorporated into current guidelines to better risk-stratify patients.

### HYPO-RT-PC

> 5yr FFS 84% vs 84%, adjusted HR 1.002 (95% CI 0.758-1.325): 42.7 Gy/7 fx non-inferior to 78 Gy/39 fx in intermediate-to-high-risk prostate, no ADT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The schedule tested is 42.7 Gy/7 fx every other weekday to a prostate-only CTV (no seminal vesicles) with a 7 mm fiducial margin, 80% 3DCRT, no ADT. That supports UHF for prostate-only volumes without ADT, not SV-inclusive or ADT-paired high-risk plans. Counsel on the 1yr G2+ GU bump (6% vs 2%).

- 🔍 Open-label randomised phase 3 non-inferiority trial, 12 centres in Sweden and Denmark, N=1200 (2005-2015)
- 🔍 Population and eligibility
  - Men up to 75 years, T1c-T3a, N0M0, PSA ≤20 ng/mL
  - 1054 (89%) intermediate risk, 126 (11%) high risk
  - No ADT permitted
- 🔍 RT technique
  - UHF 42.7 Gy/7 fx, 3 d/wk over 2.5 wk vs CF 78.0 Gy/39 fx, 5 d/wk over 8 wk
  - Dose prescribed as mean PTV dose
  - CTV prostate only, seminal vesicles not included
  - Gold fiducials: isotropic 7 mm CTV-PTV margin
  - 3DCRT 80%, VMAT/IMRT 20% in each arm
  - Median total treatment time 16 vs 57 days
- 📊 Efficacy and G2+ toxicity by arm (per-protocol)

  | Endpoint | UHF 42.7 Gy/7 fx | CF 78.0 Gy/39 fx | Comparison |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 5yr FFS | 84% (80-87) | 84% (80-87) | adj HR 1.002 (0.758-1.325) |
  | 5yr OS | 94% (92-96) | 96% (95-98) | HR 1.11 (0.73-1.69) |
  | Acute G2+ GU, end RT | 28% (158/569) | 23% (132/578) | p=0.057 |
  | G2+ GU at 1yr | 6% (32/528) | 2% (13/529) | p=0.0037 |
  | G2+ GU at 5yr | 5% (11/243) | 5% (12/249) | p=1.00 |
  | G2+ bowel at 5yr | 1% (3/244) | 4% (9/249) | p=0.14 |

- 📊 Cumulative late physician-reported G2+ toxicity

  | Endpoint | UHF | CF | Log-rank p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | GU at 2yr | 13% (11-16) | 9% (7-12) | 0.63 |
  | GU at 5yr | 18% (15-22) | 17% (14-20) | 0.63 |
  | Bowel at 2yr | 6% (5-9) | 5% (4-8) | 1.00 |
  | Bowel at 5yr | 10% (7-13) | 10% (7-13) | 1.00 |

- 📐 Upper CI bound 1.325 sits just under the prespecified NI limit of 1.338
- 📊 Erectile function fell from almost 70% to 35% at 5yr, no arm difference
- ⚠️ Redesigned mid-trial from superiority (70% → 80% FFS) to NI after blinded interim estimated 87% 5yr FFS
- ⚠️ Per-protocol primary; ITT differs by only 10 pts (1%)
- ⚠️ Open-label: physician-graded toxicity unblinded, though biochemical relapse assessed blindly
- 🔗 Moderate hypofractionation (CHHiP, RTOG 0415, PROFIT, HYPRO) already changed practice; this is the first randomised UHF readout
- 🔗 2018 ASTRO/ASCO/AUA guideline graded UHF conditional pending randomised evidence

## 🎀 Breast

Retrospective single-institution data on omitting PMRT in node-negative pts with high-risk features after mastectomy.

### High-risk T1-T2N0 breast, LRR without PMRT

> Crude LRR 4.7% at median 5.6y after mastectomy without PMRT in T1-T2N0 with ≥1 high-risk feature; only tumor size predicted LRR (HR 1.70).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Regional failure without any RT was 2.6%, already below the 4.2% control-arm regional rate in EORTC 22922, which leaves little room for nodal or chest wall RT to add benefit in this group. Tumor size is the one feature that held up (HR 1.70 continuous). Note that axilla-only failures after SLNB alone were overrepresented.

- 🔍 Single-institution (MSKCC) retrospective cohort, mastectomies 2006-2011
- 🔍 N=672 T1-T2N0 with ≥1 high-risk feature; 15 who received PMRT excluded, leaving 657
- 🔍 High-risk features defined by the authors
  - Age <40y
  - Multifocal/multicentric
  - LVI
  - Medial/central location
  - High nuclear grade
- 🔍 98% SLNB alone; 86% received adjuvant systemic therapy
- 📊 LRR 4.7% (31 pts) at median 5.6y; median time to LRR 33 mo
- 📊 Local recurrence 2.6% (17 pts), regional recurrence 2.6% (17 pts)
- 📐 Tumor size (continuous) predicted LRR: HR 1.70 (95% CI 1.26-2.29), P=.006
- 📐 Systemic therapy reduced LRR: HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.17-0.92), P=.03
- 📊 Age, grade, LVI, multifocality, location: not associated with LRR (all P>.05)
- 📊 LRR by number of high-risk features (log-rank P=.54)
- 📊 Site of LRR (31 events)
  - Chest wall alone: 14 (45%)
  - Axillary nodes alone: 9 (29%)
  - Supraclavicular alone: 2 (6%)
  - Internal mammary alone: 1 (3%)
- ⚠️ 8 of 22 isolated LRRs were axilla-only, 7 after SLNB alone; authors suspect false-negative SLNB
- ⚠️ 10 of 31 LRR pts (32%) did not receive recommended systemic therapy
- ⚠️ ≥4-feature subgroup small; the 9.4% crude rate has a wide CI (0.71-0.98)
- 🔗 vs EORTC 22922: regional recurrence 2.7% vs 4.2% with nodal RT; this cohort 2.6% regional without any RT
- ⚠️ High-volume dedicated breast surgeons; generalizability uncertain per authors

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Are there patients with T1 to T2, lymph node‐negative breast cancer who are “high‐risk” for locoregional disease recurrence?** — Mamtani, Anita; Patil, Sujata; Stempel, Michelle M. et al.. *Cancer* (2017-03). [doi:10.1002/cncr.30658](https://doi.org/10.1002/cncr.30658)
  > BACKGROUND Indications for postmastectomy radiotherapy (PMRT) in patients with T1 to T2, lymph node‐negative (N0) breast cancer with “high‐risk” features are controversial. The European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) 22922 and National Cancer Institute of Canada Clinical Trials Group MA20 trials reporting improved 10‐year disease‐free survival with lymph node irradiation included patients with high‐risk N0 disease, but, to the authors’ knowledge, benefits in patients receiving modern systemic therapy are uncertain. METHODS The authors retrospectively identified patients with T1 to T2N0 disease who were treated with mastectomy from January 2006 through December 2011. High‐risk features included age &lt;40 years, multifocality/multicentricity, lymphovascular invasion, medial/central tumor location, and high nuclear grade. RESULTS Among 672 eligible patients, only 15 received PMRT and were excluded. Of the remaining 657 patients, 187 (28%) had 1 high‐risk feature and 449 patients (68%) had ≥ 2 high‐risk features. A total of 36 patients with unknown tumor grade were excluded from risk analysis. Approximately 98% of patients underwent sentinel lymph node biopsy alone and 86% received adjuvant systemic therapy. At a median of 5.6 years of follow‐up, the locoregional disease recurrence (LRR) rate was 4.7% (31 patients). Increasing tumor size was found to be associated with LRR (hazard ratio, 1.70; P = .006), whereas other high‐risk features were not (all P &gt; .05). Receipt of systemic therapy decreased the LRR rate (hazard ratio, 0.40; P = .03). Although crude LRR rates increased from 3.8% to 9.4% with 1 versus ≥ 4 high‐risk features, the number of risk factors was not found to be significantly associated with LRR ( P = .54). CONCLUSIONS In the current study, a low crude LRR rate (4.7%) was observed in a large unselected cohort of patients with T1 to T2N0 breast cancer with high‐risk features who were treated with mastectomy and systemic therapy without PMRT. Although increasing tumor size and the omission of systemic therapy were found to be predictive, other features did not confer a higher LRR risk either independently or together, and do not by themselves mandate the use of PMRT in this patient population.

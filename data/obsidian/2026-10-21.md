# 2026-10-21

**HYPO-RT-PC: 42.7 Gy/7 fx non-inferior to 78 Gy/39 fx, 5y FFS 84% vs 84%, HR 1.002 (0.758-1.325), intermediate-to-high-risk prostate, no ADT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day with hypofractionation data from 7 to 28 fx: HYPO-RT-PC non-inferior at 5y, 70 Gy/28 fx with 10-y late G3+ GU 2% and GI 1%, and RTOG 0415 modeling rectal D5% ≤62 Gy to halve late ≥G2 GI. Breast: crude LRR 4.7% after mastectomy without PMRT in high-risk T1-T2N0.

## 🌰 Prostate

Three of four prostate cards inform hypofractionation, from rectal dose constraints at 2.5 Gy/fx to 7 fx ultra-hypo; Decipher + NCCN reclassified 30% of localized pts.

### NRG/RTOG 0415 Late Toxicity Tolerance Doses

> Rectal D5% (EQD2) ≤62 Gy predicted halving late ≥G2 GI toxicity from 20% to 10% after 70 Gy at 2.5 Gy/fx in low-risk prostate cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The tolerance dose sits in the small high-dose rectal volume, not intermediate-dose sparing: D5% (EQD2) ≤62 Gy models late ≥G2 GI from 20% to 10%, and pts with acute GI toxicity need ≤55 Gy for the same rate. The GU signal is absent, so bladder constraints won't fix the larger GU toxicity rate.

- 🔍 Dose-volume modeling of the HRT arm of NRG/RTOG 0415, low-risk prostate cancer, N=521
- 🔍 RT: 70 Gy at 2.5 Gy/fx, 3DCRT/IMRT N=108/413
- 🔍 Doses converted to EQD2Gy (rectum α/β 3 Gy, bladder α/β 6 Gy)
- 🔍 Cohort randomly split 70% training / 30% validation; logistic regression with bootstrapping
- 📊 Crude late ≥G2 GI 19%, GU 29% at median f/u 5.9 yr
- 📊 Rectal D5% (EQD2) threshold by target late ≥G2 GI rate

  | Late GI rate | All pts (Model IV) | Pts with acute GI tox (Model II) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 20% | D5% 65 Gy (observed) | D5% ≤59 |
  | 15% | D5% ≤64 Gy | D5% ≤58 |
  | 10% | D5% ≤62 | D5% ≤55 |

- 📐 Validation AUC 0.65 (Model IV, D5% alone) and 0.64 (Model II, D5% + acute GI tox)
- 📊 Proctitis: 14% observed at D5% 64 Gy; D5% ≤62 Gy models a drop to 7%
- 📊 Late GU: no dose-volume predictor; acute GU tox only a trend (AUC 0.57, p=0.07)
- ⚠️ AUC 0.64-0.65 is modest discrimination; thresholds are population-level, not per-pt risk
- ⚠️ Rectal constraint (EQD2 74 Gy ≤15%) adherence was only 77% among pts with late GI tox
- 🔗 Parent trial: late ≥G2 GI 22% vs 20% (p=0.005), GU 26% vs 21% (p=0.009), HRT vs CFRT

[doi:10.1016/j.radonc.2019.02.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2019.02.014)

### Integrated Clinical-Genomic Risk Groups (Decipher + NCCN)

> Decipher + NCCN three-tier groups: 10-yr DM 3.5%, 29.4%, 54.6% (validation 0%, 25.9%, 55.2%); 30% of localized prostate pts reclassified.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant split is inside intermediate risk: NCCN unfav-int and high-risk carried near-identical 10-yr DM (38.0% vs 39.5%), and 36.0% and 40.5% of unfav-int pts changed group with Decipher. That makes the integrated group, not NCCN tier alone, the better prognostic gate when sizing ADT with RT, though benefit by group was not tested.

- 🔍 Retrospective development: training n=756 (RP, 1997-2004), median f/u 8 yr
- 🔍 Retrospective validation: n=235 biopsy specimens, 1995-2005
- 🔍 Prospective genomic cohorts n=4,960 (RP) and n=977 (biopsy) used for reclassification only, not outcomes
- 🔍 Point system: NCCN points + Decipher points → total 0-5
  - NCCN: low 0, fav-int 1, unfav-int 2, high/very-high 3
  - Decipher: <0.45 = 0, 0.45-0.6 = 1, >0.6 = 2
  - Clinical-genomic: 0-1 low, 2-3 int, 4-5 high
- 📊 NCCN four-tier, training cohort: 10-yr outcomes

  | NCCN group | 10-yr DM | HR DM (95% CI) | 10-yr PCSM |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Low | 7.3% | Ref | 5.8% |
  | Fav-int | 9.2% | 1.2 (0.5 to 3.0), P = .64 | 2.7% |
  | Unfav-int | 38.0% | 5.5 (2.8 to 12.1) | 15.9% |
  | High | 39.5% | 6.0 (3.2 to 13.0) | 16.8% |

- 📊 Clinical-genomic three-tier: 10-yr outcomes

  | CG group | DM training | DM validation | HR DM training | PCSM training |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Low | 3.5% | 0% | Ref | 2.0% |
  | Int | 29.4% | 25.9% | 9.3 (4.8 to 21.5) | 10.7% |
  | High | 54.6% | 55.2% | 21.9 (11.1 to 50.4) | 27.3% |

- 📐 Training c-index 10-yr DM: six-tier CG 0.77 vs NCCN four-tier 0.68, three-tier 0.65, CAPRA 0.68
- 📐 Validation six-tier CG c-index 0.84 (95% CI, 0.61 to 0.93)
- 📊 Reclassification NCCN → three-tier CG (prospective cohorts I / II)
  - Overall: 33.4% / 29.9%
  - Fav-int: 56.8% / 44.4%
  - Unfav-int: 36.0% / 40.5%
  - Six-tier vs six-tier (cohort II): 66.6% reclassified
- ⚠️ ADT/RT-adjusted HRs attenuate: CG int 3.5 (1.5 to 8.3), high 6.1 (2.6 to 14.4)
- ⚠️ Validation fragile: 47 low-risk pts at risk; high-risk HR 62.5 (8.5 to 7,969.6)
- ⚠️ Abstract cites NCCN c-index 0.73 (0.60 to 0.86); Table 2 gives 0.68 (0.64 to 0.73)
- ⚠️ Training cohort all RP; prognostic performance in RT-treated pts not separately reported
- ⚠️ Many authors employed by or hold stock in GenomeDx, the Decipher manufacturer

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Development and Validation of a Novel Integrated Clinical-Genomic Risk Group Classification for Localized Prostate Cancer** — Spratt, Daniel E.; Zhang, Jingbin; Santiago-Jiménez, María et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2018-02). [doi:10.1200/jco.2017.74.2940](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2017.74.2940)
  > Purpose It is clinically challenging to integrate genomic-classifier results that report a numeric risk of recurrence into treatment recommendations for localized prostate cancer, which are founded in the framework of risk groups. We aimed to develop a novel clinical-genomic risk grouping system that can readily be incorporated into treatment guidelines for localized prostate cancer. Materials and Methods Two multicenter cohorts (n = 991) were used for training and validation of the clinical-genomic risk groups, and two additional cohorts (n = 5,937) were used for reclassification analyses. Competing risks analysis was used to estimate the risk of distant metastasis. Time-dependent c-indices were constructed to compare clinicopathologic risk models with the clinical-genomic risk groups. Results With a median follow-up of 8 years for patients in the training cohort, 10-year distant metastasis rates for National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) low, favorable-intermediate, unfavorable-intermediate, and high-risk were 7.3%, 9.2%, 38.0%, and 39.5%, respectively. In contrast, the three-tier clinical-genomic risk groups had 10-year distant metastasis rates of 3.5%, 29.4%, and 54.6%, for low-, intermediate-, and high-risk, respectively, which were consistent in the validation cohort (0%, 25.9%, and 55.2%, respectively). C-indices for the clinical-genomic risk grouping system (0.84; 95% CI, 0.61 to 0.93) were improved over NCCN (0.73; 95% CI, 0.60 to 0.86) and Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (0.74; 95% CI, 0.65 to 0.84), and 30% of patients using NCCN low/intermediate/high would be reclassified by the new three-tier system and 67% of patients would be reclassified from NCCN six-tier (very-low– to very-high–risk) by the new six-tier system. Conclusion A commercially available genomic classifier in combination with standard clinicopathologic variables can generate a simple-to-use clinical-genomic risk grouping that more accurately identifies patients at low, intermediate, and high risk for metastasis and can be easily incorporated into current guidelines to better risk-stratify patients.

### HYPO-RT-PC

> 5y FFS 84% vs 84%, adjusted HR 1.002 (95% CI 0.758-1.325): 42.7 Gy/7 fx non-inferior to 78 Gy/39 fx in intermediate-to-high-risk prostate cancer, no ADT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable unit is narrower than the headline: prostate-only CTV with no SVs, 7 mm isotropic margins on fiducials, 80% 3DCRT, no ADT. The price is early GU toxicity (G2+ at 1y 6% vs 2%, p=0.0037) that converges by 5y (5% vs 5%). This supports 7-fx UHF for intermediate-risk disease, not a specific modern 5-fx SBRT schedule.

- 📊 1° EP met: 5y FFS 84% (80-87) in both arms
- 📐 Adjusted HR 1.002 (95% CI 0.758-1.325) vs critical NI HR 1.338
- 📐 Upper CI bound 1.325 sits just inside the 1.338 limit
- 🔍 Open-label randomised phase 3 NI, 12 centres Sweden/Denmark, N=1200, median f/u 5.0y
- 🔍 42.7 Gy/7 fx (3 d/wk, 2.5 wk) vs 78.0 Gy/39 fx (5 d/wk, 8 wk), mean PTV dose
- 🔍 CTV prostate only, no SVs; 7 mm isotropic CTV-PTV margin with gold fiducials
- 💊 No ADT permitted in either arm
- 📊 Efficacy + toxicity by arm (per-protocol)

  | Endpoint | Ultra-hypo 42.7 Gy/7 fx | Conventional 78 Gy/39 fx | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 5y FFS | 84% (80-87) | 84% (80-87) | HR 1.002 (0.758-1.325) |
  | 5y OS | 94% (92-96) | 96% (95-98) | HR 1.11 (0.73-1.69) |
  | Acute G2+ GU, end of RT | 28% (158/569) | 23% (132/578) | p=0.057 |
  | G2+ GU at 1y | 6% (32/528) | 2% (13/529) | p=0.0037 |
  | G2+ GU at 5y | 5% (11/243) | 5% (12/249) | p=1.00 |
  | G2+ bowel at 5y | 1% (3/244) | 4% (9/249) | p=0.14 |

- 📊 Cumulative late G2+ toxicity (RTOG), ultra-hypo vs conventional

  | Toxicity | 2y | 5y |
  |---|---|---|
  | Urinary | 13% vs 9% | 18% vs 17% |
  | Bowel | 6% vs 5% | 10% vs 10% |

- ⚠️ Designed for superiority; converted to NI after blinded interim showed 87% 5y FFS
- ⚠️ 80% 3DCRT; transfer to IMRT/SBRT with tighter margins not tested here
- ⚠️ 89% intermediate risk; high-risk n=126 too few to stand alone
- 🔗 Extends moderate-hypofx trials CHHiP, RTOG 0415, PROFIT, HYPRO to 7 fx

### Ten-Year Outcomes of 70 Gy/28 fx Hypofractionated IMRT

> 10-y bRFS 71%, cRFS 86%, late G3+ GU 2% and GI 1% after 70 Gy/28 fx IMRT in localized prostate cancer; HR bRFS only 42%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Of 125 clinical failures, 117 were distant and only 8 local-only, so the 42% HR bRFS points to nodal/systemic undertreatment (5 of 244 HR pts got pelvic nodal RT) rather than a 2.5 Gy/fx dose deficit. Whether the toxicity transfers depends on dosimetry: the institution's current constraints cap rectal and bladder V70 at 5%, while NRG-GU005 allows 25% rectal V70.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective series: 854 consecutive pts, 1998-2012, from a prospectively maintained registry
- 🔍 Median f/u 11.3 y (max 19); median age 68, median PSA 8.6 ng/mL
- 🔍 RT technique
  - 70 Gy/28 fx (2.5 Gy/fx) IMRT, EQD2 80 Gy assuming α/β 1.5
  - CTV: prostate alone (LR); prostate + proximal SV ≥1 cm (IR/HR)
  - PTV margin 4-10 mm
  - Daily IGRT, BAT ultrasound in 94.8%
- 💊 ADT in 61%, mostly 1-6 mo; 18% received >6 mo
- ⚠️ Only 5 of 244 HR pts received elective pelvic nodal RT
- 📊 10-y outcomes by NCCN risk group (bRFS, cRFS p<.0001)

  | Endpoint | All | LR | FIR | UIR | HR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | bRFS | 71% | 88% | 78% | 71% | 42% |
  | cRFS | 86% | 95% | 91% | 85% | 72% |
  | PCSM | 7% | 2% | 5% | 5% | 15% |

- 📊 10-y OS 69% (95% CI 66-73%)
- 📊 Clinical failure in 125 pts: 117 distant mets, only 8 local-only
- 📊 Late G3+ toxicity, 10-y cumulative incidence
  - GU 2% (95% CI 0.8-3.0%), mostly urethral stricture needing dilatation
  - GI 1% (95% CI 0.5-2.0%), mostly rectal bleeding
  - One G5 GU event and one G5 GI event (rectovesical fistula)
- 🔗 RTOG 0415 used the same 70 Gy/28 fx; G3 toxicity 3-4% at 5 y, IMRT not mandated
- ⚠️ Toxicity abstracted retrospectively and physician-reported, no validated PROs, so likely underreported

## 🎀 Breast

Retrospective MSKCC series bears on PMRT omission in strictly N0 T1-T2 disease with high-risk features.

### High-Risk T1-T2N0 Breast Cancer After Mastectomy Without PMRT

> Crude LRR 4.7% at median 5.6y without PMRT in 657 T1-T2N0 pts with ≥1 high-risk feature; only tumor size predicted LRR (HR 1.70).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Failures split evenly local vs regional (2.6% each), and axilla-only recurrences mostly followed SLNB alone, so a field covering chest wall or nodes would address a small absolute pool. Tumor size was the lone predictor (HR 1.70 continuous); the ≥4-feature group reached 9.4% crude LRR on small numbers, the stratum where PMRT/RNI discussion stays live.

- 🔍 Retrospective single-institution (MSKCC) cohort, mastectomy 2006-2011, N=657 without PMRT
- 🔍 High-risk features: age <40, multifocal/multicentric, LVI, medial/central location, high nuclear grade
- 🔍 Strict N0: SLNs free of any tumor, incl. ITCs and micromets; neoadjuvant chemo excluded
- 💊 86% adjuvant systemic therapy; 98% SLNB alone (median 4 SLNs)
- 📊 LRR 31 pts (4.7%): local 17 (2.6%), regional 17 (2.6%); median time to LRR 33 mo
- 📐 Tumor size (continuous) → LRR HR 1.70 (95% CI 1.26-2.29), p=.006
- 📐 Systemic therapy → LRR HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.17-0.92), p=.03
- 📊 LRR by number of high-risk features (log-rank p=.54)
- 📊 Pattern of first LRR (n=31)
  - Chest wall alone: 14 (45%)
  - Axilla alone: 9 (29%)
  - Chest wall + axilla: 3 (10%)
  - SCV alone: 2 (6%); IMN alone: 1 (3%)
- ⚠️ 8 of 22 isolated LRRs were axilla-only; 7 of those 8 had SLNB alone, possibly false-negative SLNB
- ⚠️ 10 of 31 LRR pts (32%) did not receive recommended systemic therapy
- 🔗 vs EORTC 22922: regional recurrence 2.7% vs 4.2% with RNI; this cohort 2.6% regional with no RT
- ⚠️ Only 4 or 5 RF group n=32 (KM): wide CI, underpowered for the highest-risk stratum

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Are there patients with T1 to T2, lymph node‐negative breast cancer who are “high‐risk” for locoregional disease recurrence?** — Mamtani, Anita; Patil, Sujata; Stempel, Michelle M. et al.. *Cancer* (2017-03). [doi:10.1002/cncr.30658](https://doi.org/10.1002/cncr.30658)
  > BACKGROUND Indications for postmastectomy radiotherapy (PMRT) in patients with T1 to T2, lymph node‐negative (N0) breast cancer with “high‐risk” features are controversial. The European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) 22922 and National Cancer Institute of Canada Clinical Trials Group MA20 trials reporting improved 10‐year disease‐free survival with lymph node irradiation included patients with high‐risk N0 disease, but, to the authors’ knowledge, benefits in patients receiving modern systemic therapy are uncertain. METHODS The authors retrospectively identified patients with T1 to T2N0 disease who were treated with mastectomy from January 2006 through December 2011. High‐risk features included age &lt;40 years, multifocality/multicentricity, lymphovascular invasion, medial/central tumor location, and high nuclear grade. RESULTS Among 672 eligible patients, only 15 received PMRT and were excluded. Of the remaining 657 patients, 187 (28%) had 1 high‐risk feature and 449 patients (68%) had ≥ 2 high‐risk features. A total of 36 patients with unknown tumor grade were excluded from risk analysis. Approximately 98% of patients underwent sentinel lymph node biopsy alone and 86% received adjuvant systemic therapy. At a median of 5.6 years of follow‐up, the locoregional disease recurrence (LRR) rate was 4.7% (31 patients). Increasing tumor size was found to be associated with LRR (hazard ratio, 1.70; P = .006), whereas other high‐risk features were not (all P &gt; .05). Receipt of systemic therapy decreased the LRR rate (hazard ratio, 0.40; P = .03). Although crude LRR rates increased from 3.8% to 9.4% with 1 versus ≥ 4 high‐risk features, the number of risk factors was not found to be significantly associated with LRR ( P = .54). CONCLUSIONS In the current study, a low crude LRR rate (4.7%) was observed in a large unselected cohort of patients with T1 to T2N0 breast cancer with high‐risk features who were treated with mastectomy and systemic therapy without PMRT. Although increasing tumor size and the omission of systemic therapy were found to be predictive, other features did not confer a higher LRR risk either independently or together, and do not by themselves mandate the use of PMRT in this patient population.

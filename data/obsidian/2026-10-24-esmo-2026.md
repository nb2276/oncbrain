# 2026-10-24 — ESMO Congress 2026

**DeLLphi-305: mOS 21.1 vs 12.7 mo (HR 0.42) adding tarlatamab to durvalumab 1L maintenance in ES-SCLC, at interim OS analysis.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day: DASL-HiCaP adds darolutamide to pelvic RT + ADT in very high-risk disease (4-yr MFS-CI 95% vs 88%, HR 0.42; interim, OS immature), and PEACE-1 at 8y holds OS HR 0.68 for abiraterone over ADT+docetaxel. Single-fraction SBRT edged 30 Gy/10 on 3-mo ITT pain response (38% vs 21%) for nonspine bone mets.

### SBRT for Ultra-Central Lung Tumors Meta-Analysis

> Pooled 1-yr LC 92% and 2-yr LC 89% after ultra-central lung SBRT; G5 toxicity 4%, mostly hemoptysis, across 27 mostly retrospective studies.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The toxicity signal follows PBT hot spots, not the prescription: HILUS's 15% G5 came with a 67% IDL and median main bronchus Dmax 97 Gy. ISRS response: prescribe to 80-85% IDL, cap PBT Dmax at 133-150 Gy BED3 (EQD23 80-90 Gy), undercover PTV before overdosing airway.

- 🔍 27 studies (2010-2022), 1183 pts; all retrospective except HILUS phase II, none randomized
- 🔍 UC definition varied: PTV-PBT overlap in 100% of studies, GTV overlap in only 59%
- 🔍 Dose 30-70 Gy in 3-10 fx; BED10 range 52.5-180
- 🔍 Commonest schedules: 50 Gy/5 (18%), 60 Gy/8 (17%), 60 Gy/12 (10%)
- 📊 Pooled outcomes (random effects)

  | Endpoint | Pooled | 95% CI | Range | I² |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1-yr LC (21 studies) | 92% | 86-96 | 54-100% | 93.2% |
  | 2-yr LC (19 studies) | 89% | 82-94 | 57-100% | 94.3% |
  | Any G3-4 toxicity | 6% | 3-10 | 0-35% | 71% |
  | Any G5 toxicity | 4% | 2-6 | 0-22% | 68% |
  | G5 hemoptysis | 1% | 0-3 | 0-15% | 65% |
  | G3-4 pneumonitis | 1% | 0-2 | 0-11% | 5% |

- 📊 Primary NSCLC subset: 1- and 2-yr LC both 95%
- 📐 Meta-regression: higher BED tied to 1-yr LC (HR 2.14, p=0.03); no 2-yr association (p=0.66)
- 📐 Trend to lower 1-yr LC with larger PTV (HR 0.84, p=0.08)
- 📊 73 G5 events; hemoptysis most common (42 events, 58% of G5)
- ⚠️ Fatal-toxicity risk factors across pooled series
  - Endobronchial tumor in 8 fatal cases
  - Antiplatelet/anticoagulant use in 17 G5 hemoptysis cases
  - VEGF inhibitor within 30 days of SBRT: fatal hemoptysis HR 16.9, p<0.001 (single 88-pt series)
  - ILD: 5 G5 events (2 pneumonitis, 3 hemoptysis)
- 🔗 vs HILUS (56 Gy/8, 67% IDL, PTV margin up to 15 mm): G5 15%, median main bronchus Dmax 97 Gy
- 🔗 HILUS G5 hemoptysis 4% if tumor >1 cm from main bronchi/trachea vs 18% if ≤1 cm
- 🔗 vs RTOG 0813 (central/UC phase II, 120 pts): 2-yr LC 89.4%, in line with pooled 89%
- 🔍 ISRS practice recommendations (Table 3)

  | Category | Recommendation |
  |---|---|
  | Delivery/dosing | 60 Gy/8 or 60 Gy/15; prescribe to 80-85% IDL; global Dmax far <150% |
  | PBT constraint | Dmax <133-150 Gy BED3 (EQD23 <80-90 Gy); lower if risk factors or near trachea/main bronchi |
  | Antiplatelet/anticoagulation | Hold when pausing is low-risk; else de-escalate PBT Dmax or use non-SBRT schedule |
  | Targeted/IO | Gap 2-3 days targeted, 3-4 wk VEGF inhibitors, ≥2 days IO |
  | Endobronchial tumor | Non-SBRT (60 Gy/15); PBT Dmax <100 Gy BED3 (EQD23 <60 Gy) |
  | ILD | Lung V20 <10% strict cutoff; treat on trial |

- ⚠️ Publication bias detected in all LC and toxicity endpoints
- ⚠️ 60% of studies had median f/u <2 yr; late airway toxicity likely undercounted
- ⚠️ No association between PBT Dmax and airway toxicity on meta-regression; few studies reported dosimetry

## 🌰 Prostate

ARPI intensification on top of local therapy is the theme: darolutamide with RT + ADT (DASL-HiCaP), apalutamide around RP (PROTEUS), abiraterone with ADT+docetaxel (PEACE-1).

### PEACE-1

> mOS 6.2 vs 4.4y (HR 0.68) adding abiraterone to ADT+docetaxel at 8y FU, n=710; no treatment-by-volume interaction (P=0.5552).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Low volume is where prostate RT sits, and abiraterone still more than doubled 7-yr rPFS there (43.4% vs 20.9%, HR 0.54) with no volume interaction (p=0.5552). Models adjusted for prostate RT, but an abiraterone-by-RT interaction is not reported in source, so this does not settle whether triplet and prostate RT add independently.

### DASL-HiCaP · [[NCT04135353]]

> 4-yr MFS-CI 95% vs 88%, HR 0.42, adding darolutamide to prostate/bed + pelvic LN RT and 96 wk ADT in very high-risk prostate cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology

## 🌐 Lymphoma

### Low-Dose Involved Field RT in Indolent Lymphomas

> 4 Gy involved-field RT in recurrent indolent lymphoma: ORR 92%, CR 61%, median time to local progression 25 mo, toxicity virtually absent.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dose is the read: 2×2 Gy produced CR 61% with near-zero toxicity, and the 41 selected responders re-treated reached RR 98%, so the field can be re-irradiated. CR pts held local control for a median 42 mo, while PR pts progressed in-field at 10 mo, which frames 4 Gy as palliation that is durable mainly when it achieves CR.

- 🔍 Phase II, N=109 assessable (111 treated), 304 symptomatic sites, Nov 1997 to Mar 2002
- 🔍 Recurrent symptomatic B-cell indolent lymphoma: FL 98 (43 G1, 55 G2), MALT 9, lymphoplasmacytoid 2
- 🔍 Heavily pretreated: median 2 prior regimens (range 0-11); median 41 mo since diagnosis
- 🔍 Bulky disease (≥5 cm) in 48 of 92 pts with size data (52%); 13% had nodes ≥10 cm
- 📐 RT: 4 Gy as 2×2 Gy 48h apart (n=80) or 1×4 Gy (n=29); whole nodal area or node + 1.5 cm margin
- 📊 1° EP in-field control: CR 61%, PR 31%, SD 6%, PD 2%; median TP 14 mo, median TLP 25 mo
- 📊 Median time to progression by response to first LD-IF-RT

  | Response group | Median TP | Median TLP |
  |---|---|---|
  | All (n=109) | 14 mo | 25 mo |
  | CR (n=67) | 25 mo | 42 mo |
  | PR (n=34) | 9 mo | 10 mo |
  | Nonresponders | 2 mo | 2 mo |

- 📊 Re-treatment in 41 selected responders: RR 98%, CR 71%, PR 27%
- 📊 Response not associated with age, FL grade, RT schedule, prior lines, site count, or node size
- 📊 Toxicity: 1 transient alopecia, 1 transient hyperpigmentation; no tumor lysis
- 🔗 RR slightly better than pts' own 2nd (P=.04) and last (P=.03) prior regimens
- 🔗 vs Girinsky (Paris, 48 pts): concordant RR/CR, but 2yr local PFS in PR pts 46% there vs 10% here
- ⚠️ Median follow-up for pts in remission only 7 mo; durability estimates fragile
- ⚠️ Within-pt comparison vs prior regimens is historical, confounded by line and disease biology
- ⚠️ CR signals for ≤2 sites (P=.01) and ≤5 cm (P=.006) are multiple-comparison, not definitive
- ⚠️ 10 pts transformed to DLBCL; median TP only 3 mo despite similar response rates

- 📄 **High Response Rates and Lasting Remissions After Low-Dose Involved Field Radiotherapy in Indolent Lymphomas** — Haas, R.L.M.; Poortmans, Ph.; de Jong, D. et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2003-07). [doi:10.1200/jco.2003.09.542](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2003.09.542)
  > Purpose: To study the response rates and duration of response after low-dose (4 Gy) involved field radiotherapy (LD-IF-RT) in patients with recurrent indolent lymphoma. Patients and Methods: A total of 109 assessable patients (304 symptomatic sites) were irradiated (53 males and 56 females; median age, 62 years; range, 35 to 93), including 98 patients with follicular lymphoma (43 grade 1 and 55 grade 2), nine extranodal marginal zone lymphomas of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue-type and two patients with lymphoplasmacytoid lymphoma. Bulky disease (≥5 cm) was present in 52% of all patients. A median of two prior regimens (range, 0 to 11) preceded LD-IF-RT. The median time since diagnosis was 41 months (range, 2 to 358 months). Time to (local) progression was calculated according to the Kaplan-Meier method. Differences in response rates between treatments within the same patient were compared using the McNemar test. Results: The overall response rate was 92%; complete response was reached in 67 patients (61%), partial response in 34 patients (31%), stable disease in six patients (6%), and progressive disease in two patients (2%). The median time to progression was 14 months. The median time to local progression was 25 months. The 67 patients with complete response showed a median time to progression of 25 months and a median time to local progression of 42 months. None of the factors studied (age, sex, follicular lymphoma grade, radiotherapy regimen, number of previous regimens and previous history, number of positive sites or largest lymphoma diameter) were found to be related to response rate. Conclusion: LD-IF-RT is a valuable asset in the management of patients with follicular lymphoma and should be considered in patients with recurrent disease.

## 🤝 Supportive / QoL

Nonspine bone met SBRT: a randomized phase 2 favoring single-fraction SBRT, plus an expert survey with 35 Gy/5 the most chosen schedule.

### Nonspine Bone Metastases SBRT Practice Patterns

> 35 Gy/5 most often chosen (29/99 responses); 40 Gy/5 acceptable to 8 or 9 of 9 experts wherever listed; every schedule BED ≤100 Gy10.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The site-specific rejections are the RT read: 20 Gy/1 lost majority support in the humerus, acetabulum and femur cases, and 50 Gy/5 in the humerus and acetabulum, while 40 Gy/5 stayed acceptable to 8 or 9 of 9 wherever listed. For weight-bearing or periarticular targets, that favors moderate 5-fraction schedules over single fraction.

- 🔍 Survey of 9 international NSBM-SBRT experts on 11 nonsurgical cases, GTV pre-contoured; descriptive analysis only
- 🔍 Case sites covered
  - Long bones: femur, humerus
  - Pelvis: ilium, ischium, acetabulum, pubic symphysis
  - Thorax: rib, sternum, scapula, clavicle
- 📊 Schedules chosen by ≥3 experts (single dose volume)

  | Schedule | BED (Gy10) | Use |
  |---|---|---|
  | 35 Gy/5 | 60 | Most common: 29 of 99 responses (29%); 5 experts in ≥1 case |
  | 20 Gy/1 | 60 | ≥3 experts |
  | 30 Gy/3 | 60 | ≥3 experts |
  | 30 Gy/5 | 48 | ≥3 experts |

- 📊 Full range: 18-24 Gy/1, 24 Gy/2, 28-30 Gy/3, 30-50 Gy/5, 42-50 Gy/10
- 📊 BED range 48 to 100 Gy10; 58% of schedules at BED 60 Gy10
- 📊 SIB used routinely by 3 of 9; 30 Gy/3 + 15-24 Gy/3 most common SIB (12 of 24, 50%)
- 📊 40 Gy/5 most widely accepted: unanimous (n=9) or near-unanimous (n=8) in every case listed
- ⚠️ Schedules a majority rated not recommended, by case

  | Case | Not recommended by majority |
  |---|---|
  | Humerus (RCC) | 20 Gy/1 · 50 Gy/5 |
  | Acetabulum (breast) | 20 Gy/1 · 50 Gy/5 · 8 Gy/1 + 18 Gy/1 SIB |
  | Femur (NSCLC) | 20 Gy/1 |

- 📊 Dose de-escalation triggers (experts of 9)
  - Prior SBRT to same target >3 mo ago: all 9
  - Prior cRT to same target >3 mo ago: 7
  - Weight-bearing bone, moderate-severe cortical erosion: 7
  - Weight-bearing bone in general: 5
- 📊 Escalation a minority choice: radioresistant histology (n=3), bulky tumor (n=1), extraosseous disease (n=1)
- 📊 Unanimous no change for mild cortical erosion or non-weight-bearing bone with moderate-severe erosion
- 📊 MRI fusion routine for 5 experts (56%); 2 CT-only, 2 fuse selectively
- 🔗 vs Erler et al (cited): LC 95% at 6 mo, 87% at 24 mo, 106 NSBM after 30-35 Gy/5
- ⚠️ No formal consensus process; acceptability ratings are opinion, not LC or fracture outcomes
- ⚠️ BED calculated at α/β 10; authors note lower α/β may apply to breast and prostate cases

### Single-Fraction SBRT vs Multifraction RT for Nonspine Bone Metastases · [[NCT02163226]]

> 3-mo ITT pain response 38% vs 21% (P=.05) with 12-16 Gy single-fraction SBRT vs 30 Gy/10; 2-yr local PFS 100% vs 75.6%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Dose, not single fraction per se, carries the pain signal: 3-mo ITT response was 62% (13 of 21) with 16 Gy vs 30% (18 of 60) with 12 Gy, and 12 Gy tracked 30 Gy/10 at 9 mo (13.3% vs 15.2%). Because 16 Gy went to smaller lesions, the dose choice remains hypothesis-level.

- 🔍 Phase 2, nonblinded, randomized noninferiority trial, single institution (MD Anderson), N=160
- 🔍 Noninferiority margin 10%, one-sided alpha 0.20, 90% power
- 🔍 SBRT 12 or 16 Gy x1 by lesion size (4 cm cutoff) vs 30 Gy in 10 fx
- 🔍 PTV = GTV + 5 mm, no CTV; stereotactic immobilization in SBRT arm
- 📊 Pain response (CR+PR) by timepoint, SBRT vs MFRT

  | Analysis · timepoint | SBRT | MFRT | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ITT · 1 mo | 44% (n=36) | 30% (n=24) | .18 |
  | ITT · 3 mo | 38% (n=31) | 21% (n=17) | .05 |
  | Per-protocol · 2 wk | 34 of 55 (62%) | 19 of 52 (36%) | .01 |
  | Per-protocol · 3 mo | 31 of 43 (72%) | 17 of 35 (49%) | .03 |
  | Per-protocol · 9 mo | 17 of 22 (77%) | 12 of 26 (46%) | .04 |

- 📐 HRs at 3 mo, SBRT vs MFRT (ITT)
  - Composite pain failure (PP+IR): HR 0.73 (95% CI 0.16-3.27)
  - Pain score increase ≥2: HR 1.22 (95% CI 0.82-1.81)
  - MED increase ≥50%: HR 0.26 (95% CI 0.07-0.95)
  - Local failure: HR 0.18 (95% CI 0.02-1.47)
- 📊 Median OS 6.7 mo in both arms; no OS difference
- 📊 Toxicity: no between-arm differences reported
  - G3 fatigue 9.9% SBRT vs 5.1% MFRT (P=.37)
  - Dermatitis 1.2% vs 2.5% (P=.62)
  - Fracture 1.2% vs 0% (P=.99)
- ⚠️ Superiority claims rest on per-protocol analyses: 44 of 81 vs 35 of 79 analyzed at 3 mo
- ⚠️ Prophylactic dexamethasone recommended for SBRT arm only, biasing early pain response
- ⚠️ Comparator is 30 Gy/10, not 8 Gy single fraction
- ⚠️ 16 Gy assigned to smaller lesions, so dose effect is confounded by lesion size

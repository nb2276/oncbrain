# 2026-07-01

**RTOG 1112: adding SBRT to sorafenib in locally advanced HCC gives mOS 15.8 vs 12.3mo, HR 0.77 (90% CI 0.59-1.01), 1-sided P=.06.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Both items are RT-defining but neither settles its question. RTOG 1112 is the first randomised phase 3 of liver SBRT and misses its prespecified 1-sided OS test (P=.06) against a comparator (sorafenib) that IO-doublets have since displaced, though PFS is clean (HR 0.55). Head/neck reirradiation gets 31 consensus statements, with elective nodal irradiation rejected at 100% agreement.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

Personalized 5-fraction SBRT (27.5-50 Gy, adapted to liver function) in a 74% macrovascular-invasion population: the PFS signal holds, the OS read is hostage to an obsolete systemic backbone.

### RTOG 1112 · [[NCT01730937]]

> mOS 15.8 vs 12.3mo, HR 0.77 (90% CI 0.59-1.01, 1-sided P=.06) adding SBRT to sorafenib in locally advanced HCC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-attributable signal is PFS: HR 0.55 (0.40-0.75), a 3.7mo median gain, in a cohort 74% macrovascular-invasion positive where intrahepatic failure drives death. Personalized 27.5-50 Gy/5fx added no excess grade 3+ toxicity (47% vs 42%, P=.52), so the open decision is whether SBRT layers onto modern IO doublets, not whether it is deliverable.

- 📊 SBRT + sorafenib vs sorafenib alone

  | Endpoint | SBRT + sorafenib | Sorafenib | Effect size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Median OS (unadjusted) | 15.8 mo (90% CI 11.4-19.2) | 12.3 mo (90% CI 10.6-14.3) | HR 0.77 (90% CI 0.59-1.01), 1-sided P=.06 |
  | OS adjusted for stratification | n/a | n/a | HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.52-0.99), 2-sided P=.04 |
  | Median PFS | 9.2 mo (95% CI 7.5-11.9) | 5.5 mo (95% CI 3.4-6.3) | HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.40-0.75), P<.001 |
  | TR grade 3+ AEs | 39/83 (47%) | 37/88 (42%) | P=.52 |
  | Improved QoL at 6mo | 6/17 (35%) | 2/20 (10%) | not reported |

- 🔍 Personalized SBRT 27.5-50 Gy in 5 fractions, dose adapted to liver function
- 🔍 Phase 3, 1:1, N=193 randomized / 177 eligible, accrual April 2013 to March 2021
- 🔍 Stratified by performance status, liver function, degree of metastases, macrovascular invasion
- 📊 Macrovascular invasion in 131/177 (74.0%), the population most likely to fail intrahepatically
- ⚠️ Primary OS endpoint not met on the prespecified 1-sided test (P=.06); the P=.04 is the adjusted analysis
- ⚠️ Accrual stopped early after SOC systemic therapy changed; sorafenib is no longer a first-line comparator
- 🔗 Comparator vintage is the core limitation: IMbrave150-era atezo/bev and durva/treme now define 1L, and SBRT was never tested against them
- ⚠️ QoL denominators are tiny (17 and 20 evaluable pts at 6mo); read as hypothesis-generating, not a QoL result
- 📊 Treatment-related deaths: 2 with sorafenib (1 liver failure), 1 with SBRT + sorafenib (lung infection)

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 39699905]] **Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy vs Sorafenib Alone in Hepatocellular Carcinoma: The NRG Oncology/RTOG 1112 Phase 3 Randomized Clinical Trial.** — Dawson; Winter; Knox et al.. *JAMA oncology* (2025-02). [doi:10.1001/jamaoncol.2024.5403](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2024.5403)
  > IMPORTANCE: Most patients with locally advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) recur within the liver following systemic therapy.
  > OBJECTIVE: To determine whether stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) improves outcomes in patients with locally advanced HCC compared with sorafenib alone.
  > DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This multicenter phase 3 randomized clinical trial randomized patients with HCC 1:1 to sorafenib or SBRT followed by sorafenib, stratified by performance status, liver function, degree of metastases, and macrovascular invasion. Eligible patients had HCC unsuitable for or refractory to standard local-regional therapies and were candidates for first-line systemic therapy. Data were collected from April 2013 to March 2021, and data were analyzed from July 2022 to August 2023.
  > INTERVENTION: Personalized SBRT, 27.5 to 50 Gy in 5 fractions.
  > MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary end point was overall survival (OS). Secondary end points were progression-free survival (PFS), adverse events, and quality of life.
  > RESULTS: Of 193 patients randomized, 177 were eligible. Accrual was stopped early due to a change in standard-of-care systemic therapy. Of 177 included patients, 150 (84.7%) were male, and the median (IQR) age was 66 (60-72) years. Macrovascular invasion was seen in 131 (74.0%). As of July 1, 2022, the median OS was 12.3 months (90% CI, 10.6-14.3) with sorafenib vs 15.8 months (90% CI, 11.4-19.2) following SBRT and sorafenib (hazard ratio [HR], 0.77; 90% CI, 0.59-1.01; 1-sided P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.06). Adjusting for stratification factors, OS was improved with SBRT (HR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.52-0.99; 2-sided P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.04). Median PFS was improved from 5.5 months (95% CI, 3.4-6.3) with sorafenib to 9.2 months (95% CI, 7.5-11.9) with SBRT and sorafenib (HR, 0.55; 95% CI, 0.40-0.75; 2-sided P&#x2009;<&#x2009;.001). Treatment-related grade 3 or higher adverse events were seen in 37 of 88 (42%) and 39 of 83 (47%) of patients treated with sorafenib vs SBRT and sorafenib, respectively (P&#x2009;=&#x2009;.52). There were 2 treatment-related deaths in the sorafenib group (death not otherwise specified and liver failure) and 1 in the SBRT and sorafenib group (lung infection). At 6 months, improved quality of life was seen in 2 of 20 (10%) and 6 of 17 (35%) of patients treated with sorafenib and SBRT and sorafenib, respectively.
  > CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: In this phase 3 randomized clinical trial, among patients with locally advanced HCC, SBRT was associated with a clinically important but not statistically significant improved overall survival compared with sorafenib alone.
  > TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: [[NCT01730937]].

## 👄 Head & Neck

Opinion, not outcome: the reirradiation statements that most change practice (RPA III eligibility, 66 Gy definitive dose, SBRT dose, cord/brainstem limits) are exactly the ones that stalled at moderate consensus.

### ReCOG/ESTRO/ASTRO HNSCC Reirradiation Consensus

> 31 statements, 24 (77%) at high consensus (≥85%); elective nodal irradiation not recommended, 100% agreement.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The operational cut is volume, not phase: ≤25 cc RPA II recurrences take IMRT or SBRT interchangeably (88%), >25 cc take IMRT (88%), and postoperative takes IMRT over SBRT (94%). Elective nodal irradiation is off at 100% agreement. Larynx/hypopharynx SBRT carries the explicit caution.

- 🔍 Expert-driven clinical practice statement, single formal voting round, 17-panellist international ReCOG panel, fixed denominator (no opinion retained)
- 📐 Consensus thresholds prespecified: high ≥85%, moderate 70-84%, low <70%; evidence levels 1-4 adapted from Oxford CEBM
- 📊 Statements reaching 100% agreement (evidence level in parentheses)
  - Review previous plan to separate in-field recurrence from marginal failure (3)
  - No reirradiation for recurrence <6mo from previous RT, except urgent palliation (2)
  - Postoperative reirradiation for RPA class I with positive margins or ECE (2)
  - Elective nodal irradiation not recommended: no OS gain, more toxicity (3)
  - Carotid encasement >180° and previous ulceration elevate blowout risk (3)
  - Mandible ORN risk rises with cumulative EQD2 >114-119 Gy (3)
- 📊 Dose corridors by technique / setting (consensus %, evidence level)

  | Setting | Recommended dose | Consensus | Evidence |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Definitive IMRT | 66 Gy | moderate (82%) | 4 |
  | Postoperative IMRT | 60 Gy | high (94%) | 3 |
  | SBRT | ~40 Gy in 5 fractions | moderate (76%) | 3 |
  | Definitive CTV margin | GTV + 5 mm | high (94%) | 4 |
  | Hyperfractionation | option, no clear gain | moderate (76%) | 3 |

- 📊 Technique choice by RPA class and GTV volume (consensus %)

  | Scenario | Statement | Consensus |
  |---|---|---|
  | Postoperative | Normofractionated IMRT preferred over SBRT | high (94%) |
  | RPA II, ≤25 cc / cT1-T2 | IMRT or SBRT, similar outcomes | high (88%) |
  | RPA II, >25 cc | IMRT superior to SBRT | high (88%) |
  | Larynx / hypopharynx unresected | SBRT with caution, severe toxicity risk | high (94%) |
  | Selected OAR-limited cases | Proton therapy can be considered | high (88%) |
  | <25 cc oral cavity / oropharynx | Brachytherapy valid, expert centres | high (88%) |

- 📐 Cumulative OAR constraints, all in EQD2 (α/β 2 for late tissues)
  - Carotid: avoid >120-125 Gy (94%, level 3)
  - Spinal cord D0·1 cc ≤50 Gy, brainstem ≤55 Gy (70%, level 4)
  - Cord/brainstem surface ~60 Gy acceptable case-by-case (94%, level 4)
  - Recovery factors 10-25% only with caution and documentation (88%, level 4)
- 🔗 RPA classes from Ward: 2-yr OS 61·9% (I, n=91), 40·0% (II, n=230), 16·8% (III, n=81)
- 🔗 Caudell 505-pt cohort drives the dose statement: 2-yr OS 49·3% (≥66 Gy) vs 34·2% (60·0-65·9) vs 30·4% (<60 Gy)
- 🔗 Vargo 2018 IMRT vs SBRT in RPA II: 2-yr OS 35·4% vs 18·6%, p<0·001; no difference when SBRT ≥35 Gy/5 fx to ≤25 cc
- ⚠️ 7 of 31 statements only moderate, and they cluster on the decisions that actually matter: RPA III eligibility, 66 Gy reference dose, SBRT dose, cord/brainstem limits
- ⚠️ Single voting round, no iterative Delphi; agreement is opinion measured against opinion, not an outcome
- ⚠️ Most dose-accumulation and delineation statements sit at evidence level 4 (expert consensus / extrapolation)
- ⚠️ Pooled toxicity across 39 studies (3766 pts): G3+ acute 32%, late 29%, carotid blowout up to 4%
- ⚠️ Nasopharynx and rare histologies excluded; no randomised data on sequencing reirradiation vs first-line IO

# 2026-10-25

**SARON: consolidation RT to all sites after induction SACT in synchronous oligometastatic driver-negative NSCLC, mOS 31.0 vs 28.0 mo, HR 0.99 (0.63-1.55); PFS improved, magnitude unreported.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> SARON adds a null OS readout to LCT on a chemo backbone. It randomised after induction, so it doesn't test upfront RT. Skin: four uncontrolled single-institution HDR mould/flap series (LC 84.9-98% where reported), and an ABS survey shows no dosimetric consensus. Bladder: reTURBT in ypT0 pts declining RC, 24-mo EFS 87.6%, in a self-selected cohort.

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

HDR surface brachy for facial and pinna BCC/SCC: high LC across retrospective and prospective single-institution series, heterogeneous dose/fractionation, no randomised comparator vs EBRT or surgery.

### HDR brachytherapy with custom-made surface molds for facial skin carcinomas

> 5y actuarial LC 98% overall (99% primary, 87% recurrent) in 136 facial BCC/SCC treated with HDR Ir-192 surface molds, no severe late complications.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT read is geometry plus fractionation: a custom PMMA mold held dose at 5 mm depth within 5% of prescription over curved facial surfaces, with 180 cGy fractions to 6000-6500 cGy and no severe late effects. Whether that conformality keeps its late-toxicity profile with fewer, larger fractions was not tested here.

- 🔍 Prospective single-institution series (Barcelona), 136 consecutive facial BCC/SCC, treated 1992-1997
- 🔍 Min f/u 12 mo; median f/u not reported in source
- 🔍 Population
  - 102 BCC (75%), 34 SCC (25%)
  - 73 primary (54%), 63 recurrent after prior surgery/electroresection/Mohs (46%)
  - Mean age 67 (range 23-91); 84 women (62%)
  - Gross tumor in place for all but small lesions; margins positive in 128
  - 29 (21%) ≤2 cm, 93 (69%) 2-5 cm, 14 (10%) >5 cm
  - No nodal or distant mets at diagnosis
- 💊 RT technique
  - HDR Ir-192 (Microselectron); 117 custom PMMA molds, 19 Brock applicators
  - Mold: 5 mm PMMA bolus layer, 3-7 parallel catheters 5-10 mm apart
  - 6000-6500 cGy in 33-36 fx of 180 cGy, prescribed at 5 mm depth
  - Lesions ≥4 cm: boost 1800 cGy/10 fx after 3-wk rest, total 7500-8000 cGy
  - 5 mm lead sheet in mold near the eye; TLD + autoradiograph QA before treatment
- 📊 Local control by group

  | Group | N | Local recurrences | 5y actuarial LC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All pts | 136 | 3 | 98% |
  | Primary tumors | 73 | 1 | 99% |
  | Recurrent tumors | 63 | 2 | 87% |
  | Previously untreated >4 cm | 16 | 1 | 91% |

- 📊 All pts complete responders; no regional or distant metastases
- 📐 Dose variation at 5 mm depth within 5% of prescription; TLD agreed with plan within 5%
- 📊 Good/excellent cosmesis by timepoint (SOMA-LENT)

  | Timepoint | Good/excellent cosmesis |
  |---|---|
  | 2 wk | 38% |
  | 3 mo | 92% |
  | 6 mo | 98% |

- 📊 Acute: erythema G1 in 117, G2 in 19; G1 ulceration at 6 wk in 14 (10%), all healed by 4 mo
- 📊 No severe late complications; unfavorable cosmesis confined to recurrent tumors >4 cm
- 🔗 Authors' cited comparators (from source)
  - Lovett 1990 EBRT series (339 pts): LC 95% superficial X-ray, 77% electrons, 66% megavoltage photons
  - Lovett: overall complication rate 5.8% (18/310), incl. soft tissue and bone necrosis
  - Daly Ir-192 interstitial implants (165 pts): LC 96%, severe late effects 18%
  - Ashby 1989 wax/paraffin radon molds (642 pts): LC 96.8%
- ⚠️ No comparator arm; cross-series comparisons are era- and selection-confounded
- ⚠️ Recurrent-tumor 87% LC reported at 5y in Results but at 4y in Discussion
- ⚠️ Lesion size reporting internally inconsistent (mean 3.8 cm, stated range 3.8-8.0 cm)
- ⚠️ 33-36 daily fractions (~7 wk) is a heavy treatment burden for an elderly skin population

### Fractionated HDR brachytherapy moulds for carcinoma of the pinna

> 13 pts with superficial pinna carcinoma treated with HDR moulds, 45 Gy/8 fx: complete resolution in all, no recurrences at minimum 18 mo f/u.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable parameter is ultrasound-guided mould height: measuring tumour depth (0.84 mm in one case) lets the 80% isodose cover disease while leaving cartilage outside, which electrons cannot do for lesions thinner than 12 mm. Full-thickness cases needed a sandwich mould at reduced dose (42.5 Gy), so cartilage sparing is the selection criterion.

- 🔍 Single-institution series (Christie Hospital), 13 pts treated up to July 1996
- 🔍 Histology
  - BCC: 9 pts
  - SCC: 3 pts
  - Intraepidermal carcinoma: 1 pt
- 🔍 Lesions confined to pinna skin; dermal ultrasound confirmed no cartilage invasion in all but one
- 💊 Single-plane moulds: 45 Gy surface dose in 8 fx over 5 days, HDR microselectron Ir stepping source
- 🔍 Schedule: 1 fx Mon and Fri, 2 fx/day (≥6 h gap) on the three days between
- 🔍 Mould height chosen so ultrasound-measured tumour depth sits inside the 80% isodose
- 📐 80% isodose falls at 1.5 to 3.5 mm depending on source height; gradient about 10% per mm
- 💊 One double-plane sandwich mould (full-thickness pinna, 10 mm plane separation): dose reduced to 42.5 Gy
- 📊 Complete tumour resolution in all pts; no recurrences, no late RT complications at minimum 18 mo
- 📊 Acute reaction: moderate erythema confined to high-dose area, settled by first f/u visit at 6 wk
- ⚠️ No actuarial LC, no cosmesis scoring tool, no per-pt f/u reported
- ⚠️ Highly selected small cohort; authors state comparison with other modalities is difficult
- ⚠️ BED comparison table (Table 2) values cannot be tied to rows in extracted source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Fractionated high dose rate brachytherapy moulds – a precise treatment for carcinoma of the pinna** — Ernest Allan; Anthony Stanton; David Pye et al.. *Radiotherapy and Oncology* (1998)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/1998-allan-fractionated-high-dose-rate-brachytherapy-moulds-precise.pdf|Fractionated HDR brachytherapy moulds for carcinoma of the pinna (full text)]]

> - Local control vs surgery or electrons in matched pts

### Skin surface brachytherapy practice-pattern survey

> 16 ABS respondents: gross-disease margin 3-15mm (median 5mm), doses 30 Gy/5fx to 64 Gy/32fx (EQD2 40-65 Gy), no consensus on dosimetric constraints.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable read is pretibial dosing: 10 respondents (63%) dropped to 2-3 Gy per fraction there, and eight reported severe toxicity on the lower extremity. With max skin dose ranging 125-150% and fractionation cut points spanning 2-5 cm, a reader building a skin HDR program has a variance map, not a constraint set.

- 🔍 30-question electronic survey of American Brachytherapy Society clinician members
- 🔍 ~400 invitations, 16 fully completed surveys returned; 1 incomplete survey excluded
- 🔍 Aggregate experience ranged 8 to 1800 cases; >100-case subgroup analyzed separately
- 📊 Answers did not differ between experienced and less experienced respondents (p=0.1539)
- 🔍 Planning parameters reported (BCC / cSCC)
  - Lesion depth estimated by ultrasound pretreatment: 60%
  - Max depth of prescription: median 5 mm (range 1 to 8 mm)
  - Gross-disease margin: range 3-15mm, median 5mm
  - Catheters placed 5 to 10 mm from skin surface by most; none directly on skin
  - Wider CTV margin by histology: 70%; by lesion size: 65%
- 📐 Dosimetric parameters endorsed for plan evaluation

  | Parameter | All respondents | >100 cases in career |
  |---|---|---|
  | Max dose at skin surface | 13 (81%) | 7 (78%) |
  | Min dose to CTV | 8 (50%) | 3 (33%) |
  | Depth of 90% isodose line | 6 (38%) | 3 (33%) |
  | Min dose to skin surface | 5 (31%) | 4 (44%) |
  | V150 | 4 (25%) | 2 (22%) |
  | D95 | 2 (13%) | 1 (11%) |

- 📐 Max allowable skin dose 125-150% (median 135%); CTV coverage 90-100% (median 95%)
- 💊 Pretibial skin: 10 respondents (63%) used 2-3 Gy per fraction
- 📊 Fractionation cut point by lesion diameter: median 3 cm, range 2-5 cm
- ⚠️ Eight respondents reported severe acute/late toxicity on lower extremity, especially pretibial skin
- ⚠️ Avoidance triggers cited: tumor depth over 3 mm, perineural invasion, prior RT
- ⚠️ 16 of ~400 responded; self-selected, no outcomes data behind the 'safe' conclusion

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Skin surface brachytherapy: A survey of contemporary practice patterns** — Likhacheva, Anna O.; Devlin, Phillip M.; Shirvani, Shervin M. et al.. *Brachytherapy* (2017-01). [doi:10.1016/j.brachy.2016.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brachy.2016.10.006)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2017-likhacheva-skin-surface-brachytherapy-survey-contemporary-practice.pdf|Skin surface brachytherapy practice-pattern survey (full text)]]

### CT-based flap brachytherapy for facial non-melanoma skin cancers

> 1-yr KM local control 92% after HDR flap brachy 40 Gy/8 fx twice weekly for T1 facial BCC/SCC; no chronic G≥3 toxicity.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetry is the transferable part: median V100 85% and D90 92% with catheters 5 mm off the skin, against tumors up to 7 mm thick, so deep-margin coverage is the weak point. Three of 4 recurrences were nose, eyelid and lip, the irregular sites the flap is meant to solve.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review, 2007-2018; 50 pts, 53 lesions
- 🔍 Pts selected mostly for inability to tolerate or refusal of surgery
- 🔍 Population
  - Histologically confirmed BCC 35 (66%) or SCC 18 (34%), AJCC 8th T1
  - Median age 80 (73-101)
  - Median tumor size 18 mm (5-20), thickness 5 mm (3-7)
  - Area H 40 (75%), nose most common site 17 (32%)
  - 48 (91%) primary, 5 (9%) recurrent (3 post-op, 2 post-PDT)
- 💊 RT technique
  - 40 Gy/8 fx twice weekly (BED 60 Gy, α/β 10), Ir-192 HDR
  - CTV = visible tumor + 5 mm, no PTV margin
  - Catheter flap: channels 5 mm from surface, 10 mm apart; thermoplastic mask fixation
  - 5 mm intranasal / under-eyelid / gingivolabial spacer for OAR sparing
- 📐 Median PTV 1.7 cc (95% CI 0.9-2.3), D90 92% (82-99), V100 85% (80-92)
- 📊 1-yr KM local control 92%; 4 local recurrences (2 nasal SCC, 1 eyelid BCC, 1 lip BCC)
- 📊 Good/excellent cosmesis with complete remission in 49 lesions (92%)
- 📊 Toxicity

  | Toxicity | n (%) |
  |---|---|
  | Acute G1 (erythema) | 32 (60%) |
  | Acute G2 | 19 (35%) |
  | Acute G3 (moist desquamation) | 3 (5%) |
  | Chronic G1 at 6 mo | 5 (10%) |
  | Chronic G1 hypopigmentation at 12 mo | 2 (4%) |
  | Chronic G2 telangiectasia at 12 mo | 2 (4%) |

- 📊 All 8 eyelid/periorbital pts developed G1 conjunctivitis, resolved with 2-day antibiotic/steroid drops
- ⚠️ Median f/u 15 mo (range 6-27): too short for BCC late recurrence or late skin effects
- ⚠️ Median V100 85% sits below the stated goal of ≥95% PTV coverage
- ⚠️ Internal inconsistencies: abstract calls the 3 acute events G2, Results G3; Discussion claims no chronic G≥2 despite 2 G2 telangiectasias
- 🔗 Within the 87%-100% LC range of prior retrospective HDR surface-mold series cited by the authors

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Computed tomography-based flap brachytherapy for non-melanoma skin cancers of the face** — Gogineni, Emile; Cai, Haocheng; Carillo, Dawn et al.. *Journal of Contemporary Brachytherapy* (2021). [doi:10.5114/jcb.2021.103587](https://doi.org/10.5114/jcb.2021.103587)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2021-gogineni-computed-tomography-based-flap-brachytherapy-non.pdf|CT-based flap brachytherapy for facial non-melanoma skin cancers (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Durability of local control and late cosmesis beyond 2 years

> - Surface HDR brachytherapy vs Mohs or EBRT head to head

### Surface mould brachytherapy for skin cancers: British Columbia Cancer experience

> 2-yr LC 84.9%, CR 96.8%, no G3+ toxicity with CT-planned Ir-192 HDR surface mould in 65 mostly head-and-neck skin lesions.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning detail is the transferable part: prescription at 0.5 cm with no PTV left median V100 at 73.7% and surface dose 126.5%. LC was 88.9% with V90 >95% vs 76.9% below (p=0.85), a hint that coverage matters, but too few events to set a V90 target.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review, 2010-2017, 65 lesions in 59 pts
- 🔍 Median age 82 (range 45-97); median f/u 8 mo
- 🔍 Histology: BCC 54% (n=35), SCC 31% (n=20); also melanoma, lentigo maligna, Merkel, cutaneous NHL, 1 keloid
- 🔍 7 lesions treated with palliative intent, included in analysis
- 📊 Outcomes (whole cohort)

  | Endpoint | Estimate |
  |---|---|
  | 2-yr local control | 84.9% |
  | 2-yr PFS | 71.5% |
  | 2-yr OS | 77.6% |
  | Complete response (2-4 mo) | 96.8% (61 lesions) |
  | Local recurrence | 5 pts |

- 🔍 RT technique: Ir-192 HDR, custom Aquaplast + superflab mould, catheters ~1 cm apart
  - Most common: 40 Gy/10 fx (48.2%, n=28); range 15 Gy/3 to 60 Gy/30 fx
  - Prescribed at 0.5 cm depth; intent 90% dose covering wired target
  - CTV from radio-opaque wire, no PTV expansion
  - Median target volume 12.85 cc (range 0.63-130.1 cc)
- 📐 Achieved dosimetry (medians)
  - Surface dose 126.5%
  - V90 92.3%, V95 84.6%, V100 73.7%
  - Median dose to target volume 108.9%
- 📊 Univariate 2-yr LC by dosimetry and histology (log-rank, none significant)

  | Variable | Group | 2-yr LC (%) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | V90 | <95% vs >95% | 76.9 vs 88.9 | 0.85 |
  | Median target dose | <100% vs >100% | 75 vs 88.2 | 0.94 |
  | Histology | BCC vs SCC | 70 vs 88.2 | 0.94 |

- 📊 Toxicity (CTCAE v4.03), no G3-5 acute or late
  - Acute G2 dermatitis 21 (32%), G2 ulceration 5 (8%)
  - Acute G1 dermatitis 38 (59%), pruritus 27 (41%)
  - Late G2 necrosis 1 (2%), at ~10 mo
  - Late G1 hypopigmentation 10 (15%), fibrosis 5 (8%)
- ⚠️ 2-yr actuarial estimates rest on a median 8 mo f/u; late toxicity likely undercounted
- ⚠️ BCC LC 70% vs SCC 88.2% runs against expectation; 3 of 5 recurrences were nasal BCC
- 🔗 vs Gauden et al: 36 Gy/12 fx, 236 lesions, LC 98% at median 7 mo f/u

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Surface Mould Brachytherapy for Skin Cancers: The British Columbia Cancer Experience** — Casey, Stephanie; Awotwi-Pratt, Joseph; Bahl, Gaurav. *Cureus* (2019-12). [doi:10.7759/cureus.6412](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.6412)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2019-casey-surface-mould-brachytherapy-skin-cancers-british.pdf|Surface mould brachytherapy for skin cancers: British Columbia Cancer experience (full text)]]

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

Randomised OS null for consolidative RT in induction-responsive synchronous oligomet NSCLC; PFS signal unquantified in source.

### SARON

> mOS 31.0 vs 28.0 mo, HR 0.99 (0.63-1.55), adding consolidation RT to SACT in synchronous oligometastatic driver-negative NSCLC; PFS improved, magnitude not given.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The PFS gain from consolidation RT did not carry into OS (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.63-1.55), and QoL and AEs did not differ. RT here was comprehensive: primary (+/- nodes) by conventional RT or SABR plus SABR/SRS to every met. That frames the consolidation decision for non-progressors after induction as disease control at no measured toxicity cost, not survival.

![Randomised phase II schema: N=200 registration, N=134 randomised 1:1 after 6-8 weeks induction SACT.](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVd0giYWEAAC9az.jpg)
*Randomised phase II schema: N=200 registration, N=134 randomised 1:1 after 6-8 weeks induction SACT.*

- 📊 PFS improved with consolidation RT; no effect size reported in source
- 📊 No significant difference in QoL or AEs (per presenter tweet); no rates reported in source
- 🔍 Randomised phase II, 1:1, minimisation-stratified
- 🔍 Stratification factors
  - Histology
  - N-stage
  - Number of metastases
  - Brain metastases
  - SACT
  - Recruiting site
- 🔍 N=200 registered → 6-8 wk induction SACT → N=134 randomised
- 🔍 Eligibility
  - NSCLC without driver mutation
  - Primary (+/- nodes) suitable for radical RT or SABR
  - 1-5 synchronous mets in up to 3 organs, treatable by SABR/SRS
  - Brain mets allowed only with ≥1 extracranial met
  - PS 0-1, PET-CT and brain CT/MRI staged
  - Randomised only if CR/PR/SD and PS 0-2 after induction; PD or PS ≥3 withdrawn
- 🔍 RT arm: conventional RT or SABR to primary (+/- nodes) plus SABR/SRS to all mets
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation not reported in source
- ⚠️ Primary endpoint not identified in source; PFS magnitude absent
- ⚠️ Schema lists N=134 randomised; OS at-risk table shows 70 per arm at time 0. Unresolved in source
- ⚠️ Randomisation after induction selects non-progressors; does not test upfront RT
- 🔗 vs Gomez (Lancet Oncol 2016), Iyengar (JAMA Oncol 2018): prior LCT benefit on chemo backbone; SARON OS null
- 🔗 Consistent with Elamin (ESMO 2025), Iyengar (ASCO 2024): no LCT benefit for oligo-persistent disease on chemo-IO

## 💧 Bladder

Bladder preservation via reTURBT after neoadjuvant IO/ADC regimens. In pts who decline RC, residual disease at reTURBT separates the good outcomes from the poor ones.

### Bladder preservation after neoadjuvant/perioperative therapy in MIBC (reTURBT cohort)

> ypT0Nx at reTURBT after neoadjuvant therapy: 24-mo EFS 87.6%, OS 96.7% in MIBC pts declining RC (n=54); non-pCR pts fared far worse.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Chemo-RT here was only salvage for residual tumor or recurrence, so this tests a bladder-sparing route that skips upfront RT. The split is stark: non-pCR at reTURBT gave 24-mo OS 50.4% vs 82.5% after RC, so residual disease at reTURBT is the point where definitive local therapy (RC or chemoRT) should be on the table.

# 2026-05-20

**LS-SCLC phase 3: 54 Gy/30 fx BID SIB vs 45 Gy BID, mOS 60.7 vs 39.5 mo, HR 0.55 (0.37–0.72), p=0.003; similar toxicity, DSMB stopped at interim.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Thoracic led: 54 Gy BID SIB improved OS (HR 0.55) and local PFS (HR 0.51) in LS-SCLC without added G3–4 oesophagitis or pneumonitis, stopped at interim. In breast, exploratory pooled data show tamoxifen cut 15-yr IBR to 11.4% vs 19.0% in unirradiated good-risk DCIS; retrospective proton PMRT capsular contracture HR 1.76 lost significance on MVA (P=.083).

## 🎀 Breast

Two questions next to the RT decision, both from non-randomised comparisons: tamoxifen as the lever when good-risk DCIS skips RT, and whether PBS proton PMRT adds implant capsular contracture over IMRT.

### Pencil beam scanning proton vs IMRT photon PMRT: capsular contracture risk

> Proton vs photon PMRT CC HR 1.76 (0.93-3.32, P=.083) on MVA; 2-yr CC 50% with protons plus DTI.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The proton excess persists within both reconstruction types (2-yr CC 23% vs 12% after TE/I, 50% vs 35% after DTI) even though the adjusted HR crossed 1. Dose, fractionation and superficial constraints are not reported in source, so the PBS planning lever behind it is unknown. It bears on proton selection for implant pts.

- 🔍 Retrospective IRB-approved cohort, 2 centers within a single institution, Jan 2017 to Dec 2023
- 🔍 N=175: 89 PBS proton, 86 IMRT photon PMRT
- 🔍 Subpectoral 2-stage TE/I or DTI reconstruction; TE irradiated in all TE/I pts
- 🔍 Median age 49 (range 24-78); 63% Hispanic
- ⚠️ Cohorts imbalanced on reconstruction type (TE/I vs DTI, P<.001) and laterality (P<.001)
- 📐 Factors associated with capsular contracture (Cox)

  | Comparison | Model | HR (95% CI) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Proton vs photon | Univariate | 2.3 (1.26-4.30) | .007 |
  | Proton vs photon | Multivariable | 1.76 (0.93-3.32) | .083 |
  | DTI vs TE/I | Multivariable | 3.0 (1.7-5.5) | <.001 |

- 📊 2-yr CC rate by modality and reconstruction type (overall P<.001)

  | Reconstruction | Proton | Photon |
  |---|---|---|
  | DTI | 50% (n=36) | 35% (n=15) |
  | TE/I | 23% (n=53) | 12% (n=71) |

- 📐 Median f/u 42 mo proton vs 47 mo photon
- 📐 CC by Kaplan-Meier; Cox results checked with binary logistic regression
- ⚠️ PMRT dose, fractionation and superficial dose constraints not reported in source abstract
- ⚠️ Photon DTI stratum only n=15; that 2-yr estimate is imprecise
- ⚠️ CC definition and grading scale not stated in source abstract

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 42009110]] **Does Pencil Beam Scanning Proton Therapy Impart a Higher Risk of Capsular Contracture Compared With Intensity Modulated Photon Radiation Therapy in the Postmastectomy Reconstruction Setting?** — Zerey; Gal; Feenstra et al.. *International journal of radiation oncology, biology, physics* (2026-04). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.04.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.04.011)
  > BACKGROUND: Postmastectomy radiation therapy (PMRT) may cause adverse events in the reconstruction setting. Proton-based PMRT is increasingly used and has been shown to improve cardiac and pulmonary dosimetry. Data on the risk of capsular contracture (CC) with proton versus photon PMRT remain scarce. We compared the CC rate of the largest cohort of patients undergoing reconstruction after pencil beam scanning proton PMRT reported to date with an intensity modulated radiation therapy photon cohort, hypothesizing that the proton cohort would have a higher rate of CC.
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: An institutional review board -approved retrospective study was conducted on patients with breast cancer who underwent subpectoral 2-stage tissue expander/implant (TE/I) or direct-to-implant (DTI) breast reconstruction and received either pencil beam scanning proton or intensity modulated radiation therapy photon PMRT between January 2017 and December 2023 at 2 centers within a single institution. All patients undergoing TE/I had the TE irradiated. CC rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Cox proportional hazards analysis, denoted as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs, was used to assess variables potentially associated with the outcome, and a binary logistic regression model was used to verify the results.
  > RESULTS: The study cohort comprised 175 patients (89 proton; 86 photon). The median age was 49 years (range, 24-78), 63% were Hispanic. Patient demographics were well balanced between the groups, except in tumor laterality (P < .001) and reconstruction type (TE/I vs DTI; P < .001). The median follow-up was 42 and 47 months for the proton and photon groups, respectively. In a multivariable analysis, DTI patients had a significantly higher risk of CC compared with TE/I patients (HR, 3.0; 95% CI, 1.7-5.5; P < .001). Proton patients had a higher risk of developing CC compared with the photon group in univariate analysis (HR, 2.3; 95% CI, 1.26-4.30; P = .007), although this effect did not reach statistical significance in the multivariable model (HR, 1.76; 95% CI, 0.93-3.32; P = .083). The 2-year CC rate for patients treated with protons and DTI (n = 36), photons and DTI (n = 15), protons and TE/I (n = 53), and photons and TE/I (n = 71) was 50%, 35%, 23%, 12%, respectively (P < .001). No other factors were significantly associated with CC development.
  > CONCLUSION: In this contemporary large proton versus photon PMRT cohort, patients treated with proton showed a trend toward an increased risk of CC. Patients undergoing DTI who were treated with protons had the highest risk of CC (50%). Careful consideration of reconstruction and radiation therapy modalities, assessing CC risk, and also involving patient input, is important for treatment selection.

### NRG/RTOG 9804 + E5194 combined analysis (tamoxifen after lumpectomy, DCIS)

> 15-yr IBR 11.4% vs 19.0% with vs without tamoxifen in good-risk DCIS treated by lumpectomy without RT; invasive IBR HR 0.43.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The association was confined to invasive events (DCIS-IBR p=0.089), which made up 65 of 117 IBRs. For the RT-omission discussion, the 11.4% 15-yr IBR on tamoxifen has no RT arm in this analysis to benchmark against, and which good-risk subgroups drive it sits in Fig 3, with subgroup HRs not in source text.

- 🔍 Exploratory pooled analysis: non-RT arm of NRG/RTOG 9804 (n=317) + E5194 good-risk cohort (n=561)
- 🔍 Good-risk: low/intermediate grade DCIS, ≤2.5 cm, excision margins ≥3 mm, no RT
- 💊 Tamoxifen optional in both trials: 43.1% overall, 65.6% RTOG 9804 vs 30.3% E5194
- 🔍 Median f/u 14.85 yr (13.87 RTOG 9804, 16.15 E5194); Fine-Gray models
- 📊 IBR outcomes by tamoxifen use (117 IBRs: 65 invasive, 52 DCIS)

  | Endpoint | Tamoxifen | No tamoxifen | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Any IBR, 15-yr estimate | 11.4% (7.9-15.5) | 19.0% (15.3-22.9) | p=0.001 |
  | Any IBR, multivariable | n/a | n/a | HR 0.54 (0.35-0.83), p=0.0045 |
  | Invasive IBR | not reported | not reported | p=0.0048; MVA HR 0.43 (0.24-0.77), p=0.0042 |
  | DCIS-IBR | not reported | not reported | p=0.089 |

- ⚠️ Baseline imbalance: tamoxifen users skewed lower-risk

- 📄 [[PMID 42114785]] **Impact of Tamoxifen Only After Lumpectomy for "Good-Risk" Duct Carcinoma In Situ: Combined Analysis of the NRG Oncology/RTOG 9804 and ECOG-ACRIN E5194 Trials.** — Wright; Moughan; Woodward et al.. *International journal of radiation oncology, biology, physics* (2026-05). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.04.103](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.04.103)
  > PURPOSE: The NRG Oncology/Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) 9804 trial randomized patients with "good-risk" ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) to radiation (RT) or no RT following lumpectomy. The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group-American College of Radiology Imaging Network E5194 trial had a comparable cohort observed without RT. Tamoxifen use was optional in both trials. This ancillary exploratory analysis combining both data sets assessed the effect of tamoxifen on ipsilateral breast recurrence (IBR) in "good-risk" DCIS treated with lumpectomy alone.
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: A combined database from the non-RT arm of NRG/RTOG 9804 and the "good-risk" cohort from E5194 (low- or intermediate-grade, &#x2264;2.5 cm, excision margins &#x2265;3 mm) was created, and distributions of patient and DCIS characteristics by tamoxifen use were compared by &#x3c7;2. IBR, invasive IBR, DCIS-IBR, contralateral breast event, and overall survival were estimated, and distributions were compared between tamoxifen use groups. Univariate and multivariable Fine-Gray regression models were used to analyze factors that may be associated with endpoints.
  > RESULTS: Eight hundred and seventy-eight patients were analyzed (317 from NRG/RTOG 9804 and 561 from E5194). The use of tamoxifen overall was 43.1% (65.6% in NRG/RTOG 9804 and 30.3% in E5194). At a median follow-up of 14.85 years, there were 117 IBRs (65 invasive and 52 DCIS). There was a significant association for reduced IBR with tamoxifen use (P = .001); estimated 15-year IBR with tamoxifen is 11.4% (95% CI, 7.9-15.5) and without is 19.0% (15.3-22.9). Tamoxifen use was significantly associated with reduced invasive IBR (P = .0048) but not DCIS-IBR (P = .089). On multivariable analysis, patients who received tamoxifen were 46% less likely to have any IBR (hazard ratio, 0.54; 95% CI, 0.35-0.83; P = .0045), and 57% less likely to have invasive IBR (hazard ratio, 0.43; 95% CI, 0.24-0.77; P = 0.0042).
  > CONCLUSION: For patients with "good-risk" DCIS treated with lumpectomy without RT, tamoxifen use was significantly associated with a reduction in IBR overall and invasive IBR, not DCIS-IBR.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Unlike QD escalation in CONVERT and CALGB 30610/RTOG 0538, involved-field BID SIB escalation to 54 Gy improved OS; the 39.5 mo control arm beats prior 45 Gy BID arms, so transfer depends on selection (PET-staged, ECOG 0–1, age ≤70).

### High-dose hyperfractionated SIB RT vs standard-dose RT for limited-stage SCLC · [[NCT03214003]]

> mOS 60.7 vs 39.5 mo, HR 0.55 (0.37–0.72), p=0.003 with 54 Gy/30 fx BID SIB vs 45 Gy/30 fx BID in LS-SCLC; toxicity similar.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Escalation is a GTV/IGTV-only SIB (PTV held at 45 Gy) within the same 30 fx, 3-week BID course on PET-defined involved fields. Local PFS separated (HR 0.51) while MFS did not (HR 0.81), fitting an in-field effect. The decision it moves is BID dose, 45 vs 54 Gy, not BID vs once daily.

- 🔍 Open-label randomised phase 3, 16 hospitals in China, N=224 (108 vs 116), median f/u 46 mo
- 🔍 54 Gy/30 fx SIB to GTV/IGTV (PTV 45 Gy) vs 45 Gy/30 fx, both BID VMAT over 3 wks
- 🔍 Involved field only: PET-CT-positive primary + nodes, no elective nodal irradiation
- 💊 Concurrent cisplatin or carboplatin + etoposide × 4, TRT started 0–42 days after cycle 1
- 📊 Efficacy, ITT (54 Gy vs 45 Gy)

  | Endpoint | 54 Gy SIB | 45 Gy | HR (95% CI), p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Median OS (primary) | 60.7 mo (49.2–62.0) | 39.5 mo (27.5–51.4) | 0.55 (0.37–0.72), p=0.003 |
  | 2-yr OS | 76% | 54% | p=0.001 |
  | Median PFS | 30.5 mo (14.5–46.5) | 16.7 mo (10.7–22.7) | 0.70 (0.49–0.99), p=0.044 |
  | Median local PFS | Not reached | 40.6 mo (22.8–58.4) | 0.51 (0.33–0.78), p=0.002 |
  | Median MFS | 40.2 mo (26.6–53.8) | 23.8 mo (10.1–37.5) | 0.81 (0.57–1.16), p=0.248 |
  | DCR | 97% | 93% | p=0.218 |

- 📊 Acute G3–4 toxicity, as-treated

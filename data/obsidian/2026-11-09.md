# 2026-11-09

**ASCO advanced prostate imaging guideline: NGI should be offered for rising PSA post-RP when salvage RT is contemplated (rec 4.4), its sole high-quality, strong recommendation.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Guidance-heavy day: four consensus documents and a case-based guide, no new trial data. Prostate carried most: ASCO ties next-generation imaging to a live salvage decision, and the ESTRO ACROP Delphi reached consensus on only 14/40 items (35%) for intraprostatic salvage SBRT, leaving dose and fractionation divided. Gyn adds ABS HDR schedules with EQD2 goals and ESGO/ESTRO/ESP universal MMR/MSI testing.

## 🌰 Prostate

All guidance, no trials: imaging selection before salvage, salvage SBRT for intraprostatic relapse after RT, and a 40 Gy/5fx primary SBRT worked example.

### Radiosurgery Society SBRT guide for localized prostate cancer

> 40 Gy/5fx to prostate + proximal 1cm SV as worked example; large gland and poor baseline IPSS framed as counselling issues, not SBRT contraindications.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning detail is the transferable part: 40 Gy prescribed to the 100% isodose covering PTV with V105% (42 Gy) held under 20-30%, versus robotic practice at the 75-85% isodose. Large glands and baseline IPSS ≥15 are treated as counselling and prophylaxis problems, since PACE-B enrolled only 5% of pts at ≥80 mL.

- 🔍 Case-based guide endorsed by the Radiosurgery Society (Pract Radiat Oncol 2024); two modified real-world cases, not a trial
- 🔍 Scope: dose/fractionation, OAR constraints, dose escalation, rectal spacer, ADT with SBRT, high-risk, large prostate, IBD, MRI guidance
- 💊 Case A: 78yo, unfavorable intermediate-risk, cT1cN0M0, PSA 8.1, 112 mL gland on MRI
  - Leuprolide started 2 mo pre-SBRT; gland 61 mL on pre-SBRT MRI; 6 mo ADT total
  - Hydrogel spacer, VMAT, every-other-day delivery
  - 40Gy/5fx to 100% isodose covering PTV, V105% (42 Gy) < 20-30%
  - Acute GU flare managed with doubled tamsulosin + ibuprofen; IPSS 10 at 1 yr
- 💊 Case B: MRI-guided SBRT, 40 Gy/5fx with SIB 42 Gy/5fx to dominant intraprostatic lesion, hip prosthesis
- 📐 Prescription practice varies: 100% isodose covering 95-99% of target vs robotic 75-85% isodose (Dmax 130-140%)
- 🔗 PACE-B: only 5% of pts had prostate volume ≥80 mL, so large-gland SBRT data are thin
- 📊 Large glands: one retrospective series flagged ≥50 mL as predictor of late G2+ GU toxicity; others show favorable toxicity at ≥50-100 mL
- 📊 Baseline IPSS ≥15 with prophylactic α1-antagonists: low G3 GU rates in retrospective series
- ⚠️ Guide advises against neoadjuvant ADT solely for cytoreduction, yet Case A received it (for UIR disease)
- ⚠️ Large-gland, high-IPSS and prior-TURP guidance rests on retrospective series and extrapolation
- ⚠️ Excerpt truncated: Case 2 discussion, spacer, ADT and dose-escalation sections not in source text

### ESTRO ACROP Delphi: salvage SBRT for intraprostatic relapse

> Consensus on 14/40 items (35%) for intraprostatic relapse after RT: focal GTV-based CTV, no concurrent ADT; dose, fractionation, OAR limits unresolved.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The panel converged on the target, not the dose: CTV = mpMRI GTV plus an adaptive margin, prescribed to an isodose. Salvage dose relative to primary, fractionation and cumulative OAR constraints stayed unresolved (dosimetry Kendall's W 0.12), so a re-RT prescription still has to come from the retrospective series and Pasquier's ongoing dose-finding phase I/II.

- 🔍 3-round Delphi, Jul 22 to Dec 16, 2019; 40 questions across 3 sections
- 🔍 Panel: 18 of 33 contacted salvage SBRT authors (54.5%); all completed every round
- 🔍 Consensus defined as >80% agreement; major agreement 65-80%; divided <65%
- 📊 Consensus on 14/40 (35%); consensus or major agreement on 27/40 (68%)
- 📊 Agreement by questionnaire section

  | Section | Questions | Consensus | Kendall's W | Read |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Patient selection | 19 | 6/19 (32%) | 0.17 | Weak |
  | Imaging + biopsy | 7 | 3/7 (43%) | 0.34 | Moderate |
  | Dosimetry (CTV + OARs) | 14 | 5/14 (36%) | 0.12 | Very weak |

- 🔍 Item-level positions

  | Item | Agreement level | Responders |
  |---|---|---|
  | Age not a selection criterion | Consensus | >80% |
  | ECOG 0-1 | Consensus | 94% |
  | Prior ADT not a contraindication | Consensus | Not stated |
  | Metastatic workup before salvage | Consensus | All experts |
  | Choline PET for staging | Consensus | 89% |

  | CTV = mpMRI GTV + adaptive margin | Consensus | Not stated |

- ⚠️ Discussion cites a 5-6 fx schedule to ~35 Gy, yet results list fractionation as divided
- ⚠️ Rounds closed before most PSMA-PET detection data were published; dated imaging section
- ⚠️ Only 2 respondents outside Europe (both US); 12/18 (67%) at centres treating >2,000 pts/yr
- 🔗 vs Uro-GEC-ESTRO salvage BT Delphi: that panel split on target volume; this one agreed on focal GTV-based CTV

### ASCO guideline: imaging for advanced prostate cancer

> NGI offered only when salvage or a change in care is live. 4.4 (salvage RT contemplated after RP) is the sole high-quality, strong recommendation.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Only one recommendation is graded high quality and strong: NGI when salvage RT is contemplated after RP (4.4). The same post-RP scenario without salvage candidacy gets 'should not be offered' (4.3). Supporting data are management shifts (Habl: RT plan changed in 59%), so NGI earns its place by redrawing the salvage field, not by finding untreatable disease.

- 🔍 ASCO expert panel with SAR, ACR, SNMMI, AUA, ASTRO, SUO; systematic review 2013 to August 2018
- 🔍 Evidence base: 35 studies (17 systematic reviews, 18 primary research articles)
- 🔍 NGI defined: PSMA (where available), 11C-choline or 18F-fluciclovine PET/CT, PET/MRI, whole-body MRI, 18F-NaF PET/CT
- 🧭 NGI recommendation by clinical scenario (negative conventional imaging unless stated)

  | Scenario | Rec | NGI | Evidence · strength |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Rising PSA post-RP, not salvage candidate or unwilling | 4.3 | Should not be offered | Low · moderate |
  | Rising PSA post-RP, salvage RT contemplated | 4.4 | Should be offered | High · strong |
  | Rising PSA post-RT, salvage not planned | 4.5 | Should not be offered, except IRB trial | Intermediate · moderate |
  | Rising PSA post-RT, salvage local/regional therapy contemplated | 4.6 | Supported; could guide management | Intermediate · moderate |
  | Hormone-sensitive, metastatic on conventional imaging | 4.7 | Potential role to clarify burden | Intermediate · moderate |
  | Nonmetastatic CRPC | 4.8 | Only if change in care contemplated | Weak · moderate |
  | Metastatic CRPC, PSA progression | 4.9 | Conventional imaging; PSA alone not reason to change therapy | Intermediate · strong |
  | Metastatic CRPC, suspected subclinical mets | 4.10 | Could be contemplated, especially in a trial | Insufficient · weak |
  | Metastatic CRPC, radiographic progression | 4.11 | Not routinely offered | Insufficient · moderate |

- 📊 Habl: 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT changed the RT plan in 59%, staging in 43%
- 📊 Albisinni: 76% (99 of 131) had a treatment plan change from 68Ga results
- 📊 Pooled 68Ga-PSMA detection: 50% at PSA <0.5 vs 95% at PSA >2 ng/mL
- ⚠️ Support is mostly detection and management change, not outcome after NGI-directed therapy
- ⚠️ Search closed August 2018, before randomized imaging-guided salvage RT data
- ⚠️ Recommendations for suspected high-risk / initial staging not in source excerpt

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Optimum Imaging Strategies for Advanced Prostate Cancer: ASCO Guideline** — Trabulsi, Edouard J.; Rumble, R. Bryan; Jadvar, Hossein et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2020-06). [doi:10.1200/jco.19.02757](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.19.02757)
  > PURPOSE Provide evidence- and expert-based recommendations for optimal use of imaging in advanced prostate cancer. Due to increases in research and utilization of novel imaging for advanced prostate cancer, this guideline is intended to outline techniques available and provide recommendations on appropriate use of imaging for specified patient subgroups. METHODS An Expert Panel was convened with members from ASCO and the Society of Abdominal Radiology, American College of Radiology, Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging, American Urological Association, American Society for Radiation Oncology, and Society of Urologic Oncology to conduct a systematic review of the literature and develop an evidence-based guideline on the optimal use of imaging for advanced prostate cancer. Representative index cases of various prostate cancer disease states are presented, including suspected high-risk disease, newly diagnosed treatment-naïve metastatic disease, suspected recurrent disease after local treatment, and progressive disease while undergoing systemic treatment. A systematic review of the literature from 2013 to August 2018 identified fully published English-language systematic reviews with or without meta-analyses, reports of rigorously conducted phase III randomized controlled trials that compared ≥ 2 imaging modalities, and noncomparative studies that reported on the efficacy of a single imaging modality. RESULTS A total of 35 studies met inclusion criteria and form the evidence base, including 17 systematic reviews with or without meta-analysis and 18 primary research articles. RECOMMENDATIONS One or more of these imaging modalities should be used for patients with advanced prostate cancer: conventional imaging (defined as computed tomography [CT], bone scan, and/or prostate magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) and/or next-generation imaging (NGI), positron emission tomography [PET], PET/CT, PET/MRI, or whole-body MRI) according to the clinical scenario.

## 🌷 Gynecologic

Two reference documents: ABS HDR fractionation and dose goals, plus ESGO/ESTRO/ESP molecular testing and Lynch surveillance (its RT recommendations not in source excerpt).

### ESGO/ESTRO/ESP endometrial carcinoma guidelines

> ~3% of endometrial carcinomas are Lynch-related; the guideline advises MMR IHC or MSI testing in all EC regardless of histology (III, B) and four-test molecular classification.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The molecular classification that adjuvant decisions now lean on is valid only when all four tests are run: ~5% of carcinomas are multiple classifiers, and POLEmut requires a pathogenic exonuclease-domain variant. The guideline's adjuvant RT allocations fall past the truncation point of this excerpt, so the excerpt does not show which RT decision each molecular group moves.

- 🔍 Joint ESGO/ESTRO/ESP update of the 2014 European consensus conference
- 🔍 Evidence base: systematic MEDLINE review, Jan 2014 to Jun 2019, English only
- 🔍 Grading: adapted IDSA-USPHS system, evidence levels I-V × grades A-E
- 🔍 191 external reviewers scored relevance and feasibility; patients reviewed qualitatively
- 🧭 Graded recommendations captured in the source excerpt

  | Recommendation | Level, grade |
  |---|---|
  | Multidisciplinary tumor board planning of staging + treatment | V, A |
  | Treatment in a specialized gyn-onc center, esp. high-risk/advanced | V, A |
  | MMR IHC (+ MLH1 promoter methylation if MLH1/PMS2 loss) or MSI in all EC, any histotype | III, B |
  | Genetic counseling for EC pts at increased Lynch risk | III, B |
  | Lynch EC surveillance generally from age 35, tailored to mutation + family history | IV, B |
  | Annual TVUS + annual or biennial endometrial biopsy until hysterectomy | IV, B |
  | Hysterectomy + BSO at completion of childbearing, preferably before age 40 | IV, B |

- 📊 Lynch cumulative cancer incidence at 70 years by mutated gene

  | Gene | Endometrial carcinoma | Ovarian cancer |
  |---|---|---|
  | MLH1 | 34% | 11% |
  | MSH2 | 51% | 15% |
  | MSH6 | 49% | 0% |
  | PMS2 | 24% | 0% |

- 🔗 Gene-specific surveillance start ages cited (Ryan et al)
  - MSH2: from age 30
  - Nontruncating MLH1: from age 35
  - MSH6 and truncating MLH1: from age 40
  - Heterozygous PMS2: gynecological surveillance not warranted
- 📊 Occult malignancy at prophylactic surgery in Lynch/HNPCC up to 17% (retrospective, Lachiewicz et al)
- 🔍 Molecular classification needs all four tests: p53, MSH6, PMS2 IHC + POLE exonuclease-domain sequencing
- ⚠️ Single surrogate test insufficient: multiple classifiers occur in ~5% of carcinomas
- ⚠️ Source excerpt truncated before adjuvant treatment section; RT recommendations not captured

### ABS gynecologic HDR brachytherapy fractionation compendium

> Reference HDR schedules with EQD2 for cervix, endometrium and vagina. Cervix HR-CTV D90 goal 85-95 Gy EQD2(10); no cuff regimen ranked above another.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Point A schedules are now only a starting point. The working goal is HR-CTV D90 85-95 Gy EQD2(10) from EMBRACE, and the EMBRACE-derived limits (rectum D2cc ≤65 Gy, bladder ≤80 Gy) are tighter than the ABS 70-75 / 80-90 Gy. The cited complication signal above 7 Gy/fx limits how freely 3 x 8 or 2 x 9 Gy should be used.

- 🔍 ABS Gyn Task Force compendium built from prior ABS consensus, the literature and members' clinical experience
- ⚠️ No single optimal schedule is endorsed for any site; higher dose/fx with fewer fx is flagged as raising toxicity
- ⚠️ Complication signal cited above 7 Gy/fx; prior ABS ceiling was 7.5 Gy/fx with at least 4 fx
- 📐 Intact cervix, tandem-based
  - Point A schedules after EBRT 25 x 1.8 Gy: HDR 4 x 7, 5 x 6, 6 x 5, 5 x 5.5 Gy
  - Point A doses are pre-optimization starting points for volume-based planning
  - 3 x 8 and 2 x 9 Gy also used internationally
  - International trial: better LC with 4 x 7 Gy than with 2 x 9 Gy
  - Complete EBRT + brachy in <7-8 wk
  - Vienna series: hybrid IC/IS needed in ~45% of pts to reach the D90 goal
- 📊 Target dose goals by setting

  | Setting | Target | Goal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Intact cervix, image-guided | HR-CTV D90 | 85-95 Gy EQD2(10); 90-95 for larger tumors |
  | Cuff brachy alone, postop | Vaginal surface | 60-65 Gy LDR-equivalent (traditional) |
  | Cuff boost after pelvic EBRT | Vaginal surface, EBRT + brachy | 65-70 Gy; 70-75 Gy if superficial + margin |
  | Vaginal recurrence, uterine primary | Vaginal lesion | ≥75 Gy EQD2 |
  | Inoperable stage I uterine, HDR alone | Uterine CTV / GTV | 48-62.5 / 80-90 Gy EQD2(10) |
  | Inoperable stage I uterine, EBRT + HDR | Uterine CTV / GTV | 65-75 / 80-90 Gy EQD2(10) |
  | Vaginal interstitial | CTV | 70-75 Gy distal vagina, near vulva or RV septum; 80-85 Gy poor EBRT response |

- 📐 OAR D2cc limits: ABS vs EMBRACE-derived

  | Structure | ABS | EMBRACE-derived |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rectum D2cc EQD2(3) | 70-75 Gy | ≤65 Gy |
  | Sigmoid D2cc EQD2(3) | 70-75 Gy | Not stated |
  | Bladder D2cc EQD2(3) | ~80-90 Gy | ≤80 Gy |
  | Rectovaginal point | Not stated | ≥65 Gy EQD2(3) correlated with vaginal stenosis |

- 📊 Postop vaginal cuff brachy alone: most common regimens in the latest practice survey

  | Regimen | Prescription point | Survey frequency |
  |---|---|---|
  | 7 Gy x 3 | 0.5 cm depth | Most common |
  | 6 Gy x 5 | Vaginal surface | Second |
  | 5.5 Gy x 4 | 0.5 cm depth | Next most common |
  | 5 Gy x 5 | 0.5 cm depth | Next most common |
  | 7.5 Gy x 5 | Vaginal surface | Last of those listed |

- 📐 Cuff boost after EBRT (ABS consensus)

  | EBRT | HDR boost |
  |---|---|
  | 45 Gy/25 fx | 5-6 Gy x 3 (to surface) |
  | 50.4 Gy/28 fx | 6 Gy x 2 (to surface) |

- 🔍 Interstitial technique
  - Vaginal lesions >0.5 cm thick at brachy go interstitial
  - Cervix IC/IS: BID ~6 h apart; 9 fx over 4.5 days with one insertion
  - Vienna: 7 Gy x 4 to HR-CTV over two procedures
  - 6 Gy x 6 over two insertions also published with acceptable results
  - Needle loading kept to ≤10-20% of total dwell time
- 🔗 Sorbe RCT, n=290: 2.5 vs 5.0 Gy x 6 at 0.5 cm; equal outcomes, more vaginal shortening at the higher dose
- ⚠️ Sorbe enrolled low-risk stage IA G1-2 only; its high-dose arm exceeds currently accepted 5 mm-depth doses
- ⚠️ A 0.5 cm-depth prescription on a 2.5 cm cylinder needs modification to match 3 cm-cylinder surface dose

# 2026-11-09

**ASCO imaging guideline: NGI should be offered when salvage RT is contemplated after RP (evidence high, strong); withhold it when no salvage local therapy is planned.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> A guidance-only day, no trial readouts. Prostate carried most: ASCO ties NGI to a salvage RT decision, an ESTRO ACROP Delphi reached consensus on only 14/40 salvage SBRT items, and an RSS case guide covers intact-prostate SBRT. Gyn: ABS HDR compendium keeps a cervix HR-CTV D90 goal of 85-95 Gy EQD210; ESGO/ESTRO/ESP backs universal MMR/MSI testing.

## 🌷 Gynecologic

ABS HDR fractionation reference plus the ESGO/ESTRO/ESP endometrial update; the endometrial source text cuts off before the adjuvant RT grades.

### ESGO/ESTRO/ESP endometrial carcinoma guidelines

> Joint European guideline update: universal MMR IHC or MSI testing in all EC (III, B); molecular classification needs all four surrogate tests.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The molecular classification is valid only with all four tests (p53, MSH6, PMS2, POLE exonuclease domain), since ~5% of tumors carry multiple classifiers and a single surrogate misassigns them. That classification is reported as prognostic most clearly in high-grade and high-risk tumors, the group where adjuvant decisions are made; the RT recommendations themselves fall outside the captured excerpt.

- 🔍 Joint ESGO/ESTRO/ESP update of the 2014 European consensus on endometrial carcinoma
- 🔍 MEDLINE systematic review, Jan 2014 to June 2019, English only
- 🔍 Grading uses an adapted IDSA-USPHS system: levels of evidence I-V, grades A-E
- 🔍 191 external reviewers scored each recommendation for relevance and feasibility
- 📊 Key recommendations captured in source, with level and grade

  | Recommendation | Level, grade |
  |---|---|
  | Multidisciplinary tumor board planning of staging and treatment | V, A |
  | Treatment in a specialized center, esp. high-risk or advanced stage | V, A |
  | MMR IHC (+ MLH1 promoter methylation) or MSI in all EC, any histology | III, B |
  | Genetic counseling if increased Lynch risk | III, B |
  | Lynch endometrial surveillance generally from age 35, tailored | IV, B |
  | Annual TVUS + annual or biennial biopsy until hysterectomy in Lynch carriers | IV, B |
  | Hysterectomy + BSO after childbearing, preferably before age 40 | IV, B |

- 📊 Lynch syndrome cumulative incidence at 70 years by gene

  | Gene | Endometrial carcinoma | Ovarian cancer |
  |---|---|---|
  | MLH1 | 34% | 11% |
  | MSH2 | 51% | 15% |
  | MSH6 | 49% | 0% |
  | PMS2 | 24% | 0% |

- 📊 ~3% of all EC and ~10% of MMRd/MSI EC are germline MMR-driven
- 🔍 Molecular classification needs all four tests: p53, MSH6, PMS2 IHC plus POLE exonuclease-domain sequencing
- ⚠️ A single surrogate test alone is insufficient: ~5% of carcinomas are 'multiple classifiers'
- ⚠️ Occult malignancy at prophylactic surgery up to 17% in one retrospective series; counsel before surgery
- ⚠️ Source excerpt truncates before the adjuvant treatment recommendations; RT-specific grades not reported in source text

### ABS compendium of gynecologic HDR brachytherapy fractionation

> Cervix HR-CTV D90 goal 85-95 Gy EQD210; no single optimal HDR scheme for any site; doses >7 Gy/fx flagged for higher complication risk.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The EMBRACE-derived OAR limits sit below the ABS ones (rectal D2cc ≤65 vs 70-75 Gy, bladder ≤80 vs about 80-90 Gy EQD23), and the panel says lower doses are recommended in practice. For cuff brachy the panel picks no scheme. It flags that 0.5 cm-depth prescriptions need modifying on a 2.5 cm cylinder to keep surface dose acceptable.

- 🔍 ABS Gynecologic Task Force report (2019): literature + prior ABS guidelines + member experience, consensus via conference calls
- 🔍 Not a systematic review; recommendations made only where the panel agreed
- ⚠️ Higher dose/fx with fewer fractions raises toxicity; >7 Gy/fx needs optimal implant geometry and OAR review
- 📊 Cervix EBRT + brachy course to be completed in <7-8 weeks for better local control and survival
- 📊 Target dose goals by setting (EQD210 unless stated)

  | Setting | Target | Dose goal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Intact cervix, image-guided | HR-CTV D90 | 85-95 Gy (90-95 Gy for larger tumors) |
  | Inoperable stage I uterine, HDR alone | Uterine CTV / GTV | 48-62.5 Gy / 80-90 Gy |
  | Inoperable stage I uterine, EBRT + HDR | Uterine CTV / GTV | 65-75 Gy / 80-90 Gy |
  | Post-op vaginal cuff boost after EBRT | Vaginal surface (LDR equivalent) | 65-70 Gy; 70-75 Gy for superficial positive margins |
  | Vaginal recurrence of uterine cancer | Vaginal lesion | at least 75 Gy EQD2 (LDR equivalent) |

- 📐 OAR D2cc limits: ABS vs EMBRACE-derived (EQD23)

  | OAR | ABS limit | EMBRACE-derived |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rectum/sigmoid D2cc | 70-75 Gy | ≤65 Gy (rectum) |
  | Bladder D2cc | approximately 80-90 Gy | ≤80 Gy |

- 📊 Vaginal cuff brachy alone: most common schemes in the ABS survey, in rank order

  | Rank | Regimen | Prescription point |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 7 Gy x 3 | 0.5 cm depth |
  | 2 | 6 Gy x 5 | Vaginal surface |
  | 3 | 5.5 Gy x 4 and 5 Gy x 5 | 0.5 cm depth |
  | 4 | 7.5 Gy x 5 | Vaginal surface |

- 💊 Vaginal cuff HDR after EBRT (ABS Table 4)

  | EBRT | HDR boost |
  |---|---|
  | 45 Gy/25 fx | 5-6 Gy x 3 (to surface) |
  | 50.4 Gy/28 fx | 6 Gy x 2 (to surface) |

- 🔗 Sorbe et al.: the only cited randomized cuff data, 290 pts, 2.5 Gy x 6 vs 5.0 Gy x 6 at 0.5 cm
- ⚠️ Sorbe arms had equal outcomes but more vaginal shortening at higher dose/fx; low-risk stage IA G1-2 only
- 🔗 Cervix: 3 x 8 Gy and 2 x 9 Gy are listed as alternatives; an international trial favored 4 x 7 Gy over 2 x 9 Gy for LC
- 🔍 Interstitial cervix: BID fractions about 6 h apart in one week; Vienna alternative 7 Gy x 4 over two procedures
- ⚠️ Hybrid IC/IS was needed in about 45% of Vienna pts to reach the D90 goal
- ⚠️ Vaginal stenosis correlated with rectovaginal reference point EQD23 ≥65 Gy (EMBRACE)

## 🌰 Prostate

Three guidance documents, all expert opinion or detection data rather than outcome data: NGI before salvage RT post-RP, the unresolved dose and constraint questions in salvage SBRT, and intact-gland SBRT in large or post-TURP prostates.

### Radiosurgery Society guide to localized prostate SBRT

> Case-based RSS guide to intact-prostate SBRT (≤5 fx): large gland, high baseline IPSS and prior TURP are not contraindications if OAR constraints are met.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The useful RT detail is the prescription spread: 100% isodose with V105% < 20-30% vs 75-85% isodose with Dmax 130-140%, which makes cross-center dose comparisons unreliable. On gland size, PACE-B had only 5% of pts ≥80 mL, so offering SBRT to large glands rests on retrospective data plus spacer and bladder filling.

- 🔍 Expert case-based guide endorsed by the Radiosurgery Society; two modified real-world cases, not a trial
- 🔍 Scope: dose/fractionation, OAR constraints, dose escalation, rectal spacer, ADT with SBRT, high-risk, large prostate, IBD, MR guidance
- 🔍 Case A: unfavorable intermediate-risk, cT1cN0M0, PSA 8.1, 112 mL gland on MRI
  - Neoadjuvant leuprolide 2 mo pre-SBRT → gland 61 mL
  - Hydrogel spacer, 40 Gy/5 fx QOD to prostate + proximal 1 cm SV, VMAT
  - 6 mo ADT total; IPSS 8 baseline → 10 at 1 yr, PSA ~0.15 after T recovery
- 🔍 Case B: MR-guided 40 Gy/5 fx with SIB 42 Gy/5 fx to DIL; hip prosthesis artifact less on MRI sim
- 📐 SBRT prescription conventions differ across centers

  | Approach | Prescription | Hot spot handling |
  |---|---|---|
  | 100% isodose | Covers CTV or PTV, goal 95-99% receiving Rx | Case A limited V105% (42 Gy) < 20-30% |
  | Robotic-style | 75-85% isodose covering PTV | Dmax 130-140% of Rx; inhomogeneity deliberately allowed |

- 🔗 Only 5% of PACE-B pts had prostate volume ≥80 mL, so large-gland data are thin
- ⚠️ One retrospective series found volume ≥50 mL predicted late and any G2+ GU toxicity; others reported favorable toxicity at ≥50-100 mL
- ⚠️ Authors advise against neoadjuvant ADT solely for cytoreduction, yet Case A used it for that gland
- 🔗 MIRAGE ([[NCT04384770]]) cited as randomized support for MR guidance lowering toxicity
- ⚠️ Prior TURP data mixed; PACE-B allowed these pts. Recommendations are expert opinion, source truncated

### ESTRO ACROP Delphi: salvage SBRT for intraprostatic relapse

> Consensus on only 14/40 items (35%) after 3 Delphi rounds; dose, fractionation, biopsy need and cumulative OAR constraints remain unresolved.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetric section, at Kendall's W 0.12, is where the panel agreed least: no consensus on salvage dose relative to primary, fractionation, or cumulative rectum/bladder limits. What it does fix is the target and sequencing: focal CTV of mpMRI GTV plus an adaptive margin, no concurrent ADT, and a 2-year minimum interval from primary RT.

- 🔍 3-round Delphi, Jul-Dec 2019; 18/33 invited salvage SBRT authors (54.5%), all completed every round
- 🔍 Consensus defined as >80% agreement; major agreement 65-80%; divided <65%
- 📊 Consensus by questionnaire section

  | Section | Consensus items | Kendall's W | Agreement |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Patient selection | 6/19 | 0.17 | Weak |
  | Imaging + biopsy | 3/7 | 0.34 | Moderate |
  | Dosimetry (CTV + OARs) | 5/14 | 0.12 | Very weak |

- 📊 Consensus reached on
  - Age not a selection criterion (>80%)
  - ECOG 0-1 (94%)
  - Prior ADT not a contraindication
  - CTV = mpMRI GTV + adaptive margin
  - No concomitant ADT with salvage SBRT
  - Phoenix definition valid for follow-up after re-RT
  - Primary RT dose factored into salvage dose
  - Metastatic disease excluded before salvage
- 📊 Major agreement: ≥2 yr minimum interval from primary RT; dose prescribed to the isodose
- 📊 Schedules in use: 5-6 fx up to about 35 Gy (30-35 Gy and >35 Gy most frequent)
- ⚠️ Divided opinion persisted on
  - Prior toxicity threshold: 56% exclude after G2+, rest after G3+
  - Biopsy always needed: only 22%
  - Salvage dose higher, lower or same vs primary
  - Fractionation schedule
  - Cumulative CTV and OAR dose constraints
  - Max T, Gleason and PSA for selection
- ⚠️ Imaging: choline PET 89% vs PSMA-PET 28%; rounds closed before most PSMA-PET data published
- ⚠️ Panel Europe-weighted: only 2 of 18 from outside Europe (US)

- 📄 **Salvage stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) for intraprostatic relapse after prostate cancer radiotherapy: An ESTRO ACROP Delphi consensus** — Jereczek-Fossa, Barbara A.; Marvaso, Giulia; Zaffaroni, Mattia et al.. *Cancer Treatment Reviews* (2021-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ctrv.2021.102206](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctrv.2021.102206)
  - 📎 [[papers/prostate/2021-jereczekfossa-salvage-stereotactic-body-radiotherapy-sbrt.pdf|ESTRO ACROP Delphi: salvage SBRT for intraprostatic relapse (full text)]]

### ASCO guideline: imaging for advanced prostate cancer

> NGI should be offered when salvage RT is contemplated after RP (evidence high, strong). Withhold it when no salvage local therapy is planned.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The only high-evidence, strong recommendation is NGI before contemplated salvage RT after RP (4.4), and Habl et al reported 59% of RT plans changed by 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT. Post-RT rising PSA without salvage intent gets the opposite call (4.5), so imaging should follow the salvage decision, not precede it.

- 🧭 ASCO guideline (JCO 2020), endorsed by ASTRO, AUA, SNMMI, ACR, SAR, SUO
- 🔍 Systematic review of literature 2013 to Aug 2018: 35 studies (17 systematic reviews ± M-A, 18 primary)
- 🔍 NGI defined as PSMA (68Ga/18F ligands), 11C-choline, 18F-fluciclovine PET/CT, PET/MRI, WB-MRI, 18F-NaF PET/CT
- 📊 NGI recommendations by disease state (Rec 4.3 to 4.11; 4.1 to 4.2 not in source excerpt)

  | Scenario | NGI recommendation | Evidence quality | Strength |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Rising PSA post-RP, neg conventional imaging, no salvage planned (4.3) | Should not be offered | Low | Moderate |
  | Rising PSA post-RP, salvage RT contemplated (4.4) | Should be offered | High | Strong |
  | Rising PSA post-RT, salvage not planned (4.5) | Should not be offered, except IRB trial | Intermediate | Moderate |
  | Rising PSA post-RT, salvage local/regional therapy contemplated (4.6) | Supported; may guide management | Intermediate | Moderate |
  | Hormone-sensitive, metastatic on conventional imaging (4.7) | Potential role to clarify burden | Intermediate | Moderate |
  | Nonmetastatic CRPC (4.8) | Only if care change contemplated | Weak | Moderate |
  | Metastatic CRPC, PSA progression (4.9) | Conventional imaging; PSA alone not reason to switch | Intermediate | Strong |
  | Metastatic CRPC, high suspicion, neg conventional imaging (4.10) | Could be contemplated, ideally trial | Insufficient | Weak |
  | Metastatic CRPC, radiographic progression on conventional imaging (4.11) | Not routinely offered | Insufficient | Moderate |

- 📊 Habl et al: 59% had RT plan changed and 43% stage change from 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT
- 📊 Albisinni et al: 76% (99 of 131) had treatment plan changed by 68Ga results
- ⚠️ Evidence base is detection and management-change data, not outcome data; no survival benefit shown for NGI-guided care
- ⚠️ Literature cutoff Aug 2018 predates most prospective PSMA PET data; PSMA listed only 'where available'
- ⚠️ Rec 4.7: NGI may shift intent from oligometastatic multimodality to systemic alone; stage migration risk for MDT selection

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Optimum Imaging Strategies for Advanced Prostate Cancer: ASCO Guideline** — Trabulsi, Edouard J.; Rumble, R. Bryan; Jadvar, Hossein et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2020-06). [doi:10.1200/jco.19.02757](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.19.02757)
  > PURPOSE Provide evidence- and expert-based recommendations for optimal use of imaging in advanced prostate cancer. Due to increases in research and utilization of novel imaging for advanced prostate cancer, this guideline is intended to outline techniques available and provide recommendations on appropriate use of imaging for specified patient subgroups. METHODS An Expert Panel was convened with members from ASCO and the Society of Abdominal Radiology, American College of Radiology, Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging, American Urological Association, American Society for Radiation Oncology, and Society of Urologic Oncology to conduct a systematic review of the literature and develop an evidence-based guideline on the optimal use of imaging for advanced prostate cancer. Representative index cases of various prostate cancer disease states are presented, including suspected high-risk disease, newly diagnosed treatment-naïve metastatic disease, suspected recurrent disease after local treatment, and progressive disease while undergoing systemic treatment. A systematic review of the literature from 2013 to August 2018 identified fully published English-language systematic reviews with or without meta-analyses, reports of rigorously conducted phase III randomized controlled trials that compared ≥ 2 imaging modalities, and noncomparative studies that reported on the efficacy of a single imaging modality. RESULTS A total of 35 studies met inclusion criteria and form the evidence base, including 17 systematic reviews with or without meta-analysis and 18 primary research articles. RECOMMENDATIONS One or more of these imaging modalities should be used for patients with advanced prostate cancer: conventional imaging (defined as computed tomography [CT], bone scan, and/or prostate magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) and/or next-generation imaging (NGI), positron emission tomography [PET], PET/CT, PET/MRI, or whole-body MRI) according to the clinical scenario.

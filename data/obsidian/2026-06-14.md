# 2026-06-14

**GEC-ESTRO 2026 APBI update: two-tier eligible/contraindicated scheme, low-risk now spans pTis-pT2 ≤30mm, pN1mi, all histologies except triple negative.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Breast carries the day, and it is a selection document rather than a result: GEC-ESTRO retires the 2010 intermediate-risk tier, so the pt who previously sat in the grey zone is now either an APBI candidate or not. Widened eligibility (pN1mi, multifocal within 2 cm, DCIS at 2 mm margins) with no reported IBTR figures behind the change.

## 🎀 Breast

Only breast data today: an APBI patient-selection update, not an efficacy readout.

### GEC-ESTRO Breast Cancer Working Group APBI patient selection recommendations

> Updated APBI selection criteria collapse to two groups (low-risk eligible, high-risk contraindicated), widening eligibility to pT1-2 ≤30mm, pN1mi, any histology.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The eligibility boundary moved, not the technique: pN1mi and multifocal disease within 2 cm are now inside the low-risk group, and the ceiling is 30 mm across all histologies. The 2010 intermediate tier is gone, so the contralateral question at consent is binary. BRCA 1-2 carriers are contraindicated regardless of age.

- 🔍 Systematic search 2010-2024 (PubMed/Medline/Scopus/Cochrane), 618 articles screened; 10 prospective RCTs + 7 retrospective comparative studies with ≥5yr median f/u informed the update
- 📊 2026 GEC-ESTRO APBI selection: low-risk (good candidate) vs high-risk (contraindicated)

  | Criterion | Low risk (APBI suitable) | High risk (APBI contraindicated) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Age | > 40 years | < 40 years |
  | Germline | not specified | BRCA 1-2 mutation |
  | T stage / size | pTis, T1-2 (≤ 30 mm) | > 30 mm |
  | Focality | unifocal or multifocal within 2 cm | multicentric |
  | Nodal status | pN0 or pN1mi | ≥ pN1a, or unknown axilla (pNx) |
  | Histology | all histology types | triple negative |
  | EIC | absent | EIC positive |
  | LVI | no extensive LVI | extensive LVI |
  | Margins | negative for invasive (≥ 2 mm for DCIS) | positive for invasive (< 2 mm for DCIS) |

- 🔍 Two-category scheme only: the 2010 three-tier low/intermediate/high split is gone, so an intermediate-risk pt is now either a candidate or not
- 📊 pN1mi is now inside the low-risk group; macrometastatic nodes (≥pN1a) and unknown axillary status stay contraindications
- 📊 Multifocality is permitted when foci sit within 2 cm; multicentric disease remains a contraindication
- 📊 DCIS is admitted to the low-risk group (pTis) with a 2 mm margin requirement, stricter than the invasive requirement of a negative margin
- ⚠️ Recommendations are evidence supplemented by expert opinion; no formal grading, voting, or panel-agreement figures reported in source text
- ⚠️ Triple negative is a blanket contraindication by receptor status alone, with no size or grade qualifier stated in source
- ⚠️ Source gives no effect sizes: no IBTR or survival rates by criterion are reported in the abstract or results text available

- 📄 [[PMID 42058323]] **Patient selection for accelerated partial breast irradiation (APBI) after breast-conserving surgery: Updated evidence-based recommendations of the Groupe Europ&#xe9;en de Curieth&#xe9;rapie-European Society for Therapeutic Radiology and Oncology (GEC-ESTRO) Breast Cancer Working Group.** — Polg&#xe1;r; Gutierrez-Miguelez; Ivanov et al.. *Clinical and translational radiation oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ctro.2026.101170](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctro.2026.101170). [article](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13122701/)
  > PURPOSE: To update recommendations on patient selection criteria for accelerated partial breast irradiation (APBI) based on available clinical evidence supplemented by expert opinions.
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: Between 2010 and 2024, a systematic search of the PubMed, Medline, Scopus and Cochrane database identified 618 articles using the keywords "accelerated partial breast irradiation" and "APBI". This search was complemented by reviewing the reference lists of articles and manual reviewing of relevant conference abstracts and book chapters. Of these, ten prospective randomized clinical trials and seven retrospective comparative studies with a minimum median follow-up time of five years were identified. The authors reviewed the clinical evidence published on APBI, supplemented it with relevant clinical and pathological studies on breast-conserving therapy, and then formulated the recommendations presented in this manuscript.
  > RESULTS: Based on published new clinical evidence, the GEC-ESTRO Breast Cancer Working Group recommends two categories as guidelines for selecting patients eligible for APBI: (1) low-risk group representing good candidates for APBI including patients ageing&#xa0;>&#xa0;40&#xa0;years with unifocal or multifocal within 2&#xa0;cm, pTis,T1-2 (&#x2264;30&#xa0;mm) pN0 or pN1mi, all histology types of breast cancer without the presence of an extensive intraductal component (EIC), without extensive lympho-vascular invasion (LVI) and with negative surgical margins for invasive tumors (&#x2265;2 mm for DCIS), (2) high-risk group, for whom APBI is considered contraindicated including patients with BRCA 1-2 mutations or ageing&#xa0;<&#xa0;40&#xa0;years; having positive margins for invasive tumor (<2 mm for DCIS), and/or multicentric or large (>30&#xa0;mm), and/or triple negative tumours, and/or EIC positive, and/or extensive lympho-vascular invasion (LVI) or macrometastatic positive lymph nodes (&#x2265;pN1a) or unknown axillary status (pNx).
  > CONCLUSIONS: Based on emerging clinical evidence, the 2010 GEC-ESTRO APBI patient selection criteria can be significantly expanded, meaning that in the future, more patients may receive APBI as a part of routine clinical practice.

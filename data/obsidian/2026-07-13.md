### EANO Consensus Statement on Radiation Necrosis

> Delphi consensus, 53/57 statements ≥80% agreement: perfusion MRI + amino acid PET to call RN, bevacizumab for steroid-refractory.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-actionable content is the dose-volume section, not the algorithm: EQD2 below ~40 Gy makes RN uncommon, single-fraction V12Gy ~10 cm³ carries 5-10% symptomatic necrosis, and re-irradiation stays under 10% to cumulative EQD2 100-110 Gy but reaches 25% above 130 Gy. Those numbers gate the fractionate-vs-single-fraction and re-irradiate-vs-not decisions.

- 🔍 Three-round Delphi, 20 experts (15 initial + 5 added in round 1), consensus threshold ≥80% on 5-point Likert
- 🔍 Panel included 3 radiation/clinical oncologists, 4 neuro-oncologists, 4 med oncs, 2 neurosurgeons, neuropathologist, neuroradiologist, nuclear medicine
- 📊 57 statements formulated across epidemiology-pathophysiology (n=8), causes (n=10), imaging (n=10), histopathology (n=9), management (n=20); 53 reached consensus
- 📐 Dose-volume thresholds for RN risk, verbatim from the causes section

  | Setting | Constraint | Reported RN risk |
  |---|---|---|
  | Normal brain, any RT | EQD2 (α/β 2 Gy) below ~40 Gy | RN uncommon |
  | Single-session SRS | V12Gy ~10 cm³ | 5-10% symptomatic necrosis |
  | Hypofx SRT, 3 fx | brain+target V20Gy <20 cm³ | <10% any necrosis/edema, <4% RN needing resection |
  | Hypofx SRT, 5 fx | brain+target V24Gy <20 cm³ | <10% any necrosis/edema, <4% RN needing resection |
  | Re-irradiation, recurrent GBM | cumulative EQD2 ~100-110 Gy | generally <10% |
  | Re-irradiation, recurrent GBM | cumulative EQD2 >130 Gy | up to 25% |
  | Repeat SRS after prior SRS or WBRT | diameter >2.0-3.0 cm higher risk | 7-16% symptomatic RN |

- 📊 Diagnostic performance by modality (pooled, from the imaging section)

  | Modality | Population | Sensitivity | Specificity |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | T1 CE MRI alone | WHO grade 3-4 glioma | 68% | 77% |
  | T1 CE MRI alone | Brain metastases | 79% | 76% |
  | DSC rCBV | Grade 3-4 glioma | 87-90% | 86-88% |
  | DSC rCBV | Brain mets (418 lesions, 12 studies) | 83% | 78% |
  | MRS Cho/NAA | Glioma (455 pts) | 88% | 86% |
  | MRS Cho/NAA | WHO grade 3-4 glioma (n=203) | 91% | 95% |
  | Amino acid PET | Brain mets relapse (~400 pts) | 82% | 84% |

- 📊 RN incidence 4-30% overall, most series reporting symptomatic RN up to 10%; 4-30% in irradiated gliomas, 5-24% in brain mets
- 🔍 Timing: 6-24 months post-RT typically, with delayed cases years later; lesion then plateaus 12-24 months on conventional MRI
- 💊 Steroid-to-bevacizumab trigger: steroid-dependent symptoms >4 weeks needing dexamethasone ≥8 mg/day (87.5% agreement)
- 💊 No preferred bevacizumab schedule: 5 mg/kg q2w, 7.5 mg/kg q3w, 400 mg fixed q2w, 600 mg fixed q3w all in use, no comparative evidence (87.5% agreement)
- 🔗 No prospective data show a difference in RN incidence for protons vs photons; end-of-range uncertainty and conformality remain the counterweight to reduced integral dose
- ⚠️ Increased RN risk flagged when SRS is combined with dual checkpoint blockade (CTLA-4 + PD-1) or antibody-drug conjugates
- ⚠️ Surgery statement cleared the bar by the narrowest margin in the management table (80.0% agreement); LITT reached 100% but only in round 3
- ⚠️ No Level 1 evidence anywhere in the document, and only symptom-driven treatment is endorsed: radiological findings alone do not justify treating
- ⚠️ No definition of radiological response after RN treatment (88.9% agreement), so response assessment in future RN trials has no agreed endpoint

**Sources:**
- 📄 **The European Association for Neuro-oncology (EANO) Consensus Statement on Radiation Necrosis** — Duerinck, Johnny; Van Den Bent, Martin; Brandal, Petter et al.. *Neuro-Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1093/neuonc/noag153](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noag153)
  > Abstract Introduction Radiation necrosis (RN) complicates neuro-oncological care, mimicking tumor recurrence and lacking high-level evidence for standardized management. Methods A European Association for Neuro-Oncology (EANO) expert panel utilized a three-round Delphi process to create a comprehensive expert opinion document based on the available current scientific evidence. A series of statements, derived from the published literature were created by the experts in each field. Consensus was defined as ≥ 80% agreement using a 5-point Likert scale. Results After three rounds among 20 experts that included adaptation of statements, the Delphi process reached a consensus (≥80% agreement) on 53 statements out of 57. RN occurs in 4% to 30% of patients, typically appearing 6 to 24 months after radiotherapy for primary (glial) or metastatic brain tumors. Experts identified perfusion MRI and amino acid PET as the most suitable imaging modalities for differentiation from tumor recurrence. While histopathology remains the gold standard, identifying viable tumor cells in irradiated gliomas is challenging due to overlapping cytological features with reactive glia. For symptomatic management, corticosteroids may be tried, and bevacizumab is recommended for corticosteroid-refractory cases, with evidence suggesting profound efficacy even at low doses. Surgery is considered effective for rapid symptom relief and definitive diagnosis in accessible lesions. Laser Interstitial Thermal Therapy (LITT) can be considered an additional treatment option for symptomatic RN. Conclusions Despite the absence of Level 1 evidence, these Delphi-survey-formulated recommendations provide actionable guidance for clinical practice. There is a need for prospective randomized trials focusing on symptomatic RN.

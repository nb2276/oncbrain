# 2026-10-26 — ESMO Congress 2026

**NRG-GY020: adding 1y pembro to adjuvant VBT ± EBRT did not improve RFS3 (73% vs 70%, p=0.68) in early-stage HIR dMMR EC; G3/4 tox 43% vs 15%.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> RT stays the adjuvant backbone in early-stage HIR dMMR endometrioid EC: pembro added toxicity, not RFS (sources conflict on the RT+pembro rate). EORTC 1420 Best-of: no 12-mo MDADI difference, TOS vs DO-IMRT in T1-2 N0-1 OPSCC/SGSCC (+3.9, p=0.326), more mucosal toxicity with RT. FCCSS: MLD 5-10 Gy → SPLC sHR 3.75 in childhood survivors.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Late-effects data from childhood RT: dose-dependent second primary lung cancer risk, with a proposed screening implication.

### FCCSS second primary lung cancer risk in childhood cancer survivors

> Mean lung dose 5-10 Gy carried sHR 3.75 (1.44-9.78) for second primary lung cancer; CIF 6.50% at age 60 vs 1.79% non-irradiated.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The risk peak sits at a modest MLD of 5-10 Gy, a band that covers only 5% of survivors yet 21% of cases, and among MLD >5 Gy survivors 66.7% of SPLCs were attributable to RT. That makes reconstructed MLD, not just a yes/no history of thoracic RT, the variable to carry into survivorship referral. Risk above 10 Gy is not reported in the source.

- 🔍 French Childhood Cancer Survivors Study: 7,006 5-year CCS, median f/u 34.1 yr (IQR 27.2-42.2)
- 🔍 Individual MLD reconstructed by phantom-based dosimetry; Fine-Gray competing-risk models
- 📊 46 SPLCs, median age at dx 50.3 yr; adenocarcinoma 44%
- 📊 SIR 2.26 (95% CI 1.65-3.01) vs French general population
- 📐 Multivariable SPLC risk factors (n=5,760 with smoking data; 42 SPLCs)

  | Factor | sHR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|
  | MLD 5-10 Gy | 3.75 (1.44-9.78) | p<0.01 |
  | Smoking | 2.57 (1.40-4.71) | p<0.01 |
  | Prior chemotherapy | Not significant | Not reported |

- 📊 MLD 5-10 Gy: 5% of cohort, 21% of SPLC cases
- 📊 Population attributable fraction of RT
  - MLD >5 Gy survivors: 66.7% of SPLC cases attributable to RT
  - Overall cohort: 23.9%
- ⚠️ Risk estimates for MLD bands >10 Gy not reported in source
- ⚠️ Screening benefit itself not tested; guideline proposal extrapolated from risk data

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Second primary lung cancer risk in childhood cancer survivors from the FCCSS cohort: Risk-adapted screening guidelines** — Pamela Abdayem. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 1149RO · Rapid oral: Prevention, early detection and intervention · 2026-10-26 · presented by Pamela Abdayem (Villejuif, France)
  > Background: Low-dose chest CT (LDCT) screening reduces lung cancer (LC) mortality in high-risk tobacco-exposed individuals. Prior thoracic irradiation is an additional risk factor for LC, but specific dosimetric predictors remain poorly characterized in adult childhood cancer survivors (CCS), limiting tailored screening strategies.
  > Methods: We analyzed 7,006 5-year CCS from the French Childhood Cancer Survivors Study (FCCSS). Second Primary LCs (SPLCs) were identified through multiple data sources with histopathological confirmation. Individual mean lung doses (MLD) were reconstructed using phantom-based dosimetry. Standardized incidence ratios (SIRs) were estimated compared to the French general population. Risk factors were assessed using multivariable Fine–Gray competing-risk models and adjusted for sex, age and period of diagnosis, and smoking status. Adjusted cumulative incidence (CIF) and population attributable fraction (PAF) for MLD >5 Gy were estimated.
  > Results: After a median follow-up of 34.1 years (IQR: 27.2–42.2), 46 SPLCs were identified (median age at diagnosis 50.3 years [IQR: 41.4–54.5]), predominantly adenocarcinomas (44%). Compared to CCS without SPLC, those with SPLC were more frequently survivors of lymphoma (28% vs 17%) and bone sarcoma (15% vs 9%) and were more likely to have undergone prior thoracic irradiation (41% vs. 19%). Overall, CCS had more than a two-fold higher risk compared with the general population (SIR 2.26; 95%CI: 1.65–3.01). Multivariable analyses were restricted to CCS with complete smoking data (n=5,760; 42 SPLCs). A MLD of 5-10 Gy was associated with the highest SPLC risk (sHR 3.75;[1.44–9.78]; p<0.01), accounting for 21% of cases despite representing only 5% overall. Their CIF reached 6.50% at age 60, vs 1.79% in non-irradiated survivors. Smoking was a significant risk factor (2.57 [1.40–4.71], p<0.01), while prior chemotherapy showed no significant association. Among survivors with MLD >5 Gy, 66.7% of SPLC cases were attributable to radiotherapy, vs 23.9% in the overall cohort.
  > Conclusions: These findings support integrating dosimetric thresholds into risk-adapted LDCT screening guidelines for CCS. Earlier initiation of LDCT screening should be considered for CCS.

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does LDCT screening reduce lung cancer mortality in irradiated childhood cancer survivors?
> - Optimal screening start age by mean lung dose band
> - Does SPLC risk persist with modern conformal or proton pediatric RT?

## 🌷 Gynecologic

Randomised phase III test of adding IO to the adjuvant RT backbone in dMMR early-stage endometrial cancer.

### NRG-GY020 · [[NCT04214067]]

> Adding 1y pembrolizumab to adjuvant VBT ± EBRT did not improve RFS3 (73% RT vs 70% RT+pembro, p=0.68) in early-stage HIR dMMR endometrioid EC; G3/4 toxicity 43% vs 15%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Raw recurrences ran 18% with RT alone vs 11% with RT+pembro, but the primary endpoint scored withdrawals as failures, and withdrawals clustered in the open-label pembro arm. For the RT reader, VBT ± EBRT stays the complete adjuvant answer for HIR dMMR disease; whether IO lowers recurrence on top of VBT remains untested at adequate power.

- 🔍 Randomized phase III open-label superiority trial, 2:1, N=168 (112 RT+pembro vs 56 RT), enrolled 2/2020-8/2022
- 🔍 Newly diagnosed early-stage HIR (GOG-249 criteria) dMMR endometrioid EC; stage IA 20%, IB 63%, II 17%; 82% grade 2/3
- 🩻 RT backbone identical in both arms
  - All pts received VBT
  - EBRT allowed only for stage II and IB grade 3
  - 70% VBT alone, 30% EBRT ± VBT
  - Dose/fractionation not reported in source
- 💊 Pembrolizumab 400 mg IV q6w × 1y, started within 7 days of RT
- 📊 Outcomes by arm, median f/u 45.5 mo

  | Endpoint | RT alone | RT + pembro | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | RFS3, primary (abstract) | 73% | 70% | Z=-0.48, p=0.68 |
  | RFS3, primary (OncLive report of presentation) | 73% | 77% | Z=0.508, P=.3057 |
  | KM RFS at 3y | 81% | 88% | HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.33-1.61), p=0.21 |
  | Recurrences | 10/56 (18%) | 12/112 (11%) | not tested |
  | 3y OS | 96% | 96% | no difference |
  | G3/4 toxicity | 15% | 43% | not tested |

- ⚠️ Sources conflict on the primary result: abstract 70% vs OncLive's presentation report 77% for RT+pembro; failure counts also differ
- ⚠️ Primary endpoint counts any off-study pt as a failure, which penalizes the more toxic open-label arm
  - Abstract: non-recurrence failures 5 (9%) RT vs 22 (20%) RT+pembro
  - OncLive: unknown-status failures 5 (9%) RT vs 14 (13%) RT+pembro
  - OncLive: consent withdrawals 3 RT vs 12 RT+pembro
- ⚠️ Small N for a phase III; KM HR CI 0.33-1.61 cannot exclude a meaningful recurrence benefit or harm
- ⚠️ Excess G3/4 toxicity mostly G3 lymphopenia and hypertension
- 🔗 Eligibility built on GOG-249 HIR criteria; OncLive cites GOG 249 3y RFS 82% and 18% distant recurrence in HIR disease

**Sources:**
- 📄 **NRG-GY020: Phase III randomized trial of radiation +/- MK-3475 (pembrolizumab) for newly diagnosed early-stage high intermediate risk (HIR) mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) endometrioid endometrial cancer** — Floor Backes. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 1239O · Proffered paper: Gynaecological cancers · 2026-10-24 · presented by Floor Backes (Columbus, United States of America) · [[NCT04214067]]
  > Background: Currently, vaginal brachytherapy (VBT) is recommended as adjuvant therapy for HIR EC. Immunotherapy (IO) is highly effective for patients with recurrent dMMR EC. We hypothesized that the addition of IO to radiation (RT) would improve recurrence free survival (RFS) in early stage dMMR EC.
  > Methods: NRG-GY020 was a randomized, phase III open-label superiority trial and included patients with newly diagnosed, early-stage, HIR (GOG-249 criteria), dMMR endometrioid EC. Patients in both arms received VBT; only patients with stage II and IB grade 3 were allowed to receive external beam pelvic radiation (EBRT). Patients were randomized 2:1 to 1 year of pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks starting within 7 days of RT, vs RT alone. The primary analysis compared the proportion of RFS at 3 years (RFS3) between RT+pembro and RT using normal approximations. Patients who reached RFS3 were considered treatment successes, whereas patients who recurred or died of any cause or who came off study within 3 years for any other reason were considered treatment failures. Secondary analyses included a log-rank test, Cox proportional hazards models, and Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves with comparisons at 3 years.
  > Results: Between 2/2020-8/2022, 168 patients were enrolled. Median age was 66, 77% were white; 20% had stage IA, 63% IB, 17% stage II, with 82% grade 2/3 EC. Most patients (70%) received VBT alone, while 30% received EBRT +/- VBT. The RT+pembro arm experienced more grade 3/4 toxicity (43% vs 15%), mostly grade 3 lymphopenia and hypertension. At data cutoff (median follow up 45.5 months), the standardized statistic comparing the two proportions was Z=-0.48 (p=0.68). RFS3 was 73% for RT vs 70% for RT+pembro; 10/56 (18%) recurrences in the RT and 12/112 (11%) in the RT+pembro arm, and 5 (9%) and 22 (20%) failures for other reasons, respectively. KM estimated RFS was 81% in the RT vs 88% in the RT+ pembro arm (HR 0.72 (95%CI 0.33,1.61; p=0.21). 3-year overall survival (OS) was 96% in both arms.
  > Conclusions: The addition of pembrolizumab to RT did not meet the pre-specified criteria for superiority. RT alone (VBT and/or EBRT) remains the standard in HIR dMMR endometrioid EC.
  > Clinical trial identification: [[NCT04214067]].

## 👄 Head & Neck

Randomised TOS vs DO-IMRT in early oropharynx/supraglottis/hypopharynx: swallowing function comparable, toxicity profiles differ.

### EORTC 1420 Best-of · [[NCT02984410]]

> 12-mo MDADI adjusted difference +3.9 points (90%CI -2.7 to +10.5; p=0.326), TOS vs DO-IMRT; no swallowing difference, more mucosal toxicity with DO-IMRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT cost is mucosal, not swallowing: DO-IMRT carried mucositis 39% vs 10%, dry mouth 49% vs 14% and dysgeusia 46% vs 17%, against TOS hemorrhage 5% and fistula 2%, with G≥3 TRAEs 21% each. DO-IMRT dose, fractionation and target volume are not reported in source, so dysphagia-sparing constraints cannot be benchmarked.

- 📊 Primary endpoint MDADI total at 12 mo: adjusted difference +3.9 points (90%CI -2.7 to +10.5; p=0.326)
- 📊 Adjusted MDADI differences at 4.5, 6 and 9 mo: +6.6, +4.2 and +3.5
- 🔍 Phase III, 1:1, 86 randomised (42 TOS, 44 DO-IMRT), 25 centres, 9 countries, 2018-2024
- 🔍 Population
  - T1/T2 N0-1 OPSCC/SGSCC or T1 N0 HPSCC, suitable for either modality
  - Median age 64; 86% OPSCC; 49% HPV-positive
  - Arms balanced at baseline
- 📊 Secondary outcomes by arm

  | Endpoint | TOS | DO-IMRT | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Unrestricted diet (PSS-HN) at 12 mo | 87% | 83% | n/a |
  | Feeding tube at 12 mo | None | None | n/a |
  | Complete response at 6 mo | 98% | 82% | n/a |
  | 2y EFS | 84% | 72% | HR=1.64 (95%CI 0.77-3.50), p=0.200 |
  | OS | 97% | 100% | n/a |
  | G≥3 treatment-related AEs | 21% | 21% | n/a |

- 📊 Treatment-related AEs (all grades), TOS vs DO-IMRT

  | AE | TOS | DO-IMRT |
  |---|---|---|
  | Mucositis | 10% | 39% |
  | Dry mouth | 14% | 49% |
  | Dysgeusia | 17% | 46% |
  | Hemorrhage | 5% | n/a |
  | Fistula | 2% | n/a |

- ⚠️ 7/44 (16%) allocated to DO-IMRT declined treatment; all TOS pts had surgery
- ⚠️ 12-mo MDADI evaluable in 74% overall: 86% TOS vs 64% DO-IMRT
- ⚠️ 90%CI upper bound +10.5 does not exclude the 10-point difference the trial was powered for
- ⚠️ DO-IMRT dose, fractionation and constraints not reported in source
- 🔗 Consistent in direction with ORATOR (randomised TORS vs RT in OPSCC): function comparable, toxicity profiles differ

- 📄 **Comparable swallowing function at 12 months but divergent toxicity profiles with TOS versus DO-IMRT in early-stage oropharyngeal, supraglottic and hypopharyngeal carcinoma: Primary results of the phase III EORTC 1420 'Best-of' trial** — Christian Simon. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 1535O · Proffered paper: Head and neck cancer · 2026-10-25 · presented by Christian Simon (Lausanne, Switzerland) · [[NCT02984410]]
  > Background: Early-stage oropharyngeal (OPSCC), supraglottic (SGSCC) and hypopharyngeal (HPSCC) carcinoma achieve comparable cure with TOS or DO-IMRT, yet treatment selection has lacked randomized functional evidence. This phase III trial compared swallowing outcomes between two standards of care in this rare population.
  > Methods: T1/T2 N0-1 OPSCC/SGSCC or T1 N0 HPSCC patients suitable for TOS or DO-IMRT were randomised 1:1. Primary endpoint: MDADI total score at 12 months (ANCOVA; alpha=10% two-sided; 80% power; 10-point difference). 54 evaluable required; 84 randomised for 35% attrition. Secondary: MDADI at 4.5, 6, 9 months; PSS-HN; oncological outcomes; toxicity.
  > Results: 86 patients randomised (42 TOS, 44 DO-IMRT) from 25 centres, 9 countries (2018-2024), median age 64 years, 86% OPSCCs, 49% HPV-positive; arms balanced. All TOS patients received surgery; 7/44 (16%) allocated to DO-IMRT declined treatment. 64 patients (74%) had evaluable MDADI at 12 months (86% TOS; 64% DO-IMRT). Mean MDADI: 83.3 (SD 18.3) and 86.2 (SD 15.1); adjusted difference +3.9 points (90%CI: -2.7 to +10.5; p=0.326), consistent across sensitivity analyses. At 4.5, 6 and 9 months, adjusted differences were +6.6, +4.2 and +3.5. At 12 months, PSS-HN: unrestricted diet in 87% (TOS) versus 83% (DO-IMRT); no feeding tube in either arm. Complete response at 6 months: 98% (TOS) versus 82% (DO-IMRT); event-free survival at 2 y 84% (TOS) versus 72% (DO-IMRT) (HR=1.64; 95%CI 0.77-3.50; p=0.200); overall survival 97% (TOS) versus 100% (DO-IMRT). Grade >=3 treatment-related AEs: 21% each; all grades’ treatment-related AEs: TOS: hemorrhage 5%, fistula 2%; DO-IMRT vs TOS: mucositis (39% vs 10%), dry mouth (49% vs 14%) and dysgeusia (46% vs 17%).
  > Conclusions: No significant difference in swallowing function at 12 months was demonstrated between TOS and DO-IMRT. 16% of patients randomised to DO-IMRT declined treatment, illustrating the challenge of equipoise. Greater mucosal toxicity was observed with DO-IMRT. These findings support individualised treatment selection based on tumour characteristics, patient preference and toxicity profile.
  > Clinical trial identification: EORTC Protocol Number: 1420, [[NCT02984410]].

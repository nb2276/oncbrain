# 2026-07-09 — ASTRO Annual Meeting 2024

**HYDRA (MARCAP, N=5880): dose-escalated moderate hypofx buys no PFS over isodose MHFRT but adds G2+ GI (MD OR 1.48, PRO 1.68).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Two of today's three sources argue that RT field and dose choices are being made on evidence that never tested them. HYDRA settles the moderate-hypofx dose question at 60 Gy/20 fx, and the SUPREMO critique shows chest-wall-only fields (SCV in 12%, IMN <2%) can't license omitting comprehensive PMRT. Prostate carried the day, breast the sharper methodological read.

## 🌰 Prostate

Dose within the hypofractionated arm is the live question: HYDRA says escalating costs bowel and returns nothing, while the ASTRO 2024 UIR round-up traces the same dose-toxicity tradeoff into 5 fractions.

### HYDRA

> IPD meta-analysis, 7 phase 3 trials, N=5880: PFS equivalent for isodose and dose-escalated MHFRT, but escalated adds G2+ GI toxicity.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The decision this moves is the fractionation prescription itself: escalating dose within a hypofractionated schedule buys no PFS (HR 0.94, 0.82-1.09) while raising late G2+ GI odds 1.48 (1.14-1.92) and bowel QoL decrement 1.68 (1.07-2.61). Isodose 60 Gy/20 fx is the defensible default.

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis of 7 randomised phase 3 CFRT vs MHFRT trials, MARCAP consortium
- 🔍 Split by MHFRT intent: isodose (3 trials, 3454 pts) vs dose-escalated (4 trials, 2426 pts)
- 🔍 CFRT arms required ≥70 Gy in 2 Gy equivalents; trials without late toxicity IPD excluded
- 📊 Median f/u 5.4 yr (IQR 4.6-7.2) isodose, 7.1 yr (5.7-8.4) dose-escalated
- 📊 Efficacy + toxicity vs CFRT, by MHFRT strategy
- 📐 Dose-escalated GI signal is concordant across scorers: MD-scored OR 1.48 and PRO bowel OR 1.68
- 🔗 Authors land on 60 Gy/20 fx as the standard MHFRT regimen for localised disease
- ⚠️ No PFS gain from escalating within the hypofractionated arm, so the added GI cost buys nothing measurable
- ⚠️ Late toxicity is physician-scored across 7 trial-specific instruments; harmonisation across scales is a known IPD weak point
- ⚠️ PFS definitions varied by parent trial; no OS or prostate-cancer-specific mortality endpoint reported in source

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 40112848]] **Hypofractionated radiotherapy for prostate cancer (HYDRA): an individual patient data meta-analysis of randomised trials in the MARCAP consortium.** — Kishan; Sun; Tree et al.. *The Lancet. Oncology* (2025-04). [doi:10.1016/s1470-2045(25)00034-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1470-2045(25%2900034-8)
  > BACKGROUND: Trials comparing moderately hypofractionated radiotherapy (MHFRT) to conventionally-fractionated radiotherapy (CFRT) for prostate cancer have varied considerably in intent (non-inferiority vs superiority) and MHFRT dose. We compare the efficacy and toxicity profiles of isodose MHFRT and dose-escalated MHFRT.
  > METHODS: This was an individual patient data meta-analysis that identified randomised phase 3 trials of CFRT versus MHFRT that had published individual patient-level data on efficacy and late toxicity. A systematic literature search using MEDLINE, Embase, trial registries, the Web of Science, Scopus, and relevant conference proceedings was initially conducted on Dec 15, 2023, and was re-conducted on Jan 8, 2025. Trials that did not publish efficacy data, did not publish late toxicity data, or did not use modern dose radiotherapy (&#x2265;70 Gy in 2 Gy equivalents) in the CFRT group were excluded. Individual patient data were provided to MARCAP by study investigators. Three separate meta-analyses were designed to compare efficacy (primary endpoint was progression-free survival), physician-scored late toxicity (co-primary endpoints were late grade 2 or higher genitourinary and late grade 2 or higher gastrointestinal toxic effects), and patient-reported outcomes (co-primary endpoints were clinically-significant decrements in patient-reported urinary or bowel quality of life) between patients receiving CFRT versus MHFRT.
  > FINDINGS: We identified 1696 records for review. Seven phase 3 trials comparing MHFRT with CFRT were eligible for inclusion in our analysis. Individual patient data were obtained from these seven studies (3454 patients from three trials comparing CFRT with isodose MHFRT and 2426 patients from four trials comparing CFRT with dose-escalated MHFRT). At a median follow-up of 5&#xb7;4 years (IQR 4&#xb7;6-7&#xb7;2) for isodose MHFRT and 7&#xb7;1 years (5&#xb7;7-8&#xb7;4) for dose-escalated MHFRT, no differences in progression-free survival were detected (hazard ratio 0&#xb7;92, 95% CI 0&#xb7;81-1&#xb7;05; p=0&#xb7;21 and 0&#xb7;94, 0&#xb7;82-1&#xb7;09; p=0&#xb7;43 respectively). No increased odds of grade 2 or higher genitourinary toxic effects were identified for either isodose (odds ratio [OR] 1&#xb7;16, 95 CI% 0&#xb7;86-1&#xb7;57; p=0&#xb7;32) or dose-escalated MHFRT (1&#xb7;20, 0&#xb7;95-1&#xb7;51; p=0&#xb7;13). The odds of grade 2 or higher gastrointestinal toxic effects were significantly higher with dose-escalated (OR 1&#xb7;48, 95% CI 1&#xb7;14-1&#xb7;92; p=0&#xb7;0035) but not isodose MHFRT (1&#xb7;30, 0&#xb7;59-2&#xb7;87; p=0&#xb7;51). Isodose MHFRT was not found to show different odds of urinary quality-of-life decrement (OR 1&#xb7;03, 95% CI 0&#xb7;51-2&#xb7;09; p=0&#xb7;93) or bowel quality-of-life decrement (0&#xb7;76, 0&#xb7;40-1&#xb7;43; p=0&#xb7;39). Dose-escalated MHFRT was associated with greater odds of bowel quality-of-life decrement (OR 1&#xb7;68, 95% CI 1&#xb7;07-2&#xb7;61; p=0&#xb7;023), but no evidence of greater urinary quality-of-life decrement was found (1&#xb7;57, 0&#xb7;87-2&#xb7;85; p=0&#xb7;13).
  > INTERPRETATION: Isodose MHFRT and dose-escalated MHFRT both have similar efficacy compared with CFRT, but dose-escalated MHFRT is associated with higher physician-scored and patient-reported bowel toxicity. Isodose regimens, eg, 60 Gy in 20 fractions, should be the standard MHFRT regimen for localised prostate cancer.
  > FUNDING: None.

> - Whether rectal spacers or MRI guidance neutralise the escalated-dose GI cost

### ASTRO 2024: SBRT for Unfavorable Intermediate-Risk Prostate Cancer

> ASTRO 2024 educational session on SBRT for UIR prostate: 5 fractions over 1-2 weeks, 1-2% bothersome toxicity, focal-boost designs next.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The operational read is the urethra: PACE-B did not require urethral contouring and constrained it only if visualized, so its 5.4% vs 3.7% G2+ GU gap may be a planning artifact, not an SBRT property. If you contour and constrain the urethra, the meta-analytic dose-toxicity tradeoff (biochemical control P=.018, late G3+ GU P=.014) is the axis you are actually trading on.

- 🔍 Educational session (EDU 16) round-up by Dr. Spratt, not a new trial result
- 📊 RTOG 9408 secondary analysis: UIR vs FIR risk, and ADT effect within UIR

  | Comparison | Endpoint | Effect |
  |---|---|---|
  | UIR vs FIR | Distant metastasis | HR 2.36 (95% CI 1.44-3.89), P=.001 |
  | UIR vs FIR | PCa-specific mortality | HR 1.84 (95% CI 1.29-2.62), P=.001 |
  | ADT in UIR | Distant metastasis | HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.28-0.83), P=.008 |
  | ADT in UIR | PCa-specific mortality | HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.26-0.60), P<.001 |

- 📊 In favorable intermediate-risk, ADT did not improve distant metastasis or PCa mortality
- 🔍 UIR prognostic drivers: grade group 3 (HR 3.49), ≥2 intermediate risk factors (HR 2.40)
- 🔍 SBRT dose/fractionation schedules cited across the session
  - HYPO-RT-PC: 42.7Gy/7fx (6.1Gy) vs 78Gy/39fx, N=1,200
  - PACE-B SBRT: 36.25Gy/5fx to PTV, 40Gy CTV
  - Micro-boost phase 1a/b: 36.25Gy/5fx prostate, DIL to 45 / 47.5 / 50Gy
  - Loblaw de-escalation: 35Gy prostate, 25Gy pelvis, 50Gy DIL, 5fx
- 📊 PACE-B: no significant EFS difference; G2+ GU 5.4% SBRT vs 3.7% CFRT, p=0.28
- ⚠️ Spratt's PACE-B critique: urethra contouring not required, constrained only if visualized
- ⚠️ Likely 120%+ hotspots → ≥48Gy urethra (~121Gy EQD2), a plausible driver of the GU signal
- 📐 Meta-analysis late G3+ toxicity: GU 2.0% (1.4-2.8%), GI 1.1% (0.6-2.0%)
- 📐 Higher SBRT dose → better biochemical control (P=.018) but worse late G3+ GU (P=.014)
- 🔗 40-year arc: 45fx/9wk with 10-30% bothersome toxicity → 5fx/1-2wk with 1-2%
- ⚠️ Session excerpt truncates mid-FLAME 2.0; FORT outcomes not in source text

## 🎀 Breast

An editorial, not new data, but it goes at the field design behind the trial most often cited to omit PMRT.

### SUPREMO (critical evaluation)

> Editorial critique: chest-wall-only fields with RNI prohibited, so the trial never tested comprehensive PMRT it is being cited to omit.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant point is what SUPREMO did not irradiate: RNI was prohibited, supraclavicular coverage reached only 12% of PMRT-arm pts (n=97), internal mammary <2%. The supplemental node-positive LRR signal (4.8% to 3.3%, HR 0.51, 0.27-0.96) survived even that truncated field, so a null trial of chest wall alone does not license omitting comprehensive PMRT.

- 🔍 ASO Perspectives editorial (Naoum + Taghian), not a new dataset; argues SUPREMO tested an incomplete PMRT field
- 📐 SUPREMO 10yr chest wall recurrence 1.1% vs 2.5%, HR 0.45; no gain in overall LRR, DFS, or OS
- 📊 Supplement detail: chest-wall RT improved LRR 4.8% → 3.3%, HR 0.51 (95% CI 0.27-0.96) in node-positive, not node-negative
- ⚠️ Field-design limits the authors flag as invalidating the omission read
  - RNI prohibited by protocol; supraclavicular covered in only 12% of PMRT-arm pts (n=97)
  - Internal mammary irradiation allowed for node-positive but used in <2% across both arms
  - Non-UK centers could give RNI in the observation arm; 12 control pts got supraclavicular RNI
- ⚠️ Population mismatch with 2026 practice
  - Only 25% truly node-negative, a group not offered PMRT today per guidelines
  - Majority N1 (1-3 nodes); heterogeneity could mask benefit in node-positive pts
  - Only 14% had SLNB alone; most had ALND, opposite of contemporary axillary practice
- 🔗 EBCTCG 2014: PMRT cut 10yr LRR by 17.9% and 20yr breast cancer mortality by 8% in 1-3 node-positive; no node-negative benefit
- 🔗 MA-20 and EORTC 22922 both support comprehensive RNI in 1-3 positive nodes; EORTC 22922 had 24% mastectomy
- ⚠️ TNBC was 10% of the cohort and the forest plot favored no PMRT; authors read this as underpowering, not harm
- ⚠️ 65% HR-positive with only 10yr follow-up, short for a biology that recurs late; no genomic data
- 🔗 SWOG S8814: in mastectomy pts with 1-3 nodes and no PMRT, LRR 1.5% with low Oncotype vs 11.1% intermediate/high
- 🔗 Lymphedema at 5yr by axillary approach: 8% SLNB alone, 11% SLNB+RNI, 25% ALND alone, 30% ALND+RNI
- 🔍 ~30% of SLNB pts with 1-3 positive nodes had further involved nodes at completion ALND in the de-escalation trials

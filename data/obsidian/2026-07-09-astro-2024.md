# 2026-07-09 — ASTRO Annual Meeting 2024

**HYDRA IPD meta-analysis: moderate hypofx matches CFRT on PFS (HR 0.92 isodose, 0.94 escalated) but dose escalation adds late G2+ GI (OR 1.48).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Fractionation and field design carried the day. HYDRA keeps 60Gy/20fx as the default prostate schedule and argues against escalating past it, while the ASTRO EDU session pushes the other end of the curve (5fx SBRT, 1-2% bothersome toxicity) toward focal boost with whole-gland de-escalation. In breast, an editorial re-reads SUPREMO's null LRR as a field-design artifact: RNI was prohibited, supraclav coverage reached 12%.

## 🎀 Breast

PMRT target volume, not PMRT itself, is what SUPREMO actually tested.

### SUPREMO

> Editorial critique: chest-wall-only RT cut 10yr CW recurrence 1.1% vs 2.5% (HR 0.45) but RNI was prohibited.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant number is buried in SUPREMO's supplement: node-positive LRR 3.3% vs 4.8%, HR 0.51 (0.27-0.96), significant even with chest-wall-only fields and RNI prohibited. With supraclavicular coverage in 12% and IMN in under 2%, this trial never tested comprehensive PMRT, so it cannot settle the elective nodal decision.

- 🔍 ASO Perspectives editorial (Naoum + Taghian), not new trial data; re-reads SUPREMO against PMRT history
- 📐 SUPREMO 10yr chest-wall recurrence 1.1% vs 2.5%, HR 0.45; no LRR, DFS or OS improvement
- 📊 Supplementary LRR in node-positive pts: 3.3% vs 4.8%, HR 0.51 (95% CI 0.27-0.96); not in node-negative
- ⚠️ Field-design critique: SUPREMO tested chest wall alone, not comprehensive PMRT
  - RNI prohibited by protocol in the PMRT arm
  - Supraclavicular coverage in only 12% of PMRT-arm pts (n=97)
  - IMN irradiation allowed for node-positive but used in <2% across both arms
  - Non-UK centres could give RNI in the observation arm; 12 control pts got supraclavicular RNI
- ⚠️ Population critique: cohort does not match who would be irradiated today
  - Only 25% truly node-negative; those pts would not be offered PMRT under current guidelines
  - Majority N1 (1-3 positive nodes); heterogeneity could mask benefit in node-positive pts
  - Only 14% had SLNB alone, majority ALND, whereas most comparable pts today get SLNB
  - No genomic data, so results cannot be extrapolated to low recurrence-score pts
- ⚠️ 65% HR-positive with only 10yr follow-up; late recurrences beyond 10yr not captured
- ⚠️ TNBC was 10% of cohort and the forest plot showed worse outcomes with PMRT; authors read this as underpowering, not harm
- 🔗 vs EBCTCG 2014: PMRT cut 10yr LRR by 17.9% and 20yr breast cancer mortality by 8% in 1-3 node-positive women, with no node-negative benefit
- 🔗 vs MA-20 and EORTC 22922: RNI improved locoregional control and DFS (MA-20, lumpectomy only) and reduced breast cancer mortality and LRR (EORTC 22922, 24% mastectomy) in 1-3 node-positive pts
- 🔗 vs SWOG S8814: in mastectomy pts with 1-3 positive nodes and no PMRT, LRR was 1.5% with low Oncotype vs 11.1% with intermediate/high recurrence score
- 🔗 Chinese TNBC trial (n=681, >80% node-negative): 5yr recurrence and OS improved with a chest-wall-focused PMRT field, RNI at physician discretion
- 📊 5yr lymphedema risk by axillary + nodal RT combination (argument against ALND to avoid PMRT)

  | Approach | 5yr lymphedema risk |
  |---|---|
  | SLNB alone | 8% |
  | SLNB + RNI | 11% |
  | ALND alone | 25% |
  | ALND + RNI | 30% |

- 📐 In the ALND de-escalation trials, roughly 30% of SLNB pts with 1-3 positive nodes had further involved nodes at completion ALND

## 🌰 Prostate

Two fractionation reads pointing the same way: hypofx yes, dose escalation on top of it no.

### ASTRO 2024: SBRT for Unfavorable Intermediate-Risk Prostate Cancer

> Conference education session on SBRT for UIR prostate: 5 fractions over 1-2wks, 1-2% bothersome toxicity vs 10-30% with 45-fraction 2D era.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The actionable detail is the urethral-constraint critique of PACE-B: contouring was optional and constrained only "if visualized" (V44Gy <20%), so hotspots ≥120% put ~48Gy (≈121Gy EQD2) on the urethra. That reframes the 5.4% vs 3.7% G2+ GU gap as a planning artifact, not an SBRT property, and argues for contouring and constraining the urethra in 5-fraction prostate plans.

- 🔍 EDU 16 session, ASTRO 2024 (Washington DC, Sep 29-Oct 2); Dr Daniel Spratt on SBRT for UIR disease
- 🔍 UIR/FIR split dates to 2013; UIR carries GG3 (HR 3.49) or ≥2 intermediate risk factors (HR 2.40) for distant mets
- 📊 RTOG 9408 ([[NCT00002597]]) secondary analysis, N=1,068 intermediate-risk pts, RT ± 4mo ADT

  | Question | Endpoint | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | UIR vs FIR prognosis | Distant metastasis | HR 2.36 (95% CI 1.44-3.89), P=.001 |
  | UIR vs FIR prognosis | PCSM | HR 1.84 (95% CI 1.29-2.62), P=.001 |
  | ADT benefit in UIR | Distant metastasis | HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.28-0.83), P=.008 |
  | ADT benefit in UIR | PCSM | HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.26-0.60), P<.001 |
  | ADT benefit in FIR | DM / PCSM | No improvement |

- 📐 FIR vs UIR cumulative incidence of PCSM differed, p=0.013
- 🔍 HYPO-RT-PC phase 3 NI trial, N=1,200: 78Gy/39fx vs 42.7Gy/7fx (6.1Gy per fx) underpins the NCCN SBRT listing
- 📊 PACE-B 5yr (ASTRO 2023), low-risk 10% / intermediate-risk 90%
  - SBRT arm: 40Gy CTV (5×8Gy), 36.25Gy PTV (5×7.25Gy)
  - EFS: no significant difference vs conventional/moderate hypofx
  - G2+ GU 5.4% SBRT vs 3.7% control, p=0.28
  - No significant bowel toxicity difference
- ⚠️ PACE-B urethral contouring not required, constrained only "if visualized" (V44Gy <20%)
- ⚠️ Spratt's read: hotspots ≥120% imply ≥48Gy to urethra, ~121Gy EQD2, plausibly driving the GU signal
- 📊 Meta-analysis of SBRT series: late grade ≥3 toxicity
  - GU 2.0% (95% CI 1.4%-2.8%)
  - GI 1.1% (95% CI 0.6%-2.0%)
  - Higher SBRT dose → better biochemical control (P=.018)
  - Higher SBRT dose → worse late G3+ GU (P=.014)
- ⚠️ Counter to that meta-analysis: Spratt attributes the toxicity to absent dose constraints in the pooled prospective series, not to SBRT itself
- 🔍 Three SBRT delivery strategies presented
  - Traditional: whole prostate to uniform dose
  - Micro-focal boost: whole gland full dose + DIL boost
  - Focal boost with whole-gland de-escalation (where Spratt sees the field moving)
- 💊 Phase 1a/b micro-boost: 36.25Gy/5fx prostate with DIL escalation to 45, 47.5, 50Gy/5fx; no grade ≥3 toxicity reported
- 💊 Loblaw de-escalation regimen: 35Gy prostate, 25Gy pelvis, 50Gy DIL, all in 5fx; urinary and bowel QoL good, late toxicity comparable to no-boost cohort
- ⚠️ Source excerpt truncates mid-sentence in the FLAME 2.0 paragraph (35Gy SBRT); FORT trial named in keywords but not covered in the captured text

### HYDRA

> No PFS difference for either isodose (HR 0.92) or dose-escalated MHFRT (HR 0.94), but escalation raises late G2+ GI (OR 1.48).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The split that matters is isodose vs dose-escalated MHFRT, not hypofractionation itself: escalation adds no PFS (HR 0.94, 0.82-1.09) and costs bowel on both physician grading (OR 1.48) and patient report (OR 1.68). GU was unchanged in both strata. The schedule decision lands on 60 Gy in 20 fractions.

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis of 7 randomised phase 3 trials; 3454 pts isodose stratum, 2426 dose-escalated
- 🔍 CFRT comparator had to use ≥70 Gy in 2 Gy equivalents; trials lacking late toxicity IPD excluded
- 📐 Median f/u 5.4 yrs (IQR 4.6-7.2) isodose, 7.1 yrs (5.7-8.4) dose-escalated
- 📊 MHFRT vs CFRT by stratum: efficacy, physician-scored late toxicity, PRO
- ⚠️ Physician-scored GI and patient-reported bowel signals agree only in the dose-escalated stratum
- ⚠️ Null PFS is not equivalence: upper CI bounds 1.05 and 1.09 still admit modest harm
- ⚠️ Pooled trials differed in intent (non-inferiority vs superiority) and in MHFRT dose
- 🔗 Backs 60 Gy/20 fx (the CHHiP, PROFIT dose level) as default over escalated schedules

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 40112848]] **Hypofractionated radiotherapy for prostate cancer (HYDRA): an individual patient data meta-analysis of randomised trials in the MARCAP consortium.** — Kishan; Sun; Tree et al.. *The Lancet. Oncology* (2025-04). [doi:10.1016/s1470-2045(25)00034-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1470-2045(25%2900034-8)
  > BACKGROUND: Trials comparing moderately hypofractionated radiotherapy (MHFRT) to conventionally-fractionated radiotherapy (CFRT) for prostate cancer have varied considerably in intent (non-inferiority vs superiority) and MHFRT dose. We compare the efficacy and toxicity profiles of isodose MHFRT and dose-escalated MHFRT.
  > METHODS: This was an individual patient data meta-analysis that identified randomised phase 3 trials of CFRT versus MHFRT that had published individual patient-level data on efficacy and late toxicity. A systematic literature search using MEDLINE, Embase, trial registries, the Web of Science, Scopus, and relevant conference proceedings was initially conducted on Dec 15, 2023, and was re-conducted on Jan 8, 2025. Trials that did not publish efficacy data, did not publish late toxicity data, or did not use modern dose radiotherapy (&#x2265;70 Gy in 2 Gy equivalents) in the CFRT group were excluded. Individual patient data were provided to MARCAP by study investigators. Three separate meta-analyses were designed to compare efficacy (primary endpoint was progression-free survival), physician-scored late toxicity (co-primary endpoints were late grade 2 or higher genitourinary and late grade 2 or higher gastrointestinal toxic effects), and patient-reported outcomes (co-primary endpoints were clinically-significant decrements in patient-reported urinary or bowel quality of life) between patients receiving CFRT versus MHFRT.
  > FINDINGS: We identified 1696 records for review. Seven phase 3 trials comparing MHFRT with CFRT were eligible for inclusion in our analysis. Individual patient data were obtained from these seven studies (3454 patients from three trials comparing CFRT with isodose MHFRT and 2426 patients from four trials comparing CFRT with dose-escalated MHFRT). At a median follow-up of 5&#xb7;4 years (IQR 4&#xb7;6-7&#xb7;2) for isodose MHFRT and 7&#xb7;1 years (5&#xb7;7-8&#xb7;4) for dose-escalated MHFRT, no differences in progression-free survival were detected (hazard ratio 0&#xb7;92, 95% CI 0&#xb7;81-1&#xb7;05; p=0&#xb7;21 and 0&#xb7;94, 0&#xb7;82-1&#xb7;09; p=0&#xb7;43 respectively). No increased odds of grade 2 or higher genitourinary toxic effects were identified for either isodose (odds ratio [OR] 1&#xb7;16, 95 CI% 0&#xb7;86-1&#xb7;57; p=0&#xb7;32) or dose-escalated MHFRT (1&#xb7;20, 0&#xb7;95-1&#xb7;51; p=0&#xb7;13). The odds of grade 2 or higher gastrointestinal toxic effects were significantly higher with dose-escalated (OR 1&#xb7;48, 95% CI 1&#xb7;14-1&#xb7;92; p=0&#xb7;0035) but not isodose MHFRT (1&#xb7;30, 0&#xb7;59-2&#xb7;87; p=0&#xb7;51). Isodose MHFRT was not found to show different odds of urinary quality-of-life decrement (OR 1&#xb7;03, 95% CI 0&#xb7;51-2&#xb7;09; p=0&#xb7;93) or bowel quality-of-life decrement (0&#xb7;76, 0&#xb7;40-1&#xb7;43; p=0&#xb7;39). Dose-escalated MHFRT was associated with greater odds of bowel quality-of-life decrement (OR 1&#xb7;68, 95% CI 1&#xb7;07-2&#xb7;61; p=0&#xb7;023), but no evidence of greater urinary quality-of-life decrement was found (1&#xb7;57, 0&#xb7;87-2&#xb7;85; p=0&#xb7;13).
  > INTERPRETATION: Isodose MHFRT and dose-escalated MHFRT both have similar efficacy compared with CFRT, but dose-escalated MHFRT is associated with higher physician-scored and patient-reported bowel toxicity. Isodose regimens, eg, 60 Gy in 20 fractions, should be the standard MHFRT regimen for localised prostate cancer.
  > FUNDING: None.

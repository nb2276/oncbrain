# 2026-06-09

**FIRESTORM: dose-escalated postop RT (BED ≥79.2 Gy) lifts 5-yr PFS to 65.8% vs 38.8% in high-risk meningioma, MVA HR 0.40.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Meningioma dose owns the day: FIRESTORM's escalation signal holds after stripping SRS from the comparator (HR 0.56), while RTOG 0539 sets the 10-yr benchmark it argues against (high-risk PFS 42.5% at 60 Gy). Neither is randomised. RAPCHEM adds breast de-escalation: response-adapted nodal omission, 10-yr LRR 2.9%.

## 🧠 CNS

Two reads on the same postop meningioma dose question: RTOG 0539's mature 10-yr risk-stratified benchmark, and a retrospective IPD meta-analysis arguing 60 Gy is too low for high-risk disease, at the cost of doubled radionecrosis and no OS gain.

### NRG Oncology RTOG 0539 · [[NCT00895622]]

> 10-yr PFS 85.2% observed low-risk, 72.2% intermediate-risk at 54 Gy, 42.5% high-risk at 60 Gy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable RT read is the target: 54 Gy/30 fx for intermediate-risk and 60 Gy/30 fx for high-risk, with grade 3+ RT-attributed toxicity 9.6% and 15.1%. Recurrent grade 1 salvaged with RT reached only 67.0% 10-yr OS, worse than upfront grade 2 post-GTR at 91.0%, which argues against deferring RT in a grade 1 you expect to recur.

- 📊 Long-term outcomes by risk cohort (median f/u 12.1, 12.0, 11.1 yrs)

  | Cohort | Management | 10-yr PFS | 10-yr OS | 10-yr cum. incidence progression |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Low (grp 1, n=60) | Observation | 85.2% (75.7-94.8) | 94.1% (87.6-100) | 8.9% (3.2-18.2) |
  | Intermediate (grp 2, n=52) | RT 54 Gy | 72.2% (59.2-85.1) | 84.7% (74.2-95.2) | 21.2% (10.8-33.9) |
  | High (grp 3, n=53) | RT 60 Gy | 42.5% (28.7-56.3) | 51.1% (37.0-65.2) | 39.3% (25.8-52.5) |

- 🔍 RT delivered in 30 fractions: 54 Gy intermediate-risk, 60 Gy high-risk
- 🔍 Risk assignment by WHO grade (centrally confirmed), resection extent, recurrence status
- 📐 Cox covariates independently associated with worse outcome (whole trial)

  | Covariate | PFS HR (95% CI), p | OS HR (95% CI), p |
  |---|---|---|
  | Recurrent vs initial | 2.5 (1.01-6.18), p=0.047 | 2.86 (1.06-7.70), p=0.038 |
  | STR vs GTR | 2.58 (1.09-6.11), p=0.031 | 3.38 (1.28-8.91), p=0.014 |

- 📊 Low-risk 10-yr PFS 88.0% after GTR vs 72.7% after STR, supporting RT consideration post-STR
- 📊 Recurrent grade 1 treated with RT: 10-yr OS 67.0% vs 91.0% for upfront grade 2 post-GTR (HR 0.30, p=0.045)
- 📊 High-risk median PFS 3.8 yrs (95% CI 2.8-11.2); median OS 11.2 yrs
- 📊 Recurrent grade 2 fares worst: 10-yr PFS 15.0% vs 54.5% newly-dx grade 2 post-STR
- ⚠️ Non-randomised risk cohorts: no untreated control for grade 2 post-GTR, so RT benefit is inferred not measured
- ⚠️ Wide CIs in subgroups (recurrent grade 3 has no pts at 10 yrs); cohort-level n≈52-53 limits subgroup reads
- 🔗 Grade 2 post-GTR RT question still open pending NRG BN003 ([[NCT03180268]]) and ROAM/EORTC 1308

[doi:10.1200/jco-25-01441](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-01441)
  > NRG Oncology RTOG 0539 was a prospective phase II trial of risk-adapted radiotherapy for patients with WHO grade 1-3 meningioma. Low-risk (group 1, n = 60) was defined as a grade 1 tumor after gross total resection or subtotal resection (GTR/STR) and prospectively monitored. Intermediate-risk (group 2, n = 52) was defined as recurrent grade 1 or newly diagnosed grade 2 tumor after GTR and treated with radiotherapy (54 Gy). High-risk (group 3, n = 53) included a newly diagnosed grade 2 tumor after STR, newly diagnosed grade 3 tumor, or recurrent grade 2 or 3 tumor and treated with radiotherapy (60 Gy). Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. The median follow-up times for the low-, intermediate-, and high-risk cohorts were 12.1, 12.0, and 11.1 years, respectively. Five patients (9.6%) and eight patients (15.1%) had a grade 3+ toxicity attributed to radiotherapy in the intermediate- and high-risk cohorts, respectively. The long-term outcomes using this risk-adapted approach support observation for low-risk patients, inform radiotherapy patient selection and practice standards for intermediate- and high-risk patients, and provide comparative benchmarks for future trials.

### FIRESTORM

> 5-yr PFS 65.8% vs 38.8% with dose-escalated postop RT (BED ≥79.2 Gy) in high-risk meningioma; MVA HR 0.40.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The benefit survives the obvious confounders: excluding the 35 single-fraction SRS cases from SD-RT, DE-RT still gave 5-yr PFS 65.8% vs 41.7% (HR 0.56, 0.36-0.86), and photon-alone escalation matched carbon on PFS (68.3% vs 61.1%) with 0% grade ≥3 radionecrosis. That makes 66-70 Gy conventionally fractionated photons the practical escalation route, not particle referral.

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis, 7 institutions, N=248 high-risk meningioma (RTOG 0539 definition); median f/u 67.1 mo
- 🔍 DE-RT defined as BED ≥79.2 Gy (= 66 Gy/33 fx) or any carbon-ion boost; 59 DE-RT vs 189 SD-RT
- 📐 DE-RT median BED 82.9 Gy (79.2-93.8) vs SD-RT 70.1 Gy (26.4-78.4)
- 📊 DE-RT dose/fractionation schemes (n, % of DE-RT arm)
  - 68 Gy/31 fx (50 Gy/25 photons + 18 Gy/6 fx carbon boost): 33 cases, 55.9%
  - 70 Gy/35 fx photons alone: 18 cases, 30.5%
  - 66 Gy/33 fx photons alone: 6 cases, 10.2%
- ⚠️ SD-RT arm is not clean: 35 cases (18.5%) were SRS, mostly 12-18 Gy/1 fx, whose 5-yr PFS was 20.5%
- 📐 Excluding SRS from SD-RT, DE-RT benefit held: 5-yr PFS 65.8% vs 41.7%, MVA HR 0.56 (0.36-0.86), P = .008
- 📐 Restricting SD-RT to 59.4 Gy/33 fx or 60 Gy/30 fx: HR 0.51 (0.28-0.93), P = .03
- 📐 Further excluding prior-RT pts from that comparator: HR 0.37 (0.19-0.73), P = .004
- 📊 DE-RT vs SD-RT PFS by Simpson grade subgroup

  | Subgroup | 3-yr PFS DE-RT vs SD-RT | 5-yr PFS DE-RT vs SD-RT | MVA HR (95% CI) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Simpson 1-3 | 87.5% vs 55.9% | 70.0% vs 40.0% | 0.31 (0.08-1.14) | .08 |
  | Simpson 4-5 | 86.3% vs 55.4% | 63.3% vs 38.4% | 0.55 (0.36-0.84) | .006 |

- 💊 Carbon adds nothing over photons: 5-yr PFS 61.1% mixed carbon/photon vs 68.3% photon-alone, P = .34
- ⚠️ OS not improved: MVA HR 0.66 (0.33-1.34), P = .27; IPTW HR 0.77 (0.41-1.45), P = .42
- ⚠️ Radionecrosis: any-grade doubled with DE-RT, driven by the carbon subgroup

  | Cohort | Any-grade RN | Grade ≥3 RN |
  |---|---|---|
  | DE-RT overall | 20 of 59 (33.9%) | 3 of 59 (5.1%) |
  | DE-RT mixed carbon/photon | 15 of 33 (45%) | 3 of 33 (9.1%) |
  | DE-RT photon-alone | 5 of 26 (19.2%) | 0% |
  | SD-RT | 25 of 189 (13.2%) | 6 of 189 (3.2%) |

- ⚠️ Confounding by design: DE-RT came from only 2 of 7 institutions, one carbon-only and one photon-only
- ⚠️ Retrospective, non-randomized; P values unadjusted for multiple testing; no molecular classification available

- 📄 [[PMID 41956162]] **Improved Progression-Free Survival Following Dose-Escalated Versus Standard-Dose Postoperative Radiation Therapy for High-Risk Meningiomas: An International Multicenter Individual Patient–Level Meta-Analysis (FIRESTORM)** — Singh, Raj; Koempel, Andrew; French, Beck et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.03.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.03.036)
  > PURPOSE: We performed an individual patient-level meta-analysis of high-risk meningiomas to compare the outcomes of dose-escalated radiation therapy (DE-RT) versus standard-dose postoperative radiation therapy (SD-RT).
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: A total of 7 institutions participated. DE-RT was defined as treatment with a biologically effective dose of &#x2265;79.2 Gy (equivalent of 66 Gy in 33 fractions). We compared progression-free survival (PFS) with DE-RT versus SD-RT via Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank t tests, a Cox proportional hazards multivariable model, and propensity score analyses with inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). We also compared incidences of central nervous system radionecrosis (RN) with DE-RT versus SD-RT.
  > RESULTS: The analysis included 248 patients with high-risk meningioma (59 received DE-RT and 189 received SD-RT). One hundred and eighty-eight cases (75.8%) were World Health Organization grade 2, and 103 cases (41.5%) were recurrent meningiomas. Extent of resection was subtotal resection in 182 of 248 (75.2%). Three- and 5-year PFS rates were 62.8% (95% CI, 55.8%-69.0%) and 45.0% (95% CI, 37.3%-52.3%), respectively. DE-RT was associated with superior PFS rates (P = .0022), with 3-year (86.4% vs 55.6%) and 5-year (65.8% vs 38.8%) PFS rates favoring DE-RT. On multivariable analysis, DE-RT was associated with superior PFS (hazard ratio, 0.40; 95% CI, 0.24-0.69; P = .001). On IPTW, DE-RT continued to be associated with superior PFS (hazard ratio, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.24-0.83; P = .01). A greater incidence of any grade RN was observed following DE-RT (20 of 59; 33.9%) versus SD-RT (25 of 189; 13.2%) (P = .001) but with similar grade 3 or greater RN events (DE-RT 5.1% vs SD-RT 3.2%).
  > CONCLUSIONS: DE-RT resulted in superior PFS for patients with high-risk meningiomas over SD-RT without an increase in severe toxicities.

## 🎀 Breast

Response-adapted nodal RT after neoadjuvant chemo, held to a 10-yr endpoint, with the randomised answer (B-51) still pending.

### RAPCHEM (BOOG 2010-03) · [[NCT01872975]]

> 10yr locoregional recurrence 2.9% (24/838) with response-adapted RT after neoadjuvant chemo; 2.4% in the RT-omission-eligible low-risk group.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The allocation rule, not the recurrence rate, is the transferable part: ypN0 after mastectomy received no RT at all and still ran 2.4% at 10yr, and ypN1 pts had regional nodes omitted entirely. Dose, fractionation and target-volume detail are not reported in source, which limits direct transfer.

- 📊 1° outcome: 10yr isolated locoregional recurrence 24/838 (2.9%) across all three risk strata
- 🔍 Prospective multicentre cohort, N=848, 17 Dutch centres, accrual 2011-2015; cT1-2 (<5cm) cN1 (1-3 nodes)
- 🔍 Risk stratification was by post-chemo nodal status, and it set the RT target volume
  - Low risk (ypN0): breast RT after BCS, NO RT after mastectomy
  - Intermediate risk (ypN1, 1-3 nodes): breast/chest wall only, nodal volumes omitted
  - High risk (ypN2+, ≥4 nodes): breast/chest wall PLUS regional nodal irradiation
- 📊 10yr locoregional recurrence by risk group
- 📊 Rates are flat across strata, which is what a correctly calibrated de-escalation should look like
- ⚠️ Single-arm: no randomised comparison to full nodal RT, so the counterfactual recurrence rate is unknown
- ⚠️ Most pts had ALND, uncommon in current practice; the nodal staging that drove allocation is not today's staging
- ⚠️ No dose/fractionation, OS, DFS or toxicity data in source; only the LRR counts are reported
- 🔗 The randomised answer is NSABP B-51/RTOG 1304 ([[NCT01872975]]); investigators state results expected in ~3 years

# 2026-06-09

**FIRESTORM: dose-escalated postop RT (BED ≥79.2 Gy) for high-risk meningioma, 5-yr PFS 65.8% vs 38.8%, MVA HR 0.40, no excess G3+ necrosis.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> CNS carried the day, both readings on the same question: how much RT a meningioma needs. FIRESTORM says escalate past 66 Gy/33 fx in grade 2-3 disease (HR 0.40, PFS only, OS HR 0.66 P=.27); RTOG 0539's 12-yr f/u anchors the standard-dose benchmark at 72.2% 10-yr PFS after 54 Gy. Breast adds RAPCHEM: 2.4% 10-yr LRR omitting nodal RT after NAC response, awaiting B-51.

## 🎀 Breast

Response-adapted nodal RT omission after NAC, single-arm and ALND-era, so the randomised answer is still B-51's.

### RAPCHEM

> 10yr locoregional recurrence 2.9% (24/838) with response-adapted post-NAC RT, including 2.4% in low-risk pts given breast-only or no RT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The de-escalation variable is target volume, not dose: ypN0 pts got breast-only RT after BCS and none after mastectomy, and still ran 2.4% LRR at 10yr. That is the omission decision this informs, but the cohort is ALND-staged, so it does not transfer cleanly to today's SLN-only pts.

- 🔍 Prospective multicenter cohort (BOOG 2010-03), N=848, 17 Dutch centers, accrual 2011-2015, 10yr f/u
- 🔍 Eligibility: cT1-2 (<5cm) cN1 (1-3 involved nodes), all received neoadjuvant chemo then surgery
- 🔍 RT assigned by ypN status, not randomized: risk group determined by nodal response to NAC
- 📊 10yr locoregional recurrence by risk group (ypN-based RT allocation)
- 📐 No dose or fractionation reported in source text; RT field, not dose, was the de-escalation variable
- ⚠️ Single-arm, no untreated comparator: the paper states it did not compare pts treated with and without RT
- ⚠️ Most pts had ALND, less common now; SLN-staged pts in current practice are a different population
- ⚠️ Nodal recurrences pooled into a composite LRR endpoint; isolated regional failure by group not reported in source
- 🔗 Randomized answer pending from NSABP B-51 ([[NCT01872975]]), cited in source as ~3 years out
- 🗞️ EBCC15 presentation (Abstract 1), Mauritz et al, Maastro; no peer-reviewed manuscript cited in source

## 🧠 CNS

Two meningioma datasets bracket the dose question from opposite ends, neither randomised; NRG BN003 and ROAM remain the tests.

### RTOG 0539 · [[NCT00895622]]

> 10-yr PFS 85.2% observed low-risk, 72.2% intermediate-risk after 54Gy, 42.5% high-risk after 60Gy; median f/u 12 yrs.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 10-yr numbers now gate two RT decisions. Low-risk grade 1 post-STR fell to 72.7% PFS vs 88.0% after GTR, supporting RT consideration after STR. And recurrent grade 1 salvaged with 54Gy reached only 67.0% 10-yr OS, worse than newly-dx grade 2 post-GTR (91.0%, HR 0.30, p=0.045), which argues against banking on salvage.

- 🔍 Phase 2, non-randomised, risk-adapted: 3 prespecified cohorts, N=165 eligible of 244 consented, central path review
- 📐 RT dose by risk: 54Gy/30fx (intermediate), 60Gy/30fx (high); group 1 observed, no RT
- 📊 Median PFS not reached low- or intermediate-risk; high-risk median PFS 3.8 yrs (95% CI 2.8-11.2)
- 📊 High-risk median OS 11.2 yrs (95% CI 4.6-not reached)
- 📊 Low-risk grade 1 by extent of resection (10-yr, observation only)

  | Endpoint | GTR | STR |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10-yr PFS | 88.0% (76.9-99.1) | 72.7% (45.9-99.5) |
  | 10-yr OS | 90.9% (81.0-100.0) | 100% |

- 📊 Intermediate-risk cohort: recurrent grade 1 vs newly-dx grade 2 after GTR

  | Endpoint | Recurrent grade 1 | New grade 2 post-GTR | Comparison |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 10-yr PFS | 67.0% (39.8-94.2) | 73.2% (58.1-88.2) | p=0.61 |
  | 10-yr OS | 67.0% (39.8-94.2) | 91.0% (81.3-100) | HR 0.30 (0.09-1.04), p=0.045 |

- 📊 High-risk subsets, 10-yr outcomes (60Gy)

  | Subset | 10-yr PFS | 10-yr OS |
  |---|---|---|
  | Recurrent grade 2 | 15.0% (0-42.0) | 17.9% (0-49.3) |
  | New grade 2 post-STR | 54.5% (25.1-84.0) | 54.5% (25.1-84.0) |
  | New grade 3 post-GTR/STR | 52.9% (29.2-76.7) | 70.6% (48.9-92.2) |

- 📐 High-risk Cox: recurrent disease HR 2.5 (1.01-6.18), p=0.047 for PFS; STR HR 2.58 (1.09-6.11), p=0.031
- 📐 High-risk OS Cox: recurrent HR 2.86 (1.06-7.70), p=0.038; STR HR 3.38 (1.28-8.91), p=0.014
- ⚠️ RT-attributed grade 3+ toxicity
  - Intermediate-risk (54Gy): 5 pts (9.6%)
  - High-risk (60Gy): 8 pts (15.1%)
  - New since prior reports: 2 grade 3 (auditory, neurologic) + 1 grade 4 hemorrhage in intermediate group
  - Zubrod, MMSE, neurologic function score stable over time (Suppl Tables 4-6)
- ⚠️ No randomised comparator: adjuvant RT vs observation for GTR grade 2 remains untested here; NRG BN003 and ROAM/EORTC 1308 are the randomised tests
- ⚠️ Cohorts allocated by risk, not randomised, so cross-cohort comparisons are confounded by grade, resection, and recurrence status
- ⚠️ 25.0% of intermediate and 22.6% of high-risk pts entered as recurrent by imaging only, no re-resection pathology

[doi:10.1200/jco-25-01441](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-01441)
  > NRG Oncology RTOG 0539 was a prospective phase II trial of risk-adapted radiotherapy for patients with WHO grade 1-3 meningioma. Low-risk (group 1, n = 60) was defined as a grade 1 tumor after gross total resection or subtotal resection (GTR/STR) and prospectively monitored. Intermediate-risk (group 2, n = 52) was defined as recurrent grade 1 or newly diagnosed grade 2 tumor after GTR and treated with radiotherapy (54 Gy). ### FIRESTORM

> 5-yr PFS 65.8% vs 38.8% with BED ≥79.2 Gy postop RT for high-risk meningioma; MVA HR 0.40, no excess G3+ necrosis.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The escalation signal is photon-deliverable: photon-alone DE-RT (70 Gy/35 fx, 66 Gy/33 fx) matched mixed carbon-photon on 5-yr PFS (68.3% vs 61.1%, P=.34) while carrying 0% grade ≥3 RN versus 9.1%. Boost PTV margins were 0.5-2 mm. That moves the dose decision for STR grade 2 and grade 3 disease without needing a carbon facility.

- 📊 1° outcome PFS: 3-yr 86.4% vs 55.6%, 5-yr 65.8% vs 38.8% (P=.0022) DE-RT vs SD-RT
- 📐 MVA HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.24-0.69), P=.001; IPTW HR 0.45 (0.24-0.83), P=.01
- 📐 Grade-stratified Cox (PH assumption violated by grade, P=.04): HR 0.40 (0.22-0.73), P=.008
- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis, 7 institutions, N=248; 75.8% WHO grade 2, 41.5% recurrent, 75.2% STR/biopsy, median f/u 67.1 mo
- 🔍 DE-RT = BED ≥79.2 Gy (66 Gy/33 fx equivalent) or carbon boost; median BED 82.9 vs 70.1 Gy
- 🔍 DE-RT dose/fractionation schemes (n=59)
  - 68 Gy/31 fx (50 Gy/25 photons + 18 Gy/6 carbon boost): 33 cases, 55.9%
  - 70 Gy/35 fx photons alone: 18 cases, 30.5%
  - 66 Gy/33 fx photons alone: 6 cases, 10.2%
  - Photon boost PTV margin 0.5-2 mm; carbon boost CTV up to 6 mm
- 📊 PFS benefit persists across sensitivity subgroups (DE-RT vs SD-RT)

  | Subgroup | 3-yr PFS | 5-yr PFS | MVA HR (95% CI), P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | SD-RT radiosurgery excluded | 86.4% vs 64.8% | 65.8% vs 41.7% | 0.56 (0.36-0.86), P=.008 |
  | SD-RT conventional 59.4/60 Gy only | not reported | not reported | 0.51 (0.28-0.93), P=.03 |
  | Above, prior-RT cases excluded | not reported | not reported | 0.37 (0.19-0.73), P=.004 |
  | Simpson 4-5 / residual | 86.3% vs 55.4% | 63.3% vs 38.4% | 0.55 (0.36-0.84), P=.006 |
  | Simpson 1-3 | 87.5% vs 55.9% | 70.0% vs 40.0% | 0.31 (0.08-1.14), P=.08 |

- ⚠️ OS negative: UVA 5-yr 83.8% vs 68.4% P=.056; MVA HR 0.66 (0.33-1.34) P=.27; IPTW HR 0.77 (0.41-1.45) P=.42
- ⚠️ Radionecrosis: any grade higher with DE-RT, severe events similar

  | RN endpoint | DE-RT | SD-RT | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Any grade | 20/59 (33.9%) | 25/189 (13.2%) | .001 |
  | Grade ≥3 | 3/59 (5.1%) | 6/189 (3.2%) | .47 |
  | Grade 5 | 1.7% | 0.5% | not reported |

- ⚠️ Any-grade RN excess is carbon-driven: 15/33 (45%) mixed carbon-photon vs 5/26 (19.2%) photon-alone DE-RT; photon-alone vs SD-RT P=.41
- ⚠️ All 3 G3+ RN events in DE-RT were in the mixed carbon-photon cohort (3/33, 9.1%); photon-alone 0%
- 🔗 Photon-alone vs mixed carbon-photon DE-RT: 5-yr PFS 68.3% vs 61.1% (P=.34), so the benefit is not carbon-specific
- ⚠️ DE-RT delivered at only 2 of 7 institutions (one carbon-only, one photon-only); confounding by center, imaging (DOTATATE PET) and response assessment not capturable by IPTW
- ⚠️ No molecular data (methylation class, 34-gene score), so the population that actually needs escalation can't be isolated
- ⚠️ SD-RT arm is heterogeneous: 35 cases (18.5%) were focal SRS, mostly 12-18 Gy/1 fx, whose 5-yr PFS was 20.5%, dragging the comparator down

- 📄 [[PMID 41956162]] **Improved Progression-Free Survival Following Dose-Escalated Versus Standard-Dose Postoperative Radiation Therapy for High-Risk Meningiomas: An International Multicenter Individual Patient–Level Meta-Analysis (FIRESTORM)** — Singh, Raj; Koempel, Andrew; French, Beck et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.03.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.03.036)
  > PURPOSE: We performed an individual patient-level meta-analysis of high-risk meningiomas to compare the outcomes of dose-escalated radiation therapy (DE-RT) versus standard-dose postoperative radiation therapy (SD-RT).
  > METHODS AND MATERIALS: A total of 7 institutions participated. DE-RT was defined as treatment with a biologically effective dose of &#x2265;79.2 Gy (equivalent of 66 Gy in 33 fractions). We compared progression-free survival (PFS) with DE-RT versus SD-RT via Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank t tests, a Cox proportional hazards multivariable model, and propensity score analyses with inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). We also compared incidences of central nervous system radionecrosis (RN) with DE-RT versus SD-RT.
  > RESULTS: The analysis included 248 patients with high-risk meningioma (59 received DE-RT and 189 received SD-RT). One hundred and eighty-eight cases (75.8%) were World Health Organization grade 2, and 103 cases (41.5%) were recurrent meningiomas. Extent of resection was subtotal resection in 182 of 248 (75.2%). Three- and 5-year PFS rates were 62.8% (95% CI, 55.8%-69.0%) and 45.0% (95% CI, 37.3%-52.3%), respectively. DE-RT was associated with superior PFS rates (P = .0022), with 3-year (86.4% vs 55.6%) and 5-year (65.8% vs 38.8%) PFS rates favoring DE-RT. On multivariable analysis, DE-RT was associated with superior PFS (hazard ratio, 0.40; 95% CI, 0.24-0.69; P = .001). On IPTW, DE-RT continued to be associated with superior PFS (hazard ratio, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.24-0.83; P = .01). A greater incidence of any grade RN was observed following DE-RT (20 of 59; 33.9%) versus SD-RT (25 of 189; 13.2%) (P = .001) but with similar grade 3 or greater RN events (DE-RT 5.1% vs SD-RT 3.2%).
  > CONCLUSIONS: DE-RT resulted in superior PFS for patients with high-risk meningiomas over SD-RT without an increase in severe toxicities.

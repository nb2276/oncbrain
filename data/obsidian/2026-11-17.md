# 2026-11-17

**PMRT after NAC still has no RCT; MDACC non-randomized series: 10-yr LRR 59% vs 16% with PMRT for ≥4 pN+ (p<0.0001), so decide on clinical stage plus ypN.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Breast-only day of RT decision talks, all resting on retrospective or subset data: margin re-excision vs boost, Oncotype RS prognostic for LRR (SWOG 8814 MVA HR 2.36) but not an omission tool, and PMRT after NAC keyed to ypN. pCR is prognostic per pt (CTNeoBC EFS HR 0.48) yet a weak trial-level surrogate.

## 🎀 Breast

Five talks on breast RT decisions and planning; none adds new randomized data, so read each framework as expert synthesis of retrospective series and trial subsets.

### Margins: Impact on Local Control, Treatment Options

> Close or positive margins raise in-breast LR; re-excise when young or EIC-positive, accept RT boost alone for focal anterior/posterior margins in older pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT trade is boost dose against fibrosis: in EORTC 22881 margin-positive pts, LR was 17.5% with a 10 Gy boost vs 10.8% with 26 Gy, at severe fibrosis 3% vs 14%. That bounds how far a boost can substitute for re-excision, because the dose that closes the LR gap costs the cosmesis BCT is meant to protect.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Scott Tyldesley
> The speaker frames margin management as a risk budget: BCT matches mastectomy survival when in-breast relapse stays near 1%/yr or 10% at 10 yrs, so a margin matters to the extent it pushes a patient over that line. Close and positive margins raise LR in retrospective series, but no RCT compares re-excision with RT boost, and boost dose escalation buys control at a fibrosis and cosmesis cost. The framework re-excises grossly positive margins, and close or focally positive margins in young women with EIC, while accepting boost alone for older or high-systemic-risk pts and anterior or posterior margins. The bottom line is to individualize.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Margin-specific LR figures rest on single-institution retrospective series with inconsistent close/positive definitions and boost doses escalated by margin category, so the margin effect is confounded with selection and dose.
> - No RCT compares re-excision with RT boost for control or cosmesis; the re-excision preference for close margins is consensus, and the speaker concedes its benefit is uncertain.
> - The >2 mm negative-margin definition predates the SSO-ASTRO no-ink-on-tumour consensus for invasive disease, which would treat most close margins here as adequate with whole-breast RT.
> - Most LR estimates come from eras before current systemic therapy, and the speaker notes 5-yr LR may be premature when systemic therapy only delays relapse.

**Trials cited:** NSABP B06 · Milan 1 · MILAN II · EORTC 22881

- 🔍 Acceptable LR for survival equivalent to mastectomy: 1%/yr, or 10% at 10 yrs
- 🔍 Proposed margin framework: re-excision vs RT boost alone

  | Margin status | Favors re-excision | Favors RT boost alone |
  |---|---|---|
  | Grossly positive | Generally excise; excluded from most RCTs | Not supported |
  | Focally positive | Young, little cosmetic cost; definite if <40 yrs + EIC | Older, high systemic risk, or anterior/posterior margin |
  | Close (<2 mm) | <40 yrs + EIC; benefit uncertain but probably best | Re-excision <2 mm and declined or inappropriate (BCCA) |
  | Negative (>2 mm) | Not needed | Boost only if <50 y or grade 3 |

- 📊 Fox Chase retrospective, 1262 pts (T1-T2), LR by margin; boost 14 / 18 / 20 Gy for negative / close / positive

  | LR | Negative (>2 mm) | Close (<2 mm) | Positive |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 5 yrs | 4% | 7% | 5% |
  | 10 yrs | 7% | 14% | 12% |

- 📊 EORTC 22881, 251 margin-positive pts, 11 year follow-up: boost dose vs LR and 10-yr fibrosis

  | Endpoint | 10 Gy boost (low dose) | 26 Gy boost (high dose) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Local relapse | 17.5% | 10.8% |
  | Severe fibrosis | 3% | 14% |
  | Moderate or severe fibrosis | 27% | 68% |

- 📊 EORTC boost trial, negative margins, 5318 pts, at 5 yrs

  | Endpoint | No boost | 16 Gy boost |
  |---|---|---|
  | Local relapse | 7.3% | 4.3% (p<0.001) |
  | Cosmesis | 86% | 71% |

- 📊 Post hoc EORTC path review (Jones, JCO 2009), 1616 pts: margin status not significant on MVA for LR
- 📊 FCCC close/positive anterior or posterior margins, 200 pts: 10-yr actuarial LR 5%, median f/u 7 years
- 📊 Residual disease on re-excision by positive-margin extent (Neuschatz, Cancer 2002)
  - Focal: 30%
  - Minimal: 45%
  - Moderate: 70%
  - Extensive: 85%
- 🔍 Re-excision more strongly recommended (BCCA) when margins positive or <2 mm plus:
  - Age <40 yrs, extensive DCIS (EIC), lobular histology, LVI
  - No systemic therapy, or low systemic risk
  - Multiple margins involved, or margin status unknown
- ⚠️ No RCT of boost vs re-excision; MILAN II (QUART vs TART) is the nearest proxy
- ⚠️ Close/positive LR figures come from retrospective series with differing margin definitions
- ⚠️ Slides describe EORTC 22881 as 10 vs 25 Gy, then report a 26 Gy arm; dose label inconsistent in source

### Using Oncotype DX for Radiotherapy Decisions in Breast Cancer

> Oncotype RS is prognostic for LRR in ER+ disease across retrospective trial subsets, but RT omission on RS alone belongs on trial, not in clinic.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable RT signal is post-mastectomy without RT: RS 18+ vs 0-17 LRR 11.1% vs 1.5% in 1-3 LN+ (p=0.051), with no separation at >3 LN+ (p=0.27). That places RS inside the genuinely contested pN1 PMRT/RNI decision and out of the 4+ node decision, the same population Tailor RT tests.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Wendy A. Woodward
> The speaker argued that the 21-gene RS, built to predict distant recurrence and chemo benefit, also tracks locoregional recurrence in ER+ disease, citing secondary analyses of B-14/B-20, B-28 and SWOG 8814. In the SWOG 8814 subset, RS 18+ predicted LRR on multivariable analysis alongside 4+ nodes. The speaker cautioned that the EORTC 22922 survival benefit was independent of LRR, so an LRR-only biomarker may be too narrow, and that omitting RT on RS alone belongs on trial. The proposed use is RS as one input among clinical factors in a nodal RT algorithm, not a standalone gate.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every RS-LRR dataset is a retrospective secondary analysis of a systemic-therapy trial; none randomized RT, so RS is shown to be prognostic for LRR, never predictive of RT benefit.
> - The SWOG 8814 cohort is 316 pts drawn from optional tumor banking within a 1477-pt trial, with BCT events too few to report, so selection and a mostly mastectomy-without-RT population shape the signal.
> - The cutpoint of 18 was found by exploratory analysis in the same data, and the RS hazard ratio's lower bound of 1.02 leaves the multivariable estimate fragile.
> - Tamoxifen-era cohorts with older axillary practice and no modern endocrine or nodal staging may overstate the LRR gap a contemporary pN1 pt faces.

**Trials cited:** B-14 · B-20 · B-28 · SWOG 8814 · MA-20 · EORTC 22922 · LUMINA · PRECISION

- 🔍 RS linked to LRR in node-negative NSABP B-14/B-20 (retrospective secondary analysis)
- 🔍 RS linked to LRR in node-positive NSABP B-28, all pts on chemo (retrospective secondary analysis)
- 🔍 SWOG 8814 RS validation cohort (ER and/or PR+, node+, post-menopausal, tamoxifen ± CAF)
  - Parent trial N = 1477; RS available from optional tumor banking, N = 367
  - Excluded: mastectomy + RT (37), BCS without RT (9), unknown surgery (5)
  - Analyzed N = 316; RS 18+ (n=195) vs 0-17 (n=121)
  - RS cutpoint of 18 chosen by exploratory analysis in the same data
- 📐 LRR on multivariable analysis, SWOG 8814 RS subset

  | Variable | HR | 95% CI | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Mod/High RS | 2.36 | 1.02-5.45 | 0.04 |
  | Nodes 4+ | 3.37 | 1.69-6.72 | 0.001 |
  | Mastectomy | 1.10 | 0.45-2.71 | 0.82 |
  | Chemo | 0.58 | 0.29-1.15 | 0.12 |

- 📊 LRR, mastectomy without RT, by RS

  | Subgroup | RS 18+ | RS 0-17 | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All mastectomy, n=160 vs 92 | 16.8% | 7.7% | 0.025 |
  | 1-3 LN+, n=100 vs 65 | 11.1% | 1.5% | 0.051 |

- ⚠️ >3 LN+ after mastectomy: no RS separation (27% and 25.9%, p=0.27); nodal burden dominates
- ⚠️ BCT results not presented due to insufficient events, so the signal is mainly post-mastectomy without RT
- 🔗 EORTC 22922 IM-MS RT (50Gy): 15-yr breast cancer mortality 19.8% → 16.0% (p=0.006)
- 🔗 The speaker reads the EORTC 22922 benefit as independent of LRR, so an LRR-only biomarker may be too narrow
- 🔍 Omission and de-escalation studies cited (open at time of talk)
  - Tailor RT: N = 2140, ER+ with 1-3 positive nodes, RS <18, regional RT vs no regional RT
  - Tailor RT stratified by RS (0-10, 11-17); mastectomy pts in no-regional-RT arm get no RT
  - LUMINA: Canada, age 55+, unifocal stage I pN0, Luminal A by IHC (Ki67 not >13%), endocrine alone after BCS
  - PRECISION and IDEA (US): one uses Oncotype RS ≤18 (age 50-69), the other Prosigna PAM50 <40 + Luminal A (age 50-75)
- 💊 The speaker's MD Anderson nodal RT algorithm uses genomic score as one factor alongside age, grade, LVSI, Ki-67 and tumor location
- 🔍 Also flagged: genomically personalized dose, RSI substituted into the LQ model

### Radiation Therapy after Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy for Breast Cancer

> PMRT after NAC has no randomized basis; decide on pre-chemo clinical stage plus ypN status, using NSABP failure patterns and MDACC retrospective series.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The PMRT call combines pre-chemo clinical stage and post-chemo nodal status; neither one decides it alone. Without PMRT, NSABP mastectomy LRR exceeded 10% with 1-3 ypN+ nodes, and MDACC cT3N0 pN+ ran 53% vs 5% 5-yr LRR without vs with PMRT. For cN1 converting to ypN0, the deck's evidence stops at consensus and pending B-51.

> [!abstract] Presentation
> The speaker argued that PMRT after neoadjuvant chemotherapy has no randomized basis, since NSABP B-18 and B-27 prohibited it, so the decision has to be built from patterns of failure and institutional series. NSABP data flag cT3+, cN+, ypT+ and ypN+ (plus age <50 after BCS) as LRR predictors. MDACC retrospective series show PMRT lowering LRR and improving CSS in cT3-T4, cN2/3 and heavily node-positive disease. The proposed framework: PMRT for advanced clinical stage or residual nodal disease, consideration for stage II with pN+ or young age, and omission for stage II pts reaching pCR, as in the illustrative TNBC case.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every recommendation rests on retrospective or secondary data; the MDACC PMRT comparison was non-randomized (542 vs 132), so pts who skipped PMRT may have been selected on prognostic features.
> - NSABP LRR baselines predate receptor testing and HER2-directed therapy, gave tamoxifen regardless of ER/PR and excluded N2, so their absolute risks may not transfer to subtype-directed NAC.
> - In the case, the only positive node was a micrometastasis excised by SLNB before NAC, so ypN0 after ALND does not measure nodal response, yet nodal pCR is what the omission rests on.
> - The deck predates the B-51/RTOG 1304 readout, which has since reported no invasive recurrence benefit from regional nodal RT in cN1 converting to ypN0; the framework should be re-read against it.

**Trials cited:** NSABP B-18 · NSABP B-27 · ACOSOG Z1070 · SENTINA · NSABP B51/RTOG 1304 · Alliance A11202 · RAPCHEM

- 🔍 No RCT of PMRT after NAC: NSABP B-18 and B-27 prohibited PMRT
- 🔍 Framework built from NSABP patterns of failure plus MDACC institutional series
- 📊 NSABP B-18/B-27 LRR without PMRT (Mamounas JCO 2012)

  | Surgery | N | Overall LRR | Local | Regional |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Mastectomy | 1,947 | 12.6% | 9.0% | 3.6% |
  | Lumpectomy + breast RT | 1,100 | 10.3% | 8.1% | 2.2% |

- 📊 NSABP MVA predictors of LRR
  - Mastectomy: cT3+, cN+, ypT+, ypN+
  - BCS: same factors plus age <50
  - ypN+ with 1-3 positive nodes: LRR >10% (except cN0 ≥50yo with BCS)
- ⚠️ NSABP caveats limit transfer to modern NAC
  - No ER/PR/HER2 status
  - No N2 pts
  - Tamoxifen for all B-27 pts regardless of ER/PR status
- 📊 MDACC 10-yr LRR by subgroup, non-randomized (542 PMRT vs 132 no PMRT; Huang JCO 2004)

  | Subgroup | No PMRT | PMRT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | cT3 | 24% | 8% | 0.002 |
  | cT4 | 46% | 15% | <0.0001 |

  | pT3 | 31% | 14% | 0.002 |
  | pT4 | 52% | 13% | 0.001 |
  | ≥4 pN+ | 59% | 16% | <0.0001 |

- 📊 MDACC 10-yr CSS by subgroup (Huang JCO 2004)

  | Subgroup | No PMRT | PMRT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Clinical stage ≥IIIB | 22% | 44% | 0.002 |

  | cN2/3 | 27% | 49% | 0.024 |
  | ≥4 pN+ | 18% | 44% | 0.005 |

- 📊 MDACC selected subsets: cT3N0 (Nagar IJROBP 2011) and age <35 (Garg IJROBP 2007)

  | Cohort | Endpoint | No PMRT | PMRT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | cT3N0, all (n=162) | 5-yr LRR | 24% | 4% | <0.001 |
  | cT3N0, pN+ | 5-yr LRR | 53% | 5% | <0.001 |
  | cT3N0, pN0 | 5-yr LRR | 14% | 2% | 0.06 |

- ⚠️ Age <35 cohort: no OS or LRR benefit for clinical stage IIA
- 🔍 Decision framework on the slides: MDACC vs UC Athena consensus

  | Source | PMRT indicated | Consider PMRT | Little or no benefit |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | MDACC (Hoffman, Lancet 2012) | cT3-T4; cN2-N3; most node-positive at resection | Clinical stage II with pN+, or high-risk features: age <35, ER, poor chemo response | Not stated |
  | UC Athena consensus (Fowble, IJROBP 2012) | Not stated | <40yo; advanced clinical or pathologic stage and TNBC; LVI; ECE | Clinical stage II (T1N0-1, T2N0), >40yo, ER+, pCR or 0-3 positive nodes without LVI or ECE |

- 🔍 Axillary staging after NAC drives the ypN input

  - Axillary US alone: FNR 15%
  - SENTINA: repeat SLNB only 60% sensitive
  - Clip marking, dual tracer, >2 sentinel nodes lower FNR (NCCN 3.2015)
- 🔍 Case: 53yo TNBC, 1/3 SLN micromet pre-NAC → AC-T → bilateral mastectomy + ALND, pCR; PMRT omitted

### pCR as a Surrogate Marker for Outcomes in Breast Cancer

> pCR after neoadjuvant chemo is strongly prognostic per pt but a weak trial-level surrogate; response should guide post-neoadjuvant systemic and locoregional tailoring.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT content is the closing section, where less locoregional XRT after primary and nodal downstaging, and possible omission, are listed as promises without recurrence data. GeparTrio's subtype split gates that decision: pCR is highly prognostic in HER2+ and triple-negative disease, while luminal prognosis does not depend on pCR, so ypN0 alone is a weak omission criterion.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Terry Mamounas
> The speaker argues that pCR after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is strongly prognostic for the individual pt but an unreliable trial-level surrogate. CTNeoBC anchors the case: pCR tracks with EFS and OS, less tightly when nodal status is ignored, yet the size of a pCR gain between arms did not predict the survival effect. NSABP B-27, ALTTO and GeparTrio illustrate the disconnect in both directions, and GeparTrio ties pCR's prognostic weight to HER2+ and triple-negative disease. The proposed framework uses response to tailor what follows: escalated systemic therapy for residual disease in post-neoadjuvant trials and, prospectively, de-escalated surgery and radiotherapy after downstaging.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - The title frames pCR as a surrogate, but the speaker's own CTNeoBC slide shows pCR gains did not predict EFS or OS effect; the deck establishes patient-level prognosis, not trial-level surrogacy.
> - The offered reasons for that failure (low pCR rates, heterogeneity, larger differences needed) are untested hypotheses, and the correlation slide is simulated data, so the pCR gain that predicts benefit is never shown.
> - The locoregional promises (less axillary surgery, less XRT after downstaging, omitting some locoregional therapy) come with no locoregional recurrence data, so the talk gives no evidence on the RT omission question it raises.
> - The post-neoadjuvant argument rests on KATHERINE and Penelope shown as schemas only; with the newest cited data from ASCO 2014, the deck cannot show that escalation for residual disease improves outcomes.

**Trials cited:** CTNeoBC · NSABP B-27 · NOAH · NeoALTTO · ALTTO · NSABP B-41 · NeoSphere · TRYPHAENA

- 📊 CTNeoBC: pCR vs no pCR by pCR definition

  | Definition | n | EFS HR | OS HR |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ypT0 ypN0 | 1554 | 0.44 | 0.36 |
  | ypT0/is ypN0 (preferred FDA) | 2131 | 0.48 | 0.36 |
  | ypT0/is (nodes ignored) | 2598 | 0.60 | 0.51 |

- ⚠️ CTNeoBC: magnitude of pCR improvement between arms did not predict EFS or OS effect
- 🔍 The speaker's proposed reasons for the trial-level miss
  - Low pCR rates
  - Heterogeneous populations
  - Few targeted therapies (NOAH the exception)
  - Larger between-arm pCR differences needed, possibly subtype-specific
- 📊 NSABP B-27 (2411 pts): adding docetaxel raised pCR, yet DFS HR=0.86, p=0.10 (preop) and HR=0.91, p=0.27 (postop)
- 📊 ALTTO (N=6281): L+T v T DFS HR .84, P=0.048, short of the P < 0.025 threshold
- 📊 GeparTrio pCR (ypT0 ypN0) by interim response, conventional vs response-guided

  | Group | Conventional | Response-guided | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Responders (N=1344) | TACx6 21.0 | TACx8 23.5 | 0.27 |
  | Non-responders (N=604) | TACx6 5.3 | TAC-NX 6.0 | 0.73 |

- 🔗 GeparTrio: response-guided therapy improved survival with flat pCR; the benefit came from luminal tumors, whose prognosis does not depend on pCR
- 💊 Post-neoadjuvant setting pitched as ideal for testing new agents
  - Enrolls only high-risk residual disease, proven resistant to existing therapy
  - High event rate → small sample size, short f/u
  - KATHERINE: residual invasive tumor, T-DM1 vs Herceptin; radiation per standard guidance
  - Penelope (NSABP B-54-I): N=800 HR+/HER2- without pCR, CPS-EG score ≥3, palbociclib vs placebo
- 🔍 Locoregional section: less axillary surgery and less XRT after downstaging, possible omission of surgery or XRT. No supporting data shown

### Treatment Planning Techniques for Larger Body Habitus Breast/Chestwall and Nodal RT

> In obese pts needing breast/chest wall plus nodal RT, escalate stepwise from 3D extended tangents to photon/electron mix, VMAT, or prone by anatomy and constraints.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable content is the set of switch points: tangents stay 3D until separation exceeds ~23cm (add 18x) or ~4cm or more of lung enters the field, electron IMN fields work only when nodes sit less than ~5cm deep, and VMAT is reserved for 3D plans failing B51/RTOG 1304 constraints. ABC added 46.7% ipsilateral lung volume in one example.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Ruth Ann M. Good
> The speaker presented a single-institution planning playbook for breast/chest wall and regional nodal RT in obese pts (225lbs-363lbs) at 50Gy/25fractions, judged against B51/RTOG 1304 dose constraints. The framework escalates by anatomy: single-isocenter extended tangents first, a photon/electron mix with an electron IMN field when the contralateral breast or too much lung enters the tangents, VMAT when 3D plans fail protocol constraints, and prone positioning for very pendulous breasts. ABC breath hold is used to cut lung and heart dose on 3D plans. Each step rests on worked case examples with DVHs, not on outcome data.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every recommendation rests on single-case plan examples from one institution; no cohort dosimetry, toxicity, or locoregional control data support choosing one technique over another.
> - Switch thresholds (separation >23cm, lung ~4cm or greater, IMN depth less than ~5cm, electron width >3.5cm) are presented as rules without stated derivation or validation in a larger series.
> - The VMAT example trades lower ipsilateral lung dose for higher heart dose, and ABC is not currently used with arcs; whether breath hold with arcs removes that trade is left untested.
> - Proton therapy, an option for nodal RT when heart and lung constraints conflict, is not discussed, nor is hypofractionated nodal RT in this population.

**Trials cited:** B51/RTOG 1304

- 🔍 4 techniques for obese/extreme obese pts (weight range 225lbs-363lbs), breast/chest wall + regional nodal RT, 50Gy/25fractions
- 🔍 Decision framework: which technique, when

  | Technique | Fields | Use when | Key constraints |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Single isocenter extended tangents (3D) | 4 | Simplest 3D total nodal plan; tangents clear contralateral breast yet cover IMN PTV | Add higher photon energy if >23cm separation; tangent 20cm or less in length |
  | Photon/electron mix (3D) | 5 (8 total with dual energy) | Contralateral breast in extended tangent, or ~4cm or greater lung in tangents | IMN depth less than ~5cm for 16e or less; IM electron field >3.5cm wide |
  | Photon/electron, split mixed-energy IMC (3D) | 6 | Left side, heart mean dose target <5Gy | 16e upper, 12e lower; 1cm matchline changes after 8th and 16th fraction |
  | VMAT arcs | 2 split arcs (4 fields total) | 3D plans do not meet protocol dose constraints | ABC not currently used; may add esophagus, stomach, liver, airway ROIs |
  | Prone total nodal (3D) | 4 | Very pendulous breasts | Pt must tolerate >30 minutes on table |

- 🔍 Sim: supine on wing board, custom vac-bag, both arms up, head turned contralateral
- 📊 ABC breath hold: 46.7% increase in ipsilateral lung volume (1077cc vs 1580cc) in one example
- 🔍 Extended tangents: isocenter ~1-2cm from lateral chest wall; 6x/18x for separations larger than ~23cm
- 📊 Split-energy IMC electrons (16e upper, 12e lower) held heart mean dose at 4.12Gy
- 📊 VMAT example: lower ipsilateral lung dose than photon/electron mix, at the cost of higher heart dose
- ⚠️ Prone left total nodal plan left inferior IMNs uncovered, heart dose prioritized
- ⚠️ Photon/electron first-day imaging plus treatment timeline ran 16min 20secs
- ⚠️ No toxicity or locoregional control data; every comparison is a single-case plan example

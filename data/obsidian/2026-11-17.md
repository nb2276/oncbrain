# 2026-11-17

**PMRT after NAC: no RCTs exist; MDACC cT3N0 series shows 5-yr LRR 24% without vs 4% with PMRT, so clinical stage plus ypN gates the RT decision.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Breast RT education day: talks, not new trial readouts. Common thread is prognostic markers standing in for RT decisions: Oncotype RS is prognostic for LRR but unproven as RT-predictive (Tailor RT tests omission), pCR predicts individual but not trial-level outcome, and post-NAC PMRT rests on stage plus ypN without RCTs. Margins and large-habitus RNI planning round it out.

## 🎀 Breast

All five are educational talks. The RT decisions at stake are regional RT omission by RS, PMRT after NAC, boost vs re-excision for close or positive margins, and nodal technique in obese pts.

### Using Oncotype DX for Radiotherapy Decisions in Breast Cancer (Woodward)

> 21-gene RS is prognostic for LRR in ER+ breast cancer, but whether it predicts RT benefit is untested; RS-based omission of regional RT belongs on trial.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> In mastectomy pts without RT, the 1-3 LN+ panel, where PMRT is actually contested, separated only at p = 0.051 with 65 low-RS pts; nodal burden dominated in the >3 LN+ group (p = 0.27). EORTC 22922's mortality benefit independent of LRR means an LRR-calibrated score can miss what RNI delivers.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Wendy A. Woodward
> The speaker argued that the 21-gene RS is prognostic for locoregional recurrence in ER+ breast cancer, drawing on secondary analyses of B-14/B-20 in node-negative and B-28 and SWOG 8814 in node-positive disease. In the SWOG 8814 subset, Mod/High RS carried an LRR HR 2.36 on multivariable analysis. The speaker cautioned that LRR may be too narrow a target, since EORTC 22922 showed a mortality benefit from nodal RT independent of LRR. The proposed framework: use RS alongside clinical risk factors for RNI and PMRT decisions, and test RS-based omission on trial, as Tailor RT does.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every RS-LRR association comes from trials that randomized systemic therapy, not RT, so the data establish prognosis only; a low-risk score does not show a small RT benefit.
> - The SWOG 8814 analysis rests on 316 of 1477 pts from optional tumor banking, a selected subset, and the RS HR lower bound of 1.02 leaves little margin.
> - The contested PMRT group, mastectomy with 1-3 LN+, separated only at p = 0.051 with 65 low-RS pts, and the cutpoint of 18 was fitted in the same data.
> - All cohorts are tamoxifen-era with or without CAF; baseline LRR under modern endocrine and systemic therapy may differ, shifting any RS threshold for RT omission.

**Trials cited:** B-14 · B-20 · B-28 · SWOG 8814 · MA-20 · EORTC 22922 · LUMINA · PRECISION

- 🔍 Thesis: RS predicts LRR risk; it has not been shown to predict benefit from RT
- 🔍 Evidence base: secondary analyses of B-14/B-20 (node-negative), B-28 and SWOG 8814 (node-positive)
- ⚠️ None of these trials randomized RT; systemic therapy was the randomized question
- 🔍 SWOG 8814 subset: RS available in 367 of 1477; analysis cohort 316 after excluding mastectomy + RT (37), BCS without RT (9), unknown surgery (5)
- 📐 SWOG 8814 subset, multivariable model for LRR

  | Variable | HR | 95% CI | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Mod/High RS | 2.36 | 1.02-5.45 | 0.04 |
  | Nodes 4+ | 3.37 | 1.69-6.72 | 0.001 |
  | Mastectomy | 1.10 | 0.45-2.71 | 0.82 |
  | Chemo | 0.58 | 0.29-1.15 | 0.12 |

- 📊 LRR by RS 18+ vs 0-17 (KM slides)

  | Cohort | n (RS 18+ / 0-17) | LRR RS 18+ vs 0-17 | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All pts | 195 / 121 | 16.8% vs 9.7% | 0.018 |

- 📊 Univariate 10-yr LRR (log-rank)
  - RS 0-17 vs 18+: 10% vs 17%, p 0.018
  - Nodes 1-3 vs 4+: 9% vs 24%, p 0.0002
  - Age, HER2, grade, T vs CAF-T: NS
- ⚠️ Cutpoint 18 confirmed by exploratory analysis in the same cohort; intermediate and high RS pooled
- *BCT results not presented on the slides due to insufficient events
- 🔗 EORTC 22922 (IM-MS RT 50Gy): 15-yr breast-cancer mortality 19.8% to 16.0% (p=0.006), independent of LRR
- ⚠️ Speaker's own caveat: an LRR-only focus may be too narrow, since RT benefit is independent of LRR
- 🔍 Tailor RT (N = 2140): ER+ 1-3 LN+, RS <18, regional RT vs no regional RT; stratified by RS 0-10 vs 11-17
- 🔍 Omission cohorts (not SOC): LUMINA (Luminal A by IHC, Ki67 not >13%), plus US cohorts selecting on Oncotype RS ≤18 or PAM50 <40
- 💊 MDACC RNI/PMRT algorithm uses genomic score as one modifier among age, grade, LVSI, nodal burden and medial location, not as a standalone gate
- 🔍 Beyond RS: genomically adjusted RT dose via RSI in the LQ model (Scott, Lancet Oncol 2017)

### Radiation Therapy after Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy for Breast Cancer

> No RCTs exist for PMRT after NAC, so the speaker gates RT on clinical stage plus ypN status, drawing on NSABP patterns of failure and MDACC retrospective series.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The contested RT decision is omission for cN+ converting to ypN0. NSABP shows LRR >10% only once 1-3 nodes remain positive, and MDACC cT3N0 pN0 shows just a trend (14% vs 2%, p=0.06). The talk makes clinical stage plus ypN status, not breast pCR alone, the gate for PMRT and nodal coverage.

> [!abstract] Presentation
> The speaker argued that PMRT after neoadjuvant chemo has no randomized evidence, because NSABP B-18 and B-27 prohibited it. The decision therefore rests on those trials' patterns of failure and on MDACC retrospective series. In this reading, LRR risk follows both clinical presentation (cT3+, cN+, age <50) and pathologic response (ypN+), with 1-3 residual positive nodes carrying LRR >10%. The talk put the MDACC and UC Athena recommendation sets side by side. It closed on a TNBC cT2N1 case with pCR after mastectomy, in which PMRT was omitted, and presented B51/RTOG 1304, A11202 and RAPCHEM as the trials that will settle omission.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every PMRT benefit estimate shown comes from non-randomized MDACC series (542 vs 132 pts), where receipt of PMRT reflected physician selection across 1974-2000. Confounding by indication could inflate or deflate those LRR and CSS gaps.
> - The NSABP patterns-of-failure data lack ER/PR/HER2 status and N2 disease. The omission decision the talk builds toward therefore has no subtype-specific LRR estimates, even though the deck itself shows pCR carries prognostic weight only in some subtypes.
> - The closing TNBC case with pCR sits outside UC Athena's low-risk set, which requires ER+ disease. Omission there rests on clinical judgment rather than on either framework presented.
> - The deck lists NSABP B51/RTOG 1304 as ongoing and cites NCCN 3.2015. Its framework for cN+ converting to ypN0 predates that randomized readout and modern HER2-directed and higher-pCR regimens.

**Trials cited:** NSABP B-18 · NSABP B-27 · ACOSOG Z1070 · SENTINA · Alliance A11202 · NSABP B51/RTOG 1304 · RAPCHEM

- 🔍 No RCTs for PMRT after NAC. NSABP B-18 and B-27 prohibited PMRT.
- 🔍 The usable trial data are patterns of failure: NSABP LRR without PMRT (Mamounas JCO 2012).

- 🔍 NSABP multivariable predictors of LRR

  - BCS: same factors plus age <50
- 📊 ypN+ with 1-3 positive nodes: LRR >10% in NSABP (except cN0 pts ≥50yo with BCS)
- ⚠️ NSABP caveats: no ER/PR/HER2 status, no N2 disease, tamoxifen given regardless of ER/PR in B-27

### Margins: Impact on Local Control, Treatment Options (Tyldesley)

> Close or positive margins raise local relapse; re-excise by default, but individualize with an RT boost for focal, anterior/posterior or older, high-systemic-risk pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT lever is boost dose, and the talk prices it: in the EORTC 22881 positive-margin RCT, 10 Gy vs 26 Gy boost gave 17.5% vs 10.8%, with severe fibrosis 3% vs 14% for low vs high dose. With negative (>2 mm) margins the speaker reserves a boost for <50 y or grade 3.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Scott Tyldesley
> The speaker anchors margin management in breast-conserving therapy to a relapse budget: BCT matched mastectomy survival while in-breast relapse stayed near 1%/yr or 10% at 10 yrs. Retrospective series show higher recurrence with close or positive margins, and boost trials show better control at a cosmetic cost. The framework: excise grossly positive margins, individualize focally positive and close (< 2 mm) margins by age, EIC, histology, margin location and systemic risk, and skip re-excision for negative (> 2 mm) margins, boosting only the young or grade 3. The simple answer is re-excision; the subtle answer is individualize.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Margin-specific recurrence data come from retrospective single-institution series in which who was left unre-excised was chosen by surgeon and pt, so the series cannot show what re-excision would have bought.
> - The talk concedes no RCT compares boost with re-excision; MILAN II changed extent of surgery and RT together, so it cannot isolate boost against re-excision either.
> - The > 2 mm negative definition and re-excision default for close margins predate the SSO-ASTRO consensus adopting no ink on tumor for invasive disease, and the framework is not reconciled with it.
> - The positive-margin boost RCT closed early with 251 pts, and its high dose reads 25Gy on one slide and 26 Gy on another, leaving the dose-response estimate underpowered and imprecisely specified.

**Trials cited:** NSABP B06 · Milan 1 · MILAN II · EORTC 22881

- 🔗 Benchmark: BCT matched mastectomy OS in 6 RCTs; acceptable in-breast relapse 1%/yr or 10% at 10 yrs
- 🔍 Margin definitions used (vary across series)
  - Negative: > 2 mm
  - Close: not at ink but within 2 mm
  - Positive: at ink; limited vs extensive definitions vary
- 📊 10-yr local recurrence by margin status, retrospective series

  | Series (n) | Negative | Close | Positive |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | IGR (757) | 6% | NA | 14% |
  | FCCC (1262) | 7% | 14% | 12% |
  | Tufts (498) | 2% | 2% | 15% |
  | Curie, large tumors (257) | 17% | 32% | 24% |

- 🔍 Speaker's framework: re-excision vs RT boost by margin status

  | Margin | Re-excision | RT boost |
  |---|---|---|
  | Grossly positive | Should be excised | Not addressed; excluded from most RCTs |
  | Focally positive | If young with little cosmetic cost; definitely if <40 yrs + EIC | Boost alone if older, high systemic risk, or ant/post margin |
  | Close (<2 mm) | Probably best; re-excise if <40 yrs + EIC | Boost if re-excision declined or inappropriate (BCCA) |

### pCR as a Surrogate Marker for Outcomes in Breast Cancer (Mamounas)

> pCR after NAC predicts outcome for the individual pt, most strongly in HER2+ and TNBC. Larger pCR gains between trial arms did not predict larger EFS or OS gains.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Nodal clearance carries the prognostic weight. In CTNeoBC, a breast-only pCR definition (ypT0/is, nodes irrespective) had a weaker EFS association (HR 0.60) than ypT0/is ypN0 (HR 0.48). The talk names smaller RT fields after downstaging only as a promise, with no outcome data, so tailoring regional RT to pCR remains a question for trials.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Terry Mamounas
> The speaker argued that pCR after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a strong prognostic marker for the individual pt. It is strongest when the nodes are cleared and in HER2+ and triple-negative disease. Larger pCR gains between trial arms, however, have not reliably predicted EFS or OS gains. The case rests on the CTNeoBC pooled analysis and on trials where pCR and long-term outcome came apart: NSABP B-27, NeoALTTO/ALTTO and GeparTrio. From this the speaker proposed using residual disease to select high-risk pts for post-neoadjuvant trials. Response-adapted reduction of axillary surgery and locoregional RT was framed as a future promise.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - The deck's own CTNeoBC slide shows pCR gains did not predict EFS or OS gains. That weakens pCR as an accelerated-approval endpoint more than the talk's opening slides admit.
> - KATHERINE and Penelope appear only as schemas, and the newest data cited is ALTTO. The post-neoadjuvant thesis is argued from hypotheses rather than completed outcomes.
> - The locoregional section lists reductions in axillary surgery and RT fields with no data under any of them. Nodal downstaging as a basis for omitting RT is unsupported within this talk.
> - GeparTrio's benefit came from luminal tumors, where pCR did not predict it. The talk asks for better early predictors but shows none validated, so response-guided therapy in HR+ disease has no working marker.

**Trials cited:** CTNeoBC · NSABP B-27 · NOAH · NeoALTTO · ALTTO · NSABP B-41 · NeoSphere · TRYPHAENA

- 📊 CTNeoBC: pCR (ypT0/is ypN0) associated with EFS HR=0.48 and OS HR=0.36, nominal P < 0.001
- 📐 CTNeoBC: pCR vs no pCR by definition (EFS/OS HRs)

  | pCR definition | n | EFS HR | OS HR |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ypT0 ypN0 | 1554 | 0.44 | 0.36 |
  | ypT0/is ypN0 (preferred FDA) | 2131 | 0.48 | 0.36 |
  | ypT0/is, nodes irrespective | 2598 | 0.60 | 0.51 |

- ⚠️ CTNeoBC: the size of a pCR gain between arms did not predict the EFS or OS effect
- 🔍 Reasons the speaker gives for the trial-level miss
  - Low pCR rates
  - Heterogeneous populations
  - Few targeted therapies (NOAH the exception)
  - Larger pCR differences needed, possibly varying by subtype
- 🔗 NSABP B-27 (2411 pts): docetaxel raised pCR without a significant DFS gain
  - Preop docetaxel (ACTS): HR=0.86, p=0.10
  - Postop docetaxel (ACST): HR=0.91, p=0.27
- 🔗 ALTTO, L+T v T: DFS HR .84, P=0.048, against a prespecified P < 0.025 threshold
- 🔗 By hormone-receptor status: pCR differs across HR status, but the trastuzumab benefit does not

  | HR status | TRYPHAENA ypT0/is pCR % (3 arms) | B-31/N9831 DFS HR |
  |---|---|---|
  | ER- and PR-negative | 65.0, 79.4, 83.8 | 0.62 (95%CI 0.52-0.73) |
  | ER- and/or PR-positive | 46.2, 48.6, 50.0 | 0.61 (95%CI 0.51-0.72) |

- 📊 GeparTrio (N=2072): response-guided NAC did not change pCR (ypT0 ypN0)

  | Cohort | TACx6 pCR % | Response-guided pCR % | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Responders (N=1344) | 21.0 | 23.5 (TACx8) | 0.27 |
  | Non-responders (N=604) | 5.3 | 6.0 (TAC-NX) | 0.73 |

- 📊 GeparTrio: the survival benefit came from luminal tumors, where pCR did not predict it
- ⚠️ GeparTrio: HER2+ and triple-negative pts did not benefit, despite pCR being highly prognostic there
- 🔍 Post-neoadjuvant trials enrolling pts with residual disease after NAC
  - KATHERINE: T-DM1 vs Herceptin for residual invasive HER2+ disease
  - Penelope (NSABP B-54-I): N=800, HR+/HER2-, no pCR, CPS-EG score ≥3, palbociclib vs placebo
- ⚠️ Smaller axillary surgery and reduced locoregional RT after downstaging are presented as promises, with no outcome data shown

### Treatment Planning Techniques for Larger Body Habitus, Breast/Chestwall and RNI (Good)

> Larger-habitus breast/chestwall RNI planning escalates from single-isocenter extended tangents to photon/electron mix, VMAT, or prone, triggered by separation, lung-in-field and IMN depth.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable content is the trigger set: >23cm separation prompts mixed 6x/18x tangents, ~4cm or greater lung in tangents prompts an electron IMC match (feasible at IMN depth under ~5cm with 16e or less), and VMAT is reserved for 3D constraint failure. The prone example traded inferior IMN coverage for heart dose, a coverage call the treating physician owns.

> [!abstract] Presentation · Ruth Ann M. Good
> The speaker presented a dosimetrist's playbook for breast/chestwall plus regional nodal irradiation in obese and extreme obese pts, using institutional cases planned to 50Gy/25 fractions against NSABP B-51/RTOG 1304 constraints. Single-isocenter extended tangents are the starting point. Escalation follows anatomic triggers: higher photon energy above 23cm separation, a photon/electron IMC match when lung or contralateral breast intrudes on the tangents, VMAT when 3D plans miss constraints, and prone positioning for very pendulous breasts. ABC breath hold is used with 3D plans to spare heart and lung. The framework rests on worked plan examples and DVHs, not on cohort dosimetry or clinical outcomes.

> [!warning] Adversarial read
> - Every recommendation rests on individual worked cases from one institution; no cohort-level dosimetric comparison across the four techniques is shown.
> - The escalation thresholds (23cm separation, ~4cm lung, ~5cm IMN depth) are presented as institutional rules of thumb without data showing they predict plan failure.
> - The prone example accepted inferior IMN undercoverage for heart sparing, a target-coverage tradeoff the talk does not weigh against regional control risk.
> - VMAT is offered without ABC and without reporting low-dose lung or contralateral breast spread, so its cost relative to 3D mixed-beam plans stays unquantified.

**Trials cited:** B-51 · RTOG 1304

- 🔍 Dosimetry talk, single institution (VCU), case-based examples; no outcome data
- 🔍 All cases in obese/extreme obese BMI range, weight 225lbs-363lbs
- 🔍 Contours per RTOG Breast Cancer Atlas; constraints from NSABP B-51/RTOG 1304; 50Gy/25 fractions
- 📊 Technique selection framework (speaker's conclusions slide)

  | Technique | Use when | Key limits / parameters |
  |---|---|---|
  | Single isocenter extended tangents (3D, 4 fields) | Default, simplest 3D total nodal plan | Clear contralateral breast yet cover IMN PTV; >23cm separation add higher energy; tangent 20cm or less in length |
  | Photon/electron mix (3D, 5 fields) | Contralateral breast in extended tangent field, or ~4cm or greater lung in tangents | IMN depth less than ~5cm to use 16 electrons or less; electron IM field >3.5cm wide |
  | Arc / VMAT (2 split arcs, 4 fields) | 3D plans fail protocol dose constraints | May split arcs left to right for width/length; ABC not used; add esophagus, stomach, liver, airway ROIs |
  | Prone total nodal (3D, 4 fields) | Very pendulous breasts | Pt must tolerate >30 minutes on table |

- 📊 ABC breath hold: ipsilateral lung 1077cc free breathing vs 1580cc, 46.7% increase in lung volume
- 🔍 Split mixed-energy IMC electron variant
  - Upper field 16e, lower field 12e, to keep heart mean <5Gy
  - Two 1cm matchline changes, after the 8th and 16th fraction
  - Example heart mean dose 4.12Gy
- 📊 VMAT vs photon/electron example: arcs lowered ipsilateral lung dose (to 20Gy, metric unstated); photon/electron kept heart lower
- ⚠️ Prone left-sided total nodal example did not cover inferior IMNs, heart dose prioritized
- ⚠️ Photon/electron plan day-1 setup with double-exposure portals took 16min 20secs

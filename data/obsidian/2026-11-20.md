ReTEC cord reirradiation modeling implies faster recovery than Nelson 2009, but the 1-yr CI is 31%-95%; authors advise staying within current guidelines.

## 📊 Cross-cutting

HyTEC methods primer: a two-parameter logistic fit needs ≥20 events, and median-split screens give different answers depending on the test chosen.

### A Primer on Dose-Response Data Modeling in Radiation Therapy

> HyTEC lung example: 13/96 grade 2+ RP; logistic MLE fit beat the null by LL 6.66 (p=0.0003); single cut-points imply dependence that may not exist.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The tails of a dose-response curve, where serial-organ constraints and escalation arguments live, are the least data-constrained: with very low or very high event rates, predicted risk swings with small γ50 shifts. Read a HyTEC constraint by its NPV and data range, not the fit p-value, and treat extrapolation past the treated dose range as unvalidated.

- 🔍 Methods review from the HyTEC effort; worked example pools 2 published lung datasets, grade 2+ RP vs MLD
- 📊 13/96 events (13.5%); median MLD 3.05 Gy
- 📐 Rule of thumb cited: ≥10 events per model parameter, ≥20 for a two-parameter logistic fit
- 🔍 Four-step workflow and the method used for each

  | Step | Method | Worked-example output |
  |---|---|---|
  | Best-fit parameters | Maximum likelihood (logistic) | MLL -31.41 |
  | Parameter CIs | Profile likelihood (MLL minus 1.92) | D50 5.05-9.04; γ50 0.73-1.77 |
  | Significance vs no relationship | Likelihood ratio vs Pave | LL(Pave) -38.07; difference 6.66, p=0.0003 |
  | Prediction CIs | Bootstrap resampling | 2000 histories; 68% and 95% bands |

- ⚠️ Median-split screen depends on which test is picked
  - Several tests p<0.05
  - Fisher exact two-tailed p=0.070
  - Wilcoxon rank sum p=0.002 when assumptions go unchecked
- 📊 Setting 10% toxicity as acceptable gives MLD 3.27 Gy; NPV for MLD<3.27 Gy 52/56 = 92.9%
- ⚠️ D50 and γ50 are correlated ('banana' iso-LL); a CI rectangle from the two separate CIs is wrong
- ⚠️ Low-event (spinal cord) or high-control (prostate TCP) data sit far from D50; predictions hinge on small γ50 shifts
- ⚠️ No volume effect modeled: pooled literature lacks full DVH, so MLD cannot be converted to mean EQD2

## 🧠 CNS

The pooled ReTEC model favors faster cord recovery than Nelson 2009, but the uncertainty is too wide to loosen reirradiation limits.

### ReTEC spinal cord reirradiation NTCP

> Pooled 282 lesions (six myelopathies): cord recovery >50% in <1 yr, but 1-yr CI 31%-95%; authors advise staying within current reirradiation guidelines.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The recovery factor and the NTCP curve refitted after it work only as a pair. Mixing them gives 27% or <0.001% risk, while each matched set puts the reference case under 1%. So a recovery discount loaded into planning software has to carry its own dose-response fit, and the interval decision still rests on HyTEC and Nelson limits.

- 📊 NTCP model from 13 papers, 282 lesions, six myelopathy cases
- 📊 ML fit: tRH 4 mo, Rmax 95% (p<0.01, LRT); recovery >50% in <1 yr
- 📐 Recovery at a 1-yr interval: bootstrap 95% CI 31%-95%
- 📊 Reference case (cord Dmax 50 Gy/25 fx, then 15.5 Gy/5 fx at 1 yr) by recovery scenario

  | Scenario | Recovery assumption | Composite BED2 | Example-case risk |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | No recovery | None | 140 Gy | <1% |
  | Nelson 2009 | 33% at 1 yr | 107 Gy | <1% |
  | ML fit | tRH 4 mo, Rmax 95% | 60 Gy | <1% |

- ⚠️ Recovery factor and refitted NTCP curve only hold as a matched set
  - No-recovery dose (140 Gy) on ML curve: 27% risk
  - ML dose (60 Gy) on no-recovery curve: <0.001% risk
- 🔍 Inclusion: per-pt prior and reirradiation cord Dmax, interval, fractionation or BED, ≥1 SBRT course
- 🔍 Clinical demo with 33% recovery: composite 5-fx equivalent cord Dmax 28 Gy vs TG-101 30 Gy/5 fx
- 🔗 vs Nelson 2009: ML recovery faster and more complete than the 33% at 1 yr in clinical use
- ⚠️ Dmax-only inputs; no voxel-wise composite reirradiation plans found in the literature

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 21587185]] **Reirradiation treatment effects in the clinic (ReTEC) proposal – proof of concept based on spinal cord dose tolerance for reirradiation with stereotactic body radiotherapy** — Grimm, Jimm; Mahadevan, Anand; Obayomi‐Davies, Olusola et al.. *Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics* (2026-04). [doi:10.1002/acm2.70557](https://doi.org/10.1002/acm2.70557)
  > Abstract Background Since the inception of spinal radiosurgery decades ago, the factors influencing spinal cord recovery have remained unclear. Elucidating these factors would aid in reirradiation planning and delivery. We describe modeling reirradiation normal tissue complication probabilities (NTCP) from compiled published data, akin to the collaborative Quantitative Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic (QUANTEC) and High Dose per Fraction, Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic (HyTEC) projects. Purpose Simple dose‐time recovery factors for the spinal cord serve as a basis to propose the Reirradiation Treatment Effects in the Clinic (ReTEC) project. ReTEC aims to define human organ recovery factors to convert the total composite dose to an equivalent physical dose compatible with the de novo therapy dose tolerance limits. Additionally, we present a system for automating analyses as well as clinical implementation of radiation dose‐time recovery. Methods Published literature was queried for stereotactic voxel‐wise composite reirradiation plans involving the spinal cord, but none were found. Hence, minimum reporting criteria were used instead: spinal cord maximum dose (Dmax) of each course, time interval between courses, and number of fractions or biologically effective dose, per patient or in sufficiently homogeneous groups of patients. Recovery factors and logistic model parameters were fitted with maximum likelihood techniques. Results An NTCP model was derived from 13 papers with data from 282 lesions, including six myelopathy cases. The model suggested spinal cord recovery of more than 50% in less than 1 year, which is faster and more complete than current clinical practice, but with wide confidence intervals (CI) (bootstrap 95% CI: 31%–95% when the time interval was 1 year). Conclusion Due to limited data, we recommend remaining within current standard guidelines until more comprehensive analyses, optimally under the ReTEC initiative, refute or validate these estimates. ReTEC would require composite plans accounting for differing Dmax locations in each course. Nevertheless, this proof‐of‐concept Dmax model provides insight and serves as a basis to propose ReTEC.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

In pooled, reconstructed lesion-level data, BED >100 Gy10 matters for liver mets but not for HCC/CCA primaries.

### Local Control After SBRT for Liver Tumors (Ohri)

> Liver mets: 3y LC 93% vs 65% at BED >100 vs ≤100 Gy10 (P<.001); primaries show no BED effect (P=.972), 3y LC 86%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The TCP fit (Fig 4) makes BED 100 Gy10 a waypoint, not a ceiling: modeled 2-year LC for mets rises from 70% at 80 Gy10 (10 Gy × 4) to 76% at 100 Gy10 and 90% at 180 Gy10 (20 Gy × 3). The flat primary curve may reflect pretreated, smaller viable tumor rather than absent dose-response.

- 📊 Actuarial LC by tumor type (log-rank P=.011, primary vs mets)

  | Tumor type | 1y LC | 2y LC | 3y LC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Primary (HCC + CCA), n=431 | 93% | 89% | 86% |
  | Liver mets, n=290 | 90% | 79% | 76% |

- 📊 Actuarial LC by BED within tumor type

  | Group | 1y LC | 2y LC | 3y LC | Log-rank P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Primary, BED ≤100 Gy10 | 94% | 89% | 86% | P=.972 |
  | Primary, BED >100 Gy10 | 92% | 89% | 86% | P=.972 |
  | Mets, BED >100 Gy10 | 96% | 93% | 93% | P<.001 |
  | Mets, BED ≤100 Gy10 | 84% | 70% | 65% | P<.001 |

- 📐 TCP model, liver mets: modeled 2y LC by BED (bootstrap 95% CI), R2 0.98

  | BED (Gy10) | Example schedule | Modeled 2y LC (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 80 | 10 Gy × 4 | 70% (64%-77%) |
  | 100 | 10 Gy × 5 | 76% (70%-82%) |
  | 180 | 20 Gy × 3 | 90% (85%-95%) |

- 🔍 Pooled lesion-level analysis of 13 published series: 721 tumors (642 pts), 79 local failures
- 🔍 Lesion data reconstructed from published KM curves (Guyot method); wide-BED series excluded
- 🔍 Cohort composition
  - HCC: 5 studies, n=394
  - Liver mets: 6 studies, n=290; CRC 56% of mets
  - CCA: 2 studies, n=37
- 🔍 Doses 24-60 Gy in 1-5 fx of 7-26 Gy; median BED 88 Gy10 (IQR 72-125), α/β=10
- 📊 Primaries: 227 lesions at BED 60-72 Gy10 reached 2y LC 90%
- ⚠️ BED compared across series, so dose is confounded with institution, histology and case mix
- ⚠️ Tumor size unavailable; Yamashita et al found LC 64% vs 85% for >30 vs ≤30 mm
- ⚠️ KM LC without competing risks; many pts die before local failure
- ⚠️ HCC often pretreated (TACE/lipiodol), so viable burden at SBRT may be lower than mets

## 📊 Cross-cutting

### A Primer on Dose-Response Data Modeling in Radiation Therapy

> HyTEC methods primer: in a 13/96 G2+ pneumonitis vs MLD example, it shows how test choice, parameter coupling and extrapolation distort NTCP constraints.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Treat a HyTEC constraint as a risk tolerance read off a curve, not a threshold. In the lung example, a 10% G2+ RP tolerance maps to MLD 3.27 Gy with NPV 92.9%. Where events are rare (cord Dmax) or control is high (prostate TCP), D50/γ50 coupling makes the predicted risk depend heavily on γ50, so a plan near that constraint relies on an extrapolated curve.

- 🔍 Methods review from the HyTEC group; it reports no new clinical result
- 🔍 Worked example: G2+ radiation pneumonitis vs mean lung dose (MLD), pooled from 2 published series
- 📊 Example dataset: 13/96 (13.5%) G2+ RP; median MLD 3.05 Gy
- 📐 Modeling steps applied to the lung example

  | Step | Method | Worked-example output |
  |---|---|---|
  | Best-fit parameters | Maximum likelihood, logistic model | MLL -31.41 |
  | Parameter CIs | Profile likelihood (MLL minus 1.92) | D50 5.05-9.04; γ50 0.73-1.77 |
  | Is there a dose-response? | Likelihood ratio vs flat average-risk line | LL(Pave) -38.07; difference 6.66, p=0.0003 |
  | Prediction CIs | Bootstrap resampling, 2000 histories | 68% and 95% bands |
  | Clinical check | NPV at 10% acceptable-risk cutoff (MLD=3.27 Gy) | NPV 52/56=92.9% |

- ⚠️ Median-split p ranged from 0.070 (Fisher exact) to 0.002 (Wilcoxon, assumptions unchecked)
- 📐 Rule of thumb is ≥10 events per parameter (≥20 for 2-parameter logistic). The example has 13
- ⚠️ D50 and γ50 are coupled ('banana' iso-LL contours), so a rectangle built from the separate CIs misstates joint uncertainty
- ⚠️ With low-event (spinal cord) or high-control (prostate TCP) data, predictions depend heavily on γ50
- ⚠️ A cut-point constraint encodes a chosen risk tolerance (e.g. 20% symptomatic pneumonitis, 0.1% myelitis), not a universal limit
- 🔍 Converting MLD to EQD2 is non-linear and needs full DVH data. Authors ask that both physical dose and EQD2 be reported

## 🧠 CNS

### ReTEC: Spinal Cord Reirradiation Tolerance After SBRT

> Pooled Dmax model (282 lesions, six myelopathy cases) suggests >50% cord recovery within 1 yr, but bootstrap 95% CI 31%-95%; current limits stand.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The ML fit (tRH 4 mo, Rmax 95%) implies far more recovery than the 33% Nelson factor, but the 1-yr CI spans 31%-95%, so 33% remains the defensible discount for composite cord dose. The practical trap is coupling: pairing one model's recovery factor with another's NTCP curve moved the same case from about 1% to 27% risk.

- 📊 NTCP model pooled from 13 papers, 282 lesions, six myelopathy cases
- 📊 ML fit: cord recovery >50% in <1 yr, bootstrap 95% CI 31%-95% at 1-yr interval
- 📐 Fitted recovery half-time (tRH) 4 mo, Rmax 95% (p<0.01, LRT)
- 📊 Example case (cord Dmax 50 Gy/25 fx, then 15.5 Gy/5 fx at 1 yr): composite cord BED2 by recovery assumption

  | Recovery assumption | Parameters | Composite BED2 | Myelopathy risk |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | No recovery | None | 140 Gy | <1% |
  | Nelson 2009 | 33% recovery at 1 yr | 107 Gy | Just below 1% |
  | ML fit | tRH 4 mo, Rmax 95% | 60 Gy | Just less than 1% |

- ⚠️ Recovery factor and NTCP curve must be applied as a matched pair
  - 140 Gy no-recovery composite into ML model → 27% risk (wrong)
  - 60 Gy ML composite into no-recovery model → <0.001% risk (wrong)
  - Correctly paired: about 1% risk under every recovery model
- 🔍 Inclusion: SBRT in ≥1 course, per-course cord Dmax, interval, fractionation or BED; α/β = 2 Gy
- 🔗 HyTEC cord limits compatible at ≥12 mo (Nelson 2009 assumptions) and 5-6 mo (ML model)
- ⚠️ Dmax-only summation; no voxel-wise composite reirradiation plans found in literature
- ⚠️ Further model limits
  - Rmax search capped at 95%; ML fit reached the cap
  - Cord and cauda equina not separated (thecal sac often the surrogate)
  - Less than half of spine SBRT pts alive at 1 yr; myelopathy risk may be underestimated
  - Non-SBRT data showed longer recovery times and were excluded

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 21587185]] **Reirradiation treatment effects in the clinic (ReTEC) proposal – proof of concept based on spinal cord dose tolerance for reirradiation with stereotactic body radiotherapy** — Grimm, Jimm; Mahadevan, Anand; Obayomi‐Davies, Olusola et al.. *Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics* (2026-04). [doi:10.1002/acm2.70557](https://doi.org/10.1002/acm2.70557)
  > Abstract Background Since the inception of spinal radiosurgery decades ago, the factors influencing spinal cord recovery have remained unclear. Elucidating these factors would aid in reirradiation planning and delivery. We describe modeling reirradiation normal tissue complication probabilities (NTCP) from compiled published data, akin to the collaborative Quantitative Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic (QUANTEC) and High Dose per Fraction, Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic (HyTEC) projects. Purpose Simple dose‐time recovery factors for the spinal cord serve as a basis to propose the Reirradiation Treatment Effects in the Clinic (ReTEC) project. ReTEC aims to define human organ recovery factors to convert the total composite dose to an equivalent physical dose compatible with the de novo therapy dose tolerance limits. Additionally, we present a system for automating analyses as well as clinical implementation of radiation dose‐time recovery. Methods Published literature was queried for stereotactic voxel‐wise composite reirradiation plans involving the spinal cord, but none were found. Hence, minimum reporting criteria were used instead: spinal cord maximum dose (Dmax) of each course, time interval between courses, and number of fractions or biologically effective dose, per patient or in sufficiently homogeneous groups of patients. Recovery factors and logistic model parameters were fitted with maximum likelihood techniques. Results An NTCP model was derived from 13 papers with data from 282 lesions, including six myelopathy cases. The model suggested spinal cord recovery of more than 50% in less than 1 year, which is faster and more complete than current clinical practice, but with wide confidence intervals (CI) (bootstrap 95% CI: 31%–95% when the time interval was 1 year). Conclusion Due to limited data, we recommend remaining within current standard guidelines until more comprehensive analyses, optimally under the ReTEC initiative, refute or validate these estimates. ReTEC would require composite plans accounting for differing Dmax locations in each course. Nevertheless, this proof‐of‐concept Dmax model provides insight and serves as a basis to propose ReTEC.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

### Local Control After SBRT for Liver Tumors (HyTEC)

> 3-yr LC 86% primary vs 76% metastatic liver tumors; mets better with BED >100 Gy10 (93% vs 65%), no dose-response in primaries.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Dose escalation pays off for metastases, not primaries. The TCP fit puts 2-yr LC for mets at 70% with 10 Gy × 4, 76% with 10 Gy × 5 and 90% with 20 Gy × 3. Primaries treated to BED 60-72 Gy10 (227 lesions) reached 90% 2-yr LC, so with underlying liver disease the source favors conservative schedules over escalation.

- 🔍 Pooled reanalysis of 13 published series: 721 tumors in 642 pts
- 🔍 Lesion-level LC reconstructed from published KM curves, then aggregated
- 📊 Actuarial LC by tumor type (log-rank P=.011)

  | Group | n | 1-yr LC | 2-yr LC | 3-yr LC |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Primary (HCC + CCA) | 431 | 93% | 89% | 86% |
  | Liver mets | 290 | 90% | 79% | 76% |

- 📊 LC by BED (cutoff 100 Gy10)

  | Group | 1-yr LC | 2-yr LC | 3-yr LC | Log-rank P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Primary, BED ≤100 Gy10 | 94% | 89% | 86% | P=.972 |
  | Primary, BED >100 Gy10 | 92% | 89% | 86% | P=.972 |
  | Mets, BED >100 Gy10 | 96% | 93% | 93% | P<.001 |
  | Mets, BED ≤100 Gy10 | 84% | 70% | 65% | P<.001 |

- 📐 TCP model, predicted 2-yr LC for liver mets (R2 0.98)

  | BED | Example schedule | 2-yr LC (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 80 Gy10 | 10 Gy × 4 | 70% (64%-77%) |
  | 100 Gy10 | 10 Gy × 5 | 76% (70%-82%) |
  | 180 Gy10 | 20 Gy × 3 | 90% (85%-95%) |

- 📊 Primaries at BED 60-72 Gy10 (227 lesions): 2-yr LC 90%
- 🔍 Median f/u 18 mo primaries (IQR 11-29), 14 mo mets (IQR 8-23)
- ⚠️ Tumor size and histology not modeled; CRC was 56% of mets
- ⚠️ KM without competing-risk handling; deaths before local failure censored
- 🔗 Yamashita et al (130 pts): LC 64% vs 85% for >30 mm vs ≤30 mm, P=.040

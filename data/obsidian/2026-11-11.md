# 2026-11-11

**ASTRO BCC/cSCC guideline: definitive BED10 70-93.5 conventional, adjuvant nodal RT 6000-6600 cGy, no concurrent carboplatin with adjuvant RT for resected cSCC.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Four guidance documents, no new trial data. Skin carried the day with ASTRO's graded BCC/cSCC RT recommendations and GEC-ESTRO ACROP's depth-based brachy split (contact <5 mm, interstitial ≥5 mm). IMWG places RT for myeloma soft-tissue mass, not VCF stabilisation; COVID-era lung guidance lists 30Gy to 34Gy single-fraction SABR and 50Gy/5. Most rest on low-to-moderate or retrospective evidence.

## 🩹 Myeloma / Plasma Cell

Prior RT is not a contraindication to cement augmentation; RT treats soft-tissue mass, not VCF instability.

### IMWG consensus: cement augmentation for myeloma VCFs

> 4-8 wk window: IMWG advises offering BKP or PV for painful myeloma VCFs, with VAS ≥6 triaging to immediate augmentation and up to 3 levels per session.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT boundary is explicit: RT shrinks a soft-tissue myelomatous mass but neither stabilises a VCF nor relieves bony neural compression, and prior RT or its dose does not contraindicate later cement. That narrows RT for painful myeloma VCFs to unresolved plasmacytoma, no systemic option, or posterior-element disease precluding BKP/PV, with marrow sparing as a stated aim.

- 🔍 IMWG update of its 2008 cement-augmentation statement; panel consensus, not an outcome study
- 🔍 Pain-tier triage (Table 2)

  | Tier | Panel position |
  |---|---|
  | Acute VCF, VAS ≥ 6 | Immediate augmentation; TLSO brace can bridge while systemic therapy starts |
  | Subacute, VAS 4-6 | Chemo, bisphosphonate, analgesia first; augment if these fail or collapse risk rises |
  | VAS 1-3 | Watchful surveillance with periodic imaging |

- 🔍 Indication classes (Table 1)
  - Absolute: persistent pain from unhealed fracture confirmed on MRI STIR
  - Absolute: symptoms unresolved after 4 weeks of conservative care
  - Relative: T10-L2 fracture, progressive height loss, posterior wall or pedicle defect
  - Conditional/prophylactic: impending fracture, which the panel calls hard to determine
- 💊 RT may be appropriate for
  - Soft-tissue mass or plasmacytoma unresolved with systemic therapy
  - Pts who cannot receive systemic therapy
  - Relapsed refractory disease
  - Poor performance status, palliative approach
  - Mass with severe pain
  - Plasmacytoma location precluding BKP or PV (posterior body near cord)
- 🔍 RT shrinks soft-tissue myelomatous mass but does not stabilise the VCF
- 🔍 RT ineffective for bony neural compression; CT soft-tissue windows separate bone from tumour
- 🔍 Prior RT and prior RT dose are not contraindications to cement augmentation
- 🔍 Up to 3 levels per session; more only with caution given pulmonary cement embolus
- 🔍 Avoid instrumentation where possible: metalwork failure in weak bone, infection during immunosuppression
- 🔗 CAFE RCT: 134 cancer pts, 49 MM; 22 randomised to BKP, 27 to NSM
- ⚠️ Myeloma-specific randomised evidence rests on CAFE's 49-pt MM subset
- ⚠️ Osteoporotic sham-controlled trials (Kallmes, Buchbinder) showed no pain benefit; panel attributes this to selection and facet pain

**Sources:**
- 📄 **The role of cement augmentation with percutaneous vertebroplasty and balloon kyphoplasty for the treatment of vertebral compression fractures in multiple myeloma: a consensus statement from the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG)** — Kyriakou, Charalampia; Molloy, Sean; Vrionis, Frank et al.. *Blood Cancer Journal* (2019-02). [doi:10.1038/s41408-019-0187-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41408-019-0187-7)
  > Abstract Multiple myeloma (MM) represents approximately 15% of haematological malignancies and most of the patients present with bone involvement. Focal or diffuse spinal osteolysis may result in significant morbidity by causing painful progressive vertebral compression fractures (VCFs) and deformities. Advances in the systemic treatment of myeloma have achieved high response rates and prolonged the survival significantly. Early diagnosis and management of skeletal events contribute to improving the prognosis and quality of life of MM patients. The management of patients with significant pain due to VCFs in the acute phase is not standardised. While some patients are successfully treated conservatively, and pain relief is achieved within a few weeks, a large percentage has disabling pain and morbidity and hence they are considered for surgical intervention. Balloon kyphoplasty and percutaneous vertebroplasty are minimally invasive procedures which have been shown to relieve pain and restore function. Despite increasing positive evidence for the use of these procedures, the indications, timing, efficacy, safety and their role in the treatment algorithm of myeloma spinal disease are yet to be elucidated. This paper reports an update of the consensus statement from the International Myeloma Working Group on the role of cement augmentation in myeloma patients with VCFs.

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

Two RT guidance documents, both built on retrospective or single-institution series with no randomised surgery vs RT data.

### GEC-ESTRO ACROP skin brachytherapy recommendations

> Depth sets the technique: contact BT for skin tumours <5 mm deep, interstitial for ≥5 mm or curved surfaces; no standard dose schedule endorsed.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetric fine print gates safety: with the Valencia applicator prescribed at 3 mm, skin surface dose runs around 135%, and omitting the plastic cap raises skin dose 2.5× (Valencia) or 10× (Leipzig). Recording surface dose, not only prescription-depth dose, is what lets late toxicity be compared across schedules.

- 🔍 GEC-ESTRO ACROP recommendation document, Radiother Oncol 2018; covers technique, CTV margins, dose and QA for skin BT
- 🔍 Technique selection by lesion depth and surface

  | Technique | Indication | Prescription depth | Geometry |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Surface moulds / flaps | Regular surfaces; flaps for larger volumes; custom moulds for nose, pinna, hand, fingers | 3–5 mm | Source-to-skin distance 5 mm |
  | Shielded applicators (radionuclide or electronic) | Small lesions on regular surfaces | 3–4 mm | GTV→CTV at least 5 mm, plus 1–2 mm PTV |
  | Interstitial | Thickness 5 mm or more, irregular surfaces | PTV equals CTV | Catheters 8–12 mm apart, at least 3 mm under skin |

- 🔍 Recommended CTV margins by histology

  | Histology | CTV margin |
  |---|---|
  | BCC, well defined | 5 mm |
  | BCC, poorly defined, large, infiltrative, morphoeic or sclerotic | 7–10 mm |
  | SCC <20 mm | 10–15 mm |
  | SCC >20 mm | 15–20 mm |
  | Merkel cell | 20–30 mm peripheral; deep 5–10 mm below visible extension |
  | Post-operative | Whole surgical site; margins per histopathology |

- 💊 Listed surface BT schedules by applicator (menu of published practice, not ranked)

  | Applicator | Dose/fx | Fractions | Frequency | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Moulds / flaps | 3 Gy | 17–18 | 3×/wk | 51–54 Gy |
  | Moulds / flaps | 4 Gy | 10–12 | 3×/wk | 40–48 Gy |
  | Moulds / flaps | 5 Gy | 10–12 | 2×/wk | 50–60 Gy |
  | Moulds / flaps | 5 Gy | 8 | Twice a day, daily | 40 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 3 Gy | 17–18 | 3×/wk | 51–54 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 5 Gy | 8 | 2×/wk | 40 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 10 Gy | 3 | 1×/wk | 30 Gy |
  | Valencia / Esteya / Xoft | 7 Gy | 6 | 2×/wk | 42 Gy |
  | Valencia / Esteya / Xoft | 5 Gy | 8–10 | 2×/wk | 40–50 Gy |

- 💊 Interstitial dosing
  - LDR: 60 Gy at the 85% reference isodose over 4–6 days
  - HDR/PDR: twice daily, 2.5–4 Gy per fx, at least 6 h apart
  - PDR alternative: 0.8–1 Gy per pulse
- 📐 Valencia: typical prescription depth 3 mm with skin surface dose around 135%
- ⚠️ Plastic cap omitted → skin dose 2.5× (Valencia) and 10× (Leipzig) higher
- 📊 Keloids: post-excision HDR, single catheter placed at surgery
  - Catheter at least 4 mm under the skin
  - Prescription 4–5 mm from the source
  - 5–6 Gy × 3 fx or 5 Gy × 4 fx
  - First session same day as surgery, always before 24 h
- 🔗 Keloid systematic review cited: modality vs mean dose and recurrence

  | Modality | Mean total dose | Mean recurrence |
  |---|---|---|
  | External | 13.5 ± 3.3 Gy | 22.2 ± 16% |
  | HDR BT | 13.7 ± 2.6 Gy | 10.5 ± 15% |
  | LDR BT | 19.3 ± 1.2 Gy | 21.3 ± 2.1% |

- ⚠️ Evidence base mostly single-institution small series; authors note RCTs and meta-analyses are lacking
- ⚠️ Authors state no standard schedule can be recommended; doses rest on experience, not evidence

### ASTRO guideline: definitive and postoperative RT for BCC and cSCC

> The guideline gives explicit strength and evidence grades for definitive RT, PORT, nodal RT, BED10 dose ranges and concurrent systemic therapy in non-metastatic BCC and cSCC. Most rest on low-to-moderate evidence.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The nodal dose ceiling is the practical read. The adjuvant dose is 6000-6600 cGy after lymphadenectomy, and the panel says no data support 6600 cGy even for positive margins or ECE. It also exempts a single <3 cm cervical node without ECE from adjuvant RT, so de-escalation in both directions has explicit cover.

- 🧭 Society guideline, not a trial: 5 key questions, graded strength × quality of evidence (QoE)
- 🔍 KQ1 · definitive RT

  | Scenario | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|
  | Cannot undergo or decline surgery | Strong | Moderate |
  | Surgery would compromise function or cosmesis | Conditional | Moderate |
  | Genetic radiosensitivity (ataxia telangiectasia, Gorlin, Li-Fraumeni) | Conditionally not recommended | Expert Opinion |

- 🔍 KQ2 · PORT indications, cSCC vs BCC

  | Indication | Histology | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Gross perineural spread, clinical or radiologic | BCC + cSCC | Strong | Moderate |
  | Close/positive margins, not correctable surgically | cSCC | Strong | Low |
  | Recurrence after margin-negative resection | cSCC | Strong | Moderate |
  | T3/T4 | cSCC | Strong | Moderate |
  | Desmoplastic/infiltrative + chronic immunosuppression | cSCC | Strong | Moderate |
  | Close/positive margins, not correctable surgically | BCC | Conditional | Low |
  | Recurrence after margin-negative resection | BCC | Conditional | Low |
  | Locally advanced/neglected, into bone or muscle | BCC | Conditional | Low |

- 🔍 KQ3 · regional nodes

  | Scenario | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|
  | Clinically apparent nodes → lymphadenectomy + adjuvant RT (except single <3 cm cervical node, no ECE) | Strong | Moderate |
  | Definitive nodal RT only if inoperable or unresectable | Strong | Moderate |
  | Imaging + SLNB to guide nodal RT, high-risk cSCC | Conditional | Expert Opinion |
  | Elective nodal RT, cSCC thickness >6 mm, only if primary field overlaps basin | Conditional | Low |
  | Adjuvant after lymphadenectomy: 6000-6600 cGy at 180-200 cGy/fx | Strong | Moderate |
  | Elective, no lymphadenectomy: 5000-5400 cGy at 180-200 cGy/fx | Strong | Moderate |

- 📐 KQ4 · primary-site BED10 ranges (Strong rec, Low QoE)

  | Setting | Conventional (180-200 cGy/fx) | Hypofractionation (210-500 cGy/fx) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Definitive | BED10 70-93.5 | BED10 56-88 |
  | Postoperative | BED10 59.5-79.2 | BED10 56-70.2 |

- 🔍 Modality (ELS, HDR brachy, electrons, MV photons) left to tumor depth, contour and location
- 📐 Task force: no data support 6600 over 6000 cGy for positive margins or ECE
- 💊 KQ5 · systemic therapy with RT
  - Resected locally advanced cSCC: concurrent carboplatin with adjuvant RT not recommended (Strong, Moderate)
  - Unresected locally advanced cSCC: concurrent drug with definitive RT conditionally recommended (Low)
  - Hedgehog inhibitors, cemiplimab framed as palliative; avoid where curative surgery or RT feasible
- ⚠️ AJCC eighth edition skin staging is head and neck only; trunk/limb recs are extrapolated
- ⚠️ No randomized surgery vs RT data; definitive-RT recs rest on retrospective and single-arm series

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Shortened curative schedules rest on radiobiological equivalence and retrospective series, not randomised comparison.

### Reduced fractionation in lung RT during COVID-19

> COVID-era consensus on shorter curative lung RT: 30Gy to 34Gy single-fraction SABR, 50Gy in 5 fractions, and 15-fraction schedules modelled against 55Gy/20.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable part is the radiobiology: at α/β of 10, 52Gy/15 is quoted as isoeffective with 55Gy/20 for tumour control, and at α/β of 3, 50Gy/15 is isotoxic for late effects. That is a modelled bridge from 20 to 15 fractions, not outcome data, and OAR constraints still gate it.

- 🔍 Clinical Oncology 2020 guidance for curative-intent lung RT under pandemic resource limits
- 🔍 Built from literature review, published series and centre audits, not new trial data
- 📊 Reduced-fractionation options cited, by setting
- ⚠️ Chest wall toxicity in a cited single-fraction series
  - 30.6% for lesions abutting the chest wall
  - 8.2% for tumours ≤1cm from the chest wall
  - Overall grade ≥3 chest wall toxicity 1.4%
- 📊 SABR for larger tumours: OAR constraints must be met as for lesions ≤5cm
- ⚠️ One cited larger-tumour SABR series: 30% grade ≥3 toxicity, pneumonitis most common
- 📊 One cited concurrent chemoRT cohort: 25% ≥grade 3 oesophagitis, 10% ≥grade 3 pneumonitis
- 🔗 Cited retrospective hypofractionation data reported outcomes comparable to ≥60Gy conventional fractionation
- ⚠️ Most schedules rest on radiobiological equivalence and retrospective series, not randomised comparison

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Reduced Fractionation in Lung Cancer Patients Treated with Curative-intent Radiotherapy during the COVID-19 Pandemic** — Faivre-Finn, C.; Fenwick, J.D.; Franks, K.N. et al.. *Clinical Oncology* (2020-08). [doi:10.1016/j.clon.2020.05.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clon.2020.05.001)

# 2026-11-11

**ASTRO BCC/cSCC guideline: definitive RT graded strong for non-surgical pts; PORT strong for cSCC adverse features (T3-T4, PNI, uncorrectable margins), only conditional for BCC equivalents.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> A guideline-heavy day: all consensus documents, no new outcome data. Skin carries the most RT weight: ASTRO grades PORT by histology, GEC-ESTRO ACROP sets BT technique by depth (contact <5 mm, interstitial ≥5 mm). IMWG favours BKP/PV over RT for myeloma VCF pain, RT reserved for soft-tissue mass. UK COVID lung guidance compresses 55Gy/20 to 52Gy/15 fx.

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

Two consensus documents, neither backed by RCTs: both state their schedules rest on retrospective or experience-based data.

### GEC-ESTRO ACROP skin brachytherapy recommendations

> Consensus technique guide for skin BT: contact BT for tumours <5 mm deep, interstitial for ≥5 mm or curved surfaces; no single standard schedule recommended.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetric traps are the actionable content: a missing applicator cap raises skin dose 2.5x (Valencia) to 10x (Leipzig), Valencia surface dose runs ~135% at a 3 mm prescription, and interstitial catheters shallower than 3 mm risk necrosis. The 5 mm depth threshold, set by HF ultrasound, decides contact versus interstitial technique.

- 🔍 GEC-ESTRO ACROP recommendations, Radiotherapy and Oncology 2018; panel method not described in source
- 🔍 Technique selection by lesion geometry

  | Technique | Indication | Prescription / geometry |
  |---|---|---|
  | Surface mould / flap | Regular surfaces; flaps for larger lesions; custom moulds for nose, pinna, hand, fingers | Prescribe 3-5 mm below skin; 5 mm source-to-skin distance |
  | Shielded applicator (radionuclide or electronic) | Small lesions on regular surfaces | Usually 3-4 mm depth; HF ultrasound to define depth |
  | Interstitial | Thickness ≥5 mm and/or irregular, curved surfaces | Catheters 8-12 mm apart, ≥3 mm under skin; CT with marks for CTV |

- 📐 Recommended CTV margins in NMSC

  | Histology / setting | CTV margin |
  |---|---|
  | BCC, well defined | 5 mm |
  | BCC, poorly defined, large, infiltrative, morphoeic, sclerotic | 7-10 mm |
  | SCC, lesion <20 mm | 10-15 mm |
  | SCC, lesion >20 mm | 15-20 mm |
  | Merkel cell | 20-30 mm peripheral; deep 5-10 mm below visible extension |
  | Post-operative | Whole surgical site; margins per histopathology |

- 💊 Recommended schedules by applicator

  | Applicator | Dose/fx | Fractions | Frequency | Total dose |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Mould / flap | 3 Gy | 17-18 | 3x weekly | 51-54 Gy |
  | Mould / flap | 4 Gy | 10-12 | 3x weekly | 40-48 Gy |
  | Mould / flap | 5 Gy | 10-12 | Twice weekly | 50-60 Gy |
  | Mould / flap | 5 Gy | 8 | Twice daily | 40 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 3 Gy | 17-18 | 3x weekly | 51-54 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 5 Gy | 8 | Twice weekly | 40 Gy |
  | Leipzig | 10 Gy | 3 | Weekly | 30 Gy |
  | Valencia / Esteya / Xoft | 7 Gy | 6 | Twice weekly | 42 Gy |
  | Valencia / Esteya / Xoft | 5 Gy | 8-10 | Twice weekly | 40-50 Gy |

- 💊 Interstitial HDR/PDR: twice daily, 2.5-4 Gy per fraction, or 0.8-1 Gy per pulse PDR
- 💊 Interstitial LDR: 60 Gy at the 85% reference isodose; >60 Gy added cosmetic damage over LC gain
- 💊 Keloid prophylaxis with HDR BT
  - Single catheter placed at surgery, ≥4 mm under skin
  - Prescribe 4-5 mm from source
  - First session same day as surgery
  - 5-6 Gy x3 or 5 Gy x4
- ⚠️ Without the plastic cap, skin dose is 2.5x (Valencia) and 10x (Leipzig) higher
- ⚠️ Valencia surface dose ~135% at typical 3 mm prescription; record surface dose to correlate late effects
- ⚠️ Table 2 schedule-by-author values could not be tied to rows in extracted source; not reproduced
- 🔗 ABS report summarized 19 skin BT protocols, local control 90-100%
- ⚠️ Authors state schedules rest more on experience than evidence; no RCTs, systematic reviews or meta-analyses in skin BT

### ASTRO guideline: definitive and postoperative RT for BCC/cSCC

> ASTRO grades definitive RT strong for non-surgical BCC/cSCC; PORT strong for cSCC adverse features but only conditional for BCC equivalents.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Nodal dose is the most transferable read: 6000-6600 cGy after lymphadenectomy, 5000-5400 cGy elective, and the panel found no data supporting 6600 cGy for positive margins or extranodal extension. Elective nodal RT is conditional, reserved for cSCC >6 mm thick when the primary field already overlaps the basin, which narrows routine nodal coverage.

- 🔍 ASTRO task force, 5 key questions, systematic MEDLINE review May 1988 to June 2018
- 🔍 Modified Delphi consensus; strength of recommendation graded separately from quality of evidence
- 📊 KQ1 · Definitive RT indications

  | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|
  | BCC/cSCC, cannot undergo or decline surgery: definitive RT recommended | Strong | Moderate |
  | Surgery would compromise function or cosmesis: definitive RT | Conditional | Moderate |
  | Genetic radiosensitivity syndromes: definitive RT not recommended | Conditional | Expert opinion |

- 📊 KQ2 · PORT indications by histology

  | Histology | Indication | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | BCC + cSCC | Gross perineural spread, clinical or radiologic | Strong | Moderate |
  | cSCC | Close/positive margins not correctable by further surgery | Strong | Low |
  | cSCC | Recurrence after prior margin-negative resection | Strong | Moderate |
  | cSCC | T3 and T4 tumors (AJCC 8th) | Strong | Moderate |
  | cSCC | Desmoplastic or infiltrative tumor with chronic immunosuppression | Strong | Moderate |
  | BCC | Close/positive margins not correctable by further surgery | Conditional | Low |
  | BCC | Recurrence after prior margin-negative resection | Conditional | Low |
  | BCC | Locally advanced/neglected, bone or muscle involvement | Conditional | Low |

- 📊 KQ3 · Regional nodes

  | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|
  | Clinical nodes: lymphadenectomy then adjuvant RT, except single <3 cm cervical node without ECE | Strong | Moderate |
  | Definitive nodal RT only if medically inoperable or unresectable | Strong | Moderate |
  | High-risk cSCC: imaging + SLNB to guide nodal basin RT | Conditional | Expert opinion |
  | Elective nodal RT (thickness >6 mm) only when primary field overlaps basin | Conditional | Low |
  | Adjuvant RT after lymphadenectomy: 6000-6600 cGy, 180-200 cGy/fx | Strong | Moderate |
  | Elective RT without lymphadenectomy: 5000-5400 cGy, 180-200 cGy/fx | Strong | Moderate |

- 📐 KQ4 · Primary-site BED10 ranges (all Strong, Low QoE)

  | Setting | Fractionation | cGy/fx | BED10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Definitive | Conventional | 180-200 | 70-93.5 |
  | Definitive | Hypofractionated | 210-500 | 56-88 |
  | Postoperative | Conventional | 180-200 | 59.5-79.2 |
  | Postoperative | Hypofractionated | 210-500 | 56-70.2 |

- 💊 KQ5 · Systemic therapy with RT

  | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|
  | Resected locally advanced cSCC: concurrent carboplatin + adjuvant RT not recommended | Strong | Moderate |
  | Unresected locally advanced cSCC: concurrent drug therapy + definitive RT | Conditional | Low |

- 🔍 Electrons, brachy (LDR/HDR), kV/ELS, MV photons judged similar for LC and cosmesis
- 📊 Elective nodal RT series: median 5-year regional RFS 99% in high-risk pts (limited data)
- ⚠️ No RCT compares surgery vs RT; most recs rest on retrospective and single-arm data
- ⚠️ Local recurrence risk factors derived mostly from H&N series; trunk/limb recs extrapolated
- ⚠️ No data support 6600 cGy over 6000 cGy for positive margins or ENE, per panel
- 🔗 KQ5 frames cemiplimab as palliative; predates neoadjuvant cemiplimab and C-POST adjuvant data

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Pandemic-era UK hypofractionation guidance; source text only partly legible, so the tumour size cutoff for single-fraction SABR is missing.

### Reduced fractionation in lung RT during COVID-19

> Pandemic-era UK guidance: peripheral SABR 30Gy to 34Gy single fraction; 52Gy/15 fx isoeffective for tumour control vs 55Gy/20 fx (α/β 10).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The usable lever is the radiobiology: with α/β 10, 52Gy/15 fx is isoeffective for tumour control, and with α/β 3, 50Gy/15 fx is isotoxic to 55Gy/20 fx for late complications. That conversion is what lets a department drop five fractions from a 20-fraction course, but it is modelled equivalence, not observed outcome.

- ⚠️ Source PDF text layer largely font-encoded; card built from legible fragments only
- 🔍 Clinical Oncology 2020, 32(8): reduced-fraction schedules for curative-intent lung RT during COVID-19
- 📊 Peripheral SABR: consider 30Gy to 34Gy in a single fraction (tumour size cutoff not legible in source)
- 📐 Isoeffect conversions for compressing to 15 fractions
  - Tumour control (α/β 10): 52Gy/15 fx isoeffective
  - Late complications (α/β 3): 50Gy/15 fx isotoxic to 55Gy/20 fx
- ⚠️ Cited chest wall toxicity data by tumour proximity
  - 30.6% for lesions abutting the chest wall
  - 8.2% for tumours ≤1 cm from the chest wall
  - Overall grade ≥3 chest wall toxicity 1.4%
- 🔗 50Gy in 5 fractions used in Glasgow, based on RTOG 0813 dose constraints
- 🔍 Lesions >5cm: OAR dose constraints must be met as when treating lesions ≤5cm
- ⚠️ Cited >5cm series: 30% grade ≥3 toxicity, radiation pneumonitis most common
- ⚠️ Cited concurrent-chemo series: 25% ≥grade 3 oesophagitis, 10% ≥grade 3 pneumonitis (regimen not legible in source)

## 🩹 Myeloma / Plasma Cell

IMWG keeps RT for soft-tissue mass and pain; it does not stabilise a VCF, and no dose or fractionation is given.

### IMWG consensus: cement augmentation for myeloma VCFs

> IMWG: offer BKP or PV within 4-8 weeks for MM pts with significant VCF pain; RT reserved for soft-tissue mass, used sparingly to spare marrow.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> RT is narrowed to soft-tissue disease: effective against intracanal myelomatous mass, ineffective for bony compression, and it does not stabilise the VCF. Prior RT and its dose do not contraindicate cement, so irradiating a painful level leaves BKP/PV open 4-8 weeks later for persistent fracture pain or relative instability.

- 🔍 IMWG update of its 2008 consensus on vertebral cement augmentation in MM
- 📊 Core rec: significant pain at a fracture site → offer BKP or PV within 4-8 weeks, absent medical contraindications
- 📊 Timing by pain severity (Table 2)

  | Presentation | Panel position |
  |---|---|
  | Acute VCF, VAS ≥ 6 | Immediate augmentation; TLSO can bridge pain while systemic therapy starts |
  | Subacute, VAS 4-6 | Chemo, bisphosphonates, analgesia first; augment if these fail or collapse risk |
  | VAS 1-3 | Watchful surveillance with periodic skeletal survey |

- 📊 Indication tiers for cement augmentation (Table 1)

  | Tier | Examples |
  |---|---|
  | Absolute | Persistent pain from unhealed fracture on MRI STIR; symptoms unresolved after 4 weeks conservative care |
  | Relative | T10-L2 fracture risking kyphosis; progressive height loss; posterior wall or pedicle/pars defect |
  | Conditional / prophylactic | Functional height loss at T10-L2 only if progressive; impending fracture, needs clinical trials |

- 🔍 RT may be appropriate for
  - Soft-tissue mass or plasmacytoma not resolved with systemic therapy
  - Pts who cannot receive systemic therapy
  - Relapsed refractory disease
  - Poor performance status, palliative approach
  - Mass associated with severe pain
  - Plasmacytoma location precluding BKP/PV (posterior body near cord/nerves)
- 🔍 Prior RT and its dose are not contraindications to later BKP/PV
- 🔍 RT shrinks soft-tissue myelomatous mass but not bony compression, and does not stabilise the VCF
- 🔍 Up to 3 levels per session appropriate; more with caution (cement embolus risk)
- 💊 Systemic therapy first; augmentation after 1-2 cycles if fracture pain persists
- 🔗 CAFE RCT: 134 cancer pts, 49 with MM (22 BKP vs 27 NSM); BKP favoured on pain, function, QoL
- ⚠️ Sham-controlled osteoporotic trials (Kallmes, Buchbinder) showed no benefit vs sham; panel attributes this to selection and facet pain
- ⚠️ No panel voting, agreement figures, or recommendation grading reported in source
- ⚠️ RT section gives no dose, fractionation, or target volume

**Sources:**
- 📄 **The role of cement augmentation with percutaneous vertebroplasty and balloon kyphoplasty for the treatment of vertebral compression fractures in multiple myeloma: a consensus statement from the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG)** — Kyriakou, Charalampia; Molloy, Sean; Vrionis, Frank et al.. *Blood Cancer Journal* (2019-02). [doi:10.1038/s41408-019-0187-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41408-019-0187-7)
  > Abstract Multiple myeloma (MM) represents approximately 15% of haematological malignancies and most of the patients present with bone involvement. Focal or diffuse spinal osteolysis may result in significant morbidity by causing painful progressive vertebral compression fractures (VCFs) and deformities. Advances in the systemic treatment of myeloma have achieved high response rates and prolonged the survival significantly. Early diagnosis and management of skeletal events contribute to improving the prognosis and quality of life of MM patients. The management of patients with significant pain due to VCFs in the acute phase is not standardised. While some patients are successfully treated conservatively, and pain relief is achieved within a few weeks, a large percentage has disabling pain and morbidity and hence they are considered for surgical intervention. Balloon kyphoplasty and percutaneous vertebroplasty are minimally invasive procedures which have been shown to relieve pain and restore function. Despite increasing positive evidence for the use of these procedures, the indications, timing, efficacy, safety and their role in the treatment algorithm of myeloma spinal disease are yet to be elucidated. This paper reports an update of the consensus statement from the International Myeloma Working Group on the role of cement augmentation in myeloma patients with VCFs.

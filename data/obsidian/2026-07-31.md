# 2026-07-31

**POSEIDON IPD (n=6057): adding HT to postop RT misses OS (HR 0.87, 0.76-1.01, p=0.06); benefit concentrates at pre-RT PSA >0.5 (interaction p=0.02).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day, and both readings push RT decisions toward patient selection over blanket intensification: POSEIDON says HT with salvage RT earns its OS case only at higher pre-RT PSA (NNT 125 at ≤0.2 vs 25 at >1.0), while GÖTEBORG-1 shows 25yr PC-specific survival of 94% on AS against 68% failure-free at 22yr. Elsewhere the signal is planning-level: MIBC dose escalation cut invasive local recurrence (SHR 0.20) with no added G2+ toxicity, and immune-sparing lung SBRT spared 13.4% of ALC reduction.

## 🫘 Kidney

First IROCK consensus contours for RCC SABR, with the least agreement on exactly the cases that need it most (IVC thrombus extent, ablation cavity).

### IROCK Contouring Guidelines

> First international consensus contouring guideline for RCC SABR; median DSC 0.85 across 4 cases, 16 radiation oncologists.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The high-variance cases are the actionable part: superior extent of IVC tumor thrombus (Case 1) and the post-RFA ablation cavity (Case 4) drove the spread even among IROCK experts, and consensus is to include both entirely in the iGTV. The renal cortex definition (kidney minus iGTV, pelvis, vasculature, proximal ureter) gives a reproducible denominator for renal-function constraints.

- 🔍 Modified-Delphi style consensus: 16 rad oncs affiliated with IROCK contoured 4 scenarios, refined over 2 consensus meetings
- 🔍 Cases: >10cm tumor with IVC tumor thrombus; central tumor abutting hilum; post-nephrectomy local recurrence; ablation-cavity recurrence post-RFA
- 📐 Agreement analyzed by STAPLE (95% CI); median DSC 0.85, median MDA 2.17mm, median HD 9.00mm
- 📐 Median DSC >0.70 for every case (the paper's threshold for "good agreement")
- 📊 Consensus iGTV statements by case
  - Case 1: include primary tumor + entire tumor thrombus; exclude bland thrombus
  - Case 2: include all tumor visible on sim imaging, account for internal motion
  - Case 3: include all visible gross tumor; surgical clips need not be included
  - Case 4: include visible gross recurrence + the entire ablation cavity
- 📊 OAR dose constraints take priority over target volume coverage in planning
- 📊 Ipsilateral renal cortex = ipsilateral parenchyma minus iGTV, renal pelvis, renal vasculature, proximal ureter; hilum contour optional
- 🔍 Contrast-enhanced MRI and/or multiphasic contrast CT encouraged, with expert radiology review, to separate tumor from bland thrombus
- ⚠️ 2-way ANOVA: DSC and HD differed significantly by case but not by participant; MDA differed by both case and participant
- ⚠️ Greatest variation in Case 1 (superior extent of IVC thrombus, target size) and Case 4 (ablation cavity), so the hardest cases are the least standardized
- ⚠️ Renal cortex and renal hilum remain investigational substructures: neither has been separately correlated with renal-function outcomes
- ⚠️ Expert-opinion consensus, no outcome data; contour agreement is a surrogate, not local control or toxicity

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008)

## 💧 Bladder

Retrospective TMT series putting a SIB boost to the primary tumour bed on the table for invasive local control, without a randomised comparator.

### Dose-Escalated RT for MIBC (TMT)

> SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89) for invasive local recurrence with SIB dose escalation in TMT; no OS or MFS difference.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The escalation was a simultaneous integrated boost to the primary lesion, not a whole-bladder dose lift, so the whole bladder stayed at 55Gy/20fx or 64Gy/32fx and G2+ GU/GI toxicity was unchanged (p=0.8, p=0.9). That is the parameter that makes this transferable: the decision it moves is whether to contour a GTV boost at TMT planning, and the benefit was specific to invasive recurrence (non-invasive LC p=0.8).

- 📊 1° signal: invasive local control favored escalation, SHR 0.20 (0.05, 0.89), p=0.035, adjusted for T stage
- 🔍 Multicentre retrospective, N=107, TMT with contemporary technique, 2015-2025, median f/u 23 mo
- 🔍 RT dose by cohort (SIB to primary lesion in the escalated arm)

  | Schedule | Standard dose | Dose escalation |
  |---|---|---|
  | 20-fraction | 55Gy/20fx whole bladder | up to 60Gy/20fx to primary |
  | 32-fraction | 64Gy/32fx whole bladder | up to 70Gy/32fx to primary |

- 📊 Non-invasive local control p=0.8, MFS p=0.8, OS p=0.5: the benefit was confined to invasive recurrence
- 📐 No difference in G2+ GU (p=0.8) or G2+ GI (p=0.9) toxicity between cohorts
- 📊 No escalated pt required salvage cystectomy for invasive recurrence vs 2 standard-dose pts
- 🔗 Rationale is the mapping data cited by the authors: most invasive relapses arise near the original muscle-invasive tumour site
- ⚠️ Retrospective, non-randomised; escalation cohort selection (fitness, tumour visibility for GTV boost) is unmeasured confounding
- ⚠️ Wide CI (0.05-0.89) on few events; the point estimate of 0.20 is fragile
- ⚠️ 23-mo median f/u is short for late GU toxicity after a bladder SIB, and effect sizes for the null endpoints are reported as p-values only in the abstract

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Dose-Escalated Radiotherapy for Muscle Invasive Bladder Cancer: A retrospective analysis** — Chan, Li; Anzela, Anzela; Do, Viet et al.. *Advances in Radiation Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.adro.2026.102156](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adro.2026.102156)
  - 📎 [[papers/bladder/dose-escalated-radiotherapy-for-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-a.pdf|Dose-Escalated RT for MIBC (TMT) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Randomised phase II asking whether SBRT plan optimization around circulating-blood OARs preserves lymphocytes; surrogate endpoint only, and a preprint.

### Immune Cell Sparing RT · [[NCT04273893]]

> ALC reduction improved 13.4% (95% CI 2.8-24.0, p=0.01) with immune-sparing SBRT planning vs standard, 29.5% in central tumors.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable part is the planning recipe: optimized plans hit RTOG 0813/0915 constraints while cutting V5 to thoracic spine 87%, vena cava 58%, heart 43%, so this is achievable on existing planning systems without dose compromise to the ITV (70-80 Gy to ≥70% of ITV in both arms). Central tumors, where blood and nodal stations sit in the low-dose bath, drove the effect (29.5%, p=0.004), so that is where adding immune-rich OARs to the optimizer is worth the planning time.

- 📊 1° signal: ALC change from baseline at end-Tx / 4wk / 6mo, optimized -16%, -22%, -16% vs standard -31%, -34%, -26%
- 📐 Overall all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction 13.4 (5.3) %, 95% CI 2.8 to 24.0, p=0.01
- 📊 Central tumors carried the effect: optimized -8%, -18%, -14% vs standard -39%, -43%, -47%
- 📐 Central-tumor improvement 29.5 (9.6) %, 95% CI 10.1 to 48.9, p=0.004
- 🔍 Phase II randomized, single-institution (UVA), 55 enrolled Feb 2020 to Apr 2023, 51 analyzed (25 optimized / 26 standard)
- 🔍 Both arms met RTOG 0813/0915 dosimetric criteria; optimized plans additionally constrained heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, lymph-node stations
- 💊 G3 lymphopenia 15.4% standard vs 0% optimized; 2.8× more optimized pts had an ALC increase post-SBRT
- 📐 Average % reduction, optimized vs standard plans (integral dose / V5 / V10)
  - Thoracic spine: 57% / 87% / 92%
  - Vena cava: 37% / 58% / 75%
  - Aorta: 35% / 48% / 69%
  - Heart: 21% / 43% / 68%
  - Lymph-node stations: 37% / 58% / 68%
  - Total lung minus PTV: 5% / 8% / 0%
- ⚠️ Survival signals are underpowered and not significant: 2yr EFS 75.0% (SE 10.8) vs 59.8% (SE 11.2), p=0.10; 2yr OS 93.4% (SE 6.1) vs 69.4% (SE 10.5), p=0.14
- ⚠️ Those survival numbers are in treatment-naive pts only, and the arms were imbalanced: 88.5% treatment-naive standard vs 64.0% optimized, central tumors 36.0% optimized vs 23.1% standard
- ⚠️ medRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed; surrogate endpoint (ALC), no tumor-control or immune-function endpoint

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 16111586]] **First Measurement: Proactive Immune Cell Sparing in Radiation Therapy** — Wijesooriya, Krishni; Nguyen, Cam; Conaway, Mark R et al.. *medRxiv* (2025-01). [doi:10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011)
  > Abstract Purpose Radiation Therapy (RT) can modulate the immune system and generate anti-tumor T cells. However, this anti-tumor-activity is countered by radiation-induced immunosuppression (RIIS). Clinical advantages of proactively sparing RT dose to immune rich organs have not previously been evaluated. Methods We conducted a phase II randomized trial from 2020 to 2023, enrolling 51 early-stage lung cancer patients treated with SBRT, to evaluate the effect of dose reduction to immune rich organs on RIIS. Two groups were: RIIS-optimized-treatment (lowering the dose to blood, bone-marrow and lymph-node-stations) and standard-treatment. All treatments followed national protocol guidelines. Peripheral blood was collected at baseline, immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-treatment. Results ALC changes from baseline immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-SBRT are: optimized-arm: -16%, -22%, -16%, standard-arm: -31%, -34%, -26%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 13.4 (5.3) % (95% CI, 2.8 to 24.0; p = 0.01). Central tumors had the largest improvement in ALC from baseline: optimized-arm: - 8%, -18%, -14%, standard-arm: -39%, -43%, -47%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 29.5 (9.6) % (95% CI, 10.1 to 48.9; p = 0.004). Grade 3 lymphopenia occurred in 15.4% of standard arm patients but was absent in the optimized arm. Additionally, 2.8 times more patients in the optimized arm experienced an ALC increase post-SBRT. Dose to organs such as the heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, and lymph nodes significantly correlated with RIIS. A trend towards increased Event-Free-Survival (two-year: 75.0% (SE = 10.8%) versus 59.8% (SE = 11.2%), p =0·10) and Overall Survival (two-year: 93.4% (SE=6.1%) versus 69.4% (SE=10.5%), p =0·14) was observed with optimized-planning compared to standard-planning in treatment naïve patients. Conclusion Reducing RT dose to immune rich organs significantly reduces RIIS compared to standard-of-care. This has implications in enhancing immune system mediated anti-tumor-activity. ## 🌰 Prostate

Two long-horizon reads on when to treat and when to intensify: PSA-gated hormone therapy with postop RT, and 25-year outcomes of surveillance in a screened cohort.

### GÖTEBORG-1

> PC-specific survival 94% at 25yr on active surveillance, but failure-free survival fell to 68% at 22yr.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant signal is when surveillance ends, not whether it is safe: 44 of 488 men reached radiotherapy and 18 had PSA relapse after curative treatment with no hormones, so salvage-eligible disease keeps arriving past 20 yr. Intermediate-risk failure-free survival was 55% at 19 yr vs 85% very-low-risk, the widest split in the dataset.

- 🔍 Prospective AS cohort nested in the Göteborg-1 screening trial: 488 men, 1995-2014, follow-up closed Dec 31 2023
- 🔍 Risk mix: very-low-risk 251 (51%), low-risk 129 (26%), intermediate-risk 108 (22%); 6 high-risk excluded
- 🔍 Median age 66 (IQR 63-68), median PSA 4.1 ng/ml (IQR 3.4-5.4), median PSA density 0.12 (IQR 0.09-0.16)
- 🔍 Median follow-up 18.0 yr (IQR 14.7-22.1) among men alive at last follow-up
- 📊 Whole cohort at 22 yr: treatment-free survival 38%, failure-free survival 68%
- 📊 Outcomes by baseline risk group (KM estimates)

  | Endpoint (timepoint) | Very low risk | Low risk | Intermediate risk |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Treatment-free survival (19 yr) | 55% | 35% | 30% |
  | Failure-free survival (19 yr) | 85% | 74% | 55% |
  | PC-specific survival (24 yr) | 99% | 92% | 85% |
  | Overall survival (24 yr) | 38% | 34% | 22% |

- 📊 232 men discontinued AS; definitive local therapy in 185
  - Radical prostatectomy: 141
  - Radiation therapy: 44
  - Hormonal treatment: 47
- 📊 81 failures over follow-up
  - Hormonal treatment: 44
  - PSA relapse after curative treatment, no hormones: 18
  - PC death: 14
  - Metastatic disease: 5
- ⚠️ No predefined AS protocol; PSA q6-12mo and biopsy only on clinical progression signals
- ⚠️ Pre-MRI era: sextant biopsies until 2009, prediagnostic MRI in only 21 of 488 pts, so baseline risk was likely understaged
- ⚠️ Competing mortality dominates: 264 all-cause deaths vs 14 PC deaths, OS 32% at 25 yr for the whole cohort
- 🔗 vs ProtecT, where AS carried a higher metastasis risk than immediate treatment; here failure accrued steadily rather than plateauing

### POSEIDON

> OS HR 0.87 (0.76-1.01), p=0.06 adding hormone therapy to postop RT; 0.7% absolute at 10y, PSA-dependent interaction p=0.02.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The decision that moves is hormone therapy omission with post-prostatectomy RT: NNT 125 at PSA ≤0.2 vs 25 at PSA >1.0 ng/mL, and the long-term CI clears 1.0 only above PSA 1.6. The 3.8% 10y MFS gain failed surrogacy for OS, so it can't carry ADT on its own.

- 📊 1° EP overall survival: HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.76-1.01), p=0.06, primary not met
- 📊 10y OS 84.3% with hormone therapy vs 83.6% RT alone, projected absolute 0.7%
- 📊 10y MFS 77.9% vs 74.1%, absolute 3.8%, HR 0.79, p<0.001
- ⚠️ MFS did not hold as an OS surrogate in their analysis, so the MFS gain can't carry an OS claim
- 🔍 IPD from six randomised phase 3 trials, 6057 pts, median f/u 9.0y (IQR 7.2-10.7)
- 📊 OS effect by hormone therapy duration

  | Comparison | n | OS effect |
  |---|---|---|
  | PORT ± short-term HT (4-6 mo) | 3938 | 95% CI upper bound crossed 1.0 at every PSA value |
  | PORT ± long-term HT (24 mo) | 1088 | upper bound fell below 1.0 only at PSA >1.6 ng/mL |
  | Short vs long HT (exploratory) | n/a | HR 0.89, p=0.37 |

- 📐 Pre-PORT PSA interaction p=0.02 for >0.5 vs ≤0.5 ng/mL
- 📊 NNT 125 at PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL vs 25 at PSA >1.0 ng/mL
- ⚠️ Enrollment predates PSMA PET and Decipher, so today's low-PSA pts would be restaged first

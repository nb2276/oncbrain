# 2026-07-31

**POSEIDON (6057 pts): adding HT to postop RT gave no OS benefit (HR 0·87, 0·76–1·01), with signal confined to pre-PORT PSA >0·5.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day: POSEIDON says salvage RT alone suffices below PSA 0·5, and GÖTEBORG-1's 25-yr AS data shows 94% PC-specific survival but 68% failure-free at 22 yr. RT technique gains elsewhere: immune-sparing lung SBRT cut G3 lymphopenia to 0%, and MIBC SIB boost dropped 2y invasive local recurrence 27·5% → 5·5%.

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

First randomised test of planning to spare circulating lymphocytes rather than only meeting standard OAR constraints.

### NCT04273893 (Immune-Cell-Sparing SBRT) · [[NCT04273893]]

> Overall ALC-reduction improvement 13.4% (95% CI 2.8-24.0), p=0.01, favoring immune-organ-sparing lung SBRT planning.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning ask is concrete: add heart, great vessels, thoracic spine and lymph-node stations as low-dose OARs and drive V5/V10 down (thoracic spine V5 fell 87%, lymph-node stations 58%) without breaching RTOG 0813/0915. Benefit concentrated in central tumors and peripheral PTV >20cc; peripheral PTV <20cc gained nothing, so the effort is worth spending selectively.

- 🔍 Randomized phase II, N=51 (25 optimized / 26 standard), single institution, 2020-2023, unmasked, stratified by tumor location
- 🔍 Both arms met RTOG 0813/0915 constraints; optimized arm additionally minimized dose to heart, great vessels, lymph-node-stations, thoracic-spine
- 🔍 SBRT 45-60 Gy in 5 fx (BED 85.5-132 Gy) via IMRT/VMAT; ITV from 4DCT, PTV margin 5 mm radial / 8 mm sup-inf
- 📊 Average % reduction in optimized vs standard plans (integral dose, V5, V10)

  | Organ | Integral dose | V5 | V10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Aorta | 35% | 48% | 69% |
  | Heart | 21% | 43% | 68% |
  | Vena cava | 37% | 58% | 75% |
  | Thoracic spine | 57% | 87% | 92% |
  | Lymph-node-stations | 37% | 58% | 68% |
  | Total lung - PTV | 5% | 8% | 0% |

- 📐 ALC % change from baseline by timepoint (model-estimated)

  | Timepoint | Optimized | Standard | Between-group difference |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Immediately post-SBRT | -16% | -31% | 15.1% (95% CI 3.7-26.5), p=0.01 |
  | 4 weeks | -22% | -34% | 12.3% (95% CI 0.2-24.5), p=0.05 |
  | 6 months | -16% | -26% | 10.4% (95% CI -4.7-25.5), p=0.17 |

- 📊 Central tumors (n=15) gained most: overall ALC-reduction improvement 29.5% (95% CI 10.1-48.9), p=0.004
- 📊 Peripheral PTV >20cc (n=18): improvement 13.5%, 95% CI -3.8 to 20.9, p=0.124; peripheral PTV <20cc showed no difference
- 📊 G3 lymphopenia 15.4% standard vs 0% optimized (risk difference -15.4%, 95% CI -29.2 to -1.5, p=0.04)
- 📊 LN-stations V5 below vs above 35.4cc median: two-year OS 91.8% vs 57.5%, p=0.02
- ⚠️ OS/EFS were unplanned subgroup analyses, not powered; all-patient OS p=0.23, EFS p=0.27
- ⚠️ Baseline imbalance favors caution: more central tumors in optimized arm (36.0% vs 23.1%), more treatment-naive in standard arm (88.5% vs 64.0%)
- ⚠️ ALC is a surrogate; no immune-function or tumor-control readout ties the spared lymphocytes to the survival trend
- 🔗 Prior prospective RT+IO data cited by the authors linked lower post-RT ALC to worse PFS (p=.009) and OS (p=.026); this is the first trial to spare prospectively

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 16111586]] **First Measurement: Proactive Immune Cell Sparing in Radiation Therapy** — Wijesooriya, Krishni; Nguyen, Cam; Conaway, Mark R et al.. *medRxiv* (2025-01). [doi:10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011)
  > Abstract Purpose Radiation Therapy (RT) can modulate the immune system and generate anti-tumor T cells. However, this anti-tumor-activity is countered by radiation-induced immunosuppression (RIIS). Clinical advantages of proactively sparing RT dose to immune rich organs have not previously been evaluated. Methods We conducted a phase II randomized trial from 2020 to 2023, enrolling 51 early-stage lung cancer patients treated with SBRT, to evaluate the effect of dose reduction to immune rich organs on RIIS. Two groups were: RIIS-optimized-treatment (lowering the dose to blood, bone-marrow and lymph-node-stations) and standard-treatment. All treatments followed national protocol guidelines. Peripheral blood was collected at baseline, immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-treatment. Results ALC changes from baseline immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-SBRT are: optimized-arm: -16%, -22%, -16%, standard-arm: -31%, -34%, -26%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 13.4 (5.3) % (95% CI, 2.8 to 24.0; p = 0.01). Central tumors had the largest improvement in ALC from baseline: optimized-arm: - 8%, -18%, -14%, standard-arm: -39%, -43%, -47%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 29.5 (9.6) % (95% CI, 10.1 to 48.9; p = 0.004). Grade 3 lymphopenia occurred in 15.4% of standard arm patients but was absent in the optimized arm. Additionally, 2.8 times more patients in the optimized arm experienced an ALC increase post-SBRT. Dose to organs such as the heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, and lymph nodes significantly correlated with RIIS. A trend towards increased Event-Free-Survival (two-year: 75.0% (SE = 10.8%) versus 59.8% (SE = 11.2%), p =0·10) and Overall Survival (two-year: 93.4% (SE=6.1%) versus 69.4% (SE=10.5%), p =0·14) was observed with optimized-planning compared to standard-planning in treatment naïve patients. Conclusion Reducing RT dose to immune rich organs significantly reduces RIIS compared to standard-of-care. This has implications in enhancing immune system mediated anti-tumor-activity. (Funded by National Cancer Institute and others. ClinicalTrials.gov number, [[NCT04273893]] )

## 🫘 Kidney

First international consensus contouring guideline for RCC SABR, CT-only by design.

### IROCK RCC Contouring Guidelines

> First international consensus contouring guideline for RCC SABR; 16 experts, median DSC 0.85 across 4 scenarios.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The two decision-carrying rules are the ones most likely to be got wrong: cover the entire ablation cavity, not just the residual nodule, and the entire tumor thrombus while excluding bland thrombus. Renal hilum is deliberately not a dose-limiting OAR. Case 4 DSC 0.75 and Case 1 HD median 64.60mm mark where experts still disagree.

- 🔍 16 RCC SABR experts (US, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, India), all with ≥10 prior RCC SABR cases; 14/16 treated ≥10 in the prior 12mo
- 🔍 4 CT-only cases: >10cm tumor + IVC thrombus, central tumor abutting hilum, post-nephrectomy local recurrence, post-RFA residual
- 🔍 STAPLE 95% consensus contour per case; refined over 2 online meetings (May, June 2024); statements revised to 100% agreement
- 📊 Contour agreement vs consensus by case (median, IQR)

  | Case | DSC | MDA (mm) | HD (mm) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 · >10cm + IVC thrombus | 0.85, 0.79-0.85 | 6.69, 5.88-8.79 | 64.60, 64.44-86.40 |
  | 2 · central, abutting hilum | 0.90, 0.84-0.93 | 1.55, 1.00-2.09 | 7.92, 5.35-9.98 |
  | 3 · post-RN local recurrence | 0.91, 0.88-0.93 | 1.42, 1.18-1.89 | 6.18, 6.00-7.12 |
  | 4 · post-RFA residual | 0.75, 0.60-0.79 | 2.50, 2.12-2.99 | 9.00, 9.00-12.39 |

- 📐 Overall metrics and ANOVA
  - Median DSC 0.85 (range 0.40-0.95); >0.70 for every case
  - Median MDA 2.17mm (range 0.71-10.82); median HD 9.00mm (range 4.00-89.31)
  - DSC, MDA, HD all differed by case (P<.05); by participant only MDA differed (P=.03), DSC P=.07, HD P=.45
- 📐 Target volume rules per scenario
  - iGTV includes primary tumor + entire tumor thrombus; bland thrombus excluded
  - iGTV includes gross tumor recurrence + the entire post-RFA ablation cavity
  - Surgical clips not involved with tumor can be left out of the iGTV
  - No margin for microscopic spread; most common PTV margin was uniform 5mm on iGTV
- 📐 5mm PTV margin assumes supine vacuum-cushion immobilization, 4D-CT sim, IV contrast, daily CBCT; varies with MRI-sim or adaptive workflows
- 📐 Ipsilateral renal parenchyma = whole ipsilateral kidney minus iGTV, renal pelvis, renal vasculature, proximal ureter
- ⚠️ Renal hilum explicitly NOT a dose-limiting OAR: optional contour only, since constraining it may push dose into remaining parenchyma
- 📐 OAR constraints take priority over target coverage; heterogeneous PTV dose with steep gradient is the stated tradeoff, hotspot inside the iGTV
- 💊 Bowel/stomach mitigation: NPO for hours pre-sim and pre-treatment, simethicone or alpha-galactosidase, bowel prep, PRVs, adaptive RT if available
- 🔗 Suggested 1/3/5-fraction dose objectives and OAR constraints (Table 2) are drawn from FASTRACK-II and AQuOS-II ([[NCT05023265]]) protocols, not derived here
- ⚠️ CT-only exercise by design; participants themselves asked for MRI, and the authors concede MRI availability would likely have narrowed the variation
- ⚠️ No recontouring after the 2 consensus meetings, so the post-guideline agreement gain is asserted, not measured
- ⚠️ Widest spread was Case 1 (superior extent of IVC thrombus, HD median 64.60mm) and Case 4 (how much RFA cavity to include, DSC 0.75)

## 💧 Bladder

Retrospective SIB dose escalation in TMT, with a local-control gain that does not carry to OS or MFS.

### Dose-Escalated RT for MIBC

> 2y invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5% with SIB dose escalation in TMT; SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89), p=0.035, no OS or MFS difference.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The signal is local, not systemic: invasive recurrence fell 27.5% to 5.5% at 2y with no MFS or OS shift, and 1 of the 2 escalated failures was still inside the boost. Deliverable as SIB 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx with daily CBCT, no G2+ GU/GI penalty (p=0.8, p=0.9), so it moves the boost-volume question, not patient selection for TMT.

- 🔍 Multicentre retrospective, 3 centres, Mar 2015 to May 2025, N=107 (39 escalated / 68 standard), median f/u 23mo (range 3-104)
- 📐 Standard: 55Gy/20fx or 64Gy/32fx whole bladder. Escalation: SIB to 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx to the primary lesion
- 📊 2y cumulative incidence invasive recurrence 5.5% (escalation) vs 27.5% (standard); crude 2/39 (5.1%) vs 16/68 (23.5%)
- 📐 Univariable Fine-Gray SHR 0.21 (0.05-0.94), p=0.04; multivariable adjusted for T stage SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89), p=0.035
- 📊 Escalation vs standard across endpoints

  | Endpoint (2y) | Dose escalation | Standard | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Invasive local recurrence | 5.5% | 27.5% | SHR 0.20 (0.05, 0.89), p = 0.035 |
  | Non-invasive recurrence | 6.7% | 9.9% | SHR 0.88 (0.23 - 3.33), p = 0.98 |
  | Metastasis | 21.1% | 32.3% | p = 0.79 |
  | Overall survival | 71.1% | 64.4% | p = 0.5 |
  | G2+ GU toxicity | 7 (17.9%) | 15 (22.1%) | p=0.8 |
  | G2+ GI toxicity | 2 (5.1%) | 5 (7.4%) | p=0.9 |

- 📊 Only 1 of 2 invasive recurrences in the escalated cohort was inside the boost field; 14 of 16 standard-dose invasive recurrences were at the original tumour site
- ⚠️ Escalation cohort was cleaner at baseline: single tumour focus 39 (100%) vs 44 (65%), hydronephrosis 4 (10%) vs 19 (28%), T3 3 (8%) vs 11 (16%)
- ⚠️ Multivariable model adjusted for T stage only, so focality and hydronephrosis imbalance is unadjusted
- ⚠️ 3 standard-dose recurrences were presumed invasive on CT (no cystoscopy, comorbidity); all escalation-cohort recurrences were cystoscopy-confirmed
- 🔍 Chemo omitted in 51% for poor PS/comorbidity; absence of chemo carried worse OS (HR 2.83 [1.42, 5.63], p < 0.01 on MVA)
- 📊 Salvage cystectomy: 2 (2.9%) standard for invasive recurrence vs 1 (2.5%) escalation for BCG-refractory non-invasive disease; most invasive relapses were unfit for surgery

  - 📎 [[papers/bladder/dose-escalated-radiotherapy-for-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-a.pdf|Dose-Escalated RT for MIBC (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does the invasive local control gain survive prospective randomisation?
> - Is multifocal MIBC safely boostable?

## 🌰 Prostate

Two long-horizon datasets reframe when to add treatment: hormones to postop RT, and any treatment at all in screen-detected disease.

### GÖTEBORG-1

> PC-specific survival 94% at 25 yr on AS, but failure-free survival fell to 68% at 22 yr.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> 44 of 488 men reached RT off AS, and 18 failures were post-curative PSA relapse, so deferral shifts a share of pts into salvage rather than de novo definitive RT. Failure accrued steadily past 10 yr with no plateau, which argues for keeping the curative-intent RT window open decades out, not just through year 15.

- 🔍 488 screen-detected men on AS from the Göteborg-1 screening trial, 1995-2014; median f/u 18.0 yr (IQR 14.7-22.1)
- 🔍 Risk mix: 251 (51%) very-low, 129 (26%) low, 108 (22%) intermediate; high-risk excluded (n=6)
- 📊 KM outcomes for the whole cohort

  | Endpoint | 15 yr | 20 yr | 25 yr |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PC-specific survival | 97% (CI 95-99%) | 95% (CI 93-98%) | 94% (CI 91-98%) |
  | Overall survival | 63% (CI 58-67%) | 46% (CI 41-51%) | 32% (CI 26-38%) |
  | Treatment-free survival | 48% (CI 43-54%) | 43% (CI 37-50%) | 38% (CI 31-46%) at 22 yr |
  | Failure-free survival | 81% (CI 77-85%) | 74% (CI 68-81%) | 68% (CI 60-78%) at 22 yr |

- 📊 By risk group (PC-specific + OS at 24 yr; treatment-free at 19 yr)

  | Risk group | PC-specific survival 24 yr | Overall survival 24 yr | Treatment-free 19 yr |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Very low | 99% (CI 97-100) | 38% (CI 30-48) | 55% (CI 48-63) |
  | Low | 92% (CI 83-100) | 34% (CI 26-45) | 35% (CI 26-47) |
  | Intermediate | 85% (CI 75-95) | 22% (CI 12-37) | 30% (CI 18-48) |

- 📊 232 of 488 discontinued AS: 141 radical prostatectomy, 44 radiotherapy, 47 hormonal treatment
- 📊 81 failures: 14 PC deaths, 5 metastatic alive, 44 started HT, 18 PSA relapse post-curative treatment
- 📐 Multivariable Cox predictors of failure

  | Factor | HR | 95% CI | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Gleason 6 vs 7 | 3.12 | 1.59-6.13 | <0.001 |
  | PSA density (doubling) | 1.78 | 1.22-2.59 | 0.003 |
  | T stage T1c vs T2a-c | 1.89 | 1.08-3.30 | 0.025 |
  | PSA (doubling) | 1.37 | 0.84-2.25 | 0.2 |
  | Age (yr) | 1.03 | 0.97-1.09 | 0.4 |

- 🔗 Treatment-free survival 48% at 15 yr, vs 55% Toronto AS cohort (15 yr) and 49% PASS (10 yr)
- 🔗 PC mortality below PIVOT (11.4%) and roughly one-fifth of SPCG-4, reflecting screen-detected vs clinically diagnosed pts
- ⚠️ No predefined AS protocol or surveillance criteria; sextant biopsies until 2009, prediagnostic MRI in only 21 pts
- ⚠️ Pre-2005 Gleason grading means some "low-risk" tumors would reclassify as intermediate today
- ⚠️ 'Failure' pools PSA relapse, HT initiation, metastasis and PC death; a low-value untreated PSA relapse counts the same as a death

### POSEIDON

> IPD meta-analysis, 6057 pts: adding HT to PORT did not improve OS (HR 0·87, 95% CI 0·76–1·01, p=0·06).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The decision this moves is HT omission at early salvage: at PSA ≤0·5 ng/mL the OS point estimates numerically favoured PORT alone (≤0·20: HR 1·14, 10-yr OS 89·9% vs 87·4%), and the MFS gain never cleared the ICECaP 0·81 threshold at any PSA. Where HT is warranted (PSA >0·5), 4–6 months matched 24 (prolongation OS HR 0·89, p=0·37).

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis of 6 randomised comparisons via MARCAP (RTOG 9601, GETUG-AFU 16, RADICALS x3, NRG/RTOG 0534); 5026 pts in the primary HT-vs-no-HT comparison, median f/u 9·0 yrs (IQR 7·2–10·7)
- 📊 10-yr OS 83·6% (81·8–85·4) PORT alone vs 84·3% (82·7–86·0) PORT+HT; absolute 0·7% at 10 yrs
- 📊 MFS improved with HT: HR 0·79 (95% CI 0·70–0·89), p<0·001; 10-yr MFS 74·1% vs 77·9%
- ⚠️ MFS gain did not clear the ICECaP surrogate threshold: upper 95% CI bound crossed HR 0·81 at every pre-PORT PSA value, so the MFS signal is not expected to translate into an OS benefit
- 📊 OS by pre-PORT PSA stratum (HT added to PORT)

  | Pre-PORT PSA (ng/mL) | HR (95% CI) | p | 10-yr OS RT alone | 10-yr OS RT+HT |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | ≤0·20 | 1·14 (0·83–1·57) | 0·43 | 89·9% | 87·4% |
  | 0·21–0·50 | 0·94 (0·74–1·19) | 0·70 | 84·9% | 84·5% |
  | 0·51–1·00 | 0·72 (0·54–0·96) | 0·02 | 76·4% | 80·9% |
  | >1·00 | 0·69 (0·48–0·98) | 0·03 | 72·4% | 80·5% |

- 📐 Categorical 4-strata PSA interaction was not significant (pinteraction=0·08); the binary >0·5 vs ≤0·5 ng/mL interaction was (pinteraction=0·02), confirmed in the two-stage within-trial model (p=0·03)
- 📊 Short-term HT (4–6 mo, 4 trials, n=3938): no OS benefit (HR 0·93, 0·77–1·11) but MFS improved (HR 0·82, 0·71–0·95)
- 📊 Long-term HT (24 mo, 2 trials, n=1088): OS HR 0·79 (0·63–1·00), p=0·049; MFS HR 0·74 (0·60–0·91), p=0·004
- 📐 No significant interaction by HT duration (pinteraction=0·17); long-term-HT trials enrolled higher pre-PORT PSA and more adverse pathology
- 📊 Prolonging short-term to long-term HT (RADICALS, n=1523): OS HR 0·89 (0·68–1·16), p=0·37; MFS HR 0·76 (0·61–0·95), p=0·02
- 📐 NNT to prevent one death at 10 yrs by pre-PORT PSA (any HT added)
  - PSA 0·0–0·5 ng/mL: negative (point estimates numerically favoured PORT alone; imprecise)
  - PSA 0·51–1·00 ng/mL: 22
  - PSA >1·00 ng/mL: 12
- ⚠️ Spline analysis: for long-term HT the upper 95% CI bound for OS HR crossed 1·0 only at PSA ≥1·6 ng/mL, far above the median 0·3 ng/mL seen in modern early salvage
- ⚠️ No multiplicity correction applied; the authors state secondary subgroup findings are hypothesis-generating
- ⚠️ Trials predate Decipher and PSMA PET; discussant Koontz notes applicability is limited to PET-negative biochemical recurrence
- 🔗 Contrast with the definitive-RT setting (MARCAP 2022, Lancet Oncol): ADT with intact-prostate RT improves OS, whereas the postoperative setting here does not

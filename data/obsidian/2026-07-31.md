# 2026-07-31

**POSEIDON: adding HT to post-op RT gives OS HR 0·87 (0·76-1·01, p=0·06) overall, benefit confined to pre-PORT PSA >0·5 (HR 0·72 and 0·69).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day, both readings pushing toward less treatment earlier: POSEIDON localises the HT benefit with PORT to pre-PORT PSA >0·5 ng/mL (NNT 22 to 12), while GÖTEBORG-1 shows 94% PC-specific survival at 25yr on AS with FFS eroding to 68%. Bladder adds a retrospective SIB signal (2yr invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5%, SHR 0.20) with baseline imbalance.

## 💧 Bladder

SIB dose escalation moves local control but not OS or MFS, in a retrospective cohort whose boost arm started with better disease.

### Dose-Escalated RT for Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

> 2yr invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5% with SIB dose escalation, adjusted SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89), p=0.035; no OS or MFS difference.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The boost was a simultaneous integrated boost to the primary lesion, 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx, deliverable on daily CBCT without an elective-volume change, and G2+ GU toxicity did not rise (17.9% vs 22.1%). Half the cohort got no chemotherapy, so this speaks directly to the chemo-ineligible pt where RT intensity is the only lever left.

- 🔍 Multicentre retrospective, 3 centres, March 2015 to May 2025, N=107 (39 escalated / 68 standard), median f/u 23 mo (3-104)
- 📐 Standard: 55Gy/20fx or 64Gy/32fx whole bladder. Escalated: SIB to 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx to the primary lesion
- 📊 Invasive recurrence events: 2 (5.1%) escalated vs 16 (23.5%) standard
- 📊 Outcomes by cohort (escalated vs standard)

  | Endpoint | Dose escalation | Standard dose | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2yr invasive local recurrence (CI) | 5.5% | 27.5% | SHR 0.20 (0.05, 0.89), p=0.035 (adj T stage) |
  | 2yr non-invasive recurrence (CI) | 6.7% | 9.9% | SHR 0.88 (0.23-3.33), p=0.98 |
  | 2yr metastasis (CI) | 21.1% | 32.3% | p=0.79 univariable |
  | 2yr overall survival | 71.1% | 64.4% | p=0.5 |
  | G2+ GU toxicity | 17.9% (7) | 22.1% (15) | p=0.8 |
  | G2+ GI toxicity | 5.1% (2) | 7.4% (5) | p=0.9 |

- 📊 Pattern of failure: 1 of 2 invasive recurrences in the escalated cohort was inside the boost field, the other outside
- 📊 G3 toxicity in 2 pts (2.9%), both standard dose. No pt stopped treatment early for toxicity
- 💊 Chemo omitted in 51% overall, mostly for performance status / comorbidity. Absence of chemo carried worse OS (HR 2.83 [1.42, 5.63], p<0.01 on MVA)
- ⚠️ Baseline imbalance favours the boost arm: single tumour focus 100% vs 65%, hydronephrosis 10% vs 28%, T3 8% vs 16%. Only T stage was adjusted for
- ⚠️ 3 standard-dose recurrences were presumed invasive on CT plus MDT consensus, not cystoscopy; all escalated-cohort recurrences were cystoscopy-confirmed, so the endpoint was not ascertained identically between groups
- ⚠️ Salvage cystectomy was rare in both arms (2 standard, 1 escalated) because most pts with invasive relapse were unfit for surgery, so bladder preservation here partly reflects operability, not disease control

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Dose-Escalated Radiotherapy for Muscle Invasive Bladder Cancer: A retrospective analysis** — Chan, Li; Anzela, Anzela; Do, Viet et al.. *Advances in Radiation Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1016/j.adro.2026.102156](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adro.2026.102156)
  - 📎 [[papers/bladder/dose-escalated-radiotherapy-for-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-a.pdf|Dose-Escalated RT for Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does the local-control benefit survive randomisation and balanced tumour focality?
> - Can multifocal MIBC be boosted at all, or only unifocal disease?
> - Late GU toxicity beyond 23 months with an integrated boost

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Lymphopenia-sparing SBRT planning is testable as a randomised endpoint, with the effect concentrated in central tumours.

### Proactive Immune Cell Sparing SBRT (NCT04273893) · [[NCT04273893]]

> ALC reduction 13.4% (5.3%) less with immune-sparing planning across all timepoints (95% CI 2.8-24.0, p=0.01) in early-stage lung SBRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetric recipe is the transferable part: adding heart, great vessels, thoracic spine and lymph-node-stations as OARs down to 40 cGy/fx cut LN-station V5 by 58% and spine V5 by 87% without loosening RTOG 0813/0915 constraints or lung sparing (total lung-PTV V10 unchanged, 0%). The benefit concentrated in central tumors and peripheral PTV >20cc, which is where a planner would spend the effort.

- 🔍 Phase II randomized, N=51 analyzed (55 enrolled, 4 withdrew/ineligible), single-institution, 2020-2023, unmasked, stratified by tumor location
- 🔍 Both arms met RTOG 0813/0915 constraints; optimized arm additionally minimized dose to heart, great vessels, lymph-node-stations, thoracic spine
- 📐 SBRT 45-60 Gy in 5 fx (IMRT/VMAT), 5mm radial / 8mm sup-inf PTV margin; 52% optimized vs 53.8% standard received 60 Gy/5
- 📊 Average % reduction in optimized vs standard plans (integral dose, V5, V10)

  | Organ | Integral dose | V5 | V10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Aorta | 35% | 48% | 69% |
  | Heart | 21% | 43% | 68% |
  | Vena cava | 37% | 58% | 75% |
  | Thoracic spine | 57% | 87% | 92% |
  | Lymph-node-stations | 37% | 58% | 68% |
  | Total lung - PTV | 5% | 8% | 0% |

- 📊 ALC % change from baseline by timepoint (model estimates)

  | Timepoint | Optimized | Standard | Between-group diff |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Immediately post | -16% | -31% | 15.1% (95% CI 3.7-26.5), p=0.01 |
  | 4 weeks | -22% | -34% | 12.3% (95% CI 0.2-24.5), p=0.05 |
  | 6 months | -16% | -26% | 10.4% (95% CI -4.7-25.5), p=0.17 |

- 📊 Central tumors (n=15) drove the effect: overall improvement 29.5% (9.6%), 95% CI 10.1-48.9, p=0.004
- 📊 Peripheral PTV >20cc (n=18): 13.5% (8.6%), 95% CI -3.8 to 20.9, p=0.124; peripheral PTV <20cc showed no difference
- 📊 G3 lymphopenia 15.4% standard vs 0% optimized (risk difference -15.4%, 95% CI -29.2 to -1.5, p=0.04)
- 📊 LN V5 above vs below 35.4cc median: two-year OS 57.5% (SE 10.4%) vs 91.8% (SE 5.5%), p=0.02
- ⚠️ Randomization left arms unbalanced: central tumors 36.0% optimized vs 23.1% standard, treatment-naive 64.0% vs 88.5%
- ⚠️ Survival analyses were unplanned subgroup analyses in a trial not powered for OS or EFS; all p values non-significant
- ⚠️ Preprint (medRxiv), not peer-reviewed; single institution, one planning system (Pinnacle)

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 16111586]] **First Measurement: Proactive Immune Cell Sparing in Radiation Therapy** — Wijesooriya, Krishni; Nguyen, Cam; Conaway, Mark R et al.. *medRxiv* (2025-01). [doi:10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011)
  > Abstract Purpose Radiation Therapy (RT) can modulate the immune system and generate anti-tumor T cells. However, this anti-tumor-activity is countered by radiation-induced immunosuppression (RIIS). Clinical advantages of proactively sparing RT dose to immune rich organs have not previously been evaluated. Methods We conducted a phase II randomized trial from 2020 to 2023, enrolling 51 early-stage lung cancer patients treated with SBRT, to evaluate the effect of dose reduction to immune rich organs on RIIS. Two groups were: RIIS-optimized-treatment (lowering the dose to blood, bone-marrow and lymph-node-stations) and standard-treatment. All treatments followed national protocol guidelines. Peripheral blood was collected at baseline, immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-treatment. Results ALC changes from baseline immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-SBRT are: optimized-arm: -16%, -22%, -16%, standard-arm: -31%, -34%, -26%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 13.4 (5.3) % (95% CI, 2.8 to 24.0; p = 0.01). Central tumors had the largest improvement in ALC from baseline: optimized-arm: - 8%, -18%, -14%, standard-arm: -39%, -43%, -47%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 29.5 (9.6) % (95% CI, 10.1 to 48.9; p = 0.004). Grade 3 lymphopenia occurred in 15.4% of standard arm patients but was absent in the optimized arm. Additionally, 2.8 times more patients in the optimized arm experienced an ALC increase post-SBRT. Dose to organs such as the heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, and lymph nodes significantly correlated with RIIS. A trend towards increased Event-Free-Survival (two-year: 75.0% (SE = 10.8%) versus 59.8% (SE = 11.2%), p =0·10) and Overall Survival (two-year: 93.4% (SE=6.1%) versus 69.4% (SE=10.5%), p =0·14) was observed with optimized-planning compared to standard-planning in treatment naïve patients. Conclusion Reducing RT dose to immune rich organs significantly reduces RIIS compared to standard-of-care. This has implications in enhancing immune system mediated anti-tumor-activity. (Funded by National Cancer Institute and others. ClinicalTrials.gov number, [[NCT04273893]] )

## 🫘 Kidney

First international RCC SABR contouring consensus, with agreement tracking case complexity rather than reader.

### IROCK Contouring Guidelines (RCC SABR)

> First international consensus contouring atlas for RCC SABR: median DSC 0.85 across 16 experts, 4 scenario-specific iGTV statements.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The two hardest scenarios are named and quantified: Case 1 (IVC thrombus) and Case 4 (post-RFA cavity) carried the worst agreement (DSC 0.85 and 0.75, HD 64.60 and 9.00 mm), driven by how far superiorly thrombus was covered and how much cavity was included. Both statements push toward larger volumes, so OAR priority and a 5 mm PTV are the gates on whether that transfers.

- 🔍 16 RCC SABR experts (US, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, India), all with ≥10 prior RCC SABR cases; 14/16 treated ≥10 in the prior 12 months
- 🔍 CT-only contouring on EduCase, 4D-CT sim; STAPLE (95% CI) built the consensus contour, refined over 2 online meetings (May and June 2024)
- 📊 Contour agreement vs consensus, by case (median, IQR)

  | Case | DSC | MDA (mm) | HD (mm) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1: >10 cm RCC + IVC thrombus | 0.85, 0.79-0.85 | 6.69, 5.88-8.79 | 64.60, 64.44-86.40 |
  | 2: central tumor at hilum | 0.90, 0.84-0.93 | 1.55, 1.00-2.09 | 7.92, 5.35-9.98 |
  | 3: local recurrence post-RN | 0.91, 0.88-0.93 | 1.42, 1.18-1.89 | 6.18, 6.00-7.12 |
  | 4: post-RFA cavity recurrence | 0.75, 0.60-0.79 | 2.50, 2.12-2.99 | 9.00, 9.00-12.39 |

- 📊 Case-specific iGTV consensus statements (100% panel agreement)
  - Case 1: primary tumor + entire tumor thrombus in iGTV; bland thrombus excluded
  - Case 2: all tumor on sim imaging, accounting for internal motion; renal hilum contour optional, not dose-limiting
  - Case 3: all visible gross tumor; surgical clips need not be included
  - Case 4: gross recurrence + the entire RFA ablation cavity
- 📐 No margin for microscopic spread: no participant expanded iGTV to an ITV; most common PTV margin was uniform 5 mm
- 📐 5 mm PTV assumes supine vacuum-cushion immobilization, 4D-CT, IV contrast, daily CBCT; margin may differ with MRI sim or adaptive
- 🔍 Ipsilateral renal parenchyma defined as whole ipsilateral kidney minus iGTV, renal pelvis, renal vasculature, proximal ureter
- 💊 OAR constraints take priority over target coverage; heterogeneous coverage with a steep gradient is the stated escape valve, hotspot inside the iGTV
- 🔗 Suggested 1, 3, and 5 fraction dose objectives and OAR constraints are drawn from FASTRACK-II and AQuOS-II ([[NCT05023265]]) protocols (Table 2)
- ⚠️ Statistically different DSC, MDA, HD across the 4 cases (P < .05 for all 3); agreement tracked case complexity, not reader
- ⚠️ MDA differed between participants (P = .03) while DSC and HD did not (P = .07, P = .45), so residual inter-observer spread persists among experts
- ⚠️ CT-only by design; the panel itself notes MRI may have narrowed variation, and participants never recontoured after consensus
- ⚠️ Consensus contours are unvalidated against outcomes; no prospective test that adherence improves local control or reduces toxicity

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008)

## 🌰 Prostate

Two long-horizon reads on when to escalate: POSEIDON sets a PSA threshold for adding HT to PORT, GÖTEBORG-1 sets the 25-year price of surveillance.

### GÖTEBORG-1

> 25yr PC-specific survival 94% on active surveillance, but failure-free survival fell to 68% at 22yr.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant number is durability of the cure window: 18 of 81 failures were PSA relapse after RP or RT, and failure-free survival kept falling to 68% at 22 yr with no plateau. Intermediate-risk 19-yr failure-free survival was 55%, which frames how long a deferred definitive-RT candidate stays salvageable.

- 🔍 488 men with screen-detected low-/intermediate-risk PC on AS, Göteborg-1 screening trial, 1995-2014; median f/u 18.0 yr (IQR 14.7-22.1)
- 🔍 Risk mix: very-low 251 (51%), low 129 (26%), intermediate 108 (22%); median age 66, PSA 4.1 ng/ml, PSAD 0.12 ng/ml/cm3
- 📊 232 of 488 discontinued AS: RP 141, RT 44, hormonal treatment 47
- 📊 KM outcomes by timepoint (whole cohort)

  | Endpoint | 15 yr | 20 yr | 22-25 yr |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PC-specific survival | 97% (95-99) | 95% (93-98) | 94% (91-98) at 25 yr |
  | Overall survival | 63% (58-67) | 46% (41-51) | 32% (26-38) at 25 yr |
  | Treatment-free survival | 48% (43-54) | 43% (37-50) | 38% (31-46) at 22 yr |
  | Failure-free survival | 81% (77-85) | 74% (68-81) | 68% (60-78) at 22 yr |

- 📊 Risk-stratified outcomes (very-low / low / intermediate)

  | Endpoint | Very low risk | Low risk | Intermediate risk |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Treatment-free survival, 19 yr | 55% (48-63) | 35% (26-47) | 30% (18-48) |
  | Failure-free survival, 19 yr | 85% | 74% | 55% |
  | PC-specific survival, 24 yr | 99% (97-100) | 92% (83-100) | 85% (75-95) |
  | Overall survival, 24 yr | 38% (30-48) | 34% (26-45) | 22% (12-37) |

- 📊 81 failures: 14 PC deaths, 5 metastatic alive, 44 started hormonal treatment, 18 PSA relapse post-curative treatment
- 📐 Multivariable Cox for failure: Gleason 6 vs 7 HR 3.12 (1.59-6.13), p<0.001; PSA density per doubling HR 1.78 (1.22-2.59), p=0.003; T1c vs T2a-c HR 1.89 (1.08-3.30), p=0.025
- 🔍 44 of the 232 who left AS took RT as primary treatment; PSA relapse after RT defined as nadir +2 ng/ml, post-RP as PSA ≥0.2
- 🔗 Treatment-free survival at 15 yr 48%, in line with Toronto AS 55% (15 yr) and PASS 49% (10 yr); PC mortality below PIVOT (11.4%) and roughly a fifth of SPCG-4, reflecting screen-detected vs clinically diagnosed pts
- ⚠️ No predefined AS protocol or follow-up schedule; sextant biopsies until 2009, prediagnostic MRI in only 21 of 488
- ⚠️ Nearly half of failures occurred >10 yr after diagnosis, so any cohort truncated at 15 yr understates the risk
- ⚠️ Authors' own read: monitoring with PSA + repeat biopsy was insufficient to guarantee curability in a subset

### POSEIDON

> OS HR 0·87 (0·76–1·01), p=0·06 for adding hormone therapy to PORT; benefit only above pre-PORT PSA 0·5 ng/mL.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The gate is pre-PORT PSA, not risk features. Below 0·5 ng/mL point estimates favour PORT alone (HR 1·14 at ≤0·20); above it, HR 0·72 and 0·69 with NNT 22 and 12. Long-term HT only separates on spline at PSA ≥1·6 ng/mL, above what early salvage practice sees.

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis, 6 randomised trials, 6057 pts, median f/u 9·0 yrs (IQR 7·2–10·7); MARCAP consortium
- 🔍 Trials: RTOG 9601, GETUG-AFU 16, RADICALS-HD (two-way + three-way), NRG/RTOG 0534
- 📊 1° EP overall survival: 10-yr 83·6% PORT alone vs 84·3% PORT+HT, absolute 0·7%
- 📊 MFS improved: HR 0·79 (0·70–0·89), p<0·001; 10-yr 74·1% vs 77·9%
- 📊 OS by pre-PORT PSA stratum (PORT+HT vs PORT alone)

  | Pre-PORT PSA (ng/mL) | HR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤0·20 | 1·14 (0·83–1·57) | 0·43 |
  | 0·21–0·50 | 0·94 (0·74–1·19) | 0·70 |
  | 0·51–1·00 | 0·72 (0·54–0·96) | 0·02 |
  | >1·00 | 0·69 (0·48–0·98) | 0·03 |

- 📊 By hormone therapy duration (exploratory, separate randomisations)

  | Comparison | OS HR (95% CI) | MFS HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Short-term (4–6 mo) added to PORT | 0·93 (0·77–1·11) | 0·82 (0·71–0·95) |
  | Long-term (24 mo) added to PORT | 0·79 (0·63–1·00) | 0·74 (0·60–0·91) |
  | Prolong short to long | 0·89 (0·68–1·16) | 0·76 (0·61–0·95) |

- 📐 Duration interaction not significant (pinteraction=0·17); PSA >0·5 vs ≤0·5 interaction was (pinteraction=0·02)
- 📐 NNT to prevent one death at 10 yrs: 22 at PSA 0·51–1·00, 12 at PSA >1·00; negative below 0·5
- 🔍 Spline: OS 95% CI upper bound for long-term HT falls below 1·0 only at pre-PORT PSA ≥1·6 ng/mL; MFS ≥1·2
- ⚠️ MFS gain does not clear the ICECaP surrogate threshold (upper CI crossed 0·81) at any pre-PORT PSA
- ⚠️ Long-term-HT cohort had higher pre-PORT PSA and worse pathology, so duration contrasts are confounded across trials
- ⚠️ No multiplicity correction; secondary subgroup findings are hypothesis-generating by the authors' own statement
- ⚠️ Enrolment predates Decipher and PSMA PET; discussant Koontz limits applicability to PET-negative biochemical recurrence

# 2026-07-31

**POSEIDON IPD meta-analysis: adding hormone therapy to PORT gives OS HR 0·87 (0·76-1·01, p=0·06), benefit confined to pre-PORT PSA >0·5.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Two RT-dose/intensity questions cut opposite ways: POSEIDON restricts postoperative ADT to men salvaged late (PSA >0·5), while a 107-pt bladder series ties an SIB boost to 2yr invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5% (SHR 0.20) with no OS gain. Prostate and bladder carry the day; a randomised immune-sparing SBRT trial adds a planning lever that costs no drug.

## 💧 Bladder

Retrospective dose-escalation signal for local control, allocation not random and chemo used in under half.

### Dose-Escalated RT for Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

> 2yr invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5%, adjusted SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89), p=0.035, with no OS or MFS difference.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The boost was a simultaneous integrated boost to the GTV, not whole-bladder escalation, and whole-bladder CTV coverage stayed intact, so the technique transfers directly to a daily-CBCT TMT workflow. G2+ GU was 17.9% vs 22.1% (p=0.8), meaning the escalation cost nothing measurable in toxicity. This moves the boost-volume question, not the whole-bladder dose question.

- 📊 2yr cumulative incidence invasive local recurrence 5.5% (escalated) vs 27.5% (standard)
- 📐 MVA adjusted for T stage: SHR 0.20 (0.05, 0.89), p=0.035; UVA SHR 0.21 (0.05, 0.94), p=0.04
- 🔍 SIB technique: 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx to primary lesion vs standard 55Gy/20fx or 64Gy/32fx
- 🔍 Multicentre retrospective, 3 centres, N=107 (39 escalated / 68 standard), median f/u 23mo (3-104)
- 📊 Escalated vs standard across endpoints

  | Endpoint | Dose escalation | Standard dose | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2yr invasive LR | 5.5% | 27.5% | SHR 0.20 (0.05, 0.89), p=0.035 |
  | 2yr non-invasive LR | 6.7% | 9.9% | SHR 0.88 (0.23-3.33), p=0.98 |
  | 2yr metastasis | 21.1% | 32.3% | p=0.79 UVA |
  | 2yr OS | 71.1% | 64.4% | p=0.5 |
  | G2+ GU tox | 7 (17.9%) | 15 (22.1%) | p=0.8 |
  | G2+ GI tox | 2 (5.1%) | 5 (7.4%) | p=0.9 |

- ⚠️ Allocation was not random: escalated cohort had 100% single-focus disease vs 65%, hydronephrosis 10% vs 28%
- ⚠️ 3 standard-dose recurrences were presumed invasive on CT alone (unfit for cystoscopy); all escalated-cohort recurrences cystoscopy-confirmed
- 📊 1 of 2 invasive recurrences in the escalated cohort was inside the boost field; no non-invasive recurrence occurred in-boost
- 📊 Salvage cystectomy for invasive recurrence: 2 (2.9%) standard, 0 escalated; most invasive relapses were unfit for surgery
- 💊 Concurrent chemo in only 49% overall (44% standard, 56% escalated); chemo omission carried worse OS (HR 2.83 [1.42, 5.63], p<0.01 MVA)
- 🔗 Boost dose here (60Gy/20fx, 70Gy/32fx) sits above the BC2001/BCON-era whole-bladder standard the control arm reflects

  - 📎 [[papers/bladder/dose-escalated-radiotherapy-for-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-a.pdf|Dose-Escalated RT for Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does the boost benefit survive randomisation against multifocality selection
> - Is 60Gy/20fx the right boost dose or can it go higher
> - Does the boost help patients unfit for concurrent chemotherapy

## 🫘 Kidney

First international consensus contouring guideline for RCC SABR, CT-only and not yet prospectively validated.

### IROCK Contouring Guidelines

> First international consensus contouring guideline for RCC SABR; 16 experts, median DSC 0.85 across 4 scenarios.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable parameters are here: 5mm uniform iGTV-to-PTV expansion with no microscopic-spread margin, predicated on 4D-CT, vacuum cushion and daily CBCT, and ipsilateral parenchyma drawn as whole kidney minus iGTV, pelvis, vasculature and ureter. Agreement was worst on the post-RFA cavity (DSC 0.75, IQR 0.60-0.79), the case most likely to be contoured wrong locally.

- 🔍 16 RCC SABR experts (US, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, India), all with ≥10 prior RCC SABR cases; 14/16 treated ≥10 in the prior 12 mo
- 🔍 4 contouring scenarios: >10cm tumor with IVC thrombus, central tumor abutting hilum, post-nephrectomy local recurrence, post-RFA cavity recurrence
- 🔍 CT-only contouring on EduCase; STAPLE (95%) consensus, refined over 2 online meetings (May and June 2024), statements revised to 100% agreement
- 📊 Contour agreement vs consensus by case (median, IQR)

  | Case | DSC | MDA (mm) | HD (mm) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 · >10cm + IVC thrombus | 0.85, 0.79-0.85 | 6.69, 5.88-8.79 | 64.60, 64.44-86.40 |
  | 2 · central, abutting hilum | 0.90, 0.84-0.93 | 1.55, 1.00-2.09 | 7.92, 5.35-9.98 |
  | 3 · post-RN recurrence | 0.91, 0.88-0.93 | 1.42, 1.18-1.89 | 6.18, 6.00-7.12 |
  | 4 · post-RFA cavity | 0.75, 0.60-0.79 | 2.50, 2.12-2.99 | 9.00, 9.00-12.39 |
  | Overall | 0.85 (range 0.40-0.95) | 2.17 (range 0.71-10.82) | 9.00 (range 4.00-89.31) |

- 📐 Target-volume statements the panel agreed on
  - iGTV includes primary tumor plus entire tumor thrombus; bland thrombus excluded
  - iGTV includes the visible recurrence plus the entire ablation cavity post-RFA
  - Surgical clips need not be in the iGTV when not tumor-involved
  - OAR constraints take priority over target coverage; hotspot placed inside iGTV
- 📐 No margin for microscopic spread: no participant expanded the iGTV, so no separate ITV contour is recommended
- 📐 Most common PTV margin was a uniform 5mm on iGTV, predicated on supine vacuum-cushion setup, 4D-CT, IV contrast, daily CBCT
- 🔍 Ipsilateral renal parenchyma defined as whole ipsilateral kidney minus iGTV, renal pelvis, renal vasculature, proximal ureter; hilum optional, not dose-limiting
- ⚠️ 2-way ANOVA: DSC, MDA and HD differed by case (P < .05 for all 3); MDA also differed between participants (P = .03), DSC and HD did not (P = .07, P = .45)
- ⚠️ CT-only exercise by design; the authors state MRI availability may have reduced variation, and participants did not recontour after the consensus meetings
- 🔗 Suggested dose objectives and OAR constraints are adopted from FASTRACK-II and AQuOS-II ([[NCT05023265]]) protocols across 1, 3 and 5 fraction schedules, not derived here
- ⚠️ Not validated prospectively; the authors flag that recommendations may change with future trial results

[doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.01.008)
  - 📎 [[papers/kidney/international-radiosurgery-oncology-consortium-of-the-kidney-iro.pdf|IROCK Contouring Guidelines (full text)]]

## 🌰 Prostate

Postoperative ADT narrows to late salvage, while 25yr Göteborg-1 AS data show PC-specific survival 94% with only 44/488 needing RT.

### GÖTEBORG-1

> 25yr PC-specific survival 94% on active surveillance, but failure-free survival fell to 68% at 22yr; 44/488 needed RT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Only 44 of 488 men reached radiotherapy, and salvage-eligibility is the RT-relevant signal: 18 had PSA relapse after RP or RT without hormonal treatment, and 44 went straight to hormones, meaning cure was missed. Nearly half of failures occurred beyond 10 yr, so the surveillance window that gates curative RT does not close at 15 yr.

- 📊 PC-specific survival 94% (CI 91-98%) at 25 yr; OS 32% (CI 26-38%) at 25 yr
- 📊 Failure-free survival 68% (CI 60-78%) at 22 yr; treatment-free survival 38% (CI 31-46%) at 22 yr
- 🔍 488 screen-detected men on AS from the Göteborg-1 screening arm, 1995-2014; median f/u 18.0 yr (IQR 14.7-22.1)
- 🔍 Risk mix: very-low 251 (51%), low 129 (26%), intermediate 108 (22%); high-risk excluded (n=6)
- 📊 Outcomes at 19-24 yr by risk group (source: abstract + Supplementary Table 2)

  | Endpoint | Very-low risk | Low risk | Intermediate risk |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Treatment-free survival, 19 yr | 55% (CI 48-63) | 35% (CI 26-47) | 30% (CI 18-48) |
  | Failure-free survival, 19 yr | 85% | 74% | 55% |
  | PC-specific survival, 24 yr | 99% (CI 97-100) | 92% (CI 83-100) | 85% (CI 75-95) |
  | Overall survival, 24 yr | 38% (CI 30-48) | 34% (CI 26-45) | 22% (CI 12-37) |

- 💊 Of 232 who left AS: 141 RP, 44 RT, 47 hormonal treatment
- 📐 Multivariable Cox for failure: Gleason 6 vs 7 HR 3.12 (1.59-6.13), p<0.001; PSA density per doubling HR 1.78 (1.22-2.59), p=0.003; T1c vs T2a-c HR 1.89 (1.08-3.30), p=0.025
- ⚠️ Failure is a composite (noncurative PSA relapse, hormonal treatment, metastasis, PC death), so 68% FFS overstates hard oncologic events: only 14 PC deaths and 5 metastases
- ⚠️ Of the 14 PC deaths, 8 were determined from death certificates rather than the adjudication committee
- ⚠️ Pre-MRI era: sextant biopsies to 2009, MRI in only 21 pts, no predefined AS protocol or trigger criteria
- 🔗 Treatment-free survival 48% at 15 yr sits with the Toronto AS cohort (55% at 15 yr) and PASS (49% at 10 yr); PC mortality is far below PIVOT (11.4%) and SPCG-4, reflecting screen-detected vs clinically diagnosed disease

[doi:10.1016/j.eururo.2025.06.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2025.06.012)

### POSEIDON

> OS HR 0·87 (0·76-1·01, p=0·06) for adding hormone therapy to PORT; benefit confined to pre-PORT PSA >0·5 ng/mL.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The decision this moves is hormone therapy omission at early salvage: below PSA 0·5 ng/mL the point estimates favour PORT alone (HR 1·14 at PSA ≤0·20), and the MFS gain never clears the ICECaP 0·81 threshold, so it cannot be read as deferred survival. Above 0·5, 4-6 months suffices; prolongation to 24 months added nothing on OS (HR 0·89).

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis, six randomised phase 3 comparisons, 6057 pts, median f/u 9·0 yr (IQR 7·2-10·7); 5026 in the primary PORT +/- hormone therapy comparison
- 🔍 Trials: RTOG 9601, GETUG-AFU 16, RADICALS-HD (two-way and three-way), NRG/RTOG 0534; MARCAP consortium, one-stage Cox stratified by trial
- 📊 10-yr OS 83·6% (81·8-85·4) PORT alone vs 84·3% (82·7-86·0) with hormone therapy; absolute benefit 0·7%
- 📊 Effect by hormone therapy duration (vs PORT alone)

  | Comparison | n | OS HR (95% CI) | MFS HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Any hormone therapy | 5026 | 0·87 (0·76-1·01), p=0·06 | 0·79 (0·70-0·89), p<0·001 |
  | Short-term (4-6 mo) | 3938 | 0·93 (0·77-1·11) | 0·82 (0·71-0·95) |
  | Long-term (24 mo) | 1088 | 0·79 (0·63-1·00), p=0·049 | 0·74 (0·60-0·91), p=0·004 |
  | Short → long prolongation | 1523 | 0·89 (0·68-1·16), p=0·37 | 0·76 (0·61-0·95), p=0·02 |

- 📊 OS by pre-PORT PSA stratum (hormone therapy vs PORT alone)

  | Pre-PORT PSA (ng/mL) | HR (95% CI) | p | 10-yr absolute benefit % |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ≤0·20 | 1·14 (0·83-1·57) | 0·43 | −2·5 (−6·4 to 1·4) |
  | 0·21-0·50 | 0·94 (0·74-1·19) | 0·70 | −0·4 (−4·3 to 3·4) |
  | 0·51-1·00 | 0·72 (0·54-0·96) | 0·02 | 4·5 (−1·9 to 10·9) |
  | >1·00 | 0·69 (0·48-0·98) | 0·03 | 8·1 (−0·1 to 16·3) |

- 📐 Duration interaction ns (pinteraction=0·17); PSA >0·5 vs ≤0·5 binary interaction p=0·02, robust in the two-stage within-trial model (p=0·03)
- 📐 NNT to prevent one death at 10 yr: 22 for PSA 0·51-1·00, 12 for PSA >1·00; negative (favouring PORT alone) below 0·5
- ⚠️ MFS gain did not clear the ICECaP surrogate threshold: upper 95% CI bound of the MFS HR crossed 0·81 at every pre-PORT PSA in the pooled analysis
- ⚠️ Categorical four-stratum PSA interaction was ns on univariable analysis (pinteraction=0·08); no multiplicity correction, so subgroup reads are hypothesis-generating
- ⚠️ Enrolment predates Decipher and PSMA PET; discussant Koontz restricted applicability to PET-negative biochemical recurrence
- 🔗 Contrasts with the definitive-RT MARCAP analysis (Kishan, Lancet Oncol 2022), where adding ADT to RT for an intact prostate improved survival; the postoperative setting does not reproduce it
- 🔗 RTOG 9601 was the lone positive OS trial and enrolled late salvage (median PSA 0·5, IQR 0·3-0·9 in the long-term stratum) rather than the PSA 0·3 typical of the modern early-salvage cohort

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

Immune-organ-sparing SBRT planning preserves ALC without changing dosimetric compliance; central tumours gain most.

### Immune-Sparing SBRT for Early-Stage Lung Cancer (NCT04273893) · [[NCT04273893]]

> 13.4% less ALC loss (95% CI 2.8-24.0, p=0.014) with immune-organ-sparing SBRT planning; central tumours gained most (29.5%).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning-actionable number is LN-station V5: below the 35.4cc median, 2-yr OS was 91.8% vs 57.5% (p=0.02), unplanned but the only dose variable here tied to survival. Sparing cost nothing at the target (lung minus PTV integral dose -5%, V10 0%), so this moves whether low-dose objectives on heart, great vessels, spine and nodal stations enter your lung SBRT template.

- 📊 ALC change from baseline, by arm and timepoint (model estimates, adjusted for tumour location)

  | Timepoint | Optimized | Standard | Between-group difference (95% CI), p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | End of treatment | -16% | -31% | 15.1% (3.7 to 26.5), p=0.01 |
  | 4 weeks | -22% | -34% | 12.3% (0.2 to 24.5), p=0.05 |
  | 6 months | -16% | -26% | 10.4% (-4.7 to 25.5), p=0.17 |

- 🔍 45-60 Gy in 5 fx by IMRT/VMAT; both arms met RTOG 0813/0915 dosimetric criteria
- 📐 Average dose reduction, optimized vs standard plans, by immune-rich organ

  | Organ | Integral dose | V5 | V10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Thoracic spine | -57% | -87% | -92% |
  | Vena cava | -37% | -58% | -75% |
  | Lymph-node stations | -37% | -58% | -68% |
  | Aorta | -35% | -48% | -69% |
  | Heart | -21% | -43% | -68% |
  | Total lung minus PTV | -5% | -8% | 0% |

- 📊 ALC-reduction improvement by tumour location and PTV volume (all timepoints)

  | Subgroup | n | Improvement | 95% CI | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Central | 15 | 29.5% | 10.1 to 48.9 | 0.004 |
  | Peripheral, PTV >20cc | 18 | 13.5% | -3.8 to 20.9 | 0.124 |
  | Peripheral, PTV <20cc | 18 | no difference | n/a | n/a |

- 📊 G3 lymphopenia 15.4% standard vs none optimized; risk difference -15.4% (-29.2 to -1.5), p=0.04
- 📊 LN-station V5 below the 35.4cc median: 2-yr OS 91.8% vs 57.5%, p=0.02
- ⚠️ OS and EFS were unplanned analyses in 51 pts; all-patient OS p=0.23, EFS p=0.27
- ⚠️ Randomisation left the arms uneven in both directions (optimized vs standard)
  - Treatment naive: 64.0% vs 88.5%
  - Central tumours: 36.0% vs 23.1%
  - cT2: 0.0% vs 11.5%
- 🔗 Cited lung SBRT + IO trial lifted 4-yr EFS 53% to 77%; this gain needed no drug

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 16111586]] **First Measurement: Proactive Immune Cell Sparing in Radiation Therapy** — Wijesooriya, Krishni; Nguyen, Cam; Conaway, Mark R et al.. *medRxiv* (2025-01). [doi:10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011)
  > Abstract Purpose Radiation Therapy (RT) can modulate the immune system and generate anti-tumor T cells. However, this anti-tumor-activity is countered by radiation-induced immunosuppression (RIIS). Clinical advantages of proactively sparing RT dose to immune rich organs have not previously been evaluated. Methods We conducted a phase II randomized trial from 2020 to 2023, enrolling 51 early-stage lung cancer patients treated with SBRT, to evaluate the effect of dose reduction to immune rich organs on RIIS. Two groups were: RIIS-optimized-treatment (lowering the dose to blood, bone-marrow and lymph-node-stations) and standard-treatment. All treatments followed national protocol guidelines. Peripheral blood was collected at baseline, immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-treatment. Results ALC changes from baseline immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-SBRT are: optimized-arm: -16%, -22%, -16%, standard-arm: -31%, -34%, -26%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 13.4 (5.3) % (95% CI, 2.8 to 24.0; p = 0.01). Central tumors had the largest improvement in ALC from baseline: optimized-arm: - 8%, -18%, -14%, standard-arm: -39%, -43%, -47%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 29.5 (9.6) % (95% CI, 10.1 to 48.9; p = 0.004). Grade 3 lymphopenia occurred in 15.4% of standard arm patients but was absent in the optimized arm. Additionally, 2.8 times more patients in the optimized arm experienced an ALC increase post-SBRT. Dose to organs such as the heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, and lymph nodes significantly correlated with RIIS. A trend towards increased Event-Free-Survival (two-year: 75.0% (SE = 10.8%) versus 59.8% (SE = 11.2%), p =0·10) and Overall Survival (two-year: 93.4% (SE=6.1%) versus 69.4% (SE=10.5%), p =0·14) was observed with optimized-planning compared to standard-planning in treatment naïve patients. Conclusion Reducing RT dose to immune rich organs significantly reduces RIIS compared to standard-of-care. This has implications in enhancing immune system mediated anti-tumor-activity. (Funded by National Cancer Institute and others. ClinicalTrials.gov number, [[NCT04273893]] )

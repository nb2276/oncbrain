# 2026-07-31

**POSEIDON: adding HT to PORT missed OS across 6 RCTs (HR 0.87, p=0.06); OS benefit confined to pre-PORT PSA >0.5 ng/mL (HR 0.72 at 0.51-1.00, 0.69 >1.00).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day. POSEIDON limits HT with PORT to pre-PORT PSA >0.5. GÖTEBORG-1 shows 25-yr PCSS 94% on AS, but only 38% of men were still treatment-free at 22 yr. The RT dose and planning signals are hypothesis-generating: retrospective SIB escalation in MIBC TMT, and a preprint randomized phase II of immune-sparing lung SBRT.

## 🫘 Kidney

First international IROCK consensus on iGTV delineation for localized RCC SABR.

### IROCK RCC SABR contouring guidelines

> First international consensus on target delineation for localized RCC SABR: 16 experts, median DSC 0.85 vs consensus, >0.70 in every case.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The two contested target calls are settled: include the entire tumor thrombus and the entire RFA cavity, and these were also the cases with most variation (DSC 0.85 and 0.75; HD 64.60 mm in Case 1). The 5 mm PTV assumes 4DCT, vacuum cushion, IV contrast and daily CBCT, so a center without those shouldn't copy the margin.

- 🔍 16 IROCK radiation oncologists, each with ≥10 RCC SABR cases; US, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, India
- 🔍 4 CT-only cases contoured on EduCase; STAPLE 95% consensus contour, then 2 consensus meetings
- 🧭 Consensus target statements by scenario

  | Case | Scenario | iGTV recommendation |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Tumor >10 cm with IVC tumor thrombus | Primary tumor + entire tumor thrombus; exclude bland thrombus |
  | 2 | Central tumor abutting renal hilum | All visible tumor, account for internal motion; may extend into hilum |
  | 3 | Local recurrence post-nephrectomy | All visible gross tumor; surgical clips need not be included |
  | 4 | Residual tumor post-RFA | Visible gross recurrence + entire ablation cavity |

- 📊 Participant vs consensus contour agreement, median (IQR)

  | Case | DSC | MDA (mm) | HD (mm) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 0.85, 0.79-0.85 | 6.69, 5.88-8.79 | 64.60, 64.44-86.40 |
  | 2 | 0.90, 0.84-0.93 | 1.55, 1.00-2.09 | 7.92, 5.35-9.98 |
  | 3 | 0.91, 0.88-0.93 | 1.42, 1.18-1.89 | 6.18, 6.00-7.12 |
  | 4 | 0.75, 0.60-0.79 | 2.50, 2.12-2.99 | 9.00, 9.00-12.39 |

- 🧭 All-case planning statements
  - OAR constraints prioritized over target coverage; heterogeneous coverage with steep gradient
  - Dose hotspot placed within the iGTV
  - No microscopic-spread margin; most common PTV margin 5 mm uniform on iGTV
  - Contrast CT or MRI; nephrology referral if contrast nephrotoxicity risk
- 🧭 Renal cortex = ipsilateral kidney minus iGTV, renal pelvis, vasculature, proximal ureter
- 🧭 Renal hilum optional, not a dose-limiting OAR; no evidence it reduces renal artery stenosis
- 📐 2-way ANOVA: DSC/MDA/HD differed by case (P < .05); MDA also differed by participant (P = .03)
- ⚠️ CT-only images; no recontouring after consensus meetings to confirm reduced variation
- ⚠️ Table 2 fraction-specific OAR constraints (FASTRACK-II / AQuOS-II derived) not attributable row-wise from source text

## 🌰 Prostate

Two long-horizon datasets: which men get HT with salvage RT, gated by pre-PORT PSA, and 25-yr AS outcomes from the pre-MRI era.

### GÖTEBORG-1 active surveillance, 25-year follow-up

> 25-yr PC-specific survival 94% (CI 91-98%) on AS, but 22-yr failure-free survival 68% and treatment-free survival 38%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> RT was the exit for 44 of 488 men, but the paper reports no outcomes by treatment, so the efficacy of delayed RT after AS can't be separated out. Failure kept accruing past 10 yr in every risk group. For a man choosing AS over upfront RT, the trade is decades of monitoring, not a 10-15 yr window.

- 🔍 AS cohort nested in the GÖTEBORG-1 screening trial; AS was not randomized, and choosing it was left to the physician and pt
- 🔍 N=488 screen-detected pts: 251 very-low, 129 low, 108 intermediate risk; high-risk excluded
- 🔍 Median f/u 18.0 yr (IQR 14.7-22.1) in survivors; median age 66, median PSA 4.1 ng/ml
- ⚠️ No predefined AS protocol: PSA every 6-12 mo, rebiopsy only on clinical progression
- ⚠️ Pre-MRI era: sextant biopsies until 2009, only 21 pts had prediagnostic MRI
- 📊 Kaplan-Meier outcomes, whole cohort (TFS/FFS truncated at 22 yr)

  | Endpoint | 15 yr | 20 yr | 22 yr | 25 yr |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | PC-specific survival | 97% (CI 95-99%) | 95% (CI 93-98%) | n/a | 94% (CI 91-98%) |
  | Overall survival | 63% (CI 58-67%) | 46% (CI 41-51%) | n/a | 32% (CI 26-38%) |
  | Treatment-free survival | 48% (CI 43-54%) | 43% (CI 37-50%) | 38% (CI 31-46%) | n/a |
  | Failure-free survival | 81% (CI 77-85%) | 74% (CI 68-81%) | 68% (CI 60-78%) | n/a |

- 📊 Outcomes by risk group (TFS/FFS at 19 yr, PCSS/OS at 24 yr)

  | Endpoint | Very low (n=251) | Low (n=129) | Intermediate (n=108) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | TFS 19 yr | 55% (CI 48-63) | 35% (CI 26-47) | 30% (CI 18-48) |
  | FFS 19 yr | 85% | 74% | 55% |
  | PCSS 24 yr | 99% (CI 97-100) | 92% (CI 83-100) | 85% (CI 75-95) |
  | OS 24 yr | 38% (CI 30-48) | 34% (CI 26-45) | 22% (CI 12-37) |

- 💊 Exits from AS: 232 men discontinued
  - Radical prostatectomy: 141
  - Radiotherapy: 44
  - Hormonal treatment: 47
- 📊 Failure in 81 men, counted by the most severe event
  - PC death: 14
  - Metastatic, alive: 5
  - Started hormonal treatment: 44
  - PSA relapse after RP/RT, no HT: 18
- 📐 Multivariable Cox model for failure (baseline factors)
  - Gleason score (6 vs 7): HR 3.12 (1.59-6.13), p<0.001
  - PSA density per doubling: HR 1.78 (1.22-2.59), p=0.003
  - T stage (T1c vs T2a-c): HR 1.89 (1.08-3.30), p=0.025
  - PSA per doubling: HR 1.37 (0.84-2.25), p=0.2
  - Age: HR 1.03 (0.97-1.09), p=0.4
- 📊 Nearly half of all failures occurred >10 yr after diagnosis
- ⚠️ The broad failure definition includes untreated low-PSA relapse after RT (nadir +2) or RP (≥0.2 ng/ml)
- 🔗 PC mortality in the same range as ProtecT (3.4%) and Toronto (5.7% at 15 yr); lower than PIVOT (11.4%)
- 🔗 15-yr TFS 48% is in line with Toronto (55%, 15 yr) and PASS (49%, 10 yr)

### POSEIDON

> IPD meta-analysis of 6 RCTs: adding HT to PORT did not improve OS (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.76–1.01, p=0.06); benefit confined to pre-PORT PSA >0.5 ng/mL.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> MFS improved (HR 0.79) but never cleared the ICECaP 0.81 surrogate threshold at any pre-PORT PSA, so the bPFS/MFS gains behind GETUG-AFU 16 and RTOG 0534 were not expected to translate to OS. Duration showed no interaction (p=0.17); long-term HT OS benefit on splines appears only at PSA ≥1.6 ng/mL, above most modern early salvage.

- 🔍 IPD meta-analysis (MARCAP), 6 randomised phase 3 trials, 6057 pts, median f/u 9.0y
- 🔍 Trials: GETUG-AFU 16, NRG/RTOG 0534, RADICALS-HD (two-way + three-way), RTOG 9601
- 💊 Short-term HT 4–6 mo (mostly GnRH agonist) vs long-term 24 mo; RTOG 9601 used high-dose bicalutamide
- 📊 1° EP OS: 10y 83.6% PORT alone vs 84.3% PORT+HT; ASCO Post quotes absolute benefit 0.7%
- 📊 HT effect by randomised comparison (HR vs control)

  | Comparison | N | OS HR (95% CI) | MFS HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Any HT + PORT vs PORT | 5026 | 0.87 (0.76–1.01), p=0.06 | 0.79 (0.70–0.89), p<0.001 |
  | Short-term HT (4–6 mo) | 3938 | 0.93 (0.77–1.11) | 0.82 (0.71–0.95) |
  | Long-term HT (24 mo) | 1088 | 0.79 (0.63–1.00), p=0.049 | 0.74 (0.60–0.91), p=0.004 |
  | Short → long prolongation | 1523 | 0.89 (0.68–1.16), p=0.37 | 0.76 (0.61–0.95), p=0.02 |

- 📊 OS effect of adding HT by pre-PORT PSA

  | Pre-PORT PSA (ng/mL) | OS HR (95% CI) | 10y OS PORT vs PORT+HT | 10y NNT (death) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ≤0.20 | 1.14 (0.83–1.57) | 89.9% vs 87.4% | negative |
  | 0.21–0.50 | 0.94 (0.74–1.19) | 84.9% vs 84.5% | negative |
  | 0.51–1.00 | 0.72 (0.54–0.96) | 76.4% vs 80.9% | 22 |
  | >1.00 | 0.69 (0.48–0.98) | 72.4% vs 80.5% | 12 |

- 📐 Interaction PSA >0.5 vs ≤0.5: p=0.02; four-strata interaction p=0.08
- 📐 HT duration × effect interaction p=0.17: no evidence long-term beats short-term for OS
- 📊 Splines: long-term HT OS CI upper bound below 1.0 only at PSA ≥1.6 ng/mL
- 📐 MFS gain misses ICECaP surrogate threshold (upper CI <0.81) at every pre-PORT PSA for any HT
- ⚠️ Long-term HT trials had higher pre-PORT PSA (median 0.5 vs 0.3) and more adverse pathology
- ⚠️ No multiplicity correction; PSA interaction p varies by model (continuous: one-stage 0.19, two-stage 0.04)
- ⚠️ ASCO Post quotes NNT 125 (PSA ≤0.2) and 25 (PSA >1.0); Lancet paper reports negative NNT and 12
- ⚠️ Pre-PSMA PET, pre-Decipher enrolment; discussant: applies only to PET-negative BCR

## 💧 Bladder

### Dose-escalated RT for MIBC (retrospective)

> 2y invasive local recurrence 5.5% vs 27.5% with SIB dose escalation in MIBC TMT (adjusted SHR 0.20, p=0.035); no OS, MFS or G2+ toxicity difference.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Pattern of failure is the RT read: standard-dose invasive failures sat at the original tumour site (14 within vs 2 outside), and the escalated cohort had 1 invasive failure inside the boost. A 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx SIB to the primary on daily CBCT carried no G2+ GU/GI cost, which supports testing primary-tumour escalation in TMT.

- 🔍 Multicentre retrospective, 3 centres, March 2015 to May 2025; N=107 consecutive curative-intent MIBC
- 🔍 39 dose-escalated vs 68 standard dose; median f/u 23 mo (range 3-104)
- 🔍 RT technique
  - Standard: 55Gy/20fx or 64Gy/32fx whole bladder
  - Escalation: SIB up to 60Gy/20fx or 70Gy/32fx to primary lesion
  - Hypofractionation 97% escalated vs 87% standard
  - Empty-bladder CT sim; daily CBCT; MRI/PET fusion in select pts
- 📊 Outcomes, dose escalation vs standard

  | Endpoint | Escalated (n=39) | Standard (n=68) | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2y invasive local recurrence | 5.5% | 27.5% | SHR 0.20 (0.05-0.89), p=0.035 (MVA, T stage) |
  | 2y non-invasive recurrence | 6.7% | 9.9% | SHR 0.88 (0.23-3.33); MVA p=0.79 |
  | 2y metastasis | 21.1% | 32.3% | MVA p=0.75 |
  | 2y OS | 71.1% | 64.4% | p=0.5 |
  | G2+ GU toxicity | 17.9% | 22.1% | p=0.8 |
  | G2+ GI toxicity | 5.1% | 7.4% | p=0.9 |

- 📊 Pattern of recurrence by site (n events)

  | Cohort / site | Invasive | Non-invasive |
  |---|---|---|
  | Escalated, within boost field | 1 | 0 |
  | Escalated, outside boost field | 1 | 3 |
  | Standard, within original tumour site | 14 | 2 |
  | Standard, outside original tumour site | 2 | 5 |

- 📊 Salvage cystectomy for invasive recurrence: none escalated vs 2 (2.9%) standard
- 📊 G3 toxicity only in 2 standard-dose pts (2.9%); no early RT cessation
- 📐 OS predictors on MVA (dose not among them)
  - No chemo: HR 2.83 [1.42, 5.63], p<0.01
  - No hydronephrosis: HR 0.34 [0.16, 0.71], p<0.01
- ⚠️ Selection: escalated cohort 100% unifocal vs 65% standard
- ⚠️ Hydronephrosis 10% escalated vs 28% standard
- ⚠️ Only 2 invasive events in escalated arm; model adjusted for T stage alone
- ⚠️ 3 standard-arm recurrences imaging-only, presumed invasive; all escalated recurrences cystoscopy-confirmed
- 🔗 RAIDER tested tumour-focused adaptive escalation prospectively; that design is what this signal needs

## 🫁 Thoracic / Lung

### Proactive immune cell sparing SBRT (NCT04273893) · [[NCT04273893]]

> Immune-sparing lung SBRT planning cut ALC loss: overall improvement 13.4% (95% CI 2.8-24.0), G3 lymphopenia 0% vs 15.4%; survival trend unpowered.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dosimetric lever is low-dose spill, not the target: V5 to thoracic spine fell 87% and LN stations 58% with lung V5 down only 8%, all within RTOG 0813/0915 criteria. Benefit concentrated in central tumors (29.5% ALC gain) and was absent for peripheral PTV <20cc, so arc and beam-angle choices for central SBRT are where this plan change applies.

- 🔍 Randomized phase II, 1:1, stratified by tumor location (central vs peripheral), assignments not masked
- 🔍 55 enrolled and randomized, 4 withdrew/ineligible → 25 optimized vs 26 standard analyzed
- 🔍 Early-stage NSCLC, inoperable or declining surgery; recurrent NSCLC eligible; no systemic therapy within 1 yr before or 6 mo after SBRT
- 💊 SBRT 45-60 Gy/5 fx via IMRT/VMAT, 6X-FFF; both arms met RTOG 0813/0915 dosimetric criteria
- 🔍 Optimized arm added heart, great vessels, thoracic spine and lymph-node-stations as immune OARs
- 📊 Model-estimated % ALC change from baseline (primary endpoint)

  | Timepoint | Optimized | Standard | Between-group diff |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | End of SBRT | -16% | -31% | 15.1% (3.7-26.5), p=0.01 |
  | 4 weeks | -22% | -34% | 12.3% (0.2-24.5), p=0.05 |
  | 6 months | -16% | -26% | 10.4% (-4.7-25.5), p=0.17 |

- 📊 Central tumors (n=15): overall ALC improvement 29.5% (95% CI 10.1-48.9), p=0.004
- 📊 Peripheral PTV >20cc: 13.5%, p=0.124; peripheral PTV <20cc showed no difference
- 📊 G3 lymphopenia 15.4% standard vs 0% optimized (risk difference -15.4%, p=0.04)
- 📐 Mean V5 reduction, optimized vs standard plans
  - Thoracic spine: 87%
  - Vena cava: 58%
  - Lymph-node-stations: 58%
  - Aorta: 48%
  - Heart: 43%
  - Total lung minus PTV: 8%
- 📊 2-yr survival, unplanned exploratory analysis

  | Population · endpoint | Optimized | Standard | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All pts · OS | 82.1% | 65.1% | 0.23 |
  | All pts · EFS | 62.3% | 56.7% | 0.27 |
  | Tx-naive · OS | 93.4% | 69.4% | 0.14 |
  | Tx-naive · EFS | 75.0% | 59.8% | 0.10 |

- 📊 LN-station V5 <35.4cc vs ≥35.4cc: 2-yr OS 91.8% vs 57.5%, p=0.02 (dosimetric stratification, not randomized)
- 💊 G3 AEs 2 optimized vs 4 standard; G2 24% vs 35%; no G2+ pneumonitis in either arm
- ⚠️ Arm imbalance: central 36.0% vs 23.1%, Tx-naive 64.0% vs 88.5%
- ⚠️ Tx-naive 2-yr OS given as 93.4% in Results, 94.1% in Discussion; internal inconsistency
- ⚠️ medRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 16111586]] **First Measurement: Proactive Immune Cell Sparing in Radiation Therapy** — Wijesooriya, Krishni; Nguyen, Cam; Conaway, Mark R et al.. *medRxiv* (2025-01). [doi:10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.05.25320011)
  > Abstract Purpose Radiation Therapy (RT) can modulate the immune system and generate anti-tumor T cells. However, this anti-tumor-activity is countered by radiation-induced immunosuppression (RIIS). Clinical advantages of proactively sparing RT dose to immune rich organs have not previously been evaluated. Methods We conducted a phase II randomized trial from 2020 to 2023, enrolling 51 early-stage lung cancer patients treated with SBRT, to evaluate the effect of dose reduction to immune rich organs on RIIS. Two groups were: RIIS-optimized-treatment (lowering the dose to blood, bone-marrow and lymph-node-stations) and standard-treatment. All treatments followed national protocol guidelines. Peripheral blood was collected at baseline, immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-treatment. Results ALC changes from baseline immediately, 4-weeks and 6-months post-SBRT are: optimized-arm: -16%, -22%, -16%, standard-arm: -31%, -34%, -26%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 13.4 (5.3) % (95% CI, 2.8 to 24.0; p = 0.01). Central tumors had the largest improvement in ALC from baseline: optimized-arm: - 8%, -18%, -14%, standard-arm: -39%, -43%, -47%, leading to an overall-all-time-point improvement in ALC reduction in the optimized-arm compared to the standard-arm of 29.5 (9.6) % (95% CI, 10.1 to 48.9; p = 0.004). Grade 3 lymphopenia occurred in 15.4% of standard arm patients but was absent in the optimized arm. Additionally, 2.8 times more patients in the optimized arm experienced an ALC increase post-SBRT. Dose to organs such as the heart, great vessels, thoracic spine, and lymph nodes significantly correlated with RIIS. A trend towards increased Event-Free-Survival (two-year: 75.0% (SE = 10.8%) versus 59.8% (SE = 11.2%), p =0·10) and Overall Survival (two-year: 93.4% (SE=6.1%) versus 69.4% (SE=10.5%), p =0·14) was observed with optimized-planning compared to standard-planning in treatment naïve patients. Conclusion Reducing RT dose to immune rich organs significantly reduces RIIS compared to standard-of-care. This has implications in enhancing immune system mediated anti-tumor-activity. (Funded by National Cancer Institute and others. ClinicalTrials.gov number, [[NCT04273893]] )

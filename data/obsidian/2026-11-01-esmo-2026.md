# 2026-11-01 — ESMO Congress 2026

**MASTERPLAN: adding SBRT 40Gy/5fx to mFOLFIRINOX-based CT cut 12mth LRR 20.2% vs 52.0% (p=0.022) in high-risk operable, BRPC or LAPC pancreas.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Upper GI led: SBRT added to induction CT lowered LRR in randomised phase II MASTERPLAN, while IO added to dCRT failed twice in esophageal/GEJ cancer (KEYNOTE-975 EFS HR 0.97 in CPS ≥10; ARION 12-mo cPFS 48.6% vs 48.9%). Bone-met pain: PALEM embolization 52.5% 72-h response in RT-ineligible pts; FURTHER MR-HIFU missed primary at 69/129 accrual.

## 🤝 Supportive / QoL

Non-EBRT options for painful bone mets: embolization in RT-ineligible pts, and an underpowered MR-HIFU vs EBRT RCT.

### PALEM

> Arterial embolization gave 72-h pain relief in 52.5% (62/118; 95% CI 43.1-61.8%) of RT-ineligible painful bone mets, with no primary safety events.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The response definition comes from the palliative RT consensus criteria, so the 52.5% reads on an RT-style scale, but at 72 h, earlier than RT response is usually assessed. The population is RT-ineligible or ≥8 weeks post-RT, so this is an option when re-irradiation is off the table, not a substitute for first-line palliative RT.

- 📊 Co-primary endpoints vs prespecified thresholds

  | Endpoint | Threshold | Result | 95% CI |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Immediate efficacy (72 h) | >26% | 52.5% (62/118) | 43.1-61.8% |
  | Embolization-related AE through day 14 | <15% | No events (n=119) | 0.0-3.1% |

- 🔍 Multicenter single-arm prospective confirmatory study, N=120 enrolled, J-SUPPORT policy
- 🔍 Eligibility
  - On analgesics, pain score ≥5
  - RT-ineligible, or ≥8 weeks after prior RT
  - Most painful lesion embolized with microspheres, no anticancer drug
- 🔍 Response defined by international consensus criteria for palliative RT trials (BPI-SF item 3 + morphine-equivalent dose)
- 📐 Efficacy test one-sided α=0.025, power 0.9, assumed 40% response
- 🔍 Safety event = any CTCAE G≥4 or G≥3 CNS necrosis through day 14
- ⚠️ Only 72-h response reported in source; durability of relief unknown
- ⚠️ Response includes stable pain with lower opioid dose; component breakdown not reported in source
- ⚠️ Lesion sites, histologies and vascularity not reported in source

- 📄 **A new fast-acting treatment option for painful bone metastases: A confirmatory trial of palliative arterial embolization assessing immediate efficacy and safety (PALEM Trial)** — Yasunori Arai. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 3087RO · Rapid oral: Supportive and palliative care · 2026-10-25 · presented by Yasunori Arai (Kashiwa, Japan)
  > Background: Painful bone metastases impair quality of life in patients with cancer, yet few options beyond analgesics provide relief within days. Palliative arterial embolization may enable rapid pain control by embolizing tumor-feeding vessels. We prospectively evaluated its immediate efficacy and safety.
  > Methods: The PALEM trial was a multicenter, single-arm, prospective confirmatory hypothesis-assessment study conducted under national palliative trial standards (J-SUPPORT policy). This protocol has been published. Eligible patients [on analgesics; Pain score ≥5; radiotherapy (RT)-ineligible or ≥8 weeks after prior RT] underwent embolization for the most painful lesion using microsphere, without anticancer drugs. The co-primary endpoints were immediate efficacy and safety. Immediate efficacy was defined by international consensus criteria for palliative RT trials, incorporating pain scores and daily morphine-equivalent doses. Pain score (Brief pain Inventory - short form item 3) was assessed at 72 h. Efficasy was defined as (a) reduced pain score with reduced opioid dose, (b) reduced pain score with unchanged opioid dose, or (c) unchanged pain score with reduced opioid dose. Immediate efficacy was evaluated by testing whether the efficasy rate exceeded 26% (one-sided α=0.025, 1−β=0.9), assuming a 40% response. Safety was defined as the incidence, through day 14, of embolization-related adverse events (any Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Event grade ≥4 event or grade ≥3 central nervous system necrosis). We tested whether the adverse event rate was <15% (one-sided α=0.025, 1−β=0.9). Overall success required both immediate efficacy and safety criteria be met.
  > Results: A total of 120 patients were enrolled. Immediate efficacy was achieved for 62 of 118 evaluable patients, yielding a 52.5% pain-relief rate (95% CI, 43.1–61.8%). The safety analysis set included 119 patients; no primary safety events occurred (95% CI, 0.0–3.1%). The trial met both primary endpoints.
  > Conclusions: The PALEM trial confirmed palliative arterial embolization as a valid treatment for painful bone metastases, providing immediate pain relief with acceptable safety.
  > Clinical trial identification: UMIN-CTR ID: UMIN000040794 release date: 2020/07/01.

### FURTHER · [[NCT04307914]]

> 2-wk pain response 50% vs 38% (p = 0.41) for MR-HIFU ± EBRT vs EBRT alone; primary not met, trial accrued 69 of 129 planned.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT signal sits in the combination arm: pain response rose 26% → 81% from wk 1 to wk 6 with EBRT then MR-HIFU, vs 35% → 47% with EBRT alone. EBRT dose was not reported, so transfer to a single-fraction practice can't be checked. The decision in play is adding MR-HIFU to EBRT, not replacing EBRT.

- 🔍 Multicenter 1:1:1 RCT, six European hospitals, enrolled 2020-2025
- 🔍 Arms: MR-HIFU vs EBRT vs EBRT followed by MR-HIFU within one week
- 🔍 Painful (NRS ≥ 4) non-spinal bone mets, eligible for both EBRT and MR-HIFU
- 🔍 Excluded: life expectancy <3 months, (impending) fracture, curative intent
- 📊 2-wk pain response (BPI pain score + opioid use): MR-HIFU ± EBRT 50% vs EBRT 38%, p = 0.41
- 📊 Secondary pain outcomes by arm

  | Arm (n) | Pain score drop, first 2 wk | Pain response, wk 1 → wk 6 |
  |---|---|---|
  | EBRT (n=24) | 2.3 points | 35% → 47% |
  | EBRT + MR-HIFU (n=23) | 3.7 points | 26% → 81% |
  | MR-HIFU (n=22) | 3.6 points | 47% → 50% |

- ⚠️ Accrual 69 of 129 target despite 2-yr extension (COVID-19, low MR-HIFU eligibility)
- ⚠️ Primary pools MR-HIFU and combination arms vs EBRT; no per-arm 2-wk response in source
- ⚠️ 2-wk primary favors the fast-onset modality: authors cite 4-6 wk for EBRT effect
- ⚠️ EBRT dose and fractionation not reported in source
- ⚠️ EBRT 6-wk response 47%, below the 70% EBRT benchmark the authors cite
- 🔍 Bayesian reanalysis to offset low power promised at ESMO; not in source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **MRI-guided high-intensity focused ultrasound versus external beam radiotherapy for painful bone metastases: The FURTHER RCT** — Manon Braat. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 3084O · Proffered paper: Supportive and palliative care · 2026-10-26 · presented by Manon Braat (Utrecht, Netherlands) · [[NCT04307914]]
  > Background: External beam radiotherapy (EBRT) is the standard of treatment for patients with painful bone metastases. Its effect takes 4-6 weeks to occur and 70% of patients achieve pain response. MRI-guided High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (MR-HIFU) is proposed as alternative or addition to EBRT. The multicenter FURTHER RCT investigated whether MR-HIFU, with or without EBRT, induces earlier and better pain response in patients with painful bone metastases.
  > Methods: Patients were enrolled between 2020-2025 in six European hospitals. Patients were adults with good/reasonable performance, painful (NRS ≥ 4) non-spinal bone metastases, eligible for EBRT and MR-HIFU. Patients with life expectancy <3 months, (impending) fracture, or curative treatment intent were excluded. Patients were 1:1:1 randomized to MR-HIFU, EBRT, or the combination (EBRT followed by MR-Hifu within one week). Primary endpoint was pain response two weeks following start of treatment based on pain score and opioid use as collected by the Brief Pain Inventory. Secondary endpoints included pain scores and pain response over the first three months.
  > Results: Despite 2 year project extention, the target sample size of 129 patients was not reached due COVID-19 and lower-than-expected MR-HIFU eligibility. 69 patients were randomized to MR-HIFU (n=22), combination treatment (n=23) and EBRT (n=24). Two weeks after the start of treatment, pain response was higher for MR-HIFU (with or without EBRT) than for EBRT; the difference was not statistically significant (50% vs. 38%, p = 0.41). In the first 2 weeks following start of treatment, pain scores dropped 2.3 points in the EBRT arm, 3.7 points in the combination arm, and 3.6 points in the MR- HIFU arm. Between 1-6 weeks following start of treatment, pain response increased from 35% to 47% for EBRT, from 26% to 81% for the combination treatment and from 47% to 50% for MR- HIFU.
  > Conclusions: MR- HIFU is effective for palliation of painful bone metastases and can be considered in patients for whom EBRT is no (longer an) option. Combining EBRT with MR- HIFU may lead to a higher pain response than current standard treatment. Bayesian analyses to mitigate the study's low power and improve its interpretability will be presented at ESMO.
  > Clinical trial identification: [[NCT04307914]].

## 🍽️ GI Upper

Two negative IO + dCRT esophageal readouts sit beside a positive local-control signal for adding SBRT in pancreas.

### ARION PRODIGE 67 · [[NCT03777813]]

> Adding durvalumab to definitive CRT did not improve 12-mo cPFS (48.6% vs 48.9%, HR 0.91) in locally advanced esophageal cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT backbone is the transferable part: IMRT 50 Gy/25 fx with FOLFOX, the European dCRT standard, so the null applies directly to Western practice. No locoregional failure data are reported, so whether durvalumab changed in-field control is unknown; the OS rate gap (75.1% vs 67.9%) carries HR 0.99.

- 📊 Efficacy at 24-mo follow-up, CRT + durvalumab vs CRT alone

  | Endpoint | CRT + durvalumab (12-mo) | CRT alone (12-mo) | Adj HR (95% CI) | 1-sided p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | cPFS | 48.6% [34.6; 61.3] | 48.9% [34.7; 61.7] | 0.91 [0.55; 1.49] | 0.351 |
  | PFS | 50.1% [36.2; 62.5] | 48.1% [33.7; 61.2] | 0.92 [0.56; 1.50] | 0.369 |
  | OS | 75.1% [61.6; 84.4] | 67.9% [53.6; 78.7] | 0.99 [0.59; 1.68] | 0.489 |

- 🔍 Open-label randomized phase II, N=112 (58 vs 54), 13 French sites
- ☢️ RT backbone fixed both arms: IMRT 50 Gy/25 fx over 5 wk + 6 cycles FOLFOX
- 💊 Durvalumab 1500 mg monthly × 12 added to CRT in the experimental arm
- 🔍 Primary endpoint centrally reviewed PFS; 74 events to detect HR 0.55, 90% power, 10% one-sided alpha
- 🔍 Median age 67, 68% male, 77% SCC, 70% node-positive
- ⚠️ Powered only for HR 0.55; a modest benefit cannot be excluded
- ⚠️ Treatment-related AEs higher with durvalumab; G≥3 AE rates similar
- ⚠️ No locoregional control or pattern-of-failure data reported in source
- 🔗 Direction matches KUNLUN (durvalumab + dCRT in ESCC), also reported negative

### AGITG MASTERPLAN

> 12mth LRR 20.2% vs 52.0% (p=0.022) adding SBRT 40Gy/5fx to 3mths mFOLFIRINOX-based CT in high-risk operable, BRPC or LAPC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The local control benefit held although only 39 (80%) of the SBRT arm actually commenced SBRT, delivered as 40Gy/5fx with fiducials and real-time QA. The benefit is clearest in POPC (12mth LRR 19.7% vs 52.9%, p=0.031). That puts SBRT inside induction CT back in play for BRPC.

- 🔍 Randomised phase II, 2:1 CT+SBRT (I) vs CT alone (C), 14 sites, 75 enrolled, 1 ineligible
- 🔍 48 POPC (high-risk operable + BRPC) and 26 IPC (LAPC / medically inoperable); central radiology review
- 💊 CT clinician choice: mFOLFIRINOX 90-92%, gem/nab-paclitaxel in the remainder; 3mths before surgical evaluation
- 🔍 SBRT 40Gy in 5 fractions, fiducial marker guidance, real-time RT quality assurance
- 📊 Outcomes by arm (I n=49, C n=25)

  | Endpoint | CT+SBRT (I) | CT alone (C) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 12mth LRR | 20.2% (95% CI 9.9-33.2%) | 52.0% (95% CI 30.3-69.9%) | p=0.022 |

### KEYNOTE-975 · [[NCT04210115]]

> EFS HR 0.97 (0.77-1.23), 15.2 vs 14.7 mo in CPS ≥10: pembrolizumab added to dCRT failed its primary endpoint in unresectable esophageal/GEJ cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT read: pembrolizumab added nothing to dCRT across all three backbones (FP with 50 or 60 Gy, FOLFOX with 50 Gy), while fatal AEs ran 13% vs 10%. Whether IO changed in-field control is unknown, since locoregional and pattern-of-failure data are not reported in source. The dCRT backbone stays unchanged for unresectable disease.

- 🔍 Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, international; N=703 assigned 1:1 (349 pembro vs 354 placebo)
- 🔍 Untreated locally advanced ESCC, EAC or GEJ; ECOG 0-1; ineligible for curative surgery, suitable for dCRT
- 📊 Efficacy in PD-L1 CPS ≥10, second interim analysis (median f/u 47.1 mo)

  | Endpoint | Median, pembro vs placebo | HR (95% CI) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | EFS (investigator-assessed) | 15.2 vs 14.7 mo | 0.97 (0.77-1.23) | 0.3994 |
  | OS | 32.2 vs 35.0 mo | 1.00 (0.77-1.30) | 0.5003 (nominal) |

- 📐 EFS CPS ≥10 failure stopped hierarchical testing: CPS ≥1, overall population EFS and OS not formally tested
- 🔍 Study stopped after this analysis
- 📊 Safety, pembro + dCRT vs placebo + dCRT

  | AE | Pembro + dCRT | Placebo + dCRT |
  |---|---|---|
  | Grade ≥3 AEs | 100% | 100% |
  | Fatal AEs | 44 (13%) | 37 (10%) |
  | Discontinuation for AEs | 34% | 27% |

- ⚠️ RT dose not standardized: 50 or 60 Gy with FP, 50 Gy with FOLFOX, at investigator choice
- ⚠️ EFS investigator-assessed; no histology (ESCC vs EAC) breakdown in source
- ⚠️ No locoregional control or pattern-of-failure data in source

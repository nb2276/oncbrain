# 2026-11-01 — ESMO Congress 2026

**AGITG MASTERPLAN: SBRT 40Gy/5fx added to induction CT cut 12mth LRR to 20.2% vs 52.0% (p=0.022), with mOS 22.0 vs 14.0mo, in a randomised phase II.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Upper-GI carried the day with opposite signals. Adding SBRT to induction CT improved LRR and OS in MASTERPLAN, while adding IO to dCRT stayed null in locally advanced esophageal/GEJ cancer (KEYNOTE-975 EFS HR 0.97 in CPS ≥10; ARION cPFS HR 0.91). In bone mets, PALEM embolization met both co-primaries, and FURTHER (MR-HIFU) missed its primary on 69 of 129 pts.

## 🤝 Supportive / QoL

Two options for painful bone mets: embolization when RT is not an option or has already been given (PALEM), and MR-HIFU ± EBRT (FURTHER, underpowered).

### FURTHER · [[NCT04307914]]

> 2-week pain response 50% vs 38% for MR-HIFU ± EBRT vs EBRT alone (p=0.41) in painful non-spinal bone mets; underpowered, N=69 of 129 target.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant signal sits in the secondary data: EBRT followed by MR-HIFU reached 81% pain response by wk 6 vs 47% for EBRT alone, against a background expectation of 70%. EBRT dose is not reported in source, so whether this adds to single-fraction practice cannot be judged; it frames HIFU as an adjunct question, not a replacement.

- 📊 Primary endpoint not met: 2-wk pain response 50% (MR-HIFU ± EBRT pooled) vs 38% EBRT, p = 0.41
- 🔍 Multicenter RCT, 6 European hospitals, 1:1:1 randomization, enrolled 2020-2025
- 🔍 Arms: MR-HIFU (n=22), EBRT followed by MR-HIFU within 1 wk (n=23), EBRT (n=24)
- ⚠️ Accrual 69 vs target 129 despite 2-yr extension (COVID-19, low MR-HIFU eligibility)
- 📊 Secondary pain outcomes by arm

  | Endpoint | MR-HIFU | EBRT + MR-HIFU | EBRT |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Pain score drop, first 2 wk | 3.6 points | 3.7 points | 2.3 points |
  | Pain response, wk 1 → wk 6 | 47% → 50% | 26% → 81% | 35% → 47% |

- ⚠️ Primary comparison pools two HIFU arms vs EBRT, diluting the combination signal
- ⚠️ EBRT dose/fractionation not reported in source
- ⚠️ 81% combination response is secondary and unpowered; Bayesian reanalysis pending
- 🔍 Excluded: spinal mets, life expectancy <3 mo, (impending) fracture, curative intent

**Sources:**
- 📄 **MRI-guided high-intensity focused ultrasound versus external beam radiotherapy for painful bone metastases: The FURTHER RCT** — Manon Braat. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 3084O · Proffered paper: Supportive and palliative care · 2026-10-26 · presented by Manon Braat (Utrecht, Netherlands) · [[NCT04307914]]
  > Background: External beam radiotherapy (EBRT) is the standard of treatment for patients with painful bone metastases. Its effect takes 4-6 weeks to occur and 70% of patients achieve pain response. MRI-guided High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (MR-HIFU) is proposed as alternative or addition to EBRT. The multicenter FURTHER RCT investigated whether MR-HIFU, with or without EBRT, induces earlier and better pain response in patients with painful bone metastases.
  > Methods: Patients were enrolled between 2020-2025 in six European hospitals. Patients were adults with good/reasonable performance, painful (NRS ≥ 4) non-spinal bone metastases, eligible for EBRT and MR-HIFU. Patients with life expectancy <3 months, (impending) fracture, or curative treatment intent were excluded. Patients were 1:1:1 randomized to MR-HIFU, EBRT, or the combination (EBRT followed by MR-Hifu within one week). Primary endpoint was pain response two weeks following start of treatment based on pain score and opioid use as collected by the Brief Pain Inventory. Secondary endpoints included pain scores and pain response over the first three months.
  > Results: Despite 2 year project extention, the target sample size of 129 patients was not reached due COVID-19 and lower-than-expected MR-HIFU eligibility. 69 patients were randomized to MR-HIFU (n=22), combination treatment (n=23) and EBRT (n=24). Two weeks after the start of treatment, pain response was higher for MR-HIFU (with or without EBRT) than for EBRT; the difference was not statistically significant (50% vs. 38%, p = 0.41). In the first 2 weeks following start of treatment, pain scores dropped 2.3 points in the EBRT arm, 3.7 points in the combination arm, and 3.6 points in the MR- HIFU arm. Between 1-6 weeks following start of treatment, pain response increased from 35% to 47% for EBRT, from 26% to 81% for the combination treatment and from 47% to 50% for MR- HIFU.
  > Conclusions: MR- HIFU is effective for palliation of painful bone metastases and can be considered in patients for whom EBRT is no (longer an) option. Combining EBRT with MR- HIFU may lead to a higher pain response than current standard treatment. Bayesian analyses to mitigate the study's low power and improve its interpretability will be presented at ESMO.
  > Clinical trial identification: [[NCT04307914]].

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does EBRT plus MR-HIFU beat EBRT alone in an adequately powered trial?
> - Durability of MR-HIFU pain response beyond three months
> - Does Bayesian reanalysis change the primary read?

### PALEM

> Arterial embolization gave immediate pain relief in 52.5% (95% CI 43.1-61.8%) of RT-ineligible or post-RT painful bone mets at 72 h; no primary safety events.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Eligibility excluded RT candidates (RT-ineligible or ≥8 weeks post-RT), and response used the palliative RT consensus criteria read at 72 h. That makes embolization an option for the already-irradiated or unirradiable painful lesion, not a competitor to first-course palliative RT, which it was never compared against.

- 🔍 Multicenter, single-arm, prospective confirmatory hypothesis-testing study under J-SUPPORT palliative trial standards
- 🔍 N=120 enrolled; 118 evaluable for efficacy, 119 in safety set
- 🔍 Eligibility
  - On analgesics with pain score ≥5
  - RT-ineligible, or ≥8 weeks after prior RT
  - Most painful lesion treated only
- 💊 Microsphere embolization of tumor-feeding vessels, no anticancer drug in the embolic
- 📊 Co-primary endpoints vs prespecified thresholds (both met)

  | Endpoint | Result | 95% CI | Threshold |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Immediate efficacy at 72 h | 62/118 (52.5%) | 43.1-61.8% | Lower bound >26% |
  | Embolization-related G≥4 AE or G≥3 CNS necrosis to day 14 | None of 119 | 0.0-3.1% | <15% |

- 📐 Efficacy: one-sided α=0.025, power 0.9, assumed 40% response
- 🔗 Response borrowed international consensus criteria for palliative RT trials (pain score + morphine-equivalent dose), BPI-SF item 3
- ⚠️ Efficacy counts any pain reduction; the abstract gives no minimum point drop
- ⚠️ Safety endpoint captures only G≥4 or G≥3 CNS necrosis; lower-grade AEs not reported in source
- ⚠️ Single 72 h read; durability, retreatment and re-intervention rates not reported

- 📄 **A new fast-acting treatment option for painful bone metastases: A confirmatory trial of palliative arterial embolization assessing immediate efficacy and safety (PALEM Trial)** — Yasunori Arai. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 3087RO · Rapid oral: Supportive and palliative care · 2026-10-25 · presented by Yasunori Arai (Kashiwa, Japan)
  > Background: Painful bone metastases impair quality of life in patients with cancer, yet few options beyond analgesics provide relief within days. Palliative arterial embolization may enable rapid pain control by embolizing tumor-feeding vessels. We prospectively evaluated its immediate efficacy and safety.
  > Methods: The PALEM trial was a multicenter, single-arm, prospective confirmatory hypothesis-assessment study conducted under national palliative trial standards (J-SUPPORT policy). This protocol has been published. Eligible patients [on analgesics; Pain score ≥5; radiotherapy (RT)-ineligible or ≥8 weeks after prior RT] underwent embolization for the most painful lesion using microsphere, without anticancer drugs. The co-primary endpoints were immediate efficacy and safety. Immediate efficacy was defined by international consensus criteria for palliative RT trials, incorporating pain scores and daily morphine-equivalent doses. Pain score (Brief pain Inventory - short form item 3) was assessed at 72 h. Efficasy was defined as (a) reduced pain score with reduced opioid dose, (b) reduced pain score with unchanged opioid dose, or (c) unchanged pain score with reduced opioid dose. Immediate efficacy was evaluated by testing whether the efficasy rate exceeded 26% (one-sided α=0.025, 1−β=0.9), assuming a 40% response. Safety was defined as the incidence, through day 14, of embolization-related adverse events (any Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Event grade ≥4 event or grade ≥3 central nervous system necrosis). We tested whether the adverse event rate was <15% (one-sided α=0.025, 1−β=0.9). Overall success required both immediate efficacy and safety criteria be met.
  > Results: A total of 120 patients were enrolled. Immediate efficacy was achieved for 62 of 118 evaluable patients, yielding a 52.5% pain-relief rate (95% CI, 43.1–61.8%). The safety analysis set included 119 patients; no primary safety events occurred (95% CI, 0.0–3.1%). The trial met both primary endpoints.
  > Conclusions: The PALEM trial confirmed palliative arterial embolization as a valid treatment for painful bone metastases, providing immediate pain relief with acceptable safety.
  > Clinical trial identification: UMIN-CTR ID: UMIN000040794 release date: 2020/07/01.

> [!question] Open questions
> - Durability of pain relief beyond 72 h
> - Embolization vs re-irradiation for post-RT painful bone mets
> - Embolization as bridge to or combined with palliative RT

## 🍽️ GI Upper

Two trials found no benefit from adding concurrent IO to dCRT in esophageal cancer (KEYNOTE-975 phase 3 stopped, ARION phase II), while local SBRT showed benefit in MASTERPLAN.

### ARION PRODIGE 67 · [[NCT03777813]]

> Durvalumab + dCRT did not improve 12-mo cPFS (48.6% vs 48.9%, HR 0.91) in locally advanced esophageal cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT backbone was a modern IMRT 50 Gy/25 fx with FOLFOX, and centrally reviewed PFS was identical at 12 mo (48.6% vs 48.9%), so the null is not explained by an outdated RT platform. No local control or in-field failure data were reported, leaving open whether durvalumab changes the locoregional failure pattern that dominates after dCRT.

- 🔍 Open-label comparative phase II, N=112 randomized (58 vs 54), 13 French sites
- 🔍 Stratified by tumor stage, histology, center
- 📐 Powered for HR 0.55 (74 cPFS events, 90% power, 10% one-sided alpha)
- 📊 Efficacy at 24-mo follow-up analysis, SoC + D vs SoC

  | Endpoint | Arm A SoC + D (12-mo rate) | Arm B SoC (12-mo rate) | Adjusted HR [95% CI] | 1-sided p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | cPFS (1° EP) | 48.6% [34.6; 61.3] | 48.9% [34.7; 61.7] | 0.91 [0.55; 1.49] | 0.351 |
  | PFS | 50.1% [36.2; 62.5] | 48.1% [33.7; 61.2] | 0.92 [0.56; 1.50] | 0.369 |
  | OS | 75.1% [61.6; 84.4] | 67.9% [53.6; 78.7] | 0.99 [0.59; 1.68] | 0.489 |

- 💊 Regimen
  - RT: IMRT 50 Gy/25 daily fx over 5 wk, both arms
  - Chemo: FOLFOX x6 cycles, both arms
  - Exp arm: durvalumab 1500 mg monthly x12 infusions
- 🔍 Median age 67, 68% male, 77% SCC, 70% node-positive
- ⚠️ TRAEs higher with durvalumab; G≥3 AE rates similar. No AE numbers in source
- ⚠️ Sized for HR 0.55: the 95% CI (0.55-1.49) cannot exclude a smaller benefit
- ⚠️ No local control or pattern-of-failure data reported in abstract
- 🔗 Same question as KUNLUN (durvalumab + dCRT, ESCC); contrasts with CheckMate 577 adjuvant nivolumab after trimodality

### AGITG MASTERPLAN

> 12mth LRR 20.2% vs 52.0% (p=0.022) adding SBRT 40Gy/5fx to induction CT; mOS 22.0 vs 14.0mo (p=0.006), G3+ toxicity similar.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Technique gates transfer: 40Gy/5fx with fiducial guidance and real-time RT QA, inside a 3mth induction CT window. Only 39 of 49 (80%) commenced SBRT, yet 12mth LRR still fell to 20.2% vs 52.0%. R0 (>1mm) 62% vs 40% (p=0.44) hints at a margin effect the trial was not powered to test.

- 🔍 Randomised phase II, 2:1, CT+SBRT vs CT alone, 14 sites, 75 enrolled (1 ineligible)
- 🔍 Mixed population: 48 potentially operable (high-risk operable + BRPC), 26 inoperable (LAPC / medically inoperable)
- 💊 CT clinician choice: mFOLFIRINOX 90% (I) vs 92% (C); gem/nab-paclitaxel the rest
- 🔍 SBRT 40Gy/5fx, fiducial guidance, real-time RT QA; central radiology review of staging
- 📊 Efficacy and toxicity, CT+SBRT (I, n=49) vs CT alone (C, n=25)

  | Endpoint | CT+SBRT | CT alone | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 12mth LRR, all | 20.2% (9.9-33.2%) | 52.0% (30.3-69.9%) | p=0.022 |
  | 12mth LRR, POPC | 19.7% (7.8-35.6) | 52.9% (25.9-74.1) | p=0.031 |
  | 12mth LRR, IPC | 21.4% (4.8-45.8) | 50.0% (11.4-80.2) | p=0.16 |
  | Median PFS | 11.9 mths | 6.3 mths | p=0.002 |
  | Median OS | 22.0 mths | 14.0 mths | p=0.006 |

- 📊 Surgical outcomes, POPC only (n=48)

  | Outcome | CT+SBRT (n=31) | CT alone (n=17) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Resection performed | 21 (68%) | 10 (59%) | 0.54 |
  | R0 resection (>1mm) | 13/21 (62%) | 4/10 (40%) | 0.44 |

- ⚠️ Only 39/49 (80%) of the SBRT arm commenced SBRT; analysis population not stated
- ⚠️ IPC LRR benefit not significant (p=0.16); only 26 IPC pts
- ⚠️ Sex imbalance: 72% male (C) vs 51% (I), p=0.08
- ⚠️ OS is secondary; control arm only n=25, follow-up duration not reported in source
- 🔗 Diverges from Alliance A021501, where adding SBRT to mFOLFIRINOX in BRPC did not improve outcomes

### KEYNOTE-975 · [[NCT04210115]]

> EFS 15.2 vs 14.7 mo, HR 0.97 (0.77–1.23) in PD-L1 CPS ≥10; adding pembro to dCRT failed its primary endpoint, and the trial was stopped.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT backbone was heterogeneous (50 or 60 Gy with FP, or 50 Gy with FOLFOX), and the source gives no dose-stratified or local-control readout. The trial therefore cannot say whether IO interacts with RT dose. It does settle the concurrent question for CPS ≥10, where fatal AEs ran 13% vs 10% with no EFS gain.

- 🔍 Phase 3, randomized 1:1, double-blind, placebo-controlled, international; N=703 (349 pembro vs 354 placebo)
- 🔍 Unresectable, previously untreated locally advanced ESCC, EAC or GEJ; ECOG 0-1; dCRT-eligible
- 💊 Regimen
  - dCRT: investigator-choice FP + 50 or 60 Gy, or FOLFOX + 50 Gy
  - Pembro 200 mg q3w x8 cycles → 400 mg q6w x5 cycles, vs matching placebo
- 📊 Dual primary endpoints in PD-L1 CPS ≥10 (2nd interim analysis, median f/u 47.1 mo)

  | Endpoint | Median, mo (as reported) | HR (95% CI) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | EFS (investigator) | 15.2 vs 14.7 | 0.97 (0.77–1.23) | 0.3994 |
  | OS | 32.2 vs 35.0 | 1.00 (0.77–1.30) | 0.5003 (nominal) |

- 📐 EFS in CPS ≥10 not met, so hierarchical testing stopped; no formal EFS readout in CPS ≥1 or ITT
- ⚠️ Safety: toxicity higher with pembro + dCRT

  | AE | Pembro + dCRT | Placebo + dCRT |
  |---|---|---|
  | Grade ≥3 AEs | 100% | 100% |
  | Fatal AEs | 44 (13%) | 37 (10%) |
  | Discontinuation for AEs | 34% | 27% |

- ⚠️ Null even in the CPS ≥10 enriched population, which is where an IO signal should have been easiest to find
- ⚠️ RT dose (50 vs 60 Gy) and histology subgroups not reported in source; local control and patterns of failure not reported
- 🔗 Concurrent-with-dCRT IO, unlike the consolidation (PACIFIC, NSCLC) or post-resection adjuvant (CheckMate 577) settings

# 2026-11-03 — ESMO Congress 2026

**DASL-HiCaP: darolutamide added to 96wk ADT + pelvic nodal RT gives 4y MFS-CI 95% vs 88%, HR 0.42 (0.27-0.66) at interim, very high-risk localised or post-RP prostate.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day: DASL-HiCaP adds a second ARSI-intensification signal on an RT backbone, though at interim with OS (HR 0.88) and PCSS immature. CNS was negative: trotabresib added to RT/TMZ in IDH-wt ndGBM gave mPFS 7.9 vs 8.2 mo, 12-mo PFS 22.2% vs 38.9%, with more dose modifications. A surgical BM series links post-RT progression to FOXP3+ enrichment.

## 🧠 CNS

Two RT-adjacent readouts: a randomized phase II that found no benefit from adding a BET inhibitor to RT/TMZ, and a resection series profiling brain metastases that progressed after cranial RT.

### Trotabresib + RT/TMZ in newly diagnosed IDH-wt GBM · [[NCT04324840]]

> mPFS 7.9 vs 8.2 mo with trotabresib added to RT/TMZ; 12-mo PFS 22.2% vs 38.9% (p 0.048), favoring SOC alone in ndGBM.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 12-mo PFS gap (22.2% vs 38.9%) runs against the add-on, alongside excess G3+ thrombocytopenia (37% vs 15.3%) and dose interruptions (OR 2.43). For RT planning, the open question is whether myelosuppression from a concurrent add-on erodes chemoRT delivery; RT completion was not reported in source.

- 🔍 Randomized phase II, 2:1, N=184 (124 trotabresib + SOC vs 60 SOC alone)
- 🔍 Newly diagnosed IDH wild-type GBM; SOC = surgery, RT, temozolomide
- 💊 Trotabresib: oral BET inhibitor, brain-penetrant; 30 mg/day 4 days on/24 off was the dose established in prior SOC combination
- 📊 PFS did not favour the experimental arm at median f/u 15 mo (136 PFS events)
- 📊 Efficacy, trotabresib + SOC (arm A) vs SOC (arm B)

  | Endpoint | Arm A (T + SOC) | Arm B (SOC) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | mPFS | 7.9 mo | 8.2 mo | n/a |
  | 12-mo PFS | 22.2% | 38.9% | 0.048 |
  | mOS | 16.9 mo | 16.7 mo | 0.898 |

- ⚠️ G3+ TRAEs, arm A vs arm B
  - Thrombocytopenia: 37% vs 15.3%
  - Neutropenia: 16.8% vs 8.5%
  - Lymphopenia: 8.4% vs 11.9%
- ⚠️ TRAE-driven dose modifications, arm A vs arm B

  | Event | Arm A | Arm B | OR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Dose reduction | 43.7% | 18.6% | 3.39 (1.64-7.02) | 0.001 |
  | Dose interruption | 49.6% | 28.8% | 2.43 (1.25-4.74) | 0.009 |

- ⚠️ Authors suggest the dose modifications may have compromised SOC delivery; RT-specific interruptions or dose received not reported in source
- ⚠️ PFS by local investigator RANO, no central review stated; MGMT status not reported in source
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation not reported in source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Randomized phase II trial investigating addition of trotabresib (CC-90010) to radiotherapy and temozolomide in patients with newly diagnosed IDH wild-type glioblastoma** — Angelo Dipasquale. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 692O · Proffered paper: CNS tumours · 2026-10-25 · presented by Angelo Dipasquale (Rozzano, Italy) · [[NCT04324840]]
  > Background: Patients with IDH wild-type glioblastoma (GBM) invariably experience recurrence despite standard-of-care (SOC) including surgery, radiotherapy, and temozolomide. Bromodomain and extra-terminal proteins (BET) are epigenetic regulators of cell cycle progression overexpressed in GBM. Trotabresib (T) is an oral BET inhibitor that showed brain penetration and tolerable safety profile at a dose of 30 mg/day 4 days on/24 days off in combination with SOC.
  > Methods: Patients with newly diagnosed IDH wild-type GBM (ndGBM) were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive T + SOC (arm A) or SOC alone (arm B). The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from randomization to RANO disease progression by local investigators or death. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from randomization to death. Safety profile was assessed with CTCAE V5 and dose reductions/interruptions between arms were compared using odds ratios (ORs).
  > Results: A total of 184 patients were randomized to arm A (n=124) or arm B (n=60). At the time of database lock, 136 patients experienced a PFS event. At a median follow-up of 15 months, PFS did not favour the experimental arm, with a median PFS (mPFS) of 7.9 vs 8.2 months and 12-month PFS rates of 22.2% vs 38.9% in arms A and B, respectively (p 0.048). No difference in OS was observed (median OS: 16.9 vs 16.7 months in arms A and B, respectively; p 0.898). The most frequent ≥ grade 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and lymphopenia (37%, 16.8%, and 8.4% in arm A vs 15.3%, 8.5%, and 11.9% in arm B). Dose reductions and interruptions due to TRAEs were significantly more frequent in arm A (43.7% and 49.6%) than in arm B (18.6% and 28.8%), with ORs of 3.39 (95% CI 1.64–7.02; p 0.001) and 2.43 (95% CI 1.25–4.74; p 0.009), respectively.
  > Conclusions: The addition of trotabresib to SOC did not improve PFS in patients with ndGBM. Dose reductions and interruptions due to TRAEs were more frequent in the experimental arm and may have negatively impacted PFS. These findings suggest that novel drugs in ndGBM should carefully consider tolerability to ensure proper delivery of SOC and maximize clinical benefit. Dr. M. Simonelli and Dr. M. Vieito have contributed equally to this study.
  > Clinical trial identification: [[NCT04324840]].

> [!question] Open questions
> - Was RT or temozolomide dose intensity actually reduced in the trotabresib arm?
> - Does MGMT status explain the 12-mo PFS gap?
> - Would adjuvant-only BET inhibition avoid overlapping chemoRT toxicity?

### Inflammatory and epigenetic signatures in progressive brain metastases after cranial RT

> BM progressing after cranial RT showed CD8+ TILs 44 vs 187 cells/mm2 and FOXP3+ 215 vs 41 vs treatment-naive BM, an immunosuppressive shift.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT read is the paired relapse subset: within the same pts, irradiated relapses carried hypermethylation in immunomodulation pathways, while the cross-sectional comparison showed FOXP3+ 215 vs 41 cells/mm2. RT technique, dose and timing are absent, so this cannot yet inform whether SRS should precede or follow IO.

- 🔍 ESMO 2026 abstract 703RO (rapid oral), N=153 pts resected for progressive BM
- 🔍 Cohorts by prior treatment
  - Prior RT: n=43
  - Prior systemic therapy: n=37
  - RT + systemic: n=10
  - Treatment-naive: n=63
- 🔍 Exome sequencing, DNA methylation profiling and IHC on resected specimens
- 📊 TIL densities, progression after prior RT vs treatment-naive BM (median cells/mm2)

  | TIL subset | Post-RT | Treatment-naive |
  |---|---|---|
  | CD3+ | 71 | 494 |
  | CD8+ | 44 | 187 |
  | CD45RO+ | 104 | 302 |
  | FOXP3+ (immunosuppressive) | 215 | 41 |

- 📊 Paired relapse subset: 35 treatment-naive pts had a second resection; 34.3% got CNS-RT in between
- 📊 Irradiated relapses showed modest hypermethylation in immunomodulation and therapy-resistance pathways
- 📊 No difference in mutational profile between progressive lesions, irrespective of prior therapy
- ⚠️ p-values, CIs and effect sizes for TIL differences not reported in source
- ⚠️ RT technique (SRS vs WBRT), dose, and RT-to-resection interval not reported
- ⚠️ Surgical cohort selects resectable progression; steroid exposure and primary histology not stated

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Inflammatory and epigenetic signatures in progressive brain metastases after cranial radiotherapy** — Ariane Steindl. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 703RO · Rapid oral: CNS tumours · 2026-10-23 · presented by Ariane Steindl (Vienna, Austria)
  > Background: Given the high rate of early recurrence in brain metastases (BM), factors driving progression remain poorly understood. This study aimed to characterize genetic, epigenetic, and inflammatory signatures in progressive BM after different therapeutic strategies.
  > Methods: A total of 153 patients undergoing surgical resection for progressive BM were included and stratified according to prior treatment: radiotherapy (n=43), systemic therapy (n=37), radio- and systemic therapy (n=10), or treatment-naive (n=63). Tumor specimens were analyzed with exome-sequencing, DNA methylation profiling, and immunohistochemistry.
  > Results: Compared with treatment-naive BM, BM resected due to progression to prior radiotherapy demonstrated significantly reduced densities of immune-activating tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) such as CD3+ (median 71 vs. 494 cells/mm2,), CD8+ (44 vs. 187 cells/mm2), and CD45RO+ cells (104 vs. 302 cells/mm2). In contrast, densities of immunosuppressive TILs were increased (FOXP3+: 215 vs. 41cells/mm2). Among treatment-naive patients, 35 developed intracranial relapse and underwent a second resection. Of these, 65.7% did not receive any therapy in between, whereas 34.3% received CNS-radiotherapy between resections. Here, patients who received radiotherapy exhibited modest alterations in DNA methylation compared to those without radiotherapy. These changes were predominantly characterized by hypermethylation in irradiated lesions including pathways associated with immunomodulation, therapy resistance, and interactions with the neural and vascular microenvironment. Comparative genetic profiling did not identify differences in the mutational landscape between progressive lesions, irrespective of prior therapy.
  > Conclusions: In this study, BM progressing after prior cranial radiotherapy were associated with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, along with modest DNA methylation changes and enrichment of pathways related to immunomodulation and tumor survival. In combination with the clinical observation of varying responses to immunotherapy in progressive BM post-radiotherapy, our data warrant for further investigation for the optimal treatment sequencing in BM patients.

> - Does post-RT immunosuppression differ by SRS vs WBRT?
> - Does RT induce or select the immunosuppressed phenotype?

## 🌰 Prostate

Pelvic nodal RT in both arms, definitive or salvage, so the trial tests ARSI intensification on top of RT, not RT itself; RT dose/fractionation not reported in source.

### DASL-HiCaP · [[NCT04136353]]

> 4y MFS-CI 95% vs 88%, HR 0.42 (0.27-0.66), adding darolutamide to 96wk ADT + pelvic nodal RT in very high-risk localised or post-RP persistent/recurrent prostate cancer.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> RT was fixed in both arms (prostate or prostate bed plus pelvic nodes, 96 weeks of LHRH analog), so the 4y MFS-CI gain of 95% vs 88% is darolutamide layered on elective nodal RT. Benefit held across salvage-RT and node-involved subgroups per OncLive (subgroup HRs not in source); dose and fractionation not reported.

- 🔍 Phase 3, placebo-controlled, randomised 1:1, N=1,106 (553 per arm), randomised 30APR20 to 09AUG23
- 💊 Darolutamide 600 mg vs placebo BID for 96 wk, on a 96 wk LHRH analog backbone
- 🔍 RT to prostate/prostate bed and pelvic nodes in both arms, definitive or salvage
- 📐 Single prespecified interim after 87 MFS-CI events; disclosure boundary 2-sided stratified log-rank p<0.012
- 📊 Efficacy at interim, median f/u 3.8y (events, event-free at 4y)

  | Endpoint | Darolutamide N=553 | Placebo N=553 | HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | MFS-CI | 27 (95%) | 63 (88%) | 0.42 (0.27 to 0.66), p=0.0001 |
  | PSA-PFS | 46 (91%) | 95 (83%) | 0.47 (0.33 to 0.67) |
  | PCSS | 4 (99%) | 7 (99%) | 0.58 (0.17 to 1.97) |
  | OS | 21 (97%) | 24 (95%) | 0.88 (0.49 to 1.59) |

- 📊 MFS benefit consistent across prespecified subgroups, incl primary vs salvage RT and with/without pelvic nodal involvement (OncLive); subgroup HRs not in source
- ⚠️ AEs, darolutamide vs placebo

  | AE | Darolutamide | Placebo |
  |---|---|---|
  | Fatigue/asthenia G≥2 | 14% | 10% |
  | Hypertension G≥2 | 7% | 9% |
  | Fractures G≥2 | 2% | 3% |
  | Cardiac G≥2 | 3% | 3% |
  | Any AE G≥3 | 43% | 36% |

- ⚠️ Interim read: trials stopped early on efficacy tend to overestimate effect size
- ⚠️ OS and PCSS immature; ICECaP surrogacy of MFS-CI for OS not yet confirmed in this trial
- ⚠️ Staging and MFS endpoint by CT/bone scan, not PSMA PET
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation not reported in source
- 🔗 vs STAMPEDE abiraterone in high-risk M0: a second ARSI-intensification signal with RT, different drug and backbone

  > Background: We evaluated the addition of darolutamide to standard of care ADT and RT for very high-risk localised or biochemically persistent/recurrent prostate cancer within 1 year after radical prostatectomy (RP).
  > Methods: Participants (pts) were prescribed an LHRH analog for 96 weeks plus RT to prostate/prostate bed and pelvic nodes and randomised 1:1 to darolutamide 600 mg vs placebo, twice daily for 96 weeks. The primary endpoint was metastasis-free survival based on conventional imaging with CT and/or technetium bone scan (MFS-CI, as per ICECaP surrogacy for OS). PSA-progression free survival (PFS), prostate cancer specific survival (PCSS), and overall survival (OS) were secondary endpoints. The protocol specified a single interim analysis after 87 MFS-CI events, with IDSMC review for external disclosure if the 2-sided, stratified log-rank p-value for MFS-CI was <0.012.

Any AE grade ≥3 was experienced by 43% vs 36% of pts, respectively.

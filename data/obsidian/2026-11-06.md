# 2026-11-06

**Electron hypofractionation for BCC/SCC: 3-yr LRF 96.1% with 44 Gy/10 fx vs 97.5% with 54 Gy/18 fx, retrospective, 434 tumours.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Skin RT carried the day, all retrospective or survey data with no randomised comparator. Short hypofractionated courses held LC (3-yr LRF 96.1% at 44 Gy/10 fx). For BCC 1.1-5 cm, ≤2 Gy/fx underperformed (LC 75%). Photon vs kV effects look confounded by indication. CNS: GK beat VMAT on plan quality only for mets ≤0.4 cc.

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

Four NMSC datasets on dose-fractionation and modality. Schedule and modality were assigned by era, frailty or lesion depth, so the comparisons stay confounded.

### UK practice survey: RT for non-melanoma skin cancer

> 63 UK clinical oncologists (43 complete) converge on 18-20 Gy/1#, 35 Gy/5#, 45 Gy/10#, 55 Gy/20#; 24 different non-standard schedules proposed.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The actionable read is in the weekly and alternate-day schedules for frail pts: BED modelling flags 28 Gy/2# 6 weeks apart, 32.4 Gy/3# weekly and 36 Gy/4# weekly as carrying excess late-effect risk. For single fractions, 18 or 20 Gy is the norm (94%); 22 Gy is called unacceptable without stand-off correction.

- 🔍 Anonymous online survey sent to 58 UK clinical heads/audit leads; open 3 months
- 🔍 43 complete + 20 partial responses; scenarios posed for a fit 65yo, well 80yo, frail 85yo
- 📊 Kilovoltage available to 81% of responders
- 📊 Workload: 46.2% treat <5 skin cases/month; only 2% treat skin exclusively
- 📊 Most-suggested dose fractionations (Table 1, times suggested across scenarios)

  | Dose fractionation | Times suggested |
  |---|---|
  | 35 Gy/5# | 237 |
  | 45 Gy/10# | 170 |
  | 55 Gy/20# | 134 |
  | 50 Gy/20# | 57 |
  | 50 Gy/15# | 56 |
  | 45 Gy/9# | 51 |
  | 18 Gy/1# | 41 |
  | 40 Gy/10# | 29 |
  | 32 Gy/5# | 27 |
  | 60 Gy/30# | 26 |
  | 45 Gy/9# over 3 weeks | 24 |
  | 40.5 Gy/9# | 22 |
  | 27 Gy/3# over 2 weeks | 20 |
  | 20 Gy/1# | 18 |

- 📊 Radical single fraction: 18 or 20 Gy in 94%; authors call 22 Gy/1# no longer acceptable unless correcting for stand-off
- 📊 Site-specific practice
  - Ala nasi 1.5-cm BCC: >80% kV photons, mostly 35 Gy/5#
  - Helix 2-cm SCC: kV used by only ~25%
  - Inner canthus BCC: ~80% kV; 74% apply stand-off correction (several at gap ≥5 mm)
  - Dorsum of hand 4-cm SCC: ~70% favour ≥15 fractions; 7.7% offered no regimen
  - Shin: ~20% decline RT even in the infirm
  - Graft recurrence: 40.6% treat whole graft, 50% recurrence + margin, 9.4% vary
- ⚠️ BED modelling (α/β 3 for late effects) flags 28 Gy/2# 6 weeks apart, 32.4 Gy/3# weekly, 36 Gy/4# weekly as high late-effect risk
- ⚠️ Hypothetical scenario stems, no clinic gestalt; stated preference, not audited delivered practice
- ⚠️ 2014 survey, single country, 43 complete responses; no outcomes collected

### Radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer

> Overall local control 89% across 531 lesions; 93% untreated vs 80% recurrent, with fraction size, diameter and histology independently predicting local failure.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The actionable RT signal is fraction size: BCC 1.1-5 cm treated at ≤2 Gy/fx controlled 75% (38/51) vs 95-100% with larger fractions (p=0.01), and fraction size held on multivariate analysis. That argues against conventional 2 Gy fractionation for mid-size BCC, while SCC showed no comparable dose or fraction-size effect.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review, 1966-1997, 468 pts with 531 biopsy-proven lesions
- 🔍 389 BCC and 142 SCC; 167 lesions were recurrent at presentation
- 🔍 Median f/u 5.8 yr (range 2-24); minimum 2 yr follow-up
- 💊 RT modality mix
  - Superficial x-rays 60%
  - Electron beam 19%
  - Electron + superficial combination 20%
  - Megavoltage photons <2%
- 📊 Local tumor control by histology and presentation

  | Histology | Untreated | Recurrent | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | BCC | 270/285 (95) | 90/104 (86) | 358/389 (92%) |
  | SCC | 70/79 (89) | 43/63 (68) | 113/142 (80%) |

- 📊 Local control by modality, all lesions

  | Modality | Local control |
  |---|---|
  | Superficial x-rays | 94% (299 of 317) |
  | Electron beam | 82% (82 of 100) |
  | Combination | 82% (89 of 108) |
  | Megavoltage photons | 50% (3 of 6) |

- 📐 Modality not significant on multivariate analysis (electrons vs other, p=0.345)
- 📊 BCC 1.1-5 cm: local control by dose per fraction (p=0.01, ≤2 Gy vs larger)

  | Dose/fraction | Local control |
  |---|---|
  | ≤2 Gy | 38/51 (75) |
  | 2.01-3 Gy | 89/94 (95) |
  | 3.01-4 Gy | 10/10 (100) |
  | >4 Gy | 19/19 (100) |

- 📐 Multivariate: larger fraction size associated with better LC (p=0.01); diameter and histology p<0.01
- 📊 SCC: no clear dose-response or fraction-size effect (≤1 cm lesions p=0.13)
- 📊 SCC T4: LC 75% (9 of 12) with cartilage invasion, 67% (14 of 21) with bone invasion
- 📊 SCC nodal involvement 13% overall; 32% in recurrent lesions
- 📊 Toxicity and cosmesis
  - 92% excellent/good cosmesis among pts with cosmesis data
  - Overall complication rate 5.8%; 13% in lesions >5 cm
  - Soft-tissue necrosis: 13 (9%) of 142 SCC vs 8 (2%) of 389 BCC
  - Fair/poor cosmesis: superficial 12 (4%) of 276, electrons 10 (12%) of 81, combination 15 (19%) of 77
- ⚠️ Modality assigned by era and lesion features, not randomized; superficial arm skewed to small lesions
- ⚠️ Cosmesis scored retrospectively from notes and photographs; data missing for 15-25% of untreated lesions
- 🔗 Same institution's 1986 Lovett series found electrons worse on multivariate; authors attribute the change to larger electron margins in recent years

- 📄 **Radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer** — Locke, Jay; Karimpour, Shervin; Young, Gwen et al.. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology*Biology*Physics* (2001-11). [doi:10.1016/s0360-3016(01)01656-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0360-3016(01%2901656-x)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2001-locke-radiotherapy-epithelial-skin-cancer.pdf|Radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Does the BCC fraction-size effect survive in prospective or modern-technique cohorts?
> - RT vs Mohs surgery for recurrent cutaneous SCC
> - Optimal electron margin for SCC to match superficial x-ray control

### Hypofractionated electron beam RT for epithelial skin cancer (434 cases)

> 3-yr local recurrence-free rate 97.5% with 54 Gy/18 fx vs 96.1% with 44 Gy/10 fx, using electrons for BCC and SCC in a retrospective series.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Technique details decide whether this transfers: electron energy 4-12 MeV chosen by estimated depth, a 1 cm margin, and copper foil (0.2 or 0.4 mm) to offset electron skin sparing. SCC has the thinnest evidence: only 33 tumours got 54 Gy, with a 3-yr LRF 95% CI of 79.1-99.6%. The 10-fraction course is better supported for BCC than for SCC.

- 🔍 Single-centre retrospective series: 434 biopsy-proven tumours in 333 pts, treated 2001-2006
- 🔍 54 Gy/18 fx of 3 Gy (n=159) vs 44 Gy/10 fx of 4.4 Gy (n=275), 4 fx per week
- 📊 3-yr actuarial local recurrence-free (LRF) rate by histology and schedule

  | Histology | 54 Gy/18 fx | 44 Gy/10 fx | Log-rank p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | BCC | 97.6% (95% CI 92.7-99.2) | 96.9% (95% CI 93.1-98.8) | 0.67 |
  | SCC | 97.0% (95% CI 79.1-99.6) | 93.6% (95% CI 83.4-97.6) | 0.46 |
  | All tumours | 97.5% | 96.1% | not reported |

- 📊 T stage was the only significant factor for local recurrence (p=0.036)
- 📊 2 of 11 T3 (>5 cm) tumours recurred, both after 44 Gy; centre now gives 54 Gy for >5 cm
- 📊 15 local recurrences in total; median time to recurrence 10.4 mo for BCC, 3.3 mo for SCC
- 📊 3-yr MFS 98.8% overall; all 5 metastatic events in SCC (3-yr MFS 94.7%)
- 📊 3-yr OS 54% SCC vs 82% BCC; most deaths from other causes, 3-yr CSS 98% and 100%
- 📊 Cosmesis pilot (14 pts), good or fair outcome

  | Scored by | 54 Gy | 44 Gy |
  |---|---|---|
  | Patient | 75% | 100% |
  | Observer | 87% | 83% |

- ⚠️ Schedule assigned by era and frailty: 44 Gy cohort older (>80 y: 41.2% vs 17.0%)
- ⚠️ Follow-up shorter in the 44 Gy group, as the authors acknowledge
- ⚠️ BCC 44 Gy 3-yr LRF is 96.9% in the text but 96.7% in the Fig 1 caption

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Efficacy of a hypofractionated schedule in electron beam radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer: Analysis of 434 cases** — van Hezewijk, Marjan; Creutzberg, Carien L.; Putter, Hein et al.. *Radiotherapy and Oncology* (2010-05). [doi:10.1016/j.radonc.2010.02.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2010.02.024)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2010-vanhezewijk-efficacy-hypofractionated-schedule-electron-beam.pdf|Hypofractionated electron beam RT for epithelial skin cancer (434 cases) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Is 44 Gy in 10 fractions adequate for tumours over 5 cm?
> - Late cosmesis after 4.4 Gy fractions beyond two years
> - Does the short course hold for high-risk SCC?

### Predictors of recurrence after RT for non-melanoma skin cancer

> Recurrence 15.8% at median 18.4mo f/u in 448 RT-treated NMSCs; on MVA, age, size ≥2cm (OR 2.090), immunosuppression (OR 2.939) and photon modality predicted recurrence.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Dose did not track with control here (EQD2 OR 1.007, p=0.4735), yet LC was 84.2% on schedules of EQD2 56 and 47, below the 96.1% and 96.9% van Hezewijk reported with 44/10 and 54/18. That gap, plus photons used for deeper lesions, leaves the dose question for ≥2cm or immunosuppressed lesions unanswered rather than settled.

- 🔍 Single-centre series from a prospective database (Odette Cancer Centre), Jan 2007 to Dec 2011
- 🔍 389 pts, 448 lesions; median age 78 (41-99)
- 🔍 Lesion mix
  - Pathology: BCC n=207 (46.2%), sclerosing BCC n=115 (25.7%), SCC n=126 (28.1%)
  - Setting: primary n=239 (53.3%), recurrent n=133 (29.7%), postoperative n=75 (16.7%)
  - Size <2cm n=304 (67.9%); face n=283 (63.2%), ear n=69 (15.4%), scalp n=40 (8.9%)
  - Immunosuppressed: 9% of lesions (n=39)
- ☢️ RT delivery
  - Most common schedules: 50 Gy/20 fx (n=118, 26.3%) and 40 Gy/10 fx (n=65, 14.5%)
  - Orthovoltage 184 lesions (41.1%), electrons 146 (32.6%), photons 118 (26.3%)
  - Margin 1-2cm to field edge; electrons or photons for tumours >1cm thick or >3cm
- 📊 LC 84.2% (n=377); median time to recurrence 11.4mo (6.5-23.8)
- 📐 Multivariate predictors of recurrence (logistic regression, per tumour)

  | Factor | OR | 95% CI | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Age (per year) | 1.034 | 1.005-1.063 | 0.0197 |
  | Size ≥2 vs <2cm | 2.090 | 1.197-3.649 | 0.0095 |
  | Immunosuppression present vs absent | 2.939 | 1.321-6.539 | 0.0082 |
  | Electrons vs photons | 0.440 | 0.234-0.829 | 0.0111 |
  | Orthovoltage vs photons | 0.285 | 0.142-0.572 | 0.0004 |

- 📊 EQD2 did not predict recurrence on UVA: OR 1.007 (0.988-1.026), p=0.4735
- 📊 Pathology (SCC vs BCC OR 1.890, UVA) dropped out after backwards selection
- 📊 Recurrent vs primary lesions: trend only, OR 1.597 (0.912-2.798), p=0.1014
- ⚠️ Photon effect likely confounding by indication: photons reserved for deeper, larger lesions
- ⚠️ Logistic regression on crude recurrence with median f/u 18.4mo (range 0-132); censoring not modelled
- ⚠️ Per-tumour analysis with no correction for multiple lesions per pt
- 🔗 vs van Hezewijk 44/10 vs 54/18: 3-yr LC 96.1% vs 96.9%, higher-EQD2 schedules than here

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Predictors of Recurrence after Radiotherapy for Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer** — Khan, L.; Breen, D.; Zhang, L. et al.. *Current Oncology* (2014-04). [doi:10.3747/co.21.1727](https://doi.org/10.3747/co.21.1727)
  > Predictive factors of recurrence were examined in 448 non-melanoma skin cancers (72% basal cell carcinoma, 28% squamous cell carcinoma) treated with radiotherapy. The overall recurrence rate was 15.8% at a median follow-up of 18.4 months. In multivariate analysis, significant factors for recurrence were age (p = 0.0197), tumour size 2 cm or greater (p = 0.0095), immunosuppression (p = 0.0082), and treatment modality (p = 0.0009).
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2014-khan-predictors-recurrence-radiotherapy-non-melanoma-skin.pdf|Predictors of recurrence after RT for non-melanoma skin cancer (full text)]]

## 🧠 CNS

Dosimetry-only planning study with no local control or radionecrosis endpoint.

### Volume threshold of brain metastases for Linac-based SRS

> GK beat VMAT plan quality for brain mets ≤0.4 cc. Above 0.4 cc, SmartArc and HyperArc beat GK on RTOG CI and NT V12Gy, with ~8-14 vs 56 min beam-on.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Platform choice depends on the smallest lesion. For targets ≤0.4 cc, GK beat SmartArc on every metric, while HyperArc matched GK on NT V12Gy (0.7 vs 0.79 cc, p=0.1923) but had higher low-dose spill. Above 0.4 cc, both VMAT techniques gave lower CI and V12Gy in roughly a quarter of the beam-on time.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective planning study: 26 pts, 2-3 brain mets each, 76 lesions
- 🔍 GK SRS pts from 2018-2019, replanned as SmartArc (Pinnacle) and HyperArc (Eclipse) VMAT
- 🔍 Linac plans used HD-120 MLC + FFF beams; median 3 targets per pt
- 📐 ROC on RTOG CI + NT V12Gy (GK as baseline) set each technique's threshold volume
- 📊 ROC-derived threshold volumes

  | Technique · metric | Threshold | Sens | Spec | Accuracy |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SmartArc · RTOG CI | 0.4 cc | 81.8% | 87.8% | 85.9% |
  | SmartArc · NT V12Gy | 0.4 cc | 79.2% | 94.4% | 88.3% |
  | HyperArc · RTOG CI | 0.2 cc | 100.0% | 79.7% | 81.7% |
  | HyperArc · NT V12Gy | 0.5 cc | 89.5% | 82.9% | 85.0% |

- 📊 Median plan metrics, targets ≤0.4 cc (GK vs VMAT)

  | Metric | GK vs SmartArc | GK vs HyperArc |
  |---|---|---|
  | RTOG CI | 1.68 vs 1.85, p=0.0013 | 1.68 vs 1.46, p=0.032 |
  | NT V12Gy (cc) | 0.70 vs 1.25, p=0.0019 | 0.7 vs 0.79, p=0.1923 |
  | Paddick GI | 3.75 vs 6.62, p=0.0041 | 3.75 vs 4.96, p=0.0284 |
  | V4.5Gy (cc) | 2.58 vs 6.82, p=0.0050 | 2.58 vs 6.26, p=0.0065 |

- 📊 Median plan metrics, targets >0.4 cc (GK vs VMAT)

  | Metric | GK vs SmartArc | GK vs HyperArc |
  |---|---|---|
  | RTOG CI | 1.59 vs 1.36, p<0.0001 | 1.59 vs 1.23, p<0.0001 |
  | NT V12Gy (cc) | 3.87 vs 3.04, p<0.0001 | 4.06 vs 3.57, p<0.0001 |
  | Paddick GI | 3.19 vs 3.30, p=0.5297 | 3.19 vs 3.30, p=0.2969 |
  | V4.5Gy (cc) | 17.55 vs 16.04, p=0.0152 | 17.55 vs 13.52, p<0.0001 |

- 📊 Median beam-on: GK 56.25 min vs SmartArc 8.33 vs HyperArc 13.58, both p<0.0001 vs GK
- ⚠️ The 0.4 cc cutoff is an average of four ROC thresholds (0.4, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5 cc), not a single prespecified analysis
- ⚠️ HyperArc CI threshold PPV only 35.0%. The 0.2 cc cutoff is weakly predictive.
- ⚠️ Dosimetry only: no local control, radionecrosis or clinical endpoint reported
- 🔗 Consistent with Potrebko (1.16 cc mean-volume split) and Vergalasova (>1 cm diameter) direction. Different binning limits direct comparison.

**Sources:**

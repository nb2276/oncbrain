# 2026-11-06

**Electron RT for BCC/SCC: 3-yr LRF 97.5% (54 Gy/18fx) vs 96.1% (44 Gy/10fx), no difference in either histology; retrospective, dose assigned by frailty.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Skin RT carried the day: retrospective NMSC cohorts support hypofractionation, with 44 Gy/10fx matching 54 Gy/18fx and ≤2 Gy/fx predicting BCC failure (p=0.01), while a UK survey found 24 different non-standard regimens. In CNS, a GK-vs-VMAT replanning study puts the Linac SRS crossover near 0.4 cc, on dosimetric surrogates only.

## 🌞 Skin / Melanoma

Four NMSC RT reports, all retrospective or survey-based: fraction size and lesion size recur as the levers for local control, and real-world schedules vary widely.

### UK practice survey of NMSC radiotherapy

> Of 63 UK respondents, most chose one of four daily schedules (18-20 Gy/1#, 35 Gy/5#, 45 Gy/10#, 55 Gy/20#). Non-standard schedules varied widely, with 24 different regimens proposed.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The actionable signal is the non-standard (less-than-daily) schedules for frail pts: 24 different regimens were proposed, and the authors' BED modelling flags 28 Gy/2# 6 weeks apart, 32.4 Gy/3# weekly and 36 Gy/4# weekly as carrying too much late-effect risk. The authors also say a 22 Gy single fraction should be dropped unless it corrects for stand-off.

- 🔍 Anonymous online scenario-based survey of UK clinical oncologists; 43 complete, 20 partial responses
- 🔍 Each scenario posed for a healthy 65yo, a relatively well 80yo, and an 85yo nursing home resident with early dementia
- 🔍 kV access in 81% of responders; 46.2% treat <5 skin cases per month

- 📊 Single fraction: 18 or 20 Gy in 94% of radical single-fraction treatments

### Radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer (Locke)

> Local control 89% overall (93% untreated vs 80% recurrent) across 531 BCC/SCC lesions; dose/fx <2 Gy predicted local failure (p=0.01).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dose-per-fraction signal is confined to BCC 1.1-5 cm: 75% LC at ≤2 Gy/fx vs 95-100% at larger fx sizes (p=0.01), with no equivalent effect for SCC. Recurrent SCC is the weak group (68% LC, 32% nodal involvement), which bears on whether a recurrent SCC field should be treated more aggressively than a primary.

- 🔍 Single-institution retrospective review, 468 pts / 531 lesions treated 1966-1997
- 🔍 389 BCC, 142 SCC; 167 lesions recurrent after prior surgery, electrodessication or outside RT
- 🔍 Median f/u 5.8 yr (range 2-24); minimum 2 yr unless death or lost to f/u
- 💊 Modality: superficial x-rays 60%, electrons 19%, electrons + superficial 20%, megavoltage photons <2%
- 🔍 Treatment volume: tumor plus microscopic extension, additional margin of 1 cm depending on size
- 📊 Local control by histology and presentation

  | Group | Local control |
  |---|---|
  | All lesions | 89% |
  | Previously untreated | 93% (338 of 364) |
  | Recurrent | 80% (133 of 167) |
  | BCC overall | 92% (358 of 389) |
  | SCC overall | 80% (113 of 142) |
  | BCC recurrent | 86% (90 of 104) |
  | SCC recurrent | 68% (43 of 63) |

- 📊 BCC 1.1-5 cm: local control by dose per fraction (Table 5)

  | Dose/fx (Gy) | Local control |
  |---|---|
  | ≤2 | 38/51 (75) |
  | 2.01-3 | 89/94 (95) |
  | 3.01-4 | 10/10 (100) |
  | >4 | 19/19 (100) |

- 📐 ≤2 Gy vs larger fx sizes for BCC 1.1-5 cm: p=0.01
- ⚠️ SCC showed no clear dose response and no significant fx-size difference (p=0.13 for lesions ≤1 cm)
- 📐 Multivariate predictors of local failure
  - Daily fx size: larger fx = better control, p=0.01
  - Maximal lesion diameter and histology (SCC vs BCC): p<0.01
  - Modality (electrons vs other): p=0.345, not significant
  - Age p=0.164; number of treatment days p=0.144
- 📊 Crude LC by modality: superficial 94% (299 of 317), electrons 82% (82 of 100), combination 82% (89 of 108)
- 📊 T4 SCC: cartilage invasion 75% (9 of 12), bony invasion 67% (14 of 21)
- 📊 SCC nodal involvement 14% (11/79) untreated vs 32% (20/62) recurrent
- 📊 Cosmesis and late effects
  - 92% excellent/good among pts with cosmesis data
  - Fair/poor: superficial 12 (4%) of 276, electrons 10 (12%) of 81, combination 15 (19%) of 77
  - Overall complications 5.8% (13% in lesions >5 cm)
  - Soft-tissue necrosis: SCC 13 (9%) of 142, BCC 8 (2%) of 389
- ⚠️ Modality not randomized: electrons used for larger lesions, so crude modality differences are confounded by size
- ⚠️ Cosmesis graded retrospectively from records and photos; data missing for 15-25% of untreated lesions
- 🔗 vs Lovett et al. (same institution, 1986): electrons were worse on multivariate there; authors attribute the loss of significance to larger electron margins in later years
- 🔗 Consistent with Silva et al. (pinna lesions): <2 Gy/fx was a significant factor for recurrence on multivariate analysis

  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2001-locke-radiotherapy-epithelial-skin-cancer.pdf|Radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer (Locke) (full text)]]

### Hypofractionated electron beam RT for epithelial skin cancer, 434 cases

> 3-yr actuarial LRF 97.5% (54 Gy/18fx) vs 96.1% (44 Gy/10fx) with electrons for BCC/SCC; no significant difference in either histology.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The boundary is size: T3 tumours >5 cm recurred in 2/11, both after 44 Gy, so the centre now gives 54 Gy above 5 cm. SCC behaves differently from BCC. SCC recurrences came early (median 3.3 mo) and 3 of 5 had metastases. All BCC recurrences were local-only and treated by excision. Technique was a 1 cm margin with copper-foil compensation for skin sparing.

- 🔍 Single-centre retrospective series: 434 biopsy-proven tumours in 333 pts, treated 2001-2006
- 🔍 332 BCC (76.5%), 102 SCC (23.5%); 386 primary RT after biopsy, 48 postop for residual disease
- 🔍 RT technique
  - 54 Gy in 18 fx of 3 Gy (n=159) vs 44 Gy in 10 fx of 4.4 Gy (n=275), four fx per week
  - Electron energy 4-12 MeV, chosen by estimated tumour depth
  - Target: tumour + 1 cm margin
  - Skin-sparing compensated with copper foil (0.2 mm at 4 MeV, 0.4 mm at 6-12 MeV)
- 📊 3-yr actuarial local recurrence-free rate by histology and dose

  | Group | 54 Gy/18fx | 44 Gy/10fx | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All tumours | 97.5% | 96.1% | not stated |
  | BCC | 97.6% (95% CI 92.7-99.2%) | 96.9% (95% CI 93.1-98.8%) | 0.67 |
  | SCC | 97.0% (95% CI 79.1-99.6%) | 93.6% (95% CI 83.4-97.6%) | 0.46 |

- 📊 T stage was the only factor tied to local recurrence (log-rank p=0.036)
- 📊 T3 (>5 cm): 2/11 recurred, one BCC and one SCC, both after 44 Gy
- 📊 Patterns of failure (15 local recurrences)
  - Median time to LR 6.3 mo (range 1-65)
  - BCC: 10/332 recurred, median 10.4 mo; all local-only, all treated by complete excision
  - SCC: 5/102 recurred, median 3.3 mo; 3 of 5 also developed metastases
- 📊 3-yr MFS 98.8% overall (94.7% SCC, 100% BCC); 3-yr OS 54% SCC, 82% BCC; CSS 98% and 100%
- 📊 Cosmesis, pilot set of 14 pts (no significant difference between schedules)

  | Scorer · rating | 54 Gy | 44 Gy |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pt · good / fair / poor | 62% / 13% / 25% | 67% / 33% / 0% |
  | Observer · good / fair / poor | 38% / 49% / 13% | 33% / 50% / 17% |

- ⚠️ Dose assigned by era and frailty, not randomised: elderly, less mobile pts got 44 Gy first; standard from Jan 2004
- ⚠️ Age imbalance: >80y in 41.2% of 44 Gy vs 17.0% of 54 Gy tumours
- ⚠️ Shorter follow-up in the 44 Gy group, acknowledged by the authors
- ⚠️ Per-tumour analysis, no correction for pts with multiple lesions; univariate only (too few events)
- ⚠️ BCC 44 Gy 3-yr LRF given as 96.9% in Results text but 96.7% in the Fig 1 caption
- ⚠️ Cosmesis from 14 pts, photographed at median 66 mo (54 Gy) vs 22 mo (44 Gy): unequal time for late effects

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Efficacy of a hypofractionated schedule in electron beam radiotherapy for epithelial skin cancer: Analysis of 434 cases** — van Hezewijk, Marjan; Creutzberg, Carien L.; Putter, Hein et al.. *Radiotherapy and Oncology* (2010-05). [doi:10.1016/j.radonc.2010.02.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2010.02.024)
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2010-vanhezewijk-efficacy-hypofractionated-schedule-electron-beam.pdf|Hypofractionated electron beam RT for epithelial skin cancer, 434 cases (full text)]]

### Predictors of recurrence after RT for NMSC

> Recurrence 15.8% at median 18.4 mo across 448 RT-treated NMSCs; age, size ≥2 cm, immunosuppression and photon modality independently predicted recurrence.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Dose did not predict control (EQD2 OR 1.007, p=0.4735), while size ≥2 cm (OR 2.090) and immunosuppression (OR 2.939) did. Within the 40/10 and 50/20 schedules used here, escalation is not supported by this cohort; the lesion and host define risk. The photon OR is a marker of deeper, larger targets, not an argument against megavoltage.

- 🔍 Single-centre cohort from a prospective database, Odette Cancer Centre multidisciplinary clinic, 2007-2011
- 🔍 389 pts, 448 lesions; median age 78; BCC 46.2%, sclerosing BCC 25.7%, SCC 28.1%
- 🔍 Indication: primary 53.3%, recurrent after prior tx 29.7%, postoperative 16.7%; face 63.2% of lesions
- 📊 Overall LC 84.2%; median time to recurrence 11.4 mo (range 6.5-23.8)
- 📐 Multivariate predictors of recurrence (logistic regression, per tumour)

  | Factor | OR | 95% CL | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Age (per year) | 1.034 | 1.005, 1.063 | 0.0197 |
  | Size ≥2 vs <2 cm | 2.090 | 1.197, 3.649 | 0.0095 |
  | Immunosuppression | 2.939 | 1.321, 6.539 | 0.0082 |
  | Electrons vs photons | 0.440 | 0.234, 0.829 | 0.0111 |
  | Orthovoltage vs photons | 0.285 | 0.142, 0.572 | 0.0004 |

- 💊 RT technique
  - Orthovoltage 41.1%, electrons 32.6%, megavoltage photons 26.3%
  - Most common: 50 Gy/20 fx (26.3%) and 40 Gy/10 fx (14.5%)
  - Margin 1-2 cm to field edge; electrons/photons for tumours >1 cm thick or >3 cm
- 📐 No dose signal: EQD2 OR 1.007, p=0.4735 on univariate
- 📐 SCC vs BCC OR 1.890 on univariate; pathology dropped out after backwards selection
- 📐 Recurrent vs primary lesions: OR 1.597, p=0.1014, a trend only
- ⚠️ Photon effect likely confounding by indication: photons reserved for deeper, larger lesions (authors' own read)
- ⚠️ Logistic regression on a time-dependent outcome; censoring ignored with follow-up range 0-132 mo
- ⚠️ Per-tumour analysis, no correction for multiple lesions per pt
- 🔗 LC below the literature ranges cited by authors (BCC 87%-98%, SCC 56%-97%); van Hezewijk 44/10 vs 54/18 3y LC 96.1% vs 96.9%

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Predictors of Recurrence after Radiotherapy for Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer** — Khan, L.; Breen, D.; Zhang, L. et al.. *Current Oncology* (2014-04). [doi:10.3747/co.21.1727](https://doi.org/10.3747/co.21.1727)
  > Predictive factors of recurrence were examined in 448 non-melanoma skin cancers (72% basal cell carcinoma, 28% squamous cell carcinoma) treated with radiotherapy. The overall recurrence rate was 15.8% at a median follow-up of 18.4 months. In multivariate analysis, significant factors for recurrence were age (p = 0.0197), tumour size 2 cm or greater (p = 0.0095), immunosuppression (p = 0.0082), and treatment modality (p = 0.0009).
  - 📎 [[papers/skin/2014-khan-predictors-recurrence-radiotherapy-non-melanoma-skin.pdf|Predictors of recurrence after RT for NMSC (full text)]]

## 🧠 CNS

### Volume threshold of brain metastases for Linac-based SRS

> In a GK-vs-VMAT replanning study, GK beat VMAT on plan quality for targets ≤0.4 cc, while VMAT won for targets >0.4 cc.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> HyperArc shrinks the GK advantage on small targets: for lesions ≤0.4 cc, NT V12Gy was 0.79 vs GK 0.70 (p=0.1923), while SmartArc was clearly worse at 1.25 (p=0.0019). The cutoff depends on the platform, and VMAT beam-on time was 8.33 to 13.58 min vs GK's 56.25.

- 🔍 Retrospective planning study at one institution: 26 pts, 2-3 brain mets each, 76 lesions total
- 🔍 Pts were drawn from 121 GK pts treated 2018-2019; each was replanned with SmartArc (Pinnacle) and HyperArc (Eclipse)
- 🔍 Linac delivery used HD-120 MLC and FFF beams; median 3 targets per pt
- 🔍 Metrics: RTOG CI, Paddick GI, NT V12Gy, V4.5Gy. ROC analysis on CI and NT V12Gy set the threshold volumes
- 📐 ROC-derived threshold volumes (GK as baseline)

  | Plan · metric | Threshold | Sensitivity | Specificity | Accuracy |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SmartArc · RTOG CI | 0.4 cc | 81.8% | 87.8% | 85.9% |
  | HyperArc · RTOG CI | 0.2 cc | 100.0% | 79.7% | 81.7% |
  | SmartArc · NT V12Gy | 0.4 cc | 79.2% | 94.4% | 88.3% |
  | HyperArc · NT V12Gy | 0.5 cc | 89.5% | 82.9% | 85.0% |

- 📊 Median plan metrics split at the proposed 0.4 cc threshold (Table 4)

  | Metric | GK | SmartArc (p) | HyperArc (p) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ≤0.4 cc · RTOG CI | 1.68 | 1.85 (0.0013) | 1.46 (0.032) |
  | ≤0.4 cc · NT V12Gy (cc) | 0.70 | 1.25 (0.0019) | 0.79 (0.1923) |
  | ≤0.4 cc · GI | 3.75 | 6.62 (0.0041) | 4.96 (0.0284) |
  | ≤0.4 cc · V4.5Gy (cc) | 2.58 | 6.82 (0.0050) | 6.26 (0.0065) |
  | >0.4 cc · RTOG CI | 1.59 | 1.36 (<0.0001) | 1.23 (<0.0001) |
  | >0.4 cc · GI | 3.19 | 3.30 (0.5297) | 3.30 (0.2969) |
  | >0.4 cc · V4.5Gy (cc) | 17.55 | 16.04 (0.0152) | 13.52 (<0.0001) |

- 📊 For >0.4 cc, NT V12Gy favored VMAT: GK 3.87 vs SmartArc 3.04, and GK 4.06 vs HyperArc 3.57 (both p<0.0001)
- 📊 Median beam-on time: GK 56.25 min, SmartArc 8.33 min, HyperArc 13.58 min (p<0.0001 vs GK)
- ⚠️ The 0.4 cc cutoff is an average of four ROC thresholds (0.4, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5 cc), not a prespecified or validated threshold
- ⚠️ Text calls HyperArc CI 'comparable' to GK for ≤0.4 cc, but Table 4 shows 1.46 vs 1.68, p=0.032
- ⚠️ Dosimetric surrogates only; no local control, radionecrosis, or clinical outcome data
- ⚠️ Some adjacent targets were excluded (71 of 76 for CI, 60 for V12Gy) because their isodose volumes could not be separated
- 🔗 Potrebko et al used a 1.16 cc mean-volume split. Vergalasova et al found VMAT CI better than GK above 1 cm diameter. Bins differ, so direct comparison is limited

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Is there a volume threshold of brain metastases for Linac-based stereotactic radiotherapy?** — Chieh-Wen Liu; Saeed Ahmed; Tara Gray et al.. *Journal of Radiosurgery and SBRT* (2021)
  - 📎 [[papers/cns/2021-liu-there-volume-threshold-brain-metastases-linac-based.pdf|Volume threshold of brain metastases for Linac-based SRS (full text)]]

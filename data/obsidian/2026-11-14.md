# 2026-11-14

**RNI review: MA.20 10-yr DFS 82% vs 77%, EORTC 22922 72.1% vs 69.1%, no OS gain in either trial alone; breast nodal RT carries a review-only day.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> All five sources are narrative reviews, no new trial readouts. Breast carries the day: RNI (MA.20/EORTC 22922 DFS gains, pooled 1-2% 10-yr OS), ALND omission after Z0011/AMAROS, and response-adapted PMRT after NAC (ypN+ no-RT LRR ≥15%). Prostate urethral dose steering and liver SBRT BED ≥100 Gy10 (3-yr LC 93% vs 65%) round out RT technique.

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

Liver SBRT dose selection tracks BED and OAR distance; the evidence base stays single-arm and retrospective.

### SBRT for liver metastases (Seminars in Radiation Oncology review)

> BED ≥100 Gy10 tied to 3-year LC 93% vs 65% in liver mets; review maps fractionation to OAR distance and liver dose constraints.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The dose argument rests on one pooled analysis: BED above 100 Gy10 tracked with 3-year LC 93% vs 65% in mets, while primary liver tumors showed no gain above 100 Gy10. That separates the dose target for liver mets from HCC practice. For lesions near bowel or the central biliary tree, the review protracts fractionation rather than lowering BED.

- 🔍 Narrative review, Seminars in Radiation Oncology 2023; no systematic search method stated in source
- 📊 Pooled analysis of 13 studies: 3-year LC 76% mets vs 86% primary liver tumors
- 📐 BED >100 Gy10 in mets: 3-year LC 93% vs 65%
- 📊 Single-fraction 35-40 Gy (BED 157.5-200 Gy10), median size 2.0 cm: 4-year LC 96.6%
- 🔍 Proposed fractionation by tumor distance to OARs (non-cirrhotic liver)

  | Distance to OAR | Dose range | Fractions |
  |---|---|---|
  | >2 cm from all OARs | 45-75 Gy | 3 |
  | 1-2 cm from any OAR | 50-60 Gy | 5-6 |
  | <1 cm from luminal GI or central biliary tree | 60-67.5 Gy | 10-15 |

- 📐 ASTRO liver-primary constraints cited, non-cirrhotic

  | Metric | 3 fractions | 5 fractions |
  |---|---|---|
  | Mean liver dose | <12-15 Gy | <15-18 Gy |
  | Critical volume ≥700cc | <19 Gy | <21 Gy |

- 📊 HyTEC: rVdose ≥700cc <15-17Gy as a planning rule → G3+ toxicity 9.3%
- 🔗 SBRT vs RFA meta-analysis, 2238 pts: 2-year LC 84% vs 60% in liver mets studies, no OS difference
- 📊 CRC liver mets meta-analysis, 656 pts: 2-year LC 59%, OS 57%, G3+ liver toxicity 8.7%
- ⚠️ Central liver zone (2 cm shell around portal vein): caution, biliary stricture risk
- ⚠️ Evidence base is single-arm prospective and retrospective series with heterogeneous dose; constraints are conservative, not tolerance-derived
- ⚠️ Kress, RPA and CRC prognostic scores are retrospectively derived and not externally validated

**Sources:**
- 📄 **The Role of Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy in the Management of Liver Metastases** — Lee, Sangjune Laurence; Bassetti, Michael F.; Rusthoven, Chad G.. *Seminars in Radiation Oncology* (2023-04). [doi:10.1016/j.semradonc.2022.11.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semradonc.2022.11.008)
  - 📎 [[papers/oligo-mets/2023-lee-role-stereotactic-body-radiation-therapy-management.pdf|SBRT for liver metastases (Seminars in Radiation Oncology review) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Optimal dose and fractionation for liver metastases
> - Randomized comparison of SBRT vs RFA for liver metastases
> - Benefit of MR-guided adaptive SBRT over ITV-based planning

## 🎀 Breast

Three reviews (2012-2018) span the nodal RT decision chain: ALND omission, RNI field extent, PMRT after NAC; post-NAC guidance predates Alliance A011202 and NSABP B-51 results.

### Surgery and radiotherapy for regional nodes in breast cancer

> 10-yr LRR 6.2% ALND vs 5.3% SLNB alone (P = .36) in Z0011 anchors this review of ALND omission and nodal RT field choice.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For RT, the decision here is field design. Z0011 used WBRT with no mandated nodal fields, and the review credits incidental axillary dose for its low failure rate. AMAROS required coverage of the undissected axilla plus the supraclavicular region. The source titles a field-design figure and PMRT algorithms, but their content is not in the captured text.

- 🔍 Narrative review (Oncology, 2018): no ALND vs ALND vs axillary RT after positive SLNB in T1-T2 cN0
- 📊 Prognosis: 5-yr survival <65% with more than four involved nodes vs >95% node-negative
- 🔍 Isolated tumor cells, pN0(i+) or pN0(mol+): manage as node-negative for systemic therapy, surgery and RT
- 📊 Historically <40% had additional positive nodes on completion ALND
- 📊 ALND omission trials as summarized in the review

  | Feature | Z0011 | IBCSG 23-01 | AMAROS |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | N | 891 | 931 | Not stated in source |
  | SLN burden | One or two positive SLNs | Micrometastases (N1mi) | Positive SLNB |
  | Breast surgery / RT | BCS + WBRT | 91% BCS + WBRT; mastectomy allowed | RT fields: undissected axilla + supraclavicular |
  | Comparison | Completion ALND vs SLNB alone | Completion ALND vs SLNB alone | Completion dissection vs no further surgery |
  | Outcome | Similar 5-yr OS/DFS; 10-yr LRR 6.2% vs 5.3%, P = .36 | No DFS/OS difference | No surgical benefit for LC, DFS or OS |

- 🔗 NSABP B-04: about 40% predicted axillary disease without dissection, regional recurrence in only 20%
- 🔍 Review credits incidental axillary dose from WBRT for low Z0011 axillary failure
- 📐 2016 ASCO guideline: no ALND for T1-T2 (≤ 5 cm), one or two SLN mets, BCS + conventionally fractionated WBRT
- ⚠️ Tumors larger than 5 cm and gross extranodal extension were not enrolled; omit ALND cautiously there
- ⚠️ Trial limits per review: low accrual, low event rate, high-risk pts underrepresented
- ⚠️ Excerpt truncated: PMRT tables, field-design figure and decision algorithms captured as titles only

**Sources:**

  - 📎 [[papers/breast/2018-mitchell-use-surgery-radiotherapy-treatment-regional-nodes-breast.pdf|Surgery and radiotherapy for regional nodes in breast cancer (full text)]]

### Clinical perspective on regional nodal irradiation for breast cancer

> 10-yr DFS 82% vs 77% (MA.20) and 72.1% vs 69.1% (EORTC 22922) with RNI, no OS gain in either trial alone.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The transferable detail is target volume: MA.20 treated IMC, SCV and level III axilla after BCS in a cohort roughly 85% 1-3 node-positive, while EORTC 22922 treated IMC plus medial SCV with 44.4% node-negative in the RNI arm. Both DFS gains (82% vs 77%; 72.1% vs 69.1%) support nodal fields in higher-risk pN1, with IMC coverage planned against cardiac dose.

- 🔍 MA.20 vs EORTC 22922 enrollment (Table 1)

  | Characteristic | NCIC MA.20 | EORTC 22922 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Accrual | 2000-2007 | 1996-2004 |
  | N | 1832 | 4004 |
  | BCS | 100% | 76.1% |
  | RNI targets | IMC + SCV + level III axilla | IMC + medial SCV |
  | Median age (yr) | 53-54 | 54 |
  | 0 ALN+ (RNI vs control) | 9.6% vs 9.7% | 44.4% vs 44.5% |
  | 1-3 ALN+ (RNI vs control) | 84.9% vs 85.1% | 42.9% vs 43.3% |
  | >3 ALN+ (RNI vs control) | 5.5% vs 5.1% | 12.2% vs 12.2% |
  | Chemotherapy (RNI vs control) | 90.7% vs 90.5% | 54.6% vs 55.1% |

- 📊 10-yr outcomes, RNI vs control

  | Endpoint | NCIC MA.20 | EORTC 22922 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10-yr DFS | 82% vs 77%, P=0.01 | 72.1% vs 69.1%, P=0.04 |
  | 10-yr distant DFS | Not stated in source text | 78% vs 75.5%, P=0.02 |
  | 10-yr BC mortality | Not stated in source text | 12.5% vs 14.4%, P=0.02 |
  | 10-yr OS | No long-term benefit | No long-term benefit |

- 📊 Pooled MA.20 + EORTC 22922 + French IMC trial: absolute 1-2% 10-yr OS gain with RNI
- 🔗 EBCTCG PMRT meta-analysis, 1-3 LN+, at 10 yr
  - Overall recurrence RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.57-0.82), P<0.001
  - BC mortality RR 0.8 (CI 0.67-0.95), P=0.01
- ⚠️ Cardiac cost of IMC coverage
  - Coronary events rose 7.4% per Gy mean heart dose (2168 women, 1958-2001)
  - Retrospective: CAD 18% with vs 7% without IMC fields, P=0.01
- 🔗 Axilla: RT vs dissection after limited SLN disease
  - AMAROS: 5-yr axillary recurrence 1.19% RNI vs 0.43% ALND; less lymphedema; underpowered for NI
  - ACOSOG Z0011 (N=891, cT1-2N0, 1-2 SLN+): LRR 1.6% SLND vs 3.1% ALND, P=0.11
- ⚠️ Z0011: of 228 with RT records, ~50% got high tangents and 19% targeted RNI
- ⚠️ SLN microsectioning converts 10-50% of negative SLNs: trial-era pN1 is not modern pN1
- 🔍 Proposed RNI risk modifiers: LNR, premenopausal age, grade III, LVI, ECE, subtype, Oncotype RS
- 🔍 Post-NAC: cN1 with residual nodal disease → comprehensive nodal RT is standard per authors
- ⚠️ 2017 review: Alliance A011202 and NSABP-B51 were open at writing, so NAC guidance predates their results

### Radiation decisions after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and mastectomy

> No randomised post-NAC PMRT data: retrospective MDACC/NSABP series put ypN+ LRR without RT at ≥15%, cT1–T2 ypN0 <10%, supporting response-adapted PMRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The omission case rests on small denominators: NSABP pCR + ypN0 (n=68) had 5.9% 8-yr LRF without PMRT, MDACC cT1–2 ypN0 (n=19) 5%. Clinical T3N0 converting to ypN0 is the contested cell (MDACC 14% vs 2%, p=0.06; NSABP 6.2% and 11.8%), so chest wall and nodal RT stay individualized there.

- 🔍 Lancet Oncol 2012 Personal View; Medline/Embase searched to October 2011
- ⚠️ No randomised PMRT-vs-none data after NAC + mastectomy; evidence is retrospective or non-randomised
- 🔍 NSABP B-18/B-27 prohibited PMRT after mastectomy, giving a no-RT LRR baseline
- 📊 MDACC 676 pts post-NAC mastectomy, 10-yr LRR without vs with PMRT

- ⚠️ MDACC cohorts overlap across publications and span eras of changing staging, imaging, chemo and surgery
- ⚠️ Mostly doxorubicin-based NAC; one trastuzumab-based series converted 74% of cN+ to ypN0

## 🌰 Prostate

Urethral dose constraints are the main lever on late GU toxicity, most of all for SBRT.

### Urinary toxicity from prostate radiotherapy: mechanisms, mitigation, management

> Narrative review: urethral dose steering (FLAME-derived D0.1 cm³ <105% Rx; SBRT Dmax <107% → est. 7.7% late moderate GU) is the main mitigation lever.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning numbers are the transferable part: urethra D0.1 cm³ <105% of prescription from the FLAME dosimetric analysis, and Dmax <107% at 40 Gy/5 fx tied to an estimated 7.7% late moderate GU rate. Sparing below prescription stays conditional on MRI or PSMA PET localisation with tumor clear of the urethra.

- 🔍 Narrative review, Lancet Oncol 2022; [[PMID 1995]] to April 2022, no quantitative synthesis
- 📊 Urinary toxicity by anatomic site and phase
  - Bladder mucosa: acute irritation; late fibrosis, contracture, telangiectasia, bleeding
  - Prostatic/membranous urethra: acute irritation; subacute ulceration; late stricture
  - Prostate: acute swelling → obstruction; late fibrosis → fewer obstructive symptoms
  - Trigone: acute irritation; late neurogenic chronic urgency
  - Vesicoureteric junctions: late fibrosis, stricture
- 📊 Acute irritative symptoms in ~50%; typically resolve within 4–6 weeks post-RT
- 🔗 Dutch RCT: 68 → 78 Gy conventional dose escalation did not raise urinary toxicity
- 📊 FLAME DIL boost: bDFS 92% vs 85%, HR 0.45 (0.28–0.71), p<0.001
- 📐 FLAME dosimetric analysis: urethral dose-effect, no clear threshold; urethra D0.1 cm³ <105% Rx recommended
- 📐 SBRT 40 Gy/5 fx: urethral Dmax <107% → estimated late moderate GU 7.7% (23 trials, 2232 pts)
- 📊 MSKCC phase 1 at 40 Gy/5 fx without urethral sparing: late moderate-severe GU 12 of 53 (34.3%)
- 📊 Selected urethral steering/sparing series (review Table 1)

  | Series | N | Dose | Urethral constraint | GU toxicity |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | LDR brachy | 2693 | I-125 144 Gy or Pd-103 130 Gy (median) | Max <217 Gy; D30% <125% | Late G2 19%, G3 4% |
  | HDR boost | 110 | EBRT 35.75 Gy/13 fx + HDR 2 × 8.5 Gy | Max <120% Rx; D10% <120 Gy EQD2 | Late G2 31% at 7 yr |
  | Virtual HDR boost | 135 | 19 or 20 Gy/2 fx + EBRT 46 Gy/23 fx | Urethra PRV Dmax 0.1 cc <110% | Acute G2 26.6%; late G≥2 6.3% at 36 mo |
  | SBRT | 48 | 36.25 Gy/5 fx | Urethra PRV max D0.1 cm³ ≤38.78 Gy | Late G2 4% |
  | SBRT | 101 | 36.25 Gy/5 fx | PTVurethra relatively spared to 32.5 Gy | Acute G2 23.8%; late G2 4% |
  | SBRT | 52 | 35 or 37.5 Gy/5 fx | Max urethral dose ≤35 Gy | Acute G2 10%; late G2 4% |
  | SBRT | 36 | 38 Gy/4 fx | 80–90% of Rx | Acute G2 16.7%; late G2 8.3% |

- ⚠️ Table 1 series differ in grading, follow-up and reporter; rates don't rank modalities
- 🔗 ASCENDE-RT stricture: 18.4% of 188 brachy boost vs 5.2% of 195 EBRT, p<0.001
- ⚠️ Anticoagulants: 4-yr G3+ bleeding 15.5% vs 3.6% (single institution, 568 pts)
- 📊 Post-RT TURP incontinence: 18% (7 of 38) post-brachy; 27% (30 of 111) post-EBRT/brachy
- 📊 Secondary malignancy after EBRT: absolute increase 0.1% to 3.8% (21 studies), higher in younger pts

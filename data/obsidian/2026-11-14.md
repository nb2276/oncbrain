## 🎀 Breast

Three reviews on regional nodal decisions: ALND omission after positive SLNB, RNI selection, and PMRT after NACT and mastectomy.

### Surgery and radiotherapy for regional nodes in breast cancer

> Narrative review (2018): Z0011, IBCSG 23-01 and AMAROS support ALND omission in T1-T2 cN0 pts with low-volume SLN disease undergoing BCS + WBRT.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For RT, the transferable detail is the field. Z0011 omission rests on WBRT, with incidental axillary dose credited by the authors, while AMAROS required undissected axilla plus supraclavicular fields. ASCO 2016 conditions ALND omission on conventionally fractionated WBRT, so hypofractionated or tangent-only technique falls outside the guideline as quoted.

- 🔍 Narrative review, Oncology (Williston Park) 2018; ALND vs axillary RT after positive SLNB, plus RNI/PMRT decisions
- 📊 Nodal prognosis: 5-yr survival <65% with >4 involved nodes vs >95% for pN0
- 🔍 Isolated tumor cells, pN0(i+), are managed like node-negative disease for systemic therapy, surgery and RT
- 📊 Historically <40% of SLN-positive pts had more involved nodes at completion ALND
- 📊 Randomized axillary de-escalation trials as reported in the review

  | Trial | N | Population | Comparison | Reported outcome |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | ACOSOG Z0011 | 891 | T1-T2 cN0, 1-2 positive SLNs, BCS + WBRT | Completion ALND vs SLNB alone | 10-yr LRR 6.2% vs 5.3%, P = .36; similar 5-yr OS, DFS |
  | IBCSG 23-01 | 931 | T1-T2 cN0, SLN micromets (N1mi); 91% BCS + WBRT | Completion ALND vs SLNB alone | No significant DFS or OS difference |
  | EORTC AMAROS | Not stated in source | T1-T2 cN0, positive SLNB | ALND vs no further surgery; RT to undissected axilla + supraclavicular | No surgical benefit in local control, DFS or OS |

- 🔗 NSABP B-04: about 40% predicted axillary risk without dissection, regional recurrence only 20%
- 🔍 ASCO 2016: no ALND for T1-T2 (≤5 cm), 1-2 SLN mets, BCS + conventionally fractionated WBRT
- ⚠️ Z0011 RT fields not described in source text; authors credit incidental axillary irradiation
- ⚠️ Caveats the authors attach to the trial base
  - Low accrual and low event rates
  - High-risk pts underrepresented
  - Tumors >5 cm and gross extranodal extension not enrolled, so omission there is cautious
- ⚠️ Source excerpt truncated: mastectomy, PMRT, RNI field design and post-NACT sections not in source text

### Clinical perspective on regional nodal irradiation for breast cancer

> RNI raised DFS in MA.20 (82% vs 77%) and EORTC 22922 (72.1% vs 69.1%), neither showing OS gain; 2017 patient-selection review.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The two trials irradiated different volumes: MA.20 added level III axilla to IMC + SCV in a cohort with 84.9% having 1-3 positive nodes, while 22922 treated IMC + medial SCV only, with 44.4% node-negative. IMC coverage for node-negative medial tumors rests on 22922; full nodal fields in low-volume pN1 rest on MA.20.

- 🔍 Narrative review (The Breast 2017); no new data, no systematic search described
- 🔍 MA.20 vs 22922: enrollment and RNI targets (RNI vs control where paired)

  | Characteristic | NCIC MA.20 | EORTC 22922 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Accrual | 2000-2007 | 1996-2004 |
  | N | 1832 | 4004 |
  | BCS | 100% | 76.1% |
  | RNI targets | IMC + SCV + level III axilla | IMC + medial SCV |
  | 0 ALN+ | 9.6% vs 9.7% | 44.4% vs 44.5% |
  | 1-3 ALN+ | 84.9% vs 85.1% | 42.9% vs 43.3% |
  | Chemotherapy | 90.7% vs 90.5% | 54.6% vs 55.1% |

- 📊 RNI vs control outcomes by trial

  | Endpoint | NCIC MA.20 | EORTC 22922 |
  |---|---|---|
  | DFS | 10-yr 82% vs 77%, P=0.01 | 72.1% vs 69.1%, P=0.04 |
  | Breast cancer mortality | Not reported in source text | 12.5% vs 14.4%, P=0.02 |
  | Distant DFS | Not reported in source text | 78% vs 75.5%, P=0.02 |
  | OS | No long-term benefit | No long-term benefit |

- 🔗 Meta-analysis of MA.20, 22922 + a French IMC trial: absolute 1-2% 10-yr OS gain with RNI
- 📊 Early Breast Cancer Trialists' meta-analysis, PMRT in 1-3 positive nodes, 10 yr
  - Overall recurrence RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.57-0.82), P<0.001
  - Breast cancer mortality RR 0.8 (CI 0.67-0.95), P=0.01
- ⚠️ Coronary events rose 7.4% per Gy mean heart dose (n=2168, treated 1958-2001)
- ⚠️ IMC fields: CAD 18% vs 7% without IMC RT, P=0.01 (retrospective)
- 🔗 ACOSOG Z0011 (n=891): LRR 1.6% SLND alone vs 3.1% ALND, P=0.11
- ⚠️ Z0011 RT uncontrolled: ~50% of 228 pts with RT records had high tangents, 19% nodal fields
- 🔗 AMAROS: 5-yr axillary recurrence 1.19% RNI vs 0.43% ALND, underpowered for non-inferiority
- ⚠️ SLN microsectioning/IHC upstages 10-50% of negative nodes → modern pN1 more favorable than trial cohorts
- 🔍 Post-NAC cN1 with residual nodal disease → comprehensive nodal RT per authors

  - 📎 [[papers/breast/2017-moreno-clinical-perspective-regional-nodal-irradiation-breast.pdf|Clinical perspective on regional nodal irradiation for breast cancer (full text)]]

### Radiation decisions after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and mastectomy

> Retrospective MDA and NSABP data: PMRT after NACT and mastectomy cuts LRR in cT3-T4, cN2-N3 and ypN+ disease; omission after ypN0 has no randomised test.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The unresolved zone is cT3N0 converting to ypN0: MDA shows 5-yr LRR 14% vs 2% with PMRT (p=0.06), while NSABP no-RT cohorts report 6.2% after breast pCR, so omission there depends on which cohort you trust. Same split for cStage III pCR: MDA 33% vs 7.3%, Le Scodan no benefit.

- 🔍 Personal View (Lancet Oncol 2012): no randomised PMRT data exist after NACT and mastectomy
- 🔍 Evidence base: retrospective MD Anderson series plus NSABP B-18/B-27 mastectomy pts, in whom RT was prohibited
- 📊 NSABP B-18/B-27 without PMRT: 8-yr LRF 14.9% if ypN+ (n=447) vs 5.9% with pCR and ypN0 (n=68)
- 📊 MDA 10-yr LRR with vs without PMRT by subgroup (n=676, 80% irradiated, median f/u 69 mo)

  | Subgroup | No PMRT | PMRT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Clinical T3 | 22% | 8% | 0.002 |
  | Clinical T4 | 46% | 15% | <0.0001 |
  | Clinical N2-3 | 40% | 12% | <0.0001 |
  | Clinical N1 | 14% | 9% | 0.062 |
  | Clinical N0 | 23% | 10% | 0.014 |
  | Path tumour 2.1-5.0 cm | 31% | 14% | 0.002 |
  | Path tumour >5.0 cm | 52% | 13% | 0.001 |
  | Four or more nodes at resection | 59% | 16% | <0.0001 |

- ⚠️ cT3N0 after NACT: the series disagree on PMRT need
  - MDA (n=162): 5-yr LRR 24% without vs 4% with PMRT (p<0.001)
  - MDA ypN+ (n=73): 53% vs 5% (p<0.001)
  - MDA ypN0 (n=89): 14% vs 2% (p=0.06), borderline
  - NSABP no-PMRT 10-yr LRF: 14.0% ypN+ (n=179), 11.8% ypN0 with residual breast disease (n=95), 6.2% ypN0 with breast pCR (n=16)
- ⚠️ Good responders: pCR / ypN0 series conflict for clinical stage III

  | Series | Population | Endpoint | No PMRT | PMRT | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | MDA (McGuire) | cStage III, pCR | 10-yr LRR | 33% | 7.3% | 0.04 |
  | MDA (McGuire) | cStage II, pCR | LRR | none observed | none observed | n/a |
  | Le Scodan | cStage II, ypN0 | 5-yr locoregional RFS | 92.9% | 97.4% | >0.4 |
  | Le Scodan | cStage III, ypN0 | 5-yr locoregional RFS | 94.7% | 90.9% | >0.2 |

- 📐 Le Scodan ypN0 overall: adjusted HR 0.37 (95% CI 0.09-1.61), p=0.18 for PMRT
- 📊 Age <35, cStage IIA-IIIC (MDA, n=107): 5-yr LRR 37% without vs 12% with PMRT (p=0.001)
- 📊 Same cohort: 5-yr OS 48% without vs 67% with PMRT (p=0.031)
- 🔗 NACT shifts risk: four or more nodes at surgery, 5-yr LRR 53% after NACT vs 23% after upfront surgery
- ⚠️ MDA pts recur across several publications; concordance between MDA series is partly double-counting
- ⚠️ Era effects in staging, imaging, chemo and surgery across cohorts treated 1974 to 2005
- 🔍 Authors' MDA practice: PMRT for cStage III, cT3 and most ypN+; selective in cStage II (young, ER-negative, poor response)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Optimising radiation treatment decisions for patients who receive neoadjuvant chemotherapy and mastectomy** — Karen E Hoffman; Elizabeth A Mittendorf; Thomas A Buchholz. *Lancet Oncol* (2012)
  - 📎 [[papers/breast/2012-hoffman-optimising-radiation-treatment-decisions-patients-who.pdf|Radiation decisions after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and mastectomy (full text)]]

## 🌰 Prostate

### Urinary toxicity from prostate radiotherapy: mechanisms, mitigation, management

> Lancet Oncol 2022 review: urethral dose is the modifiable driver of GU toxicity; FLAME boost raised bDFS (HR 0.45) with GU toxicity 28% vs 23%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The planning number to carry is FLAME's urethra D0.1 cm³ <105% of prescription, derived because FLAME boosted the DIL with no urethral limit. At 40 Gy in five fx the gap is wide: 34.3% late moderate-to-severe GU without sparing vs 7.7% expected with Dmax <107%, which bears directly on urethral steering for SBRT dose escalation.

- 🔍 Narrative review, Lancet Oncol 2022; PubMed MeSH search 1995 to April 2022, English only
- 📊 Acute irritative symptoms in ~50% of pts during and immediately after RT; typically resolve within 4-6 weeks
- 🔗 Dutch randomised trial: 68 → 78 Gy (2 Gy/fx) did not increase urinary toxicity
- 📊 FLAME: focal DIL boost vs homogeneous dose
  - bDFS 92% (95% CI 87-94%) vs 85% (80-89%), HR 0.45 (0.28-0.71), p<0.001
  - Moderate-to-severe GU toxicity 28% boost vs 23% control
  - No urethral constraint mandated; post hoc urethral dose-effect, no clear threshold
  - Recommended urethra D0.1 cm³ <105% of prostate prescription
- 📊 Ultrahypofractionation and urethral dose
  - MSKCC phase 1 to 40 Gy in five fx without urethral sparing: late moderate-to-severe GU 12 of 53 (34.3%)
  - Systematic review, 23 prospective trials, 2232 pts: urethral Dmax <107% at 40 Gy/five fx → expected late moderate GU 7.7%
  - Four urethral-sparing trials in that review: low toxicity, no signal of high relapse
- 📊 Selected urethral-steering/sparing series (Table 1)

  | Series | N | Dose | Late urinary |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | LDR brachy | 2693 | I-125 median 144 Gy or Pd-103 median 130 Gy | G2 19%, G3 4% |
  | HDR boost | 110 | EBRT 35.75 Gy/13 fx + two 8.5 Gy HDR fx | G2 31% at 7 years |
  | Virtual HDR boost | 135 | 19 or 20 Gy in two fx + EBRT 46 Gy/23 fx | G≥2 6.3% at 36 months |
  | SBRT | 48 | 36.25 Gy, five fx | G2 4% |
  | SBRT, urethral sparing | 101 | 36.25 Gy, five fx; PTVurethra 32.5 Gy | G2 4% |
  | SBRT | 52 | 35 or 37.5 Gy, five fx; urethral max ≤35 Gy | G2 4% |
  | SBRT | 36 | 38 Gy, four fx; urethra 80-90% of Rx | G2 8.3% |

- 📊 ASCENDE-RT stricture: 18.4% of 188 brachy boost vs 5.2% of 195 EBRT, p<0.001
- 📊 Single-institution, 568 pts: 4-yr G3+ bleeding 15.5% on warfarin/clopidogrel vs 3.6% without
- 📊 Meta-analysis, 21 studies, ~200 000 pts: absolute second-malignancy increase after EBRT 0.1% to 3.8%
- 📊 TURP after RT, new incontinence
  - Channel TURP post-brachy (median 11 mo): 18% (7 of 38)
  - Larger post-EBRT/brachy series: 27% (30 of 111) after first TURP
- 🔗 Ongoing randomised ultrahypofractionation trials with urethral steering/sparing (Table 2)

  | Trial | N | Control | Experimental | Status |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | NINJA | 472 | 40 Gy, five fx | Virtual HDR boost 20 Gy/two fx + EBRT 36 Gy/12 fx | Recruiting |
  | PACE-B | 874 | 78 Gy/39 fx or 62 Gy/20 fx | SBRT 36.25 Gy, five fx | Not stated in source |
  | HEAT | 456 | 70.2 Gy/26 fx | 36.25 Gy, five fx | Recruiting |
  | NRG GU005 | 692 | 70 Gy/28 fx | 36.25 Gy, five fx | Closed to accrual June 2022 |

- ⚠️ ABS HDR consensus lists 11 different urethral constraints; no agreed urethral limit
- ⚠️ Urethral dose-toxicity data mostly retrospective, physician-graded, inconsistent timescales
- ⚠️ CTCAE/RTOG and IPSS under-capture chronic urgency; EPIC-26 irritative domain preferred

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

### SBRT in the management of liver metastases

> BED >100 Gy10 linked to 3-yr LC 93% vs 65%; review maps liver-met SBRT fractionation to OAR distance and liver/luminal constraints.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Being near bowel or the central biliary tree does not mean giving up ablative intent: the review moves tumors <1 cm from luminal GI or central biliary tree to 60-67.5 Gy in 10-15 fx, and uses SIB for the 1-2 cm zone. Pair that with the BED >100 Gy10 target and luminal organs, not the liver, set the fractionation.

- 🔍 Narrative review, Semin Radiat Oncol 2023; evidence summarized is prospective phase I/II series plus retrospective databases
- 📊 427-pt CyberKnife database: 2-yr LC 72%, 2-yr OS 49%, no G3+ toxicity
- 📊 CRC liver-met meta-analysis (656 pts): 2-yr LC 59%, 2-yr OS 57%
- ⚠️ G3+ liver toxicity 8.7% in that CRC meta-analysis
- 📐 BED >100 Gy10 in metastases: 3-yr LC 93% vs 65%
- 📊 Metastases vs primary liver tumors: 3-yr LC 76% vs 86%
- 📊 Single-fraction 35-40 Gy (BED 157.5-200 Gy10), median size 2.0 cm: 4-yr LC 96.6%
- 🔍 Suggested dose/fractionation by distance to OAR (non-cirrhotic liver)

  | Distance to OAR | Dose range | Fractions |
  |---|---|---|
  | >2 cm from all OARs | 45-75 Gy | 3 |
  | 1-2 cm from any OAR | 50-60 Gy | 5-6 |
  | <1 cm from luminal GI or central biliary tree | 60-67.5 Gy | 10-15 |

- 📐 ASTRO liver-primary guideline constraints, non-cirrhotic

  | Schedule | Mean liver dose | Critical volume ≥700cc |
  |---|---|---|
  | 3-fraction SBRT | <12-15 Gy | <19 Gy |
  | 5-fraction SBRT | <15-18 Gy | <21 Gy |

- 📐 HyTEC (12 studies): ≥700cc liver <15-17 Gy as planning rule → G3+ toxicity 9.3%
- 📐 QUANTEC: MLD 15 Gy/3 fx or 20 Gy/6 fx → G3 liver enzyme risk <20%
- 🔗 SBRT vs RFA meta-analysis (2238 pts): 2-yr LC 84% vs 60% in liver-met studies
- ⚠️ No OS difference vs RFA despite the LC gain
- ⚠️ Liver constraints derive from conservative trial experience, not tolerance-defining dose escalation
- ⚠️ CRC radioresistance contested: some series show no histology gap at high dose
- ⚠️ Prognostic scores (Kress: median survival 34.4 to 2.8 mo, score 0 to 3) lack external validation

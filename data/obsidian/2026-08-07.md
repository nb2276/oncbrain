# 2026-08-07

**STELLAR: 3yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3% (HR 0.883, NI P<.001) with LRR 8.4% vs 11.0% — 25 Gy/5fx TNT holds locoregional control against 50/25 chemoRT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Two Chinese phase III LARC trials move the RT question in opposite directions: STELLAR shortens RT to 5x5 with no LRR cost (8.4% vs 11.0%), while TNTCRT keeps LCRT unchanged in both arms and buys DFS purely through distant control (MFS HR 0.655, locoregional failure flat at 6.0% vs 6.2%). Lower GI carried the day; prostate was PSMA-PET methodology, not practice.

## 🌰 Prostate

Both prostate items are PSMA-PET measurement questions, not treatment trials: what a PET finding means in BCR after salvage RT, and whether post-ARSI uptake change can be read as response at all.

### PSMA-Positive BCR Natural History Study

> Baseline of first 130 pts: most BCR men with PSADT >9-12mo still have PSMA-PET findings once PSA >5, mostly nodal.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant read is that PSMA positivity is near-ubiquitous once PSA exceeds 5, even at PSADT >12mo, so a positive scan alone does not identify who needs metastasis-directed SBRT. Prostate-bed recurrence appears in a salvage-RT-exhausted cohort, which gates re-irradiation and nodal-field decisions.

- 🔍 Prospective NCI natural-history cohort, PSMA-PET serial imaging, no mandated therapy; baseline data on first 130 pts
- 🔍 Eligibility: BCR after definitive therapy, PSA ≥0.5, all had salvage RT or declined it
- 🔍 Imaging schedule: baseline PET; negative → annual, any finding (incl. equivocal) → q6mo
- 📊 Vast majority of pts with PSADT >9mo and >12mo had PSMA-PET findings when PSA above 5
- 📊 Findings described as nodal disease, occasional small equivocal bone lesions, and prostate-bed recurrence
- 💊 Only about a third of pts have gone on therapy; majority observed off treatment
- 💊 Permitted therapy is short-course only (<6 months): intermittent ADT, SBRT, intermittent ARPI or Radium-223 trials
- 📊 Companion cohort (Madan, ~1.5yr f/u, first ~150 pts): 5 pts progressed on conventional imaging
- ⚠️ Numbers are investigator-stated in a video interview, not a peer-reviewed report; no CIs, denominators, or per-stratum counts given
- ⚠️ Single-center NCI referral cohort; PSA ≥0.5 floor and salvage-RT-exhausted requirement select against the lowest-volume BCR seen in community practice
- ⚠️ Cross-sectional baseline read only; median f/u ~2yr, longitudinal PET evolution data not yet reported

### PSMA PET Response Assessment Debate (USPCC)

> Uptake change after ARSI is prognostic (HR 5.03 for δ-percent SUVmax, n=16) but not a validated response measure; AR signaling drives PSMA expression.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The reading that transfers: uptake change here is prognostic, not a burden measure. High δ-percent SUVmax carried median OS 17.8 vs 37.2 mo (HR 5.03, 95% CI 1.17-21.65) at n=16. For metastasis-directed planning, a PSMA PET obtained 2-4 months after ARSI or ADT start cannot be read as new disease without corroboration.

- 🔍 Position talk: PSMA uptake is androgen-receptor regulated, so post-therapy change may not track tumor burden
- 🔍 Cited series imaged pts immediately before and 2-4 mo after starting abiraterone or enzalutamide
- 📊 Outcomes by δ-SUVmax response pattern (cited prospective series, 16 evaluable)

  | Group | Median time to therapy change | Median OS |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low DPSM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | 37.2 mo (95% CI 28.9-NA) |
  | High DPSM | 6.5 mo (95% CI 4.6-NA), p=0.0001 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.02 |
  | Low DASM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | NA (95% CI 37.2-NA) |
  | High DASM | 6.9 mo (95% CI 6.1-NA), p=0.003 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.002 |

- 📐 Survival hazard ratios, wide intervals at n=16
  - DPSM: HR 5.03 (95% CI 1.17-21.65)
  - DASM: HR 9.31 (95% CI 1.81-47.87)
- 📊 Whole cohort, 16 evaluable pts
  - Median time to therapy change 9.6 mo (95% CI 6.9-14.2)
  - Median OS 28.6 mo (95% CI 18.3-NA)
- 📊 BAT: 3 of 6 men progressed on PSMA PET at 3 mo, before conventional imaging
- 🔍 RECIP 1.0 combines PSMA PET with CT to categorise PD, PR, SD, CR after radioligand therapy
- ⚠️ Repeatability work flags lesion identification and segmentation variability across serial SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVtotal, volume
- ⚠️ Evidence base is 16 and 6 pts, single-arm, no independent validation cohort

## 🌀 GI Lower

Two TNT phase IIIs that agree the added chemo works on distant failure and disagree only on how much RT you need to hold the pelvis.

### STELLAR · [[NCT02533271]]

> 3yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3%, HR 0.883, noninferior: 5x5 Gy then CAPOX matches long-course CRT in LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Every pt received IMRT with a defined CTV (mesorectum, presacral, internal iliac, obturator, ischiorectal fossa; external iliacs only cT4b), the first of these TNT trials to do so, and 3yr LRR was 8.4% vs 11.0%. Compressing RT to 25 Gy/5fx did not cost locoregional control, which is the decision this moves.

- 📊 1° EP 3yr DFS: 64.5% (58.3-70.7) TNT vs 62.3% (56.1-68.5) CRT; HR 0.883, one-sided 95% CI to 1.11, P<.001 for NI (margin 1.43)
- 🔍 Open-label phase III, N=599, 16 Chinese centres, distal/middle-third cT3-4 and/or N+; median f/u 35.0 mo
- 🔍 RT: TNT arm 25 Gy/5fx then 4 cycles CAPOX; CRT arm 50 Gy/25fx with concurrent capecitabine; TME 6-8 wks after preop treatment
- 🔍 All pts got IMRT in both arms, CTV covered mesorectum, presacral space, internal iliac, obturator and ischiorectal fossa; external iliacs only for cT4b
- 📊 3yr outcomes by arm

  | Endpoint | TNT (5x5 + CAPOX) | CRT (50/25) | Effect size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | DFS | 64.5% | 62.3% | HR 0.883 (one-sided CI to 1.11), NI P<.001 |
  | OS | 86.5% | 75.1% | HR 0.67 (0.46-0.97), P=.033 |
  | MFS | 77.1% | 75.3% | HR 0.88 (0.63-1.24), P=.475 |
  | LRR | 8.4% | 11.0% | HR 0.80 (0.45-1.44), P=.461 |

- 📊 LRR was low and statistically indistinguishable, so shortening RT to 25 Gy/5fx cost nothing in locoregional control
- 📊 pCR + sustained cCR 21.8% TNT vs 12.3% CRT (P=.002); 9.4% vs 3.4% went to nonoperative management
- ⚠️ Acute grade III-V toxicity during preop treatment 26.5% vs 12.6% (P<.001), driven by haematologic G3-4 15.8% vs 2.0%
- ⚠️ OS gain sits on a secondary endpoint with no subgroup consistency, and Polish II's early OS advantage vanished by 8 years
- ⚠️ Preop completion was worse in TNT: 82.6% vs 95.2% any-dose, 74.8% vs 93.2% full-dose (both P<.001)
- 🔗 3yr LRR 8.4% / 11.0% sits with RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23 (4%-8.3%) and well below Polish II (21%-22%)
- 🔗 Unlike RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23, no 3yr DM reduction here (22.8% vs 24.7%), matching Polish II

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (STELLAR)** — Jin, Jing; Tang, Yuan; Hu, Chen et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.21.01667](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.21.01667)
  > PURPOSE To ascertain if preoperative short-term radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is not inferior to a standard schedule of long-term chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients with distal or middle-third, clinical primary tumor stage 3-4 and/or regional lymph node–positive rectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) to short-term radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions over 1 week) followed by four cycles of chemotherapy (total neoadjuvant therapy [TNT]) or chemoradiotherapy (50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks, concurrently with capecitabine [chemoradiotherapy; CRT]). Total mesorectal excision was undertaken 6-8 weeks after preoperative treatment, with two additional cycles of CAPOX (intravenous oxaliplatin [130 mg/m 2 , once a day] on day 1 and capecitabine [1,000 mg/m 2 , twice a day] from days 1 to 14) in the TNT group and six cycles of CAPOX in the CRT group. The primary end point was 3-year disease-free survival (DFS). RESULTS Between August 2015 and August 2018, a total of 599 patients were randomly assigned to receive TNT (n = 302) or CRT (n = 297). At a median follow-up of 35.0 months, 3-year DFS was 64.5% and 62.3% in TNT and CRT groups, respectively (hazard ratio, 0.883; one-sided 95% CI, not applicable to 1.11; P &lt; .001 for noninferiority). There was no significant difference in metastasis-free survival or locoregional recurrence, but the TNT group had better 3-year overall survival than the CRT group (86.5% v 75.1%; P = .033). Treatment effects on DFS and overall survival were similar regardless of prognostic factors. The prevalence of acute grade III-V toxicities during preoperative treatment was 26.5% in the TNT group versus 12.6% in the CRT group ( P &lt; .001). CONCLUSION Short-term radiotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgery was efficacious with acceptable toxicity and could be used as an alternative to CRT for locally advanced rectal cancer.

### TNTCRT · [[NCT03177382]]

> 3yr DFS 74.8% vs 66.0%, HR 0.674, P=.016 for doublet CAPOX TNT with LCRT vs standard nCRT in high-risk LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> RT was constant across arms (LCRT both), so every bit of the DFS gain is chemotherapy intensification: locoregional failure sat at 6.03% v 6.19%. Nothing here licenses changing dose, target volume, or dropping RT. The decision it moves is chemo sequencing around an unchanged LCRT backbone, and pCR nearly tripled (26.37% v 9.80%), which matters if you are building toward organ preservation.

- 📊 1° EP DFS met: 3yr 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674 (95% CI 0.489-0.929), P=.016
- 📊 MFS 77.7% v 67.6%, HR 0.655 (0.469-0.915) · the DFS gain is distant control, not local
- 📊 pCR 26.37% v 9.80%, P<.001
- 📊 Locoregional failure flat: 6.03% v 6.19%, P=.943 · LCRT was identical in both arms
- 📊 3yr OS 90.2% v 87.5%, P=.167 · no survival separation at 51mo median f/u
- 🔍 Phase III, N=458 (232 doublet-LC TNT v 226 nCRT), stage II/III LARC, ≥1 MRI high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, MRF+, cT3c-d with EMVI), age 18-70
- 💊 Experimental: 1 cycle induction CAPOX → LCRT with 2 cycles concurrent CAPOX → 3 cycles consolidation CAPOX → TME. Control: LCRT + concurrent capecitabine → TME → 6 cycles adjuvant CAPOX
- ⚠️ Neoadjuvant-phase G3+ AEs 27.59% v 8.56%, P<.001; whole-course severe toxicity comparable (28.02% v 24.32%, P=.371)
- ⚠️ Both arms delivered LCRT, so this trial cannot speak to SCRT-based TNT (RAPIDO/STELLAR) or RT omission
- ⚠️ ~87% v ~90% went to TME · surgery rates not identical, and no watch-and-wait endpoint reported in source
- 🔗 vs RAPIDO/PRODIGE-23/STELLAR: those tested TNT vs nCRT with different RT or chemo scaffolds; TNTCRT isolates uninterrupted doublet chemo around an unchanged LCRT backbone

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial** — Wang, Xin; Tang, Yuanling; Lu, Junyang et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-03110](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-03110)
  > PURPOSE High-risk locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) carries a substantial risk of distant recurrence, which limits disease-free survival (DFS). We compared total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) integrating long-course radiotherapy (LCRT) with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy (doublet-LC TNT) versus conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT). METHODS In this multicenter, randomized, phase III trial, patients with stage II/III LARC and at least 1 high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, mesorectal fascia involvement, or cT3c-d with extramural vascular invasion) were enrolled. Patients were assigned to doublet-LC TNT (induction, concurrent, and consolidation capecitabine plus oxaliplatin with LCRT) before surgery or nCRT (capecitabine with LCRT) followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary end point was DFS (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: [[NCT03177382]] ). RESULTS Between June 6, 2017, and December 27, 2023, 458 patients were randomly assigned to doublet-LC TNT (n = 232) or nCRT (n = 226). At a median follow-up of 51 months, doublet-LC TNT improved 3-year DFS (74.8% v 66.0%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.674 [95% CI, 0.489 to 0.929]; P = .016). Metastasis-free survival (MFS; 77.7% v 67.6%; HR, 0.655 [95% CI, 0.469 to 0.915]) and pathologic complete response rates (pCR; 26.37% v 9.80%; P &lt; .001) were higher with doublet-LC TNT, whereas locoregional failure remained low and comparable (6.03% v 6.19%; P = .943). Although grade ≥3 adverse events during the neoadjuvant phase were more frequent with doublet-LC TNT (27.59% v 8.56%; P &lt; .001), severe toxicities during the entire treatment course (28.02% v 24.32%; P = .371) and major postoperative complications (3.98% v 2.94%; P = .567) were comparable. CONCLUSION Compared with conventional nCRT, doublet-LC TNT improved DFS, MFS, and pCR rates with manageable toxicity. These findings support this intensified, doublet-based regimen as a standard option within the modern TNT paradigm. Further comparative studies are warranted to evaluate these results against other short-course radiotherapy‑based or nondoublet-concurrent TNT regimens.

# 2026-08-07

**STELLAR: 5x5 Gy + CAPOX noninferior to 50 Gy/25f CRT in LARC (3yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3%, HR 0.883), with 3yr OS 86.5% vs 75.1%.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Rectal carried the day: two phase III TNT trials, both showing locoregional control is already saturated by either RT schedule (STELLAR LRR 8.4% vs 11.0%; TNTCRT 6.03% vs 6.19%), so the intensification argument lives entirely in distant failure and toxicity sequencing. Prostate was imaging, not therapy: PSMA PET signal without an established response-assessment role.

## 🌰 Prostate

Both entries are PSMA PET, and both are about what the scan does NOT yet license: a natural-history cohort with no tabulated concordance, and a point-counterpoint arguing uptake tracks AR signaling rather than burden.

### PSMA-Positive BCR Natural History Study

> Baseline data, first 130 pts: most BCR pts with PSA doubling time >9-12mo still have PSMA PET findings when PSA >5.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant read is where the disease sits: prostate bed recurrence and nodal disease are the dominant PSMA findings even in PSADT >9-12mo pts, i.e. the population being sent for metastasis-directed SBRT. Only ~1/3 went on any therapy and 5/150 progressed on conventional imaging, which argues observation remains a defensible arm when weighing SBRT for a PET-only lesion.

- 🔍 Prospective NCI natural history cohort; eligibility PSA ≥0.5 after definitive therapy incl. salvage RT (or declined salvage RT)
- 🔍 Baseline PSMA PET, then annual imaging if negative, every 6 months if anything (incl. equivocal) is seen
- 📊 Baseline analysis of first 130 pts; median follow-up ~2 years, study ongoing
- 📊 Only ~1/3 of pts have gone on therapy; the majority are observed off treatment
- 📊 Of the first ~150 pts, 5 have progressed on conventional imaging (Madan poster, ~1.5yr f/u)
- 📊 Reported that >95% have not had drastic sudden PET changes discordant with PSA/PSADT
- 🔍 Permitted on-study therapy is short-course only: intermittent ADT, SBRT, intermittent ARPI or Radium-223 trials, anything <6 months
- 🔗 Designed pre-EMBARK, so the comparator framing is observation vs intermittent therapy, not continuous ADT/enzalutamide
- ⚠️ No effect sizes, no concordance statistics, no denominators for the doubling-time subgroups reported in source; percentages above are the investigator's spoken figures in an interview, not tabulated results
- ⚠️ Interview transcript of a poster, not a peer-reviewed report; single-institution NCI cohort with trial-enriched referral pattern

### USPCC 2026 Point-Counterpoint: PSMA PET as Response Assessment

> High δ-SUVmax on serial 18F-DCFPyL PET carried OS HR 5.03 (DPSM) and 9.31 (DASM), but uptake tracks AR signaling, not burden.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Uptake change after an ARSI start is prognostic, not volumetric: high δ-absolute SUVmax carried OS HR 9.31 (95% CI 1.81-47.87). For metastasis-directed RT, a PSMA PET obtained 2-4 months into androgen-axis therapy can shift lesion conspicuity, so target selection off a post-treatment scan is not equivalent to a baseline one.

- 🔍 Point-counterpoint talk (Rowe, UT Southwestern) arguing PSMA PET is not yet a response-assessment tool
- 🔍 Core biologic claim: PSMA expression is dynamically AR-regulated, not a static marker of tumor burden
- 📊 Zukotynski prospective single-arm series, n=16 evaluable: median time to therapy change 9.6 mo (95% CI 6.9-14.2)
- 📊 Same series: median OS 28.6 mo (95% CI 18.3-NA); imaging pre-ARSI and 2-4 mo after start
- 📊 Uptake-change stratifiers vs outcomes (18F-DCFPyL, n=16, abiraterone or enzalutamide)

  | Stratifier | Median time to therapy change | Median OS | HR (95% CI), OS analysis |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Low DPSM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | 37.2 mo (95% CI 28.9-NA) | reference |
  | High DPSM | 6.5 mo (95% CI 4.6-NA), p=0.0001 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.02 | 5.03 (1.17-21.65) |
  | Low DASM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | NA (95% CI 37.2 mo-NA) | reference |
  | High DASM | 6.9 mo (95% CI 6.1-NA), p=0.003 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.002 | 9.31 (1.81-47.87) |

- 📊 Mixed but predominantly increased uptake pattern tracked shorter time to therapy change and shorter OS
- 📊 Bipolar androgen therapy series (n=6): 3 progressed on PSMA PET at 3 mo, earlier than conventional imaging
- 🔍 RECIP 1.0 pairs PSMA PET with CT to call CR / PR / SD / PD after radioligand therapy; validation pending
- ⚠️ Repeatability work (Bland-Altman, ICC, within-subject CV) shows lesion identification and segmentation vary on serial scans
- ⚠️ Repeatability analyses were restricted to lesions >1.5 cm³, so small-lesion measurement error stays uncharacterized
- ⚠️ Evidence base is two tiny series (n=16, n=6); OS HR CIs span 1.17-21.65 and 1.81-47.87

## 🌀 GI Lower

Two phase III TNT trials, one testing short-course 5x5 and one testing doublet chemo around long-course RT, both leaving locoregional failure untouched.

### STELLAR · [[NCT02533271]]

> 3yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3%, HR 0.883 noninferior for 5x5 Gy + CAPOX vs 50Gy/25f CRT in LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Every pt got IMRT, the only trial in this set that did, and LRR was 8.4% at 3yr, so 5x5 Gy delivered with modern planning holds local control against 50 Gy/25f. The added grade 3-4 burden is hematologic (15.8% v 2.0%), from CAPOX not the RT, which shifts the 5x5-vs-long-course choice to chemo tolerance rather than pelvic dose.

- 🔍 Multicenter open-label phase III, N=599, 16 Chinese hospitals, 1:1, NI margin HR 1.43; median f/u 35.0mo
- 🔍 Distal/middle-third rectal adenoca, cT3-4 and/or cN+, age 18-70, ECOG 0-1; 85.8% vs 83.5% stage III
- 📐 All pts received IMRT (unusual for this trial set); CTV covered mesorectum, presacral space, internal iliac, obturator, ischiorectal fossa; external iliac only if cT4b
- 📐 TNT arm: 5 Gy x 5 then 4 cycles CAPOX, TME 6-8wk later, then 2 CAPOX. CRT arm: 50 Gy/25f + capecitabine 825 mg/m2 BID, TME, then 6 CAPOX
- 📊 3yr OS 86.5% vs 75.1%, HR 0.67 (0.46-0.97), log-rank P=.033, a secondary endpoint in a NI trial
- ⚠️ OS gain rests on 47 vs 63 deaths and no OS subgroup was significant except cT4 (HR 0.362, 0.152-0.859) and ≤5cm from verge (HR 0.540, 0.318-0.916); Polish II's early OS benefit vanished by 8yr
- 📊 3yr LRR 8.4% vs 11.0% (HR 0.80, 0.45-1.44, P=.461); 3yr MFS 77.1% vs 75.3% (HR 0.88, 0.63-1.24, P=.475)
- 📊 pCR + sustained cCR 21.8% TNT vs 12.3% CRT (P=.002); cCR on re-evaluation 11.1% vs 4.4%
- ⚠️ Acute grade III-V toxicity 26.5% vs 12.6% (P<.001), driven by hematologic G3-4 15.8% vs 2.0%
- ⚠️ Preop full-dose completion 74.8% TNT vs 93.2% CRT (P<.001); RT itself was completed by every TNT pt without dose reduction
- 📊 Acute grade 3-4 toxicity during preoperative treatment, TNT vs CRT

  | Toxicity | TNT (n=298) | CRT (n=293) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hematologic | 47 (15.8) | 6 (2.0) |
  | Leukopenia | 17 (5.7) | 5 (1.7) |
  | Thrombocytopenia | 33 (11.1) | 2 (0.7) |
  | GI | 31 (10.4) | 22 (7.5) |
  | Proctitis | 14 (4.7) | 9 (3.1) |
  | Diarrhea | 19 (6.4) | 14 (4.8) |

- 🔗 vs RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23: those cut 3yr DM significantly, STELLAR and Polish II did not; STELLAR LRR 8.4% sits in the RAPIDO/PRODIGE range (4%-8.3%), well under Polish II (21%-22%)
- 🔗 Surgical morbidity was flat: grade III+ complications 14.0% vs 15.7% (P=.625); R0 91.5% vs 87.8% (P=.189)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (STELLAR)** — Jin, Jing; Tang, Yuan; Hu, Chen et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.21.01667](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.21.01667)
  > PURPOSE To ascertain if preoperative short-term radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is not inferior to a standard schedule of long-term chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients with distal or middle-third, clinical primary tumor stage 3-4 and/or regional lymph node–positive rectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) to short-term radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions over 1 week) followed by four cycles of chemotherapy (total neoadjuvant therapy [TNT]) or chemoradiotherapy (50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks, concurrently with capecitabine [chemoradiotherapy; CRT]). Total mesorectal excision was undertaken 6-8 weeks after preoperative treatment, with two additional cycles of CAPOX (intravenous oxaliplatin [130 mg/m 2 , once a day] on day 1 and capecitabine [1,000 mg/m 2 , twice a day] from days 1 to 14) in the TNT group and six cycles of CAPOX in the CRT group. The primary end point was 3-year disease-free survival (DFS). RESULTS Between August 2015 and August 2018, a total of 599 patients were randomly assigned to receive TNT (n = 302) or CRT (n = 297). At a median follow-up of 35.0 months, 3-year DFS was 64.5% and 62.3% in TNT and CRT groups, respectively (hazard ratio, 0.883; one-sided 95% CI, not applicable to 1.11; P &lt; .001 for noninferiority). There was no significant difference in metastasis-free survival or locoregional recurrence, but the TNT group had better 3-year overall survival than the CRT group (86.5% v 75.1%; P = .033). Treatment effects on DFS and overall survival were similar regardless of prognostic factors. The prevalence of acute grade III-V toxicities during preoperative treatment was 26.5% in the TNT group versus 12.6% in the CRT group ( P &lt; .001). CONCLUSION Short-term radiotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgery was efficacious with acceptable toxicity and could be used as an alternative to CRT for locally advanced rectal cancer.

### TNTCRT · [[NCT03177382]]

> 3yr DFS 74.8% vs 66.0%, HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 for doublet TNT with LCRT vs nCRT in high-risk LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Locoregional failure was 6.03% vs 6.19% (P=.943): intensifying concurrent chemo around an unchanged LCRT backbone bought nothing locally, and the entire DFS gain is metastatic (MFS 77.7% vs 67.6%). RT dose/fractionation are not in the source text. This is a chemo-sequencing decision, not an RT one.

- 🔍 Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, N=458 (232 doublet-LC TNT vs 226 nCRT); enrolled Jun 2017 to Dec 2023; median f/u 51mo
- 🔍 Eligibility: stage II/III LARC, age 18-70, ≥1 MRI high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, MRF involvement, or cT3c-d with EMVI)
- 🔍 Baseline high-risk burden: cT4 47.82%, cN2 75.98%, threatened MRF 70.96%, EMVI 54.80%
- 💊 Doublet arm sequencing: 1 cycle induction CAPOX → LCRT with 2 cycles concurrent CAPOX → 3 cycles consolidation CAPOX → TME
- 💊 Control arm: LCRT with concurrent capecitabine → TME → 6 cycles adjuvant CAPOX starting 4-8wk post-op
- ⚠️ RT dose, fractionation, and target volume not reported in source text; both arms used long-course RT, so the randomized variable is the concurrent/sequential chemo, not the RT
- 📊 Locoregional failure identical between arms: 6.03% vs 6.19%, P=.943; the DFS gain is distant-only (MFS 77.7% vs 67.6%, HR 0.655 [0.469-0.915])
- 📊 3yr OS 90.2% vs 87.5%, P=.167, not significant
- 🔍 ~87% (doublet) vs ~90% (nCRT) proceeded to TME
- ⚠️ Neoadjuvant-phase G3/4 AEs 27.59% vs 8.56%, P<.001, but whole-course severe toxicity 28.02% vs 24.32% (P=.371): the toxicity is front-loaded, not added
- ⚠️ Age capped at 70; benefit-risk in older pts untested (author-stated limitation)
- 🔗 Sits alongside RAPIDO, PRODIGE-23, STELLAR as TNT vs nCRT; no head-to-head vs short-course RT or non-doublet-concurrent TNT regimens

**Sources:**

  - 📎 [[papers/lower-gi/total-neoadjuvant-therapy-with-long-course-rt-and-uninterrupted.pdf|TNTCRT (full text)]]
- 📄 **Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial** — Wang, Xin; Tang, Yuanling; Lu, Junyang et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-03110](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-03110)
  > PURPOSE High-risk locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) carries a substantial risk of distant recurrence, which limits disease-free survival (DFS). We compared total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) integrating long-course radiotherapy (LCRT) with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy (doublet-LC TNT) versus conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT). METHODS In this multicenter, randomized, phase III trial, patients with stage II/III LARC and at least 1 high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, mesorectal fascia involvement, or cT3c-d with extramural vascular invasion) were enrolled. Patients were assigned to doublet-LC TNT (induction, concurrent, and consolidation capecitabine plus oxaliplatin with LCRT) before surgery or nCRT (capecitabine with LCRT) followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary end point was DFS (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: [[NCT03177382]] ). RESULTS Between June 6, 2017, and December 27, 2023, 458 patients were randomly assigned to doublet-LC TNT (n = 232) or nCRT (n = 226). At a median follow-up of 51 months, doublet-LC TNT improved 3-year DFS (74.8% v 66.0%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.674 [95% CI, 0.489 to 0.929]; P = .016). Metastasis-free survival (MFS; 77.7% v 67.6%; HR, 0.655 [95% CI, 0.469 to 0.915]) and pathologic complete response rates (pCR; 26.37% v 9.80%; P &lt; .001) were higher with doublet-LC TNT, whereas locoregional failure remained low and comparable (6.03% v 6.19%; P = .943). Although grade ≥3 adverse events during the neoadjuvant phase were more frequent with doublet-LC TNT (27.59% v 8.56%; P &lt; .001), severe toxicities during the entire treatment course (28.02% v 24.32%; P = .371) and major postoperative complications (3.98% v 2.94%; P = .567) were comparable. CONCLUSION Compared with conventional nCRT, doublet-LC TNT improved DFS, MFS, and pCR rates with manageable toxicity. These findings support this intensified, doublet-based regimen as a standard option within the modern TNT paradigm. Further comparative studies are warranted to evaluate these results against other short-course radiotherapy‑based or nondoublet-concurrent TNT regimens.

# 2026-08-07

**STELLAR: 5x5 Gy + CAPOX TNT non-inferior on 3-yr DFS (64.5% v 62.3%, HR 0.883) with 3-yr OS 86.5% v 75.1% (HR 0.67).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Two Chinese phase 3 TNT trials land the same week and split on where the gain comes from: STELLAR's 5x5 short-course buys OS (86.5% v 75.1%) with no LRR or DM signal behind it, while TNTCRT's long-course doublet buys distant control (MFS HR 0.655) with locoregional failure flat at ~6% in both arms. Lower GI carried the day; prostate was PSMA PET imaging, not therapy.

## 🌀 GI Lower

Both TNT trials leave locoregional control untouched vs nCRT, so the RT question is now sequencing and fractionation, not whether pelvic RT is doing the work.

### STELLAR · [[NCT02533271]]

> 3-yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3% (HR 0.883, NI margin 1.43) for 5x5 Gy + CAPOX vs 50 Gy/25f CRT in LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Every pt got IMRT, both arms, and 3-yr LRR was 8.4% with 5 Gy x5 alone, so the elective-nodal CTV (mesorectum, presacral, internal iliac, obturator, ischiorectal fossa) holds at a hypofractionated dose. The added grade 3-4 toxicity was hematologic (15.8% v 2.0%), from the CAPOX, not the pelvis: proctitis and diarrhea were flat.

- 🔍 Multicenter open-label phase III, 16 Chinese hospitals, N=599 randomized 1:1, ITT; median f/u 35.0 mo
- 🔍 cT3-4 and/or cN+ distal/middle-third rectal adenocarcinoma, age 18-70, ECOG 0-1, MRI-staged centrally by 3 radiologists
- 📐 NI margin prespecified at HR 1.43 (equivalently 3-yr DFS ≥54% assuming 65% in CRT arm), one-sided alpha 0.05, ≥194 DFS events
- 📊 3-yr efficacy, TNT (5 Gy x5 → 4 CAPOX) vs CRT (50 Gy/25f + capecitabine)

  | Endpoint | TNT | CRT | Effect size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | DFS (1°) | 64.5% (58.3-70.7) | 62.3% (56.1-68.5) | HR 0.883, one-sided 95% CI to 1.11, P<.001 NI |
  | OS | 86.5% (82.1-90.8) | 75.1% (69.4-80.8) | HR 0.67 (0.46-0.97), P=.033 |
  | MFS | 77.1% (71.7-82.6) | 75.3% (70.0-80.7) | HR 0.88 (0.63-1.24), P=.475 |
  | LRR | 8.4% (4.6-12.2) | 11.0% (6.5-15.5) | HR 0.80 (0.45-1.44), P=.461 |
  | pCR + sustained cCR | 21.8% | 12.3% | P=.002 |
  | R0 resection | 91.5% | 87.8% | P=.189 |

- 📐 All TNT pts completed 5 Gy x5 without dose reduction; CRT arm had 1.4% RT dose reduction, 2.4% interruption
- 🔍 IMRT in ALL pts, both arms: CTV mesorectum, presacral, internal iliac, obturator, ischiorectal fossa; external iliac only if cT4b; superior border sacral promontory, inferior 2-3 cm below tumor; PTV expansion 0.5-1.0 cm
- ⚠️ Acute grade 3-4 toxicity during preop treatment, 26.5% vs 12.6% (P<.001), driven by heme not bowel
  - Hematologic G3-4: 15.8% TNT vs 2.0% CRT (P<.001)
  - Thrombocytopenia G3-4: 11.1% vs 0.7% (P<.001)
  - Proctitis G3-4: 4.7% vs 3.1% (P=.307)
  - Diarrhea G3-4: 6.4% vs 4.8% (P=.398)
  - Grade III+ surgical complications: 14.0% vs 15.7% (P=.625)
- 📊 Preop full-dose completion 74.8% TNT vs 93.2% CRT (P<.001); adjuvant chemo completion 60.0% vs 48.3% (P=.009)
- 🔗 3-yr LRR 8.4%/11.0% in line with RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23 (4%-8.3%), far below Polish II (21%-22%), the only one of the four without mandated adjuvant chemo
- ⚠️ OS gain is a secondary endpoint on 107 cancer deaths, not powered for it; authors note Polish II's OS benefit vanished by 8 yr
- ⚠️ Unlike RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23, no 3-yr DM reduction with TNT (22.8% v 24.7%), so the OS signal has no MFS or LRR mechanism behind it
- ⚠️ Chinese-only cohort, age capped at 70 (median 55), middle/low rectum only; upper rectum untested

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (STELLAR)** — Jin, Jing; Tang, Yuan; Hu, Chen et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.21.01667](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.21.01667)
  > PURPOSE To ascertain if preoperative short-term radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is not inferior to a standard schedule of long-term chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients with distal or middle-third, clinical primary tumor stage 3-4 and/or regional lymph node–positive rectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) to short-term radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions over 1 week) followed by four cycles of chemotherapy (total neoadjuvant therapy [TNT]) or chemoradiotherapy (50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks, concurrently with capecitabine [chemoradiotherapy; CRT]). Total mesorectal excision was undertaken 6-8 weeks after preoperative treatment, with two additional cycles of CAPOX (intravenous oxaliplatin [130 mg/m 2 , once a day] on day 1 and capecitabine [1,000 mg/m 2 , twice a day] from days 1 to 14) in the TNT group and six cycles of CAPOX in the CRT group. The primary end point was 3-year disease-free survival (DFS). RESULTS Between August 2015 and August 2018, a total of 599 patients were randomly assigned to receive TNT (n = 302) or CRT (n = 297). At a median follow-up of 35.0 months, 3-year DFS was 64.5% and 62.3% in TNT and CRT groups, respectively (hazard ratio, 0.883; one-sided 95% CI, not applicable to 1.11; P &lt; .001 for noninferiority). There was no significant difference in metastasis-free survival or locoregional recurrence, but the TNT group had better 3-year overall survival than the CRT group (86.5% v 75.1%; P = .033). Treatment effects on DFS and overall survival were similar regardless of prognostic factors. The prevalence of acute grade III-V toxicities during preoperative treatment was 26.5% in the TNT group versus 12.6% in the CRT group ( P &lt; .001). CONCLUSION Short-term radiotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgery was efficacious with acceptable toxicity and could be used as an alternative to CRT for locally advanced rectal cancer.

> [!question] Open questions
> - Durability of the OS difference beyond 3 years
> - Late toxicity and quality of life after 5 Gy x 5 plus CAPOX
> - Whether more preoperative chemotherapy cycles reduce distant metastasis

### TNTCRT · [[NCT03177382]]

> 3-yr DFS 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674, for doublet-CAPOX TNT vs nCRT in high-risk LARC; locoregional failure unchanged.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Locoregional failure was identical (6.03% v 6.19%, P=.943) with the entire chemo course moved preoperatively, so intensifying systemic therapy around LCRT costs nothing in local control. RT dose, fractionation and target volume are absent from the source text, which gates transfer. The decision this moves is chemo sequencing, not RT technique.

- 📊 Efficacy by arm (3-yr rates unless noted)

  | Endpoint | Doublet-LC TNT | nCRT | Effect size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3-yr DFS (1° EP) | 74.8% | 66.0% | HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 |
  | 3-yr MFS | 77.7% | 67.6% | HR 0.655 (0.469-0.915), P=.013 |
  | pCR | 26.37% | 9.80% | P<.001 |
  | Locoregional failure | 6.03% | 6.19% | P=.943 |
  | 3-yr OS | 90.2% | 87.5% | P=.167 |

- 📊 Toxicity and surgical morbidity by arm

  | Event | Doublet-LC TNT | nCRT | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | G≥3 AE, neoadjuvant phase | 27.59% | 8.56% | <.001 |
  | Severe toxicity, entire course | 28.02% | 24.32% | .371 |
  | Major postoperative complications | 3.98% | 2.94% | .567 |

- 🔍 Phase 3, N=458 (232 v 226), accrued 2017-2023, median f/u 51 mo, age 18-70
- 💊 Regimen by arm
  - TNT: induction CAPOX x1 → LCRT + concurrent CAPOX x2 → consolidation CAPOX x3 → TME
  - nCRT: LCRT + concurrent capecitabine → TME → adjuvant CAPOX x6 at 4-8 wks
- 📐 LCRT in both arms; RT dose, fractionation, target volume not reported in source text
- 🔍 High-risk MRI features at baseline
  - cT4 47.82%
  - cN2 75.98%
  - Threatened mesorectal fascia 70.96%
  - EMVI 54.80%
- ⚠️ DFS gain rests on distant control; OS curves have not separated at 3 years
- ⚠️ Age capped at 70; author states benefit-risk above that needs further study
- 🔗 Source names RAPIDO, PRODIGE-23, STELLAR as TNT precedents; no head-to-head data

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial** — Wang, Xin; Tang, Yuanling; Lu, Junyang et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-03110](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-03110)
  > PURPOSE High-risk locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) carries a substantial risk of distant recurrence, which limits disease-free survival (DFS). We compared total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) integrating long-course radiotherapy (LCRT) with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy (doublet-LC TNT) versus conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT). METHODS In this multicenter, randomized, phase III trial, patients with stage II/III LARC and at least 1 high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, mesorectal fascia involvement, or cT3c-d with extramural vascular invasion) were enrolled. Patients were assigned to doublet-LC TNT (induction, concurrent, and consolidation capecitabine plus oxaliplatin with LCRT) before surgery or nCRT (capecitabine with LCRT) followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary end point was DFS (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: [[NCT03177382]] ). RESULTS Between June 6, 2017, and December 27, 2023, 458 patients were randomly assigned to doublet-LC TNT (n = 232) or nCRT (n = 226). At a median follow-up of 51 months, doublet-LC TNT improved 3-year DFS (74.8% v 66.0%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.674 [95% CI, 0.489 to 0.929]; P = .016). Metastasis-free survival (MFS; 77.7% v 67.6%; HR, 0.655 [95% CI, 0.469 to 0.915]) and pathologic complete response rates (pCR; 26.37% v 9.80%; P &lt; .001) were higher with doublet-LC TNT, whereas locoregional failure remained low and comparable (6.03% v 6.19%; P = .943). Although grade ≥3 adverse events during the neoadjuvant phase were more frequent with doublet-LC TNT (27.59% v 8.56%; P &lt; .001), severe toxicities during the entire treatment course (28.02% v 24.32%; P = .371) and major postoperative complications (3.98% v 2.94%; P = .567) were comparable. CONCLUSION Compared with conventional nCRT, doublet-LC TNT improved DFS, MFS, and pCR rates with manageable toxicity. These findings support this intensified, doublet-based regimen as a standard option within the modern TNT paradigm. Further comparative studies are warranted to evaluate these results against other short-course radiotherapy‑based or nondoublet-concurrent TNT regimens.

## 🌰 Prostate

Both entries are PSMA PET interpretation, not treatment: what a positive scan in BCR obligates, and whether uptake change can be read as response at all.

### PSMA PET BCR Natural History Study

> Baseline data on 130 PSMA-positive BCR men; most with PSADT >9-12mo still had PET findings, yet only ~1/3 started therapy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant read is that a positive PET at PSA >5 is near-universal even at PSADT >12mo, so PET positivity alone does not identify who needs metastasis-directed SBRT. Prostate bed recurrence was among the described sites, which reopens a salvage-RT target question in men already treated with salvage RT.

- 🔍 Prospective NCI natural-history cohort, N=130 at baseline; ongoing, median f/u ~2yr
- 🔍 Eligibility: post-definitive therapy BCR, PSA ≥0.5, salvage RT already given or declined
- 🔍 Imaging schedule: baseline PSMA PET; negative → annual, any finding (incl equivocal) → q6mo
- 📊 Majority with PSADT >9mo and >12mo had PSMA PET findings when PSA was above 5
- 📊 Sites of disease described as nodal, prostate bed recurrence, occasional small equivocal bone lesions
- 📊 Only about a third have gone on therapy so far; majority observed off treatment
- 📊 Progression on conventional imaging (companion Madan poster, ~1.5yr f/u)
  - 5 patients out of the first ~150 progressed on conventional imaging
  - Stated >95% had no abrupt PET change discordant from PSA kinetics
- 🔍 Therapy on study limited to courses <6mo: intermittent ADT, intermittent ARPI trials, Radium-223, SBRT allowed
- ⚠️ Designed pre-EMBARK, so the observation arm predates current BCR treatment norms
- ⚠️ Source is a conference interview transcript, not a peer-reviewed report; no CIs, no denominators for the PSADT strata
- ⚠️ Selection bias: NCI referral cohort, patients willing to be observed; not a consecutive registry

### USPCC 2026: PSMA PET Response Assessment

> n=16 prospective data: PSMA uptake change is prognostic (mOS 17.8 vs 37.2mo) but AR-regulated, so not a valid response measure.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Serial PSMA PET after starting abiraterone or enzalutamide reads AR signaling, not burden, so a post-treatment scan is not a response map. The stratification is real (median OS 17.8 vs 37.2 mo by DPSM) but prognostic, and RECIP 1.0 was built for radioligand therapy, not AR-axis agents.

- 📊 Rowe's thesis: PSMA expression is AR-regulated, so post-treatment uptake change may not track burden
- 🔍 Point-counterpoint talk, not new data; built on Zukotynski 2021, Markowski 2021, RECIP 1.0
- 📊 Time to therapy change and OS by PSMA PET response metric (Zukotynski, n=16)

  | Metric | Median time to therapy change | Median OS |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low DPSM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | 37.2 mo (95% CI 28.9-NA) |
  | High DPSM | 6.5 mo (95% CI 4.6-NA), p=0.0001 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.02 |
  | Low DASM | 12.2 mo (95% CI 11.3-NA) | NA (95% CI 37.2-NA) |
  | High DASM | 6.9 mo (95% CI 6.1-NA), p=0.003 | 17.8 mo (95% CI 13.9-NA), p=0.002 |

- 📐 OS by unfavorable DPSM: HR 5.03 (95% CI 1.17-21.65)
- 📐 OS by unfavorable DASM: HR 9.31 (95% CI 1.81-47.87)
- ⚠️ Only 16 evaluable pts; HR CIs run 1.17-21.65 and 1.81-47.87, magnitude unstable
- 💊 BAT series: 3 of 6 men progressed on PSMA PET at 3mo, before conventional imaging
- 🔍 Repeatability assessed with Bland-Altman, ICC, within-subject CoV across SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVtotal, tumor volume
- 🔗 RECIP 1.0 grades PD/PR/SD/CR by combining PSMA PET with CT, designed for radioligand therapy

# 2026-08-07

**TNTCRT: 3yr DFS 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929) for doublet-LC TNT, but locoregional failure identical (6.03% v 6.19%).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Rectal owned the day, and both trials say the same thing to a rad onc: the RT question is settled, the sequencing question isn't. TNTCRT moved DFS entirely through distant control with LRR flat; STELLAR showed 5x5Gy IMRT non-inferior to 50Gy/25fx (3y LRR 8.4% v 11.0%) with toxicity driven by chemo, not RT. Prostate stayed imaging-only: PSMA PET still not a validated response biomarker.

## 🌰 Prostate

Both items are PSMA PET interpretation, not therapy: androgen-axis regulation of PSMA expression and BCR natural history off treatment.

### USPCC 2026 Point-Counterpoint: PSMA PET as Response Assessment

> Conference point-counterpoint arguing PSMA uptake tracks AR signaling, not tumor burden, so serial PSMA PET is not yet a response biomarker.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For an RT reader the operative caution is oligomet SBRT selection: if a post-ADT scan drives whether to treat 3 lesions or 5, ARSI-induced uptake change can move lesion count without moving disease. RECIP 1.0 was built for post-radioligand response, not for pre-SBRT target selection.

- 🔍 USPCC 2026 point-counterpoint session (Steven Rowe, UT Southwestern); position: PSMA PET has no present role in response assessment
- 🔍 Core argument: PSMA expression is dynamically regulated by the androgen axis, not a static marker of tumor burden
- 📊 Zukotynski 18F-DCFPyL trial (n=16 evaluable, pre-ARSI and 2-4 mo post): median time to therapy change 9.6 mo (95% CI 6.9-14.2)
- 📊 Same cohort: median OS 28.6 mo (95% CI 18.3-NA)
- 📊 Uptake-change metrics stratify outcomes (Zukotynski, 18F-DCFPyL pre/post ARSI)

  | Metric | Median time to therapy change | Median OS | HR |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Low DPSM | 12.2 mo (11.3-NA) | 37.2 mo (28.9-NA) | 5.03 (1.17-21.65) |
  | High DPSM | 6.5 mo (4.6-NA), p=0.0001 | 17.8 mo (13.9-NA), p=0.02 | same comparison |
  | Low DASM | 12.2 mo (11.3-NA) | NA (37.2-NA) | 9.31 (1.81-47.87) |
  | High DASM | 6.9 mo (6.1-NA), p=0.003 | 17.8 mo (13.9-NA), p=0.002 | same comparison |

- ⚠️ Both HR CIs are enormous (1.17-21.65 and 1.81-47.87) off 16 pts; prognostic separation, not a validated response metric
- 📊 Markowski BAT series: 6 men imaged at baseline and 3 mo, 3 progressed; PSMA PET flagged progression earlier than conventional imaging
- ⚠️ Flare vs true progression is unresolved: ADT/ARSI initiation itself moves PSMA expression, so an early rising SUV is uninterpretable as burden
- 🔍 Repeatability data reviewed (Bland-Altman, ICC, within-subject CV) across SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVtotal, tumor volume; lesion identification and segmentation variability flagged as limiting serial quantitation
- 🔗 RECIP 1.0 (Gafita, JNM 2022) offered as the counterweight: PET plus CT categories (PD/PR/SD/CR) built for post-radioligand response, still needing validation outside that setting
- ❓ Take-home: kinetics of androgen-axis effects on PSMA and AI-assisted lesion detection are the named prerequisites

### PSMA-PET BCR Natural History Study

> Baseline of first 130 PSMA-positive BCR men: most with PSA-DT >9-12mo still show PET lesions, yet ~2/3 observed off therapy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant read is that these men are all post-salvage-RT failures, so PET-detected nodal and prostate-bed lesions land squarely in the metastasis-directed-therapy decision. SBRT was permitted, not protocolized, and no per-lesion local control is reported in source, so this bounds the observation arm rather than defining an SBRT indication.

- 🔍 Prospective NCI natural-history cohort; baseline data on first 130 pts, study ongoing, median f/u ~2 yrs
- 🔍 Eligibility: BCR after definitive therapy, all post-salvage RT or declined it; PSA ≥0.5 required for entry
- 🔍 Imaging schedule: baseline PSMA PET; negative → annual, any finding (incl. equivocal) → q6mo
- 📊 Only ~1/3 of pts have gone on therapy; the majority are observed off treatment
- 📊 Vast majority of pts with PSA-DT >9mo and >12mo had PSMA PET findings when PSA >5
- 📊 Sites described: nodal disease, prostate-bed recurrence, occasional small equivocal bone lesions
- 📊 Companion Madan poster: conventional-imaging progression in 5 of the first ~150 pts at ~1.5yr f/u
- 📊 >95% had no abrupt PET change discordant with PSA/PSA-DT behavior
- ⚠️ Baseline cross-sectional read only; no per-arm outcomes, no HR, no effect size reported in source
- ⚠️ Therapy not mandated: intermittent ADT, ARPI, SBRT, Radium-223 trials all permitted, so any evolution signal is confounded
- ⚠️ Single-center NCI referral cohort; enrichment for trial-seeking pts limits generalizability
- ⚠️ Source is a conference video interview, not a peer-reviewed report; numbers are spoken approximations

## 🌀 GI Lower

Two phase III LARC trials, both keeping RT constant within arm: TNTCRT tests the chemo backbone under LCRT, STELLAR tests 5x5Gy against 50Gy/25fx, and neither moves locoregional failure.

### TNTCRT · [[NCT03177382]]

> 3yr DFS 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 for doublet-LC TNT over nCRT in high-risk LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Locoregional failure was identical (6.03% v 6.19%, P=.943) while MFS moved (77.7% v 67.6%), so intensifying the concurrent and sandwich chemo buys distant control, not local control, on an unchanged LCRT backbone. RT dose and target volume are not in the source text, which limits transfer. The decision this moves is chemo intensity around RT, not the RT prescription.

- 🔍 Phase 3 multicenter RCT, N=458 (232 doublet-LC TNT v 226 nCRT), enrolled Jun 2017 to Dec 2023, median f/u 51mo
- 🔍 Eligibility: stage II/III LARC, age 18-70, ≥1 MRI high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, MRF+, or cT3c-d with EMVI)
- 🔍 High-risk enrichment real: cT4 47.82%, cN2 75.98%, threatened MRF 70.96%, EMVI 54.80%
- 💊 Experimental: 1 cycle induction CAPOX → LCRT with 2 cycles concurrent CAPOX → 3 cycles consolidation CAPOX → TME
- 💊 Control: LCRT with concurrent capecitabine → TME → 6 cycles adjuvant CAPOX starting 4-8wk post-op
- ⚠️ RT dose/fractionation and target volume not reported in source text; both arms got LCRT, so the trial isolates the chemo backbone, not the RT question
- 📊 Efficacy and safety, doublet-LC TNT v nCRT

  | Endpoint | Doublet-LC TNT | nCRT | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3yr DFS (1°) | 74.8% | 66.0% | HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 |
  | 3yr MFS | 77.7% | 67.6% | HR 0.655 (0.469-0.915) |
  | pCR | 26.37% | 9.80% | P<.001 |
  | Locoregional failure | 6.03% | 6.19% | P=.943 |
  | 3yr OS | 90.2% | 87.5% | P=.167 |
  | G3+ AE, neoadjuvant phase | 27.59% | 8.56% | P<.001 |
  | G3+ AE, entire course | 28.02% | 24.32% | P=.371 |
  | Major postop complications | 3.98% | 2.94% | P=.567 |

- 📊 TME rate ~87% doublet-LC TNT v ~90% nCRT after neoadjuvant therapy
- 🔗 Sits alongside RAPIDO, PRODIGE-23, STELLAR; unlike RAPIDO/STELLAR this keeps LCRT rather than short-course, so the LCRT-v-SCRT question stays open
- ⚠️ All benefit is distant: MFS moved, locoregional failure did not (6.03% v 6.19%), and OS is not separated at 51mo
- ⚠️ Age cap 70; benefit-risk in older pts untested per the authors

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial** — Wang, Xin; Tang, Yuanling; Lu, Junyang et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-03110](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-03110)
  > PURPOSE High-risk locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) carries a substantial risk of distant recurrence, which limits disease-free survival (DFS). We compared total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) integrating long-course radiotherapy (LCRT) with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy (doublet-LC TNT) versus conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT). METHODS In this multicenter, randomized, phase III trial, patients with stage II/III LARC and at least 1 high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, mesorectal fascia involvement, or cT3c-d with extramural vascular invasion) were enrolled. Patients were assigned to doublet-LC TNT (induction, concurrent, and consolidation capecitabine plus oxaliplatin with LCRT) before surgery or nCRT (capecitabine with LCRT) followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary end point was DFS (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: [[NCT03177382]] ). RESULTS Between June 6, 2017, and December 27, 2023, 458 patients were randomly assigned to doublet-LC TNT (n = 232) or nCRT (n = 226). At a median follow-up of 51 months, doublet-LC TNT improved 3-year DFS (74.8% v 66.0%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.674 [95% CI, 0.489 to 0.929]; P = .016). Metastasis-free survival (MFS; 77.7% v 67.6%; HR, 0.655 [95% CI, 0.469 to 0.915]) and pathologic complete response rates (pCR; 26.37% v 9.80%; P &lt; .001) were higher with doublet-LC TNT, whereas locoregional failure remained low and comparable (6.03% v 6.19%; P = .943). Although grade ≥3 adverse events during the neoadjuvant phase were more frequent with doublet-LC TNT (27.59% v 8.56%; P &lt; .001), severe toxicities during the entire treatment course (28.02% v 24.32%; P = .371) and major postoperative complications (3.98% v 2.94%; P = .567) were comparable. CONCLUSION Compared with conventional nCRT, doublet-LC TNT improved DFS, MFS, and pCR rates with manageable toxicity. These findings support this intensified, doublet-based regimen as a standard option within the modern TNT paradigm. Further comparative studies are warranted to evaluate these results against other short-course radiotherapy‑based or nondoublet-concurrent TNT regimens.

### STELLAR · [[NCT02533271]]

> 3y DFS 64.5% vs 62.3%, HR 0.883 (upper bound 1.11 < 1.43 margin): 5x5Gy + CAPOX noninferior to long-course CRT in LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> For an RT reader the real finding is delivery, not survival: every TNT pt finished 5Gy x5 with no dose reduction vs 1.4% reduced and 2.4% interrupted on 50Gy/25fx, and 3y LRR stayed at 8.4% with IMRT and standard mesorectal plus internal iliac coverage. The excess grade 3-4 toxicity was hematologic (15.8% v 2.0%), so shortening the RT course costs one week of clinic time, not local control.

- 🔍 Multicenter open-label phase III, 16 Chinese hospitals, N=599 (TNT 302 / CRT 297), median f/u 35.0mo
- 🔍 cT3-4 and/or cN+ distal or middle-third rectal ca; 85.8% vs 83.5% stage III; MRF+ 56% both arms
- 📐 RT technique: all pts IMRT. TNT 5Gy x5; CRT 50Gy/25fx with concurrent capecitabine 825mg/m² BID
- 📐 CTV covered mesorectum, presacral space, internal iliac + obturator nodes, ischiorectal fossa; external iliac only if cT4b; superior border sacral promontory, inferior 2-3cm below tumor; CTV→PTV 0.5-1.0cm
- 📊 3y LRR 8.4% (95% CI 4.6-12.2) TNT vs 11.0% (6.5-15.5) CRT, HR 0.80 (0.45-1.44), p=.461
- 📊 3y MFS 77.1% vs 75.3%, HR 0.88 (0.63-1.24), p=.475: no distant-metastasis gain, unlike RAPIDO/PRODIGE 23
- 📊 3y OS 86.5% vs 75.1%, HR 0.67 (0.46-0.97), p=.033 (secondary EP, open-label, unadjusted for multiplicity)
- ⚠️ OS gain is the weak link: no MFS or LRR difference to explain it, and Polish II's early OS benefit vanished at 8y
- 📊 pCR + sustained cCR 21.8% TNT vs 12.3% CRT, p=.002; cCR 11.1% vs 4.4%, organ preservation without surgery 9.4% vs 3.4%
- 📊 RT delivery favored short-course: all TNT pts completed 5Gy x5 without dose reduction vs 1.4% dose reduction and 2.4% interruption in CRT
- ⚠️ Preop grade III-V toxicity 26.5% vs 12.6% (p<.001), driven by hematologic 15.8% vs 2.0%: the cost is chemo, not RT
- 🔗 3y LRR (8.4%/11.0%) in line with RAPIDO and PRODIGE 23 (4%-8.3%) and far below Polish II (21%-22%), where all pts got IMRT here
- ⚠️ Preop completion 82.6% vs 95.2% and full-dose 74.8% vs 93.2% (both p<.001): the TNT advantage was postop, 60.0% vs 48.3% completing planned adjuvant cycles (p=.009)
- ⚠️ Chinese-only population, age 18-70, median 55: younger than a typical Western LARC cohort
- 📊 Grade 3-4 acute toxicity during preoperative treatment

  | Toxicity | TNT (n=298) | CRT (n=293) | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Hematologic | 47 (15.8) | 6 (2.0) | <.001 |
  | Leukopenia | 17 (5.7) | 5 (1.7) | .01 |
  | Thrombocytopenia | 33 (11.1) | 2 (0.7) | <.001 |
  | GI | 31 (10.4) | 22 (7.5) | .223 |
  | Proctitis | 14 (4.7) | 9 (3.1) | .307 |
  | Diarrhea | 19 (6.4) | 14 (4.8) | .398 |

- 📐 OS subgroup HRs favoring TNT (Fig 3), the only ones reaching significance

  | Subgroup | OS HR | 95% CI | P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | cT4 | 0.362 | 0.152 to 0.859 | .021 |
  | Distance to anal verge ≤5 cm | 0.540 | 0.318 to 0.916 | .022 |
  | cT2-3 | 0.752 | 0.493 to 1.149 | .187 |
  | Distance to anal verge >5 cm | 0.808 | 0.468 to 1.394 | .443 |

- ⚠️ Those two OS subgroups are unadjusted post-hoc splits of an already-secondary endpoint; DFS was flat in both (cT4 HR 0.621, ≤5cm HR 0.706)
- ⚠️ Surgical burden similar: grade III+ complications 14.0% vs 15.7% (p=.625); R0 91.5% vs 87.8% (p=.189)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (STELLAR)** — Jin, Jing; Tang, Yuan; Hu, Chen et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.21.01667](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.21.01667)
  > PURPOSE To ascertain if preoperative short-term radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is not inferior to a standard schedule of long-term chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients with distal or middle-third, clinical primary tumor stage 3-4 and/or regional lymph node–positive rectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) to short-term radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions over 1 week) followed by four cycles of chemotherapy (total neoadjuvant therapy [TNT]) or chemoradiotherapy (50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks, concurrently with capecitabine [chemoradiotherapy; CRT]). Total mesorectal excision was undertaken 6-8 weeks after preoperative treatment, with two additional cycles of CAPOX (intravenous oxaliplatin [130 mg/m 2 , once a day] on day 1 and capecitabine [1,000 mg/m 2 , twice a day] from days 1 to 14) in the TNT group and six cycles of CAPOX in the CRT group. The primary end point was 3-year disease-free survival (DFS). RESULTS Between August 2015 and August 2018, a total of 599 patients were randomly assigned to receive TNT (n = 302) or CRT (n = 297). At a median follow-up of 35.0 months, 3-year DFS was 64.5% and 62.3% in TNT and CRT groups, respectively (hazard ratio, 0.883; one-sided 95% CI, not applicable to 1.11; P &lt; .001 for noninferiority). There was no significant difference in metastasis-free survival or locoregional recurrence, but the TNT group had better 3-year overall survival than the CRT group (86.5% v 75.1%; P = .033). Treatment effects on DFS and overall survival were similar regardless of prognostic factors. The prevalence of acute grade III-V toxicities during preoperative treatment was 26.5% in the TNT group versus 12.6% in the CRT group ( P &lt; .001). CONCLUSION Short-term radiotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgery was efficacious with acceptable toxicity and could be used as an alternative to CRT for locally advanced rectal cancer.

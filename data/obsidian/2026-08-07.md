# 2026-08-07

**TNTCRT: 3yr DFS 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929) for doublet-LC TNT, with LRR ~6% in both arms so the gain is chemo, not RT.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Rectal owns the day, and both TNT trials leave the RT question open in different ways: TNTCRT holds LCRT constant across arms (locoregional failure 6.03% v 6.19%), so its DFS/MFS win is chemo intensity and sequencing; STELLAR swaps 50 Gy/25 fx for 5x5Gy and clears DFS noninferiority (HR 0.883) at double the acute G3-5 toxicity (26.5% v 12.6%). Prostate contributes imaging-only signal: PSMA PET response and BCR natural-history data are prognostic, not yet a trigger for salvage RT timing.

## 🌰 Prostate

Both entries are PSMA PET imaging arguments rather than treatment trials; neither yet tells you when to fire salvage or metastasis-directed RT.

### USPCC 2026 Point-Counterpoint: PSMA PET as Response Assessment

> Point-counterpoint arguing PSMA uptake is AR-regulated, so post-treatment change may track signaling, not tumor burden.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> PSMA uptake falling after ADT or an ARSI can reflect AR signaling change rather than tumor kill, which matters wherever a post-treatment PSMA PET drives an RT decision: metastasis-directed SBRT target selection, or calling in-field response after treatment. Timing relative to systemic therapy start is the gating variable, and RECIP 1.0 is validated for radioligand therapy, not for RT response.

- 🔍 USPCC 2026 point-counterpoint session, Steven Rowe (UT Southwestern); position: PSMA PET has no role at present as response assessment
- 🔍 Core argument: PSMA expression is dynamically regulated by the androgen axis, not a static marker of tumor burden
- 📊 Zukotynski J Nucl Med 2021: prospective single-arm 18F-DCFPyL PET/CT before and 2-4mo after abiraterone/enzalutamide, 16 evaluable pts
- 📊 Cohort-wide median time to therapy change 9.6mo (95% CI 6.9-14.2); median OS 28.6mo (95% CI 18.3-NA)
- 📊 Zukotynski outcomes by δ-SUVmax response pattern (low vs high)

  | Metric | Low DPSM | High DPSM | Low DASM | High DASM |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Median time to therapy change | 12.2mo (11.3-NA) | 6.5mo (4.6-NA), p=0.0001 | 12.2mo (11.3-NA) | 6.9mo (6.1-NA), p=0.003 |
  | Median OS | 37.2mo (28.9-NA) | 17.8mo (13.9-NA), p=0.02 | NA (37.2-NA) | 17.8mo (13.9-NA), p=0.002 |
  | HR | ref | 5.03 (1.17-21.65) | ref | 9.31 (1.81-47.87) |

- ⚠️ n=16 evaluable, single-arm, wide CIs (DASM HR 9.31, 1.81-47.87); prognostic separation, not validated response criteria
- 📊 Markowski J Nucl Med 2021: 6 men on bipolar androgen therapy, PSMA PET at baseline + 3mo; 3 progressed on imaging, detected earlier than conventional imaging
- 🔍 Repeatability data reviewed (Bland-Altman, ICC, within-subject CV) across SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVtotal, tumor volume; no repeatability values given in source
- 🔗 RECIP 1.0 (Gafita, J Nucl Med 2022) named as the emerging PSMA PET response framework for radioligand therapy, integrating PET with CT; presenter flags it as needing further validation before routine use

### PSMA-Positive BCR Natural History Study

> Baseline data, first 130 pts: most BCR men with PSA DT >9-12mo still show PSMA-PET findings at PSA >5.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant read is that prostate-bed recurrence and nodal disease turn up on PET in men with PSA DT >9-12mo once PSA exceeds 5, the exact group historically watched. That reframes who gets referred for metastasis-directed SBRT versus observed, but with only a baseline snapshot the cohort cannot yet say which PET pattern justifies treating.

- 🔍 Prospective NCI natural-history cohort; PSMA-positive BCR after definitive therapy incl. salvage RT (or declined it)
- 🔍 Eligibility gate: PSA ≥0.5 for baseline PET; negative baseline PET → annual imaging, any finding (even equivocal) → q6mo
- 📊 Baseline read of first 130 pts: PSMA-PET findings vs PSA doubling time at a single timepoint
- 📊 Vast majority of slow-DT pts (PSA DT >9mo, >12mo) had PSMA-PET findings once PSA >5
- 📊 Sites described: nodal disease, prostate-bed recurrence, small questionable bone lesions
- 💊 Therapy investigator-choice, not mandated; anything <6mo allowed (intermittent ADT, SBRT, intermittent ARPI, Radium-223 trials)
- 📊 Only ~1/3 have gone on therapy so far; majority observed off treatment
- 📊 Companion analysis (Madan, ~1.5yr f/u, first ~150 pts): 5 with conventional-imaging progression; >95% without abrupt PET change beyond what PSA DT predicted
- ⚠️ Baseline cross-sectional data only; median f/u ~2yr, interim outcome data not yet reported
- ⚠️ No effect size, no HR, no comparator reported in source; percentages other than ">95%" and "about a third" not quantified
- ⚠️ Single-institution NCI cohort with trial-enriched population (concurrent intermittent-therapy protocols) limits generalizability

## 🌀 GI Lower

Two phase III TNT trials from the same era, one keeping long-course CRT and intensifying chemo, one shortening RT to 5x5Gy: local control is already ~94% in both, so RT's remaining lever is fractionation and toxicity, not dose.

### TNTCRT · [[NCT03177382]]

> 3yr DFS 74.8% v 66.0%, HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 favoring doublet-LC TNT over nCRT in high-risk LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Locoregional failure was 6.03% v 6.19% with LCRT in both arms, so the RT question this trial answers is negative by design: intensifying chemotherapy around a fixed long-course backbone buys distant control (MFS 77.7% v 67.6%), not local. It moves the sequencing decision (consolidation CAPOX before surgery, not adjuvant after), not dose or target volume. RT dose and fractionation are not in the source text.

- 🔍 Phase III multicenter RCT, N=458 (232 doublet-LC TNT v 226 nCRT), enrolled 6/2017-12/2023, median f/u 51mo
- 🔍 Eligibility: stage II/III LARC, age 18-70, ≥1 MRI high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, MRF+, or cT3c-d with EMVI)
- 💊 TNT arm: 1 cycle induction CAPOX → LCRT with 2 cycles concurrent CAPOX → 3 cycles consolidation CAPOX → surgery
- 💊 Control: LCRT with concurrent capecitabine → surgery → 6 cycles adjuvant CAPOX starting 4-8wk post-op
- 📊 Efficacy, doublet-LC TNT v nCRT

  | Endpoint | Doublet-LC TNT | nCRT | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3yr DFS (1°) | 74.8% | 66.0% | HR 0.674 (0.489-0.929), P=.016 |
  | 3yr MFS | 77.7% | 67.6% | HR 0.655 (0.469-0.915), P=.013 |
  | pCR | 26.37% | 9.80% | P<.001 |
  | Locoregional failure | 6.03% | 6.19% | P=.943 |
  | 3yr OS | 90.2% | 87.5% | P=.167 |

- 📐 Both arms got LCRT; RT is constant across arms, so the delta is chemo intensity + sequencing, not radiation
- 📊 Toxicity + surgical outcomes
  - G≥3 AEs, neoadjuvant phase: 27.59% v 8.56%, P<.001
  - Severe toxicity, entire course: 28.02% v 24.32%, P=.371
  - Major postop complications: 3.98% v 2.94%, P=.567
  - TME rate: ~87% TNT v ~90% nCRT
- ⚠️ Locoregional failure ~6% in both arms: no local-control headroom left for RT intensification here
- ⚠️ Age capped at 70; per the authors, benefit-risk in older pts is unstudied
- ⚠️ OS not significant at 51mo (90.2% v 87.5%, P=.167); DFS/MFS gain has not translated to survival yet
- 🔗 vs RAPIDO / STELLAR (SCRT-based TNT) and PRODIGE-23 (FOLFIRINOX induction): this is LCRT + uninterrupted CAPOX, not tested head-to-head against either
- 🔍 RT dose, fractionation, and target volume not reported in source text

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial** — Wang, Xin; Tang, Yuanling; Lu, Junyang et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-07). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-03110](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-03110)
  > PURPOSE High-risk locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) carries a substantial risk of distant recurrence, which limits disease-free survival (DFS). We compared total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) integrating long-course radiotherapy (LCRT) with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy (doublet-LC TNT) versus conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT). METHODS In this multicenter, randomized, phase III trial, patients with stage II/III LARC and at least 1 high-risk feature (cT4a-b, cN2, mesorectal fascia involvement, or cT3c-d with extramural vascular invasion) were enrolled. Patients were assigned to doublet-LC TNT (induction, concurrent, and consolidation capecitabine plus oxaliplatin with LCRT) before surgery or nCRT (capecitabine with LCRT) followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary end point was DFS (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: [[NCT03177382]] ). RESULTS Between June 6, 2017, and December 27, 2023, 458 patients were randomly assigned to doublet-LC TNT (n = 232) or nCRT (n = 226). At a median follow-up of 51 months, doublet-LC TNT improved 3-year DFS (74.8% v 66.0%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.674 [95% CI, 0.489 to 0.929]; P = .016). Metastasis-free survival (MFS; 77.7% v 67.6%; HR, 0.655 [95% CI, 0.469 to 0.915]) and pathologic complete response rates (pCR; 26.37% v 9.80%; P &lt; .001) were higher with doublet-LC TNT, whereas locoregional failure remained low and comparable (6.03% v 6.19%; P = .943). Although grade ≥3 adverse events during the neoadjuvant phase were more frequent with doublet-LC TNT (27.59% v 8.56%; P &lt; .001), severe toxicities during the entire treatment course (28.02% v 24.32%; P = .371) and major postoperative complications (3.98% v 2.94%; P = .567) were comparable. CONCLUSION Compared with conventional nCRT, doublet-LC TNT improved DFS, MFS, and pCR rates with manageable toxicity. These findings support this intensified, doublet-based regimen as a standard option within the modern TNT paradigm. Further comparative studies are warranted to evaluate these results against other short-course radiotherapy‑based or nondoublet-concurrent TNT regimens.

### STELLAR

> 3yr DFS 64.5% vs 62.3% (HR 0.883) noninferior for 5x5Gy + CAPOX TNT vs long-course CRT in LARC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT read is fractionation, not sequence: 25 Gy/5 fx followed by four cycles of CAPOX held 3yr DFS against 50 Gy/25 fx with concurrent capecitabine, with no difference in locoregional recurrence. That collapses 5 weeks of daily treatment into 1 week for cT3-4/N+ mid-to-low rectum. The cost is acute G3-5 toxicity at 26.5% vs 12.6%.

- 🔍 Phase III multicenter RCT, N=599 (TNT 302 / CRT 297), 1:1, China, accrual Aug 2015-Aug 2018
- 🔍 Eligibility: distal/middle-third rectal adenoca, cT3-4 and/or N+, no mets, age 18-70, ECOG 0-1
- 📐 RT: 25 Gy/5 fx over 1 week (TNT) vs 50 Gy/25 fx over 5 weeks with concurrent capecitabine (CRT)
- 🔍 TME 6-8 weeks after preop treatment in both arms; chemo load shifted preop in TNT (4 cycles CAPOX preop + 2 postop) vs 6 cycles postop in CRT
- 📊 3yr outcomes, TNT vs CRT (median f/u 35.0 mo)

  | Endpoint | TNT | CRT | Effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3yr DFS (1°) | 64.5% | 62.3% | HR 0.883, one-sided 95% CI to 1.11, P<.001 noninferiority |
  | 3yr OS | 86.5% | 75.1% | P=.033 |
  | MFS | not reported in source | not reported in source | no significant difference |
  | Locoregional recurrence | not reported in source | not reported in source | no significant difference |
  | Acute G3-5 tox (preop) | 26.5% | 12.6% | P<.001 |

- ⚠️ OS was secondary and unpowered; an 11.4-point 3yr OS gap without an MFS or LRR difference is biologically hard to explain
- ⚠️ DFS noninferiority margin passed on a one-sided CI upper bound of 1.11, so a real DFS decrement of up to ~11% is not excluded
- ⚠️ Acute G3-5 toxicity roughly doubled with TNT (26.5% vs 12.6%), all of it front-loaded before surgery
- 🔗 vs RAPIDO (5x5Gy + CAPOX/FOLFOX TNT): RAPIDO improved distant-metastasis-free survival but not OS or locoregional control; STELLAR is the mirror image, OS-positive with no MFS or LRR signal
- 🔗 vs PRODIGE 23 (induction FOLFIRINOX then long-course CRT): also DFS-positive, but keeps the 50 Gy/25 fx backbone rather than replacing it with 5x5Gy

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial of Short-Term Radiotherapy Plus Chemotherapy Versus Long-Term Chemoradiotherapy in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (STELLAR)** — Jin, Jing; Tang, Yuan; Hu, Chen et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2022-05). [doi:10.1200/jco.21.01667](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.21.01667)
  > PURPOSE To ascertain if preoperative short-term radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy is not inferior to a standard schedule of long-term chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients with distal or middle-third, clinical primary tumor stage 3-4 and/or regional lymph node–positive rectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) to short-term radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions over 1 week) followed by four cycles of chemotherapy (total neoadjuvant therapy [TNT]) or chemoradiotherapy (50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks, concurrently with capecitabine [chemoradiotherapy; CRT]). Total mesorectal excision was undertaken 6-8 weeks after preoperative treatment, with two additional cycles of CAPOX (intravenous oxaliplatin [130 mg/m 2 , once a day] on day 1 and capecitabine [1,000 mg/m 2 , twice a day] from days 1 to 14) in the TNT group and six cycles of CAPOX in the CRT group. The primary end point was 3-year disease-free survival (DFS). RESULTS Between August 2015 and August 2018, a total of 599 patients were randomly assigned to receive TNT (n = 302) or CRT (n = 297). At a median follow-up of 35.0 months, 3-year DFS was 64.5% and 62.3% in TNT and CRT groups, respectively (hazard ratio, 0.883; one-sided 95% CI, not applicable to 1.11; P &lt; .001 for noninferiority). There was no significant difference in metastasis-free survival or locoregional recurrence, but the TNT group had better 3-year overall survival than the CRT group (86.5% v 75.1%; P = .033). Treatment effects on DFS and overall survival were similar regardless of prognostic factors. The prevalence of acute grade III-V toxicities during preoperative treatment was 26.5% in the TNT group versus 12.6% in the CRT group ( P &lt; .001). CONCLUSION Short-term radiotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy followed by surgery was efficacious with acceptable toxicity and could be used as an alternative to CRT for locally advanced rectal cancer.

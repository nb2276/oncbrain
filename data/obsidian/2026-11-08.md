# 2026-11-08

**PMRT ASTRO-ASCO-SSO guideline: PMRT recommended for pN1 (Strong/High) and post-NST cT4/cN2-3 or ypN+, conditional for ypN0 after cT1-3N1/cT3N0, moderate hypofx preferred.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Breast carried the day: two joint guidelines set PMRT indications by pN and post-NST ypN status and keep RT open to BRCA1/2 carriers, with intact-breast RT contraindicated only for germline TP53. HyTEC models anchor vestibular schwannoma SRS (12 Gy x1 → 3-5y TCP 91.2% or 95.6%) and adrenal SBRT (1-yr LC >95% at BED10 116.4 Gy).

## 🧠 CNS

HyTEC TCP modeling for vestibular schwannoma SRS alongside a LINAC planning comparison of DCA variants vs VMAT.

### SRS for Vestibular Schwannomas: TCP Analyses (HyTEC)

> Pooled model of 35 series and 5162 pts: 12 Gy single-fraction predicts 3-5y TCP of 91.2% (LQ) or 95.6% (LQ-L). No outcome data below 11 Gy.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The two models disagree on α/β (12.4 vs 2.97 Gy), which changes how fSRS ranks: LQ puts 18 Gy/3fx (93.6%) above 12 Gy x1 (91.2%), while LQ-L puts it below (94.0% vs 95.6%). Calling fractionation 'equivalent' therefore depends on the model chosen. Single-fraction dose reduction below 11 Gy has no outcome data behind it.

- 🔍 HyTEC pooled TCP model: tumor control data from 35 publications, 5162 pts
- 🔍 3y control taken from 21 articles (60%), 5y from 14 (40%)
- 📊 3-5y control >90% to 95% in 89% of reviewed studies. Only 4 studies, typically smaller, reported 80% to 90%
- 📊 Model-predicted 3-5y TCP by regimen (TCP at 0 Gy set to 30%)

  | Regimen | LQ (α/β 12.4) | LQ-L (α/β 2.97) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10 Gy x1 | 85.0% | 89.7% |
  | 11 Gy x1 | 88.4% | 93.1% |
  | 12 Gy x1 | 91.2% | 95.6% |
  | 13 Gy x1 | 93.5% | 97.33% |
  | 18 Gy/3 fx | 93.6% | 94.0% |
  | 25 Gy/5 fx | 97.2% | 96.9% |

- 📐 α/β 12.4 Gy (95% CI 9.0-19.3) with LQ vs 2.97 Gy (95% CI 1.72-4.27) with LQ-L
- 📐 LQ-L gave a marginally better fit with tighter CIs. Both fits P < .0001
- ⚠️ No outcome data below 11 Gy single-fraction equivalent, so lower-dose TCP is extrapolation
- ⚠️ Only 46% of articles gave KM estimates. The rest used crude control at median f/u
- ⚠️ Prescription dose assumed to be the minimum GTV dose. PTV margin and dose heterogeneity ignored
- ⚠️ NF2 excluded. NF2 TCP is approximately 75% at 5y per a cited systematic review
- ⚠️ Few series have f/u beyond 10y, so durability is unconfirmed
- 🔍 Recommended reporting standards for future VS series
  - KM analysis, not crude incidence, with censor marks and number at risk
  - Conformity, gradient and homogeneity indices plus complete DVH values
  - Stated failure definition separating post-SRS swelling from true progression
  - Reason for salvage: clinical progression vs imaging enlargement vs both

### LINAC SRS Technique Comparison: DCA vs VMAT

> Paddick CI 0.78 VMAT vs 0.75 DCA+SSO+VDR vs 0.6 plain DCA across 11 replanned cases. VMAT needed 41 ± 17% more MU than DCA+SSO+VDR.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Against the optimized DCA+SSO+VDR variant, VMAT's dosimetric advantage shrinks to CI 0.78 vs 0.75 (p=0.03); GI (p=0.29) and V12Gy 11.14 vs 11.75 cc (p=0.38) did not differ, at the cost of MU 4040 vs 2818. On an Elekta 5 mm-leaf platform, SSO+VDR, not VMAT, captures most of the gain over plain DCA.

- 🔍 Retrospective planning study: 11 brain cases, 4 plans per case, paired t-tests
- 🔍 Case mix and prescription
  - 7 solitary mets, 2 meningioma, 1 acoustic schwannoma, 1 pituitary adenoma
  - 9 SRS, 2 SRT; total dose 18 to 30Gy
  - PTV 0.93 to 22.5 cc (median 4.99cc); PTV = GTV + 2 mm
- 🔍 Planning platform
  - Elekta Versa HD, Agility MLC with 5 mm leaves, 6 FFF
  - Monaco 5.11.03, Monte Carlo, 0.3 cm grid
  - All DCA variants noncoplanar; VMAT coplanar, single full or partial arc
- 📊 Mean plan metrics by technique

  | Metric | DCA | DCA+SSO | DCA+SSO+VDR | VMAT | p (DCA vs SSO / SSO vs VDR / VDR vs VMAT) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Paddick CI | 0.6 | 0.69 | 0.75 | 0.78 | 0.0004 / 0.015 / 0.03 |
  | RTOG HI | 1.17 | 1.22 | 1.26 | 1.28 | 0.008 / 0.04 / 0.06 |
  | Paddick GI | 1.35 | 1.21 | 1.20 | 1.17 | 0.03 / 0.33 / 0.29 |
  | MU | 2507 | 2724 | 2818 | 4040 | 0.001 / 0.20 / 0.0007 |
  | Brain V12Gy (cc) | 16.75 | 14.13 | 11.75 | 11.14 | 0.10 / 0.01 / 0.38 |

- 📊 VMAT V12Gy lower than every DCA variant in 9 of 11 pts
- ⚠️ VMAT vs DCA+SSO+VDR: only CI (p=0.03) and MU (p=0.0007) differ; GI, HI, V12Gy did not
- ⚠️ Geometry confounded with technique: noncoplanar DCA vs coplanar VMAT, so modulation effect is not isolated
- ⚠️ 5 mm leaf MLC is coarse for sub-cc targets; transfer to finer-leaf platforms untested
- ⚠️ Only adjacent pairs tested, no multiplicity correction; no delivery QA or measured beam-on time reported

**Sources:**
- 📄 **The Optimal Choice of Technique for Stereotactic Radiosurgery—A LINAC-Based Dosimetric Study between DCA, DCA-SSO, DCA-SSO-VDR, and VMAT** — Venugopal, Ramya; S., Sowmya Narayanan; Tiwari, Richa et al.. *Asian Journal of Oncology* (2022-07). [doi:10.1055/s-0042-1751117](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0042-1751117)
  > Abstract Introduction Advanced radiation therapy delivery techniques require greater understanding of various planning sequences and methods. The aim of this study is to determine a class solution that finds the best possible technique to deliver for stereotactic radiosurgery between dynamic conformal arc (DCA) techniques using various options such as DCA, DCA + SSO (segment shape optimization), and DCA + SSO + VDR (variable dose rate) using noncoplanar beam arrangement and volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) using coplanar beams. Materials and Methods In this dosimetric study, 11 brain cases were retrospectively planned for various techniques and analyzed for the Paddick conformity index (CI), Radiation Therapy Oncology Group homogeneity index (HI), Paddick gradient index (GI), treatment time in terms of monitor units (MU) and normal brain dose (V12Gy). The paired t-test was performed to know the statistical significance between the techniques. Results In terms of CI, GI, and control of the normal brain dose, the VMAT plan was superior to other techniques. But, HI was found to be better with DCA. Above all, VMAT delivered higher MU than any other technique. The p-values between DCA + SSO and DCA, DCA + SSO + VDR and DCA + SSO, and VMAT and DCA + SSO + VDR are as follows: CI: 0.0004, 0.015, and 0.03; GI: 0.03, 0.33, and 0.29; HI: 0.008, 0.04, and 0.06; V12 Gy of normal brain: 0.1, 0.01, 0.38. VMAT requires approximately 41 ± 17% more MU than DCA + SSO + VDR. Conclusion VMAT using coplanar beams is preferable among all the techniques, considering the dosimetric parameters studied. If VMAT is not available in the facility, DCA + SSO + VDR technique using non coplanar beams can be used to deliver SRS treatment.

## 🎀 Breast

Two joint ASTRO/ASCO/SSO guidelines: PMRT indications and volumes, plus RT safety by germline mutation status (TP53 the lone contraindication to intact-breast RT).

### Hereditary Breast Cancer ASCO-ASTRO-SSO Guideline

> RT not withheld for mutation status (strong rec), except germline TP53, where intact-breast RT is contraindicated; BCT stays open to BRCA1/2 carriers.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The panel graded the RT recommendations unevenly. Not withholding RT for BRCA1/2 or moderate-penetrance status is a strong rec on intermediate evidence, but the ATM rec rests on low evidence, so a toxicity concern is a counseling topic, not a reason to omit RT. TP53 is the only exclusion, and even there PMRT stays available with significant LRR risk.

- 🔍 Joint ASCO/ASTRO/SSO panel: systematic review plus formal consensus process
- 🔍 58 articles underpin local therapy recs; 6 RCTs underpin systemic recs
- 🔍 28 studies addressed RT in germline mutation carriers
- 📊 Radiotherapy recommendations by germline status

  | Rec | Population | Recommendation | Evidence | Strength |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 6.1 | BRCA1/2 or moderate-penetrance gene | RT not withheld for mutation status after BCT or when PMRT considered | Intermediate | Strong |
  | 6.2 | ATM carriers | Offer RT when clinically indicated; discuss with those considering BCT | Low | Moderate |
  | 6.3 | Germline TP53 carriers | Intact-breast RT contraindicated; mastectomy; PMRT only with significant LRR risk | Low | Moderate |

- 📊 Management grid: BRCA1/2 vs moderate-penetrance genes (PALB2, CHEK2, ATM)

  | Decision | BRCA1/2 | Moderate-penetrance |
  |---|---|---|
  | BCT for index cancer | Not precluded; discuss CBC and ipsilateral new primary risk | Offer when BCT appropriate |
  | CRRM | Discuss; lowers CBC, no proven survival gain | Not based predominantly on mutation status |
  | Nipple-sparing mastectomy | Reasonable in selected pts | Reasonable in selected pts |
  | Surveillance without bilateral mastectomy | Annual mammogram + MRI | Annual mammogram + MRI |
  | Metastatic chemo | Platinum preferred over taxane if platinum-naive | No platinum efficacy data |
  | PARPi, metastatic HER2-negative | Olaparib or talazoparib, 1L to 3L | No supporting data |
  | (Neo)adjuvant | No routine platinum add-on; PARPi not recommended | No data |

- 📐 Round 1 agreement 72% to 98% (mean 90%), 47 to 49 respondents per rec
- 📐 1 of 17 recs missed the 75% threshold (moderate-gene CRRM); revised version reached 90% (n=50)
- ⚠️ Abstract and Table 2 say BRCA1/2 CRRM 'should be discussed'; Bottom Line Rec 4.1 says 'should be offered'
- ⚠️ RT safety recs are consensus on intermediate or low evidence; ATM data called limited and inconsistent
- 🔗 Platinum preference rests on TNT, which included only 11 BRCA carriers with ER-positive disease
- 🔗 PARPi recs anchor on OlympiAD and EMBRACA; OlympiA adjuvant results were pending at writing

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Management of Hereditary Breast Cancer: American Society of Clinical Oncology, American Society for Radiation Oncology, and Society of Surgical Oncology Guideline** — Tung, Nadine M.; Boughey, Judy C.; Pierce, Lori J. et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2020-06). [doi:10.1200/jco.20.00299](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.20.00299)
  > PURPOSE To develop recommendations for management of patients with breast cancer (BC) with germline mutations in BC susceptibility genes. METHODS The American Society of Clinical Oncology, American Society for Radiation Oncology, and Society of Surgical Oncology convened an Expert Panel to develop recommendations based on a systematic review of the literature and a formal consensus process. RESULTS Fifty-eight articles met eligibility criteria and formed the evidentiary basis for the local therapy recommendations; six randomized controlled trials of systemic therapy met eligibility criteria. RECOMMENDATIONS Patients with newly diagnosed BC and BRCA1/ 2 mutations may be considered for breast-conserving therapy (BCT), with local control of the index cancer similar to that of noncarriers. The significant risk of a contralateral BC (CBC), especially in young women, and the higher risk of new cancers in the ipsilateral breast warrant discussion of bilateral mastectomy. Patients with mutations in moderate-risk genes should be offered BCT. For women with mutations in BRCA1/ 2 or moderate-penetrance genes who are eligible for mastectomy, nipple-sparing mastectomy is a reasonable approach. There is no evidence of increased toxicity or CBC events from radiation exposure in BRCA1/ 2 carriers. Radiation therapy should not be withheld in ATM carriers. For patients with germline TP53 mutations, mastectomy is advised; radiation therapy is contraindicated except in those with significant risk of locoregional recurrence. Platinum agents are recommended versus taxanes to treat advanced BC in BRCA carriers. In the adjuvant/neoadjuvant setting, data do not support the routine addition of platinum to anthracycline- and taxane-based chemotherapy. Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors (olaparib and talazoparib) are preferable to nonplatinum single-agent chemotherapy for treatment of advanced BC in BRCA1/ 2 carriers. Data are insufficient to recommend PARP inhibitor use in the early setting or in moderate-penetrance carriers. Additional information available at www.asco.org/breast-cancer-guidelines .

### PMRT ASTRO-ASCO-SSO Guideline

> PMRT recommended for pN1 (Strong/High) and post-NST cT4/cN2-3 or ypN+; conditional for ypN0 after cT1-3N1/cT3N0; moderate hypofractionation preferred.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The pN1 omission remark is written for pts after ALND, so it does not directly cover the SLNB-only axilla now common in practice. Moderate hypofractionation is preferred with or without reconstruction, and IMN coverage is individualized by tumor location, size and nodal extent, which moves fractionation and nodal-volume defaults for most PMRT cases.

- 🔍 ASTRO-ASCO-SSO task force, four KQs: upfront-surgery indications, post-NST indications, volumes/fractionation, technique
- 🔍 Systematic review, MEDLINE + Embase Jan 2005 to Oct 2023, updated through Oct 15, 2024; 110 records included
- 📊 KQ1: PMRT after upfront mastectomy

  | Scenario | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | pN1 | PMRT recommended | Strong | High |
  | Any pT4 | PMRT recommended, even without other risk factors | Strong | High |
  | pT3N0 | PMRT conditionally recommended | Conditional | High |
  | pT1-2N0 | PMRT not recommended | Strong | Low |
  | Positive margins, no other indication | Chest wall/reconstructed breast alone | Conditional | Expert opinion |

- 📊 pN1 omission may be appropriate: pN1mic or low-burden pN1a after ALND with favorable features
  - pT1-2 disease
  - Low-to-intermediate grade HR+/HER2-
  - Postmenopausal
  - No LVI
  - Low 21-gene recurrence score
- 📊 KQ2: PMRT after neoadjuvant systemic therapy

  | Scenario | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Initial cT4 or cN2-3 | PMRT regardless of pathologic response | Strong | Moderate |
  | ypN1 | PMRT recommended | Strong | Moderate |
  | cT1-3N1 or cT3N0 → ypN0 | PMRT conditionally recommended | Conditional | Moderate |
  | cT1-2N0 → ypN0 | PMRT not recommended | Strong | Moderate |
  | Positive margins after NST | PMRT recommended | Strong | Expert opinion |

- 📊 ypN0 after cT1-3N1/cT3N0: PMRT may be omitted at ypT0N0
- 📊 Young age, LVI, high residual breast burden flagged as features favoring PMRT in ypN0
- 📊 KQ3: volumes, fractionation, boost

  | Scenario | Recommendation | Strength | QoE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Any PMRT | Chest wall/reconstructed breast + axillary, supra/infraclavicular, IMNs | Strong | High |
  | No reconstruction | Moderate hypofractionation | Strong | High |
  | Reconstruction | Moderate hypofx (preferred) or conventional | Strong | Moderate (hypofx) |
  | T4 or close/positive margins | Chest wall/scar boost | Conditional | Low |
  | Nodal disease not surgically addressed | Nodal boost | Strong | Expert opinion |

- 📊 Chest wall alone allowed in select pts (eg, pT3N0); IMN coverage individualized by location, size, nodal extent
- 🔍 KQ4 technique
  - CT-based volumetric planning with 3D-CRT recommended
  - IMRT when 3D-CRT cannot meet treatment goals
  - DIBH recommended for normal tissue sparing
  - Bolus for skin involvement, positive superficial margins and/or LVI
  - Routine tissue-equivalent bolus not recommended
- ⚠️ pT1-2N0 'not recommended' carries Strong strength on Low QoE
- ⚠️ EBCTCG base limited to trials initiated by 1995; predates HER2-directed therapy and IO
- 🔗 ypN0 evidence cites ACOSOG Z1071 analyses, NSABP B-51/RTOG 1304 abstract, RAPCHEM registry

## 🎯 Oligometastatic / Mets

### Adrenal SBRT Tumor Control Probability Modeling (HyTEC)

> Poisson TCP fit to pooled adrenal SBRT series: 1-yr LC >95% at BED10 116.4 Gy (EQD2 ≈97 Gy), fit P<.0001.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 95% LC point sits at BED10 116.4 Gy (EQD2 ≈97 Gy), reachable with 45 Gy/3 fx (112.5) or 55 Gy/5 fx (115.5); NRG-BR001 already prescribes 45 Gy/3 fx. Dose inputs were IDL-corrected median prescriptions, not GTV DVHs, and every series used motion management, so the target transfers only with ITV, compression, breath-hold or gating in place.

- 📊 1-yr LC >95% modeled at BED10 116.4 Gy (EQD2 ≈97 Gy)
- 📐 Poisson dose-response fits, 1-yr endpoints (EQD2, α/β 10 Gy)

  | Parameter | Local control | Overall survival |
  |---|---|---|
  | D50 (Gy EQD2) | 34.6 (95% CI 27.1-45.4) | 44.7 (95% CI 33.5-60.6) |
  | γ50 | 0.4996 (95% CI 0.248-0.814) | 0.4546 (95% CI 0.157-0.796) |
  | Fit χ2 | 57.5, P<.0001 | 44.7, P<.001 |

- 💊 Schedules cited against the LC target

  | Schedule | BED10 |
  |---|---|
  | 45 Gy / 3 fx | 112.5 Gy |
  | 55 Gy / 5 fx | 115.5 Gy |
  | Casamassima: 36 Gy / 3 fx to 70% IDL | just over 139 Gy at isocenter |

- 🔍 Pooled small, mostly single-institution retrospective series: 6-49 lesions (6-48 pts) each
- 🔍 Series median dose 40 Gy, median 5 fx (range 1-27)
- 📐 Dose per study = median prescription adjusted for IDL; no DVH data
- 📊 Crude 1-yr LC ranged 44% to 100% across series
- 🔗 Later meta-analysis predicted 1-yr LC 92.9% at BED10 100 Gy, within this model's CI
- ⚠️ No LC correlation with PTV margin (0-10 mm) or IDL (70-100%); IDL not statistically tested
- ⚠️ OS fit CIs wide; OS reflects factors beyond adrenal dose
- ⚠️ Reirradiation: only 2 pts in one series; model not applicable

# 2026-10-22

**RTOG 9601: 24mo bicalutamide 150mg added to salvage RT lifts 12-yr OS 76.3% vs 71.3% (HR 0.77) in pN0 post-RP pts, PSA 0.2-4.0.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate carried the day: RTOG 9601's OS gain from adding bicalutamide to salvage RT concentrates at entry PSA ≥0.7 (post hoc; HR 1.13 at <0.7), and focal salvage HDR for radiorecurrence (F-SHARP) posts 3-yr bPFS 62.7% with no acute G3+. Seminoma data favor smaller RT fields: Classen 6-yr RFS 95.3% (IIA); nodal mapping places 99% of nodes within a vascular CTV.

## ♂️ Germ Cell / Other GU

Two seminoma datasets back reduced RT fields for stage II disease, one with long-term outcomes, one a recurrence map only.

### Nodal metastasis mapping in seminoma (RT fields)

> Across 145 imaging-positive nodes in 90 pts, 99% sat within a 2.5cm lateral/posterior and 2.1cm anterior arterial expansion, smaller than Classen fields.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The superior border is the volume lever: no left-sided node sat above L1 or in the renal hilum, supporting a border 1.5cm above the left renal artery rather than T10/T11. Prior inguinal or pelvic surgery is the exception, needing an inferior border 1cm below the femoral head.

- 🔍 Retrospective imaging-mapping series, 90 pts, 4 academic centers, diagnosed 1996-2011
- 🔍 CS IIA/B or recurrent CS I seminoma after orchiectomy; nodes ≤5cm, no distant mets
- ⚠️ Node positivity by imaging only: >1cm short axis on CT (86 pts) or nanoparticle-enhanced MRI (4 pts); not biopsy-proven
- 📊 Nodal distribution by region (145 nodes)

  | Region | % of all nodes | % of pts with ≥1 node |
  |---|---|---|
  | Para-aortic | 84% | 88% |
  | Common iliac | 9% | 12% |
  | Pelvic | 7% | 9% |

- 📊 4 pts (4%) had pelvic-only relapse
- 📊 Laterality patterns
  - Left-sided: no PA nodes right of the aorta; none in renal hilum or above L1
  - Right-sided: 8 contralateral PA nodes (13%) in 5 (12%) pts; none above L2
  - Right-sided: single right renal hilar node, 3.5cm from aorta
- 📐 Proposed vascular-based CTV
  - 2.5cm lateral and posterior expansion of arterial vasculature, excluding vertebral bodies
  - 2.1cm anterior expansion
  - Superior border 1cm above right or 1.5cm above left renal artery, whichever is more inferior
  - ART for CS I: caudal border 1cm inferior to aortic bifurcation
- ⚠️ 5 nodes (3.5%) in 4 pts fell outside a Classen field: 3 left inguinal, 1 renal hilar, 1 gonadal-vein
- 🔗 Classen field (prospective phase II, 2003) showed no relapse increase vs larger fields; this data supports its acetabular inferior border
- ⚠️ Recurrence map, not field-failure data: no pt treated with the proposed CTV; authors restrict use to research setting

### Classen reduced-portal RT for stage IIA/B seminoma

> 6-yr RFS 95.3% (IIA) and 88.9% (IIB) with para-aortic/ipsilateral high iliac RT, 30/36 Gy; 100% in-field control, no late toxicity.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Field size is the transferable parameter: dropping the contralateral pelvis and inguinal chain to a T11-to-acetabulum hockey-stick gave no pelvic or inguinal failures and 100% in-field control. 3 of 4 relapses involved the mediastinum, outside any infradiaphragmatic field, so the residual risk is systemic, not a portal or dose problem.

- 🔍 Prospective multicentre observational study, 30 German centres, enrolled 1991-1994
- 🔍 N=94 enrolled, 87 assessable (7 lost to f/u, excluded); median f/u 70 mo (range 3-111)
- 🔍 Pure testicular seminoma, Royal Marsden stage IIA (<2 cm) or IIB (2-5 cm); elevated AFP excluded
- 🔍 RT technique: hockey-stick, infradiaphragmatic only
  - Para-aortic/paracaval + ipsilateral high iliac nodes, gross nodes + 2 cm margin
  - Upper border cranial rim T11; lower border cranial rim ipsilateral acetabulum
  - AP/PA opposed fields, 4-20 MV photons, 2.0 Gy/fx
  - IIA 30 Gy/15 fx; IIB 36 Gy; no boost
- 📊 Outcomes by stage

  | Measure | Stage IIA | Stage IIB |
  |---|---|---|
  | Assessable pts | 66 | 21 |
  | Dose | 30 Gy | 36 Gy |
  | 6-yr RFS (95% CI) | 95.3% (88.9-100%) | 88.9% (74.4-100%) |
  | Relapse sites | 1 mediastinal, 1 field-edge | 1 mediastinal, 1 mediastinal/pulmonary |
  | G3 nausea | 8% | 10% |
  | G3 diarrhea | 6% | 10% |

- 📊 Patterns of failure: 100% in-field control; no pelvic, inguinal, or isolated supraclavicular relapse
- 📊 All 4 relapses salvaged with platinum chemo, all in continuous CR; DSS 100% both stages
- 📊 No grade 4 acute toxicity; late effects limited to G1 skin hyperpigmentation in 1 pt
- ⚠️ Protocol violations in 26 pts (29.9%), 22 major; efficacy reported as ITT
- ⚠️ IIB arm is only 21 pts; CI lower bound 74.4%, so IIB control estimate is imprecise
- ⚠️ 70-mo median f/u too short to capture second-malignancy and cardiac late effects
- 🔗 Source contrasts modified EP (9% relapse, 33% G3/4 leukopenia) and carboplatin alone (15% failure)

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Radiotherapy for Stages IIA/B Testicular Seminoma: Final Report of a Prospective Multicenter Clinical Trial** — Classen, Johannes; Schmidberger, Heinz; Meisner, Christoph et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2003-03). [doi:10.1200/jco.2003.06.065](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2003.06.065)
  > Purpose: A prospective multicenter trial was initiated to evaluate the role of modern radiotherapy with reduced treatment portals for stage IIA and IIB testicular seminoma. Patients and Methods: Patients with stages IIA/B disease (Royal Marsden classification) were assessable for the trial. Staging comprised computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis as well as analysis of tumor markers alpha-fetoprotein and beta human chorionic gonadotropin. Linac-based radiotherapy was delivered to para-aortic and high ipsilateral iliac lymph nodes. The total doses were 30 Gy for stage IIA and 36 Gy for stage IIB disease. Results: Between April 1991 and March 1994, 94 patients were enrolled for the trial by 30 participating centers throughout Germany. Seven patients were lost to follow-up. Median time to follow-up of 87 assessable patients was 70 months. There were 66 stage IIA and 21 stage IIB patients. One mediastinal and one field-edge relapse were observed in the stage IIA group. In the stage IIB group, there was one mediastinal and one mediastinal/pulmonary relapse. All patients were treated with a salvage regimen of platinum-based chemotherapy. Actuarial relapse-free survival at 6 years was 95.3% (95% confidence interval [CI], 88.9% to 100%) and 88.9% (95% CI, 74.4% to 100%) for stage IIA and IIB groups, respectively. Maximum acute side effects were 8% grade 3 nausea for stage IIA and 10% grade 3 nausea and diarrhea for stage IIB groups. No late toxicity was observed. Conclusion: Radiotherapy for stages IIA/B seminoma with reduced portals yields excellent tumor control at a low rate of acute toxicity and no late toxicity, which supports the role of radiotherapy as the first treatment choice for these patients.

## 🌰 Prostate

Salvage throughout: systemic add-on to prostate-bed RT, focal HDR re-irradiation for radiorecurrence, plus an SBRT margin audit.

### F-SHARP

> No acute G3+ toxicity and 3-yr bPFS 62.7% after dose-escalated focal salvage HDR (up to 30 Gy/1-2 fx) for intraprostatic radiorecurrence, N=62.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The single-fraction arm (24 pts) matched two fractions on every endpoint (all P > .05), making a one-visit focal salvage HDR schedule a real option. The GC tail is the second read: all 4 pts with score >0.85 recurred locally and distantly, so focal-only salvage looks like the wrong tool for them.

- 🔍 Multi-institutional phase I/II, single-arm, N=62
- 🔍 Intraprostatic radiorecurrence (IPR) after any form of definitive RT for localized prostate cancer
- 💊 DE-fsHDR up to 30 Gy in 1-2 fractions: 24 pts 1 fx, 38 pts 2 fx
- 📊 1° objective: acute RT-related G3+ GU/GI toxicity (CTCAE v4.03): none occurred
- 📊 Late G3 toxicity in 2 pts (3.2%)
- 📊 QoL (EPIC-26, IPSS) changes did not meet MCID criteria
- 📊 3-yr time-to-event outcomes, median f/u 39.4 mo

  | Endpoint | 3-yr rate |
  |---|---|
  | bPFS | 62.7% |
  | rPFS | 67.5% |
  | LRFS | 73.0% |
  | MFS | 85.9% |

- 📐 No difference 1 vs 2 fractions across endpoints (all P > .05)
- 📊 GC (Veracyte transcriptome) in 31 pts: higher score associated with worse bPFS, rPFS, LRFS, MFS
- 📊 All pts with GC score >0.85 (n = 4) had biochemical, local and metastatic recurrence
- ⚠️ GC analysis covers only 31/62 pts; >0.85 cutpoint rests on n = 4
- ⚠️ No whole-gland or alternative salvage comparator; efficacy is single-arm

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Dose-Escalated Focal Salvage High-Dose-Rate Brachytherapy and Genomic Classifier Risk Stratification in Radiorecurrent Prostate Cancer: The F-SHARP Phase I/II Clinical Trial** — Solanki, Abhishek A.; Adams, William; Proudfoot, James A. et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2026-10). [doi:10.1200/jco-25-02730](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco-25-02730). [article](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-25-02730)
  > Purpose Prospective data on the management of intraprostatic radiorecurrence (IPR) after definitive radiotherapy (RT) and use of genomic classifiers (GC) in this setting are limited. We hypothesized that dose-escalated focal salvage high-dose-rate brachytherapy (DE-fsHDR) would be safe and effective, and that GC would risk stratify patients who may benefit from treatment intensification. Methods F-SHARP is a multi-institutional phase I/II trial of DE-fsHDR for IPR after any form of RT for localized prostate cancer. Patients received up to 30 Gy in 1-2 fractions. The primary objective was to determine the acute RT-related grade ≥3 CTCAE v4.03 genitourinary and GI toxicity rates. Secondary end points included all-grade toxicity, quality of life (QoL; EPIC-26 and International Prostate Symptom Score), disease control, and survival. GC analysis used a clinical-grade transcriptome assay (Veracyte, San Diego, CA). Time-to-event outcomes included biochemical progression-free survival (bPFS), radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS), local recurrence-free survival (LRFS), and metastasis-free survival (MFS). Results Twenty-four participants received one fraction and 38 received two fractions (N = 62). The median follow-up was 39.4 months. No acute grade 3 toxicities occurred. Two patients (3.2%) had late grade 3 toxicity. QoL changes did not meet criteria for a minimal clinically important difference. Three-year survival rates were 62.7% (bPFS), 67.5% (rPFS), 73.0% (LRFS), and 85.9% (MFS); there were no differences between fractionation groups (all P &gt; .05). Among 31 patients with GC results, higher scores were associated with worse bPFS, rPFS, LRFS, and MFS. All patients with GC score &gt;0.85 (n = 4) experienced biochemical, local, and metastatic recurrence. Conclusion DE-fsHDR is a safe and effective salvage treatment for IPR. GC score is a promising prognostic tool in patients undergoing salvage local therapy for IPR and warrants further study to guide treatment intensification.

### Prostate SBRT intra-fraction motion and margins

> Current 6 mm/3 mm posterior PTV margins covered IFPM in 92% of fractions. BIR-guideline margins ran from 3.3 mm laterally and posteriorly to 7.0 mm inferiorly.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The 7.0 mm inferior margin is driven mainly by apex delineation error on MRI-only contours, and dropping one outlier contour gives 5.8 mm. Lateral margins of 3.3 and 3.5 mm suggest room to trim. The posterior miss (6 of 97 fractions past 3 mm) sits at the rectal edge, which argues for bladder/rectal consistency or tracking rather than a wider posterior PTV.

- 🔍 Retrospective single-centre audit: 20 pts, 36.25 Gy in 5 fx SBRT, no intra-fraction tracking
- 🔍 97 pre/post-treatment CBCT pairs; fiducial-based daily online IGRT match
- 📊 Averaged 3D RMS IFPM 2.5 mm (2SD 4.2 mm)
- 📊 Motion predominantly posterior (70%) and inferior (63%)
- 📊 IFPM exceeded current margins in 8 of 97 fractions, six in the posterior direction
- 📊 Current clinical margins vs BIR-guideline-derived margins by direction

  | Direction | Current margin | BIR-derived margin |
  |---|---|---|
  | Right | 6 mm | 3.5 mm |
  | Left | 6 mm | 3.3 mm |
  | Superior | 6 mm | 3.7 mm |
  | Inferior | 6 mm | 7.0 mm |
  | Anterior | 6 mm | 4.4 mm |
  | Posterior | 3 mm | 3.3 mm |

- 📐 IFPM-only margins ≤3.5 mm; only 3% of fractions would have needed >6 mm in any direction
- 📐 Weak correlation of motion with time: posterior ρ = 0.38, inferior ρ = 0.36; mean imaging interval 9 min
- 🔍 Identified causes in 4 of 6 evaluable out-of-margin fractions
  - Bladder volume difference: n=3
  - Rectal volume difference: n=2
  - Pt movement in one further fraction
- ⚠️ Delineation error is the largest margin component, measured on MRI-only contours, so likely an underestimate vs CT or fused MR/CT
- ⚠️ Excluding one outlier contour cuts the inferior margin to 5.8 mm from 7 mm
- ⚠️ Match error assumed negligible (31 observers) but not measured
- ⚠️ No toxicity or biochemical control endpoint; margin changes untested clinically

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 28434799]] **Prostate stereotactic body radiotherapy: quantifying intra-fraction motion and calculating margins using the new BIR geometric uncertainties in daily online IGRT recommendations** — McNeice, Joseph M; Sanilkumar, Nandu; Alexander, Sophie E et al.. *The British Journal of Radiology* (2023-06). [doi:10.1259/bjr.20220852](https://doi.org/10.1259/bjr.20220852)
  > Objectives: To measure the magnitude of intra-fraction prostate motion (IFPM) during stereotactic radiotherapy (SBRT) delivered without intra-fraction tracking. To assess if current margins adequately cover IFPM. To derive margins using new guidelines. Methods: IFPM was determined in 20 patients receiving 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions using 97 pairs of pre- and post-treatment cone beam CT (CBCT) scans. Correlation of time between CBCT acquisitions and motion was determined. The magnitude of IFPM was compared to current margins (6 mm isotropic, 3 mm posterior). Margins were calculated using IFPM alone and updated guidelines. Results: The averaged 3D root mean square IFPM was 2.5 mm (4.2 mm). Independent prostate motion was predominantly posterior (70%) and inferior (63%). There was weak correlation between posterior (ρ = 0.38) and inferior (ρ = 0.36) IFPM and time. IFPM greater than current margins occurred in 8 of 97 fractions, six in the posterior direction. Margins were ≤3.5 mm using IFPM alone and ≤3.3 mm Left 3.5 mm Right, 7.0 mm inferior, 3.7 mm superior, 4.4 mm anterior and 3.3 mm posterior using new guidelines, compensating for motion in 92% of fractions. Conclusions: Our current SBRT margins account for 92% of IFPM, predominantly posterior and inferior. Although updated guidelines suggest an increase in margins inferiorly, any increase must be balanced against the possibility of increased toxicity, particularly if biochemical control and side-effects are favourable with current practice. Advances in knowledge: The difference between current clinical margins and those determined using updated guidance is demonstrated. The implications must be considered against clinical outcomes.

### RTOG 9601 · [[NCT00002874]]

> 12-yr OS 76.3% vs 71.3%, HR 0.77 (0.59-0.99), adding 24mo bicalutamide 150mg to salvage RT in pN0 post-RP pts with PSA 0.2-4.0.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The benefit follows pre-SRT PSA: 12-yr OS HR 0.45 (0.25-0.81) above 1.5 ng/ml and 0.61 at 0.7-1.5, but HR 1.13 (0.77-1.65) below 0.7, where the median entry PSA of 0.6 sat. RT was prostate bed only, 64.8 Gy/36 fx, so this informs hormone addition separately from the pelvic nodal coverage decision.

- 🔍 Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 840 randomized 1998-2003, 760 eligible analyzed
- 🔍 Median f/u 13 yrs among survivors; median age 65, median entry PSA 0.6 ng/ml
- 💊 Bicalutamide 150 mg daily × 24 mo vs placebo, started with RT
- 🔍 RT: 64.8 Gy/36 fx (1.8 Gy) to prostate bed + membranous urethra; no pelvic nodes (all pN0)
- 📊 12-yr outcomes, RT + bicalutamide vs RT + placebo

  | Endpoint | RT + bicalutamide | RT + placebo | HR (95% CI), P |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | OS (1° EP) | 76.3% | 71.3% | 0.77 (0.59-0.99), P=0.04 |
  | Death from PCa | 5.8% | 13.4% | 0.49 (0.32-0.74), P<0.001 |
  | Metastatic PCa | 14.5% | 23.0% | 0.63 (0.46-0.87), P=0.005 |
  | 2nd biochemical recurrence | 44.0% | 67.9% | 0.48 (0.40-0.58), P<0.001 |

- 📊 12-yr OS by PSA at trial entry (post hoc; PSA the only significant interaction)

  | PSA at entry | n (%) | Bicalutamide | Placebo | HR (95% CI), P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | <0.7 ng/ml | 405 (53.3) | 76.8% | 80.7% | 1.13 (0.77-1.65), 0.53 |
  | 0.7-1.5 ng/ml | 237 (31.2) | 77.0% | 67.5% | 0.61 (0.39-0.95), 0.03 |
  | >1.5 ng/ml | 118 (15.5) | 73.5% | 48.9% | 0.45 (0.25-0.81), 0.007 |

- ⚠️ Median entry PSA 0.6, so the no-benefit stratum (<0.7) is the majority of the trial
- 📊 Toxicity, bicalutamide vs placebo
  - Gynecomastia any grade 69.7% vs 10.9% (P<0.001), no prophylaxis allowed
  - Late GU G3 7.0% vs 6.0%; G4 0.3% vs 0.8%
  - No between-group difference in early urinary, bowel, or hematologic reactions
- ⚠️ Bicalutamide 150 mg superseded by GnRH agonists; ADT equivalence not tested here
- ⚠️ Staging CT + bone scan only; RT QA showed 4.4% unacceptable variation
- 📐 Absolute OS difference 5.0 points; NNT 20 to avoid one death over 12 yrs

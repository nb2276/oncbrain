# 2026-10-22

**RTOG 9601: 24mo bicalutamide 150mg added to 64.8Gy salvage RT in pN0 post-RP PSA recurrence → 12-yr OS 76.3% vs 71.3%, HR 0.77 (0.59-0.99).**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Prostate leads: at 13-yr f/u, RTOG 9601's bicalutamide OS benefit tracks entry PSA (HR 1.13 at <0.7 vs 0.45 at >1.5 ng/ml, post hoc). SBRT motion data flag the inferior margin (BIR-derived 7.0 mm). In seminoma, Classen IIA/B RT (6-yr RFS 95.3%/88.9%, 100% in-field control) and Paly's nodal map inform para-aortic field design.

## ♂️ Germ Cell / Other GU

Two seminoma RT field papers: a prospective outcomes series for the dog-leg field and a nodal map arguing for smaller vascular-based CTVs.

### Nodal metastasis mapping in seminoma (Paly et al.)

> 84% of 145 imaging-positive nodes were para-aortic; 99% fell within a 2.5cm lateral/posterior, 2.1cm anterior arterial expansion, supporting smaller vascular-based CTVs.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The superior border is where the volume saving lives: no nodes sat above L1 for left-sided or above L2 for right-sided primaries, so a border 1 to 1.5 cm above the renal arteries replaces the T10/T11 edge. Laterality also matters: left-sided primaries produced no PA nodes right of the aorta, which questions routine right-of-IVC coverage.

- 🔍 Retrospective, 4 academic centers, N=90, diagnosed 1996-2011
- 🔍 CS IIA/B or recurrent CS I seminoma after orchiectomy; nodes ≤5 cm, no distant mets
- 🔍 145 radiographically positive nodes; >1 cm short axis on CT (86 pts) or nanoparticle MRI uptake (4 pts)
- ⚠️ Nodes not typically biopsy-proven; positivity by imaging alone, single-radiologist review
- 📊 Nodal distribution by region

  | Region | % of all nodes | % of pts with ≥1 node |
  |---|---|---|
  | Para-aortic | 84% | 88% |
  | Common iliac | 9% | 12% |
  | Pelvic | 7% | 9% |

- 📊 Pelvic-only relapse: 4 pts (4%)
- 📊 Laterality patterns
  - Left-sided: no PA nodes right of the aorta, none renal hilar or above L1
  - Right-sided: 8 contralateral PA nodes (13%) in 5 (12%) pts
  - Right-sided: none above L2; one right renal hilar node
- 📊 Proposed CTV: 2.5 cm lateral/posterior (excluding vertebral bodies), 2.1 cm anterior from arterial vasculature → covers 99% of nodes
- 📊 Superior border: 1 cm above right renal artery or 1.5 cm above left renal artery, whichever lies more inferiorly
- 📊 5 (3.5%) nodes in 4 pts fell outside a Classen field: 3 left inguinal, 1 gonadal-vein, 1 right renal hilar
- 🔗 vs Classen extended field (NCCN / EGCCCG-endorsed): inferior border at acetabular rim supported; covers pelvic but not inguinal nodes
- ⚠️ Pattern-of-relapse map, not an outcomes study: no in-field control or SMN data for the reduced field
- ⚠️ Many pts had only one CT, so original node position before reaching size threshold is estimated

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Mapping patterns of nodal metastases in seminoma: Rethinking radiotherapy fields** — Paly, Jonathan J.; Efstathiou, Jason A.; Hedgire, Sandeep S. et al.. *Radiotherapy and Oncology* (2013-01). [doi:10.1016/j.radonc.2012.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2012.12.002)
  - 📎 [[papers/gu-other/2013-paly-mapping-patterns-nodal-metastases-seminoma-rethinking.pdf|Nodal metastasis mapping in seminoma (Paly et al.) (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Marginal and out-of-field failure rate with vascular-based reduced CTV
> - Measured second malignancy reduction with smaller seminoma fields
> - Role of nanoparticle-enhanced MRI in defining sub-centimetre nodes

### Classen stage IIA/B seminoma RT trial

> 6-yr RFS 95.3% (IIA, 30 Gy) and 88.9% (IIB, 36 Gy) with para-aortic/ipsilateral high iliac RT; 100% in-field control, DSS 100%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The target-volume read is the transferable one: a hockey-stick field limited to para-aortic plus ipsilateral high iliac nodes produced no pelvic or inguinal failure and one field-edge relapse, with all other failures supradiaphragmatic. That argues against both bilateral pelvic and prophylactic mediastinal or supraclavicular coverage at 30 Gy (IIA) and 36 Gy (IIB).

- 🔍 Prospective multicenter observational study, 30 German centers, enrolled 1991-1994
- 🔍 N=94 enrolled; 87 assessable (66 IIA, 21 IIB); 7 lost to follow-up and excluded
- 🔍 Median f/u 70 mo (range 3-111)
- 🔍 Pure seminoma, Royal Marsden IIA (<2 cm) or IIB (2-5 cm); AFP-elevated pts excluded
- 🔍 RT technique
  - AP-PA hockey-stick: para-aortic/paracaval + ipsilateral high iliac nodes
  - Upper border cranial rim T11; lower border cranial rim ipsilateral acetabulum
  - Enlarged nodes covered with 2-cm safety margin; no boost
  - 2.0 Gy/fx: 30 Gy for IIA, 36 Gy for IIB; 4-20 MV photons
- 📊 Primary endpoint: RFS at 5 yr; reported as actuarial 6-yr RFS
- 📐 6-yr RFS IIA 95.3% (95% CI 88.9-100%); IIB 88.9% (95% CI 74.4-100%)
- 📊 Patterns of failure (4 relapses, all per-protocol pts)
  - IIA: 1 mediastinal, 1 left-sided field-edge, at 33 and 40 mo
  - IIB: 1 mediastinal, 1 mediastinal/pulmonary, at 10 and 17 mo
  - No pelvic or inguinal recurrence; 100% in-field control
  - All salvaged with platinum-based chemo; all in continuous CR
- 📊 Acute G3: nausea 8% IIA, nausea and diarrhea 10% IIB; no G4
- 📊 No late toxicity beyond 1 case of G1 skin hyperpigmentation; no second malignancy to date
- ⚠️ Single-arm, no randomised comparator vs chemo; IIB arm only 21 pts, wide CI
- ⚠️ Major protocol violations in 22 pts (25.3%), including dose deviations and bilateral iliac fields
- ⚠️ 70-mo median f/u too short for late cardiac or second-cancer risk
- 🔗 Sourced comparators: modified EP relapse 9% with 33% G3/4 leukopenia; carboplatin alone failure 15%

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Radiotherapy for Stages IIA/B Testicular Seminoma: Final Report of a Prospective Multicenter Clinical Trial** — Classen, Johannes; Schmidberger, Heinz; Meisner, Christoph et al.. *Journal of Clinical Oncology* (2003-03). [doi:10.1200/jco.2003.06.065](https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2003.06.065)
  > Purpose: A prospective multicenter trial was initiated to evaluate the role of modern radiotherapy with reduced treatment portals for stage IIA and IIB testicular seminoma. Patients and Methods: Patients with stages IIA/B disease (Royal Marsden classification) were assessable for the trial. Staging comprised computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis as well as analysis of tumor markers alpha-fetoprotein and beta human chorionic gonadotropin. Linac-based radiotherapy was delivered to para-aortic and high ipsilateral iliac lymph nodes. The total doses were 30 Gy for stage IIA and 36 Gy for stage IIB disease. Results: Between April 1991 and March 1994, 94 patients were enrolled for the trial by 30 participating centers throughout Germany. Seven patients were lost to follow-up. Median time to follow-up of 87 assessable patients was 70 months. There were 66 stage IIA and 21 stage IIB patients. One mediastinal and one field-edge relapse were observed in the stage IIA group. In the stage IIB group, there was one mediastinal and one mediastinal/pulmonary relapse. All patients were treated with a salvage regimen of platinum-based chemotherapy. Actuarial relapse-free survival at 6 years was 95.3% (95% confidence interval [CI], 88.9% to 100%) and 88.9% (95% CI, 74.4% to 100%) for stage IIA and IIB groups, respectively. Maximum acute side effects were 8% grade 3 nausea for stage IIA and 10% grade 3 nausea and diarrhea for stage IIB groups. No late toxicity was observed. Conclusion: Radiotherapy for stages IIA/B seminoma with reduced portals yields excellent tumor control at a low rate of acute toxicity and no late toxicity, which supports the role of radiotherapy as the first treatment choice for these patients.
  - 📎 [[papers/gu-other/2003-classen-radiotherapy-stages-iia-b-testicular-seminoma-final.pdf|Classen stage IIA/B seminoma RT trial (full text)]]

> [!question] Open questions
> - Can dose drop below 30 Gy in stage IIA seminoma?
> - RT vs RPLND or reduced-intensity chemo for stage IIA/B
> - Late cardiac and second-cancer risk beyond 6 years

## 🌰 Prostate

Salvage RT: the case for adding hormones depends on entry PSA; SBRT: a margin audit with posterior and inferior misses.

### Prostate SBRT intra-fraction motion and margins

> IFPM exceeded 6 mm/3 mm-posterior margins in 8/97 fractions, mostly posterior; BIR guidance gives 3.3-7.0 mm margins, larger only inferiorly.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The inferior margin is the only one the BIR guidance would widen (7.0 mm vs 6 mm), and that comes mostly from apex delineation error, not motion. Dropping one contouring outlier brings it to 5.8 mm. The real miss is posterior: 6 of 8 out-of-margin fractions breached the 3 mm rectal-side margin, so rectal and bladder consistency matter more than inferior expansion.

- 🔍 Retrospective analysis: 20 pts, 97 pre/post-treatment CBCT pairs
- 🔍 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions, no intra-fraction tracking, fiducial-based online match
- 🔍 Current clinical margins: 6 mm isotropic, 3 mm posterior
- 📊 Averaged 3D RMS IFPM 2.5 mm (2SD 4.2 mm)
- 📊 Motion mostly posterior (70%) and inferior (63%); RL evenly divided
- 📊 IFPM exceeded current margins in 8 of 97 fractions, 6 of them posterior
- 📊 Margins by direction: current vs BIR updated guidance (mm)

  | Direction | Current | BIR-derived |
  |---|---|---|
  | Right | 6 | 3.5 |
  | Left | 6 | 3.3 |
  | Superior | 6 | 3.7 |
  | Inferior | 6 | 7.0 |
  | Anterior | 6 | 4.4 |
  | Posterior | 3 | 3.3 |

- 📐 Weak motion-time correlation: posterior ρ = 0.38, inferior ρ = 0.36
- 📐 Mean pre-to-post imaging interval 9 min, median 8 min 46 s
- 🔍 Causes of excess motion (6 fractions evaluated)
  - Bladder volume differences: n=3
  - Rectal volume differences: n=2
  - Pt movement: 1 fraction
  - No identified cause: 1 fraction
- ⚠️ Delineation error is the largest margin component; excluding one outlier drops inferior margin 7 → 5.8 mm
- ⚠️ Delineation errors came from an MRI-only study, likely smaller than on CT or MR/CT fusion
- ⚠️ Match error assumed negligible, never measured
- ⚠️ Geometric surrogate only: no toxicity or biochemical control data

**Sources:**
- 📄 [[PMID 28434799]] **Prostate stereotactic body radiotherapy: quantifying intra-fraction motion and calculating margins using the new BIR geometric uncertainties in daily online IGRT recommendations** — McNeice, Joseph M; Sanilkumar, Nandu; Alexander, Sophie E et al.. *The British Journal of Radiology* (2023-06). [doi:10.1259/bjr.20220852](https://doi.org/10.1259/bjr.20220852)
  > Objectives: To measure the magnitude of intra-fraction prostate motion (IFPM) during stereotactic radiotherapy (SBRT) delivered without intra-fraction tracking. To assess if current margins adequately cover IFPM. To derive margins using new guidelines. Methods: IFPM was determined in 20 patients receiving 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions using 97 pairs of pre- and post-treatment cone beam CT (CBCT) scans. Correlation of time between CBCT acquisitions and motion was determined. The magnitude of IFPM was compared to current margins (6 mm isotropic, 3 mm posterior). Margins were calculated using IFPM alone and updated guidelines. Results: The averaged 3D root mean square IFPM was 2.5 mm (4.2 mm). Independent prostate motion was predominantly posterior (70%) and inferior (63%). There was weak correlation between posterior (ρ = 0.38) and inferior (ρ = 0.36) IFPM and time. IFPM greater than current margins occurred in 8 of 97 fractions, six in the posterior direction. Margins were ≤3.5 mm using IFPM alone and ≤3.3 mm Left 3.5 mm Right, 7.0 mm inferior, 3.7 mm superior, 4.4 mm anterior and 3.3 mm posterior using new guidelines, compensating for motion in 92% of fractions. Conclusions: Our current SBRT margins account for 92% of IFPM, predominantly posterior and inferior. Although updated guidelines suggest an increase in margins inferiorly, any increase must be balanced against the possibility of increased toxicity, particularly if biochemical control and side-effects are favourable with current practice. Advances in knowledge: The difference between current clinical margins and those determined using updated guidance is demonstrated. The implications must be considered against clinical outcomes.

### RTOG 9601 · [[NCT00002874]]

> 12-yr OS 76.3% vs 71.3%, HR 0.77 (0.59-0.99), adding 24mo bicalutamide 150mg to 64.8Gy prostate-bed salvage RT in pN0 post-RP PSA recurrence.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The OS gain came with prostate-bed-only RT (64.8 Gy/1.8 Gy, no nodes because all pts were pN0), so it doesn't test hormones alongside an elective pelvic field. Entry PSA splits the read: OS HR 1.13 below 0.7 ng/ml vs 0.45 above 1.5 ng/ml (post hoc), which argues for gating hormone use on pre-SRT PSA.

- 🔍 Double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial, enrolled 1998-2003
- 🔍 760 eligible of 840 randomized; median f/u among survivors 13 yr
- 🔍 Eligibility
  - RP with lymphadenectomy, pN0
  - pT2 with positive margin, or pT3
  - Detectable PSA 0.2-4.0 ng/ml ≥8 wk post-RP; median entry PSA 0.6 ng/ml
  - No prior hormones beyond 2-6 mo preop short-term therapy (6.4%)
  - CT abdomen/pelvis + bone scan negative for metastases
- 💊 Bicalutamide 150 mg daily ×24 mo vs placebo, started with RT
- 🔍 SRT 64.8 Gy/36 fx of 1.8 Gy, 6-10 MV photons, prostate bed + membranous urethra
- 🔍 No pelvic nodal RT: all pts had negative lymph-node dissections
- 📊 12-yr outcomes, bicalutamide vs placebo (all + SRT)

  | Endpoint | Bicalutamide | Placebo | HR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | OS (primary) | 76.3% | 71.3% | 0.77 (0.59-0.99) | 0.04 |
  | Death from prostate cancer | 5.8% | 13.4% | 0.49 (0.32-0.74) | <0.001 |
  | Metastatic prostate cancer | 14.5% | 23.0% | 0.63 (0.46-0.87) | 0.005 |
  | Second biochemical recurrence | 44.0% | 67.9% | 0.48 (0.40-0.58) | <0.001 |

- 📊 Absolute OS difference 5.0 points; NNT 20 to avoid one death over 12 yr
- 📊 No significant between-group difference in non-disease-specific death
- 📊 12-yr OS by PSA at trial entry (post hoc)

  | Entry PSA | N | Bicalutamide | Placebo | HR (95% CI) | p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | <0.7 ng/ml | 405 | 76.8% | 80.7% | 1.13 (0.77-1.65) | 0.53 |
  | 0.7-1.5 ng/ml | 237 | 77.0% | 67.5% | 0.61 (0.39-0.95) | 0.03 |
  | >1.5 ng/ml | 118 | 73.5% | 48.9% | 0.45 (0.25-0.81) | 0.007 |

- ⚠️ Subgroups post hoc; interaction significant only for PSA level
- 📊 Gleason 8-10 OS HR 0.76 (0.44-1.30), p=0.32: few pts, not significant
- 📊 Toxicity, bicalutamide vs placebo
  - Late GU G3: 7.0% vs 6.0%; G4: 0.3% vs 0.8%
  - Gynecomastia any grade: 69.7% vs 10.9% (P<0.001)
  - Late G3 hepatic: 0.8% vs 0.3%
  - No significant differences in early urinary, bowel, hematologic reactions
- ⚠️ Bicalutamide 150 mg monotherapy since superseded by GnRH agonists
- ⚠️ RT QA on random subset only: 64.8% per protocol, 4.4% unacceptable variation
- ⚠️ Analysis restricted to 760 eligible pts; 79 ineligible + 1 withdrawal removed post-randomization

**Sources:**
- 📄 **Radiation with or without Antiandrogen Therapy in Recurrent Prostate Cancer** — Shipley, William U.; Seiferheld, Wendy; Lukka, Himanshu R. et al.. *New England Journal of Medicine* (2017-02). [doi:10.1056/nejmoa1607529](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1607529)
  - 📎 [[papers/prostate/2017-shipley-radiation-without-antiandrogen-therapy-recurrent.pdf|RTOG 9601 (full text)]]

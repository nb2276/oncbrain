# 2026-10-28 — ESMO Congress 2026

**SMART-KEY: 2y LFS 65.1% (80% CI 54.9-73.5) with cisplatin-free induction → IMRT + pembro in stage III-IVB glottic/supraglottic SCC, vs 64.9% in RTOG 9111.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Head-neck carried the day: IO and ADCs are moving into curative-intent RT pathways, but every readout is single-arm, interim or retrospective. KEYNOTE-412 ctDNA: mid-CRT clearance tracked EFS in HPV- LA HNSCC (P values only). Multi-site: RADIANT RT foundation model hit GTV Dice 83.52%, external OS C-index 0.73-0.79.

## 👄 Head & Neck

Four curative-intent readouts that add IO or an ADC around RT or surgery; none yet has a randomised efficacy comparison.

### GORTEC 2024-03 IDEAL · [[NCT06959108]]

> Interim safety analysis in LA-HNSCC: no G4 AEs; G3 TEAEs in 6 pts with ADC+anti-PD-1 vs 2 pts with ADC alone (10 per arm).

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT question is whether EGFR-ADC induction, with or without anti-PD-1, compromises delivery of IMRT 70 Gy + cisplatin; the source reports cycle-1 toxicity only, with no CRT compliance data. Arm A had more G3 TEAEs (6 vs 2 pts), including ALT increase and neutropenia, worth watching before cisplatin.

- 🔍 Randomized (1:1) phase II, 2 arms each evaluated independently (one-stage Fleming design, H0=30%)
- 🔍 106 pts planned; 49 randomized Oct 2025 to Apr 2026
- 🔍 First interim safety analysis after cycle 1 in the first 20 pts (10 per arm)
- 💊 Regimens
  - Arm A: pucotenlimab 200 mg + becotatug vedotin 2.3 mg/kg Q3W ×3 → CRT → adjuvant pucotenlimab Q3W ×8
  - Arm B: becotatug vedotin alone Q3W ×3 → CRT
  - CRT: IMRT 70 Gy + concurrent cisplatin, both arms
- 🔍 Primary endpoint: CR at 12 weeks post-CRT; no primary endpoint result in source
- 📊 First interim safety analysis by arm

  | Outcome | Arm A (ADC + pucotenlimab) | Arm B (ADC alone) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pts evaluated | 10 | 10 |
  | Pts with G3 TEAE | 6 | 2 |
  | G4 AEs | None | None |
  | Deaths | None | 1 (post-gastrostomy, deemed unrelated) |

- 📊 G3 TEAEs by arm
  - Arm A: ALT increase + constipation, myalgia + ALT increase + hypertension, neutropenia, diabetes, diarrhea, hyponatremia
  - Arm B: peripheral arterial ischemia, fatigue
- 📊 Post-induction ORR (RECIST 1.1) 71% of first 15 evaluable pts, pooled across arms, preliminary
- ⚠️ Safety is after cycle 1 only; no data on CRT delivery, cisplatin dose intensity or RT interruptions
- ⚠️ ORR not split by arm and not mature; 'encouraging' conclusion rests on 15 pts
- ⚠️ Population mixes p16+ and p16- oropharynx (19 p16+ of 31 OPC) with larynx, hypopharynx, oral cavity

### Neoadjuvant NivoDP in high-grade salivary gland carcinoma · [[NCT05727410]]

> MPR 60.9% and pCR 32.6% among 46 operated pts after 3 cycles nivolumab + docetaxel/cisplatin in node-positive high-grade SGC; 24-mo DFS 76.0%.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> pCR 32.6% after neoadjuvant NivoDP raises the RT question the trial did not test: whether a pathologic responder still needs full adjuvant RT. Adjuvant RT was risk-adapted, but criteria, dose and volumes are not reported in source, so de-escalation after pCR remains untested.

- 🔍 Phase II, single-center, single-arm; N=50 analyzed of 55 enrolled
- 🔍 Treatment-naïve resectable high-grade SGC with clinically positive nodes; salivary duct carcinoma 74.0%
- 💊 Nivolumab 360 mg + docetaxel 60 mg/m2 + cisplatin 60 mg/m2 q3w × 3, then surgery + risk-adapted adjuvant RT
- 📊 ORR 52.0%; 92.0% reached surgery; R0 93.5% of operated pts
- 📊 MPR 60.9% (n=28) exceeded predefined MPR ≥50% threshold; pCR 32.6% (n=15)
- 📊 Pathologic response by histology (best responders reported)

  | Histology | MPR | pCR |
  |---|---|---|
  | Salivary duct carcinoma | 67.6% | 29.4% |
  | Adenocarcinoma | 75.0% | 75.0% |
  | Poorly differentiated carcinoma | 66.7% | 66.7% |

- 📐 DFS MPR vs non-MPR: NR vs 24.2 mo, HR 0.19 (95% CI 0.05-0.71), p=0.013
- 📊 12-/24-mo DFS 89.0%/76.0%; 24-mo OS 91.2%; median f/u 16.2 mo from surgery
- ⚠️ 4 pts never reached surgery: 2 progression, 1 G3 pneumonitis, 1 withdrawal
- ⚠️ Adjuvant RT dose, volume and risk-adaptation criteria not reported in source
- ⚠️ MPR-vs-non-MPR DFS is a responder comparison, not evidence the regimen improves DFS

**Sources:**
- 📄 **A phase II trial of neoadjuvant nivolumab, docetaxel, and cisplatin followed by surgery and radiation therapy for resectable high-grade salivary gland carcinoma** — Sehhoon Park. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 1540RO · Rapid oral: Head and neck cancer · 2026-10-25 · presented by Sehhoon Park (Seoul, Republic of Korea) · [[NCT05727410]]
  > Background: Resectable high-grade salivary gland carcinoma (SGC) with clinical node metastasis carries a high risk of distant metastasis despite curative surgery and adjuvant radiotherapy, and effective neoadjuvant strategies remain undefined. We evaluated neoadjuvant nivolumab plus docetaxel and cisplatin (NivoDP) in this aggressive disease.
  > Methods: This phase II, single-center, single-arm trial enrolled treatment-naïve patients with resectable high-grade SGC and clinically positive lymph nodes. Patients received 3 cycles of nivolumab (360 mg) plus docetaxel (60 mg/m2) and cisplatin (60 mg/m2) q3w, followed by curative surgery and risk-adapted adjuvant radiotherapy. The primary endpoint was major pathologic response (MPR; ≤10% viable tumor).
  > Results: Of 55 enrolled, 4 screen failures and 1 with benign histology were excluded, leaving 50 analyzed (median age 62; 88.0% male; ECOG 0/1: 12.0%/88.0%). AJCC 8th stage was III/IVA/IVB in 34.0%/64.0%/2.0%. Salivary duct carcinoma predominated (74.0%), followed by adenocarcinoma (10.0%) and mucoepidermoid carcinoma (8.0%). ORR was 52.0%. Forty-six patients underwent surgery (92.0%); 4 did not (1 withdrawal, 2 progression, 1 G3 drug-induced pneumonitis). Among 46 operated patients, R0 resection was 93.5%, MPR 60.9% (n=28), and pCR 32.6% (n=15), exceeding the predefined threshold (MPR ≥50%). Among pathology subtypes, salivary duct carcinoma (MPR 67.6%, pCR 29.4%), adenocarcinoma (MPR 75.0%, pCR 75.0%), and poorly differentiated carcinoma (MPR 66.7%, pCR 66.7%) showed the best response to NivoDP. With a median follow-up of 16.2 months (95% CI 12.0–20.1) from surgery, 12- and 24-month DFS were 89.0% and 76.0%, and 24-month OS 91.2%. The MPR subgroup showed significantly longer DFS than non-MPR (NR vs 24.2 mo; HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.05–0.71, p=0.013). Most TRAEs were grade 1–2; 6 grade ≥3 TRAEs were observed, which were all manageable with no treatment-related deaths.
  > Conclusions: Neoadjuvant NivoDP showed promising clinical efficacy in node-positive high-grade SGC, with high MPR and pCR rates. MPR strongly predicted superior DFS. These results support neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy as a promising strategy in resectable high-grade SGC.
  > Clinical trial identification: [[NCT05727410]].

### SMART-KEY (LACOG 0720) · [[NCT04943445]]

> 2y laryngectomy-free survival 65.1% (80% CI 54.9-73.5) with cisplatin-free induction, then IMRT plus pembrolizumab, in stage III-IVB glottic/supraglottic SCC.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The concurrent phase drops cisplatin entirely, with IMRT given alongside pembrolizumab alone, yet 2y larynx preservation was 86% (RTOG 9111: 88%). Locoregional failure at 18.8% dominates over distant at 7.4%, so the RT backbone carries local control here. The dose and volumes are not in the source, which limits how well this carries over to your own fractionation.

- 🔍 Single-arm, multicenter phase 2, N=43 across 9 centers, enrolled Feb 2022 to Aug 2023
- 🔍 Newly diagnosed stage III-IVB glottic/supraglottic SCC; 62.8% glottic, 83.7% former/current smokers
- 💊 Induction: carboplatin AUC6 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 + pembrolizumab 200 mg, three 21-day cycles
- 💊 Then IMRT with concurrent pembrolizumab alone (no concurrent chemo), then fourteen 21-day cycles of pembrolizumab consolidation
- ⚠️ RT dose, fractionation and target volumes not reported in source
- 📊 2y outcomes vs historical controls (as tabulated by the investigators)

  | Outcome | SMART-KEY | RTOG 9111 (cisplatin+RT) | GORTEC 2000-01 (TPF/RT) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2y LFS | 65.1% | 64.9% | NE |
  | 2y larynx preservation | 86% | 88% | 75.8% |
  | 2y larynx dysfunction-free survival | 83.7% | NE | 70.3% |
  | 2y OS | 74.4% | 74% | 73% |
  | Alive with larynx | 65.1% | 64.9% | NE |
  | Alive after laryngectomy | 9.3% | 9.1% | NE |
  | Dead after laryngectomy | 4.7% | 2.9% | NE |
  | Dead, no laryngectomy | 20.9% | 23.1% | NE |

- 📊 Patterns of failure, 2y cumulative incidence
  - Locoregional recurrence: 18.8%
  - Distant recurrence: 7.4%
- ⚠️ Toxicity
  - G≥3 TRAEs 41.9%, including one G5
  - Immune-mediated G≥3 TRAEs 16.3%
- ⚠️ Efficacy reported with 80% CI, not 95%
- ⚠️ Rationale is lower late cisplatin/RT morbidity and mortality, which 2y data cannot yet show

### KEYNOTE-412 ctDNA analysis · [[NCT03040999]]

> Mid-CRT (C3D1) ctDNA clearance tracked EFS in HPV- LA HNSCC in both arms; baseline ctDNA was prognostic in HPV- but not HPV+ pts.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The ctDNA draw at C3D1 falls after 5 of 6-7 wk of CRT, so clearance is effectively a mid-RT response read. In HPV- pts it tracked EFS in both arms (P = 0.001 and 0.011), the signal an adaptive escalation trial would need. Baseline ctDNA carried no prognostic value in HPV+ pts, so it cannot yet select HPV+ pts for de-escalation.

- 🔍 Retrospective ctDNA analysis of phase 3 KEYNOTE-412: pembro or pbo + concurrent CRT, newly diagnosed unresected LA HNSCC
- 🔍 Tumor-informed Natera Signatera 16-plex; HPV status by p16
- 🔍 473/804 pts evaluable at both BL and C3D1 (pembro n = 233; pbo n = 240)
- 🔍 C3D1 drawn in pts who completed 5 of 6-7 wk of CRT, so a mid-RT timepoint
- 📊 BL ctDNA positivity 95.6% HPV+, 95.3% HPV-
- 📊 BL ctDNA correlated with BL target lesion size (Spearman ρ = 0.49 HPV-, 0.34 HPV+)
- 📊 HPV-: higher BL ctDNA → worse OS (pembro arm), worse EFS and OS (pbo arm), P < 0.05
- 📊 HPV+: no BL ctDNA association with outcome in either arm (P > 0.05)
- 📊 C3D1 ctDNA clearance rate by arm and HPV status

  | HPV status | Pembro + CRT | Pbo + CRT |
  |---|---|---|
  | HPV- | 72.6% | 63.6% |
  | HPV+ | 76.8% | 62.7% |

- 📐 C3D1 clearance vs outcome (P values only; no HRs reported in source)

  | Subgroup · endpoint | Pembro arm | Pbo arm |
  |---|---|---|
  | HPV- · EFS | P = 0.001 | P = 0.011 |
  | HPV- · OS | P < 0.001 | P = 0.075 |
  | HPV+ · EFS | no association reported | P = 0.028 |
  | HPV+ · OS | no association reported | P = 0.013 |

- ⚠️ No effect size reported in source: P values only, no HR or absolute EFS/OS by ctDNA group
- ⚠️ HPV- BL association was dependent on tumor size and stage, so ctDNA may not add beyond TNM
- ⚠️ Landmark at C3D1 restricted to pts completing CRT; dropouts excluded from the clearance analysis
- ⚠️ Many arm × HPV × endpoint tests; the HPV+ pbo-only signal is hypothesis-generating

  > Background: ctDNA is an emerging biomarker in multiple solid tumor types, but its value in LA HNSCC is unclear. KEYNOTE-412 ([[NCT03040999]]) evaluated pembro + CRT vs pbo + CRT in participants (pts) with newly diagnosed, unresected LA HNSCC.   > Methods: The tumor-informed Natera Signatera 16-plex assay was used to evaluate ctDNA levels in treated pts. Association of BL ctDNA levels and C3D1 ctDNA clearance with EFS and OS were assessed in each arm and by HPV status (by p16 testing) per Cox proportional hazards regression.

In HPV+ pts, ctDNA clearance at C3D1 was associated with improved EFS (P = 0.028) and OS (P = 0.013) only in the pbo arm.
  > Conclusions: The prognostic value of BL ctDNA was observed in HPV- pts with LA HNSCC but not in HPV+ pts. In HPV- pts, a stronger link between C3D1 ctDNA clearance and long-term survival was observed in the pembro arm. These findings suggest distinct ctDNA prognostic values in HNSCC based on HPV status, and long-term outcome associations may differ by treatment modality.

### RADIANT

> RT foundation model: GTV Dice 83.52%, CTV 81.53%; external C-index 0.73-0.79 OS, 0.76-0.86 LC/LRC across lung, oesophageal, rectal, cervical cancers.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The RT-relevant signal is toxicity and local control: AUC 0.78-0.81 for radiation pneumonitis, 0.84-0.85 for rectal proctitis, and LC/LRC C-index 0.76-0.86, all from a dose-aware encoder. If calibration holds, this informs dose-constraint and de-escalation decisions at planning, but auto-contour acceptability and plan-quality endpoints are absent from source.

- 🔍 3D Swin Transformer encoder, masked-autoencoder pretraining on 119,782 RT cases from 14 centres
- 🔍 Inputs paired: planning CT, RT structure sets, 3D dose distributions
- 🔍 Encoder refined via hierarchical GTV/CTV segmentation plus dose generation
- 🔍 Hold-out test set: 22,058 cases, 10 most prevalent tumour sites
- 📊 Hold-out segmentation and dose-generation performance

  | Task | Metric | Value |
  |---|---|---|
  | GTV segmentation | Overall Dice | 83.52% |
  | CTV segmentation | Overall Dice | 81.53% |
  | Dose generation | ΔHI error | 0.0695 |
  | Dose generation | ΔD98 error | 0.0706 |
  | Dose generation | ΔD2 error | 0.0129 |
  | Dose generation | ΔDmax error | 0.0012 |
  | Dose generation | ΔDmean error | 0.0371 |

- 📊 Downstream prediction in independent validation (frozen encoder + generated-dose branch, 21 endpoints)

  | Endpoint | Metric | Value |
  |---|---|---|
  | pCR after oesophageal nICT | AUC | 0.72 |
  | Rectal complete response | AUC | 0.84 |
  | Radiation pneumonitis | AUC | 0.78-0.81 |
  | Rectal radiation proctitis | AUC | 0.84-0.85 |
  | Cervical radiation proctitis | AUC | 0.84 |
  | OS | External C-index | 0.73-0.79 |
  | PFS | External C-index | 0.71-0.78 |
  | LC/LRC | External C-index | 0.76-0.86 |

- 🔗 Reported to outperform state-of-the-art baselines, especially in external cohorts; baseline metrics not given in source
- 🔍 Validation cohorts: China, US, Netherlands; retrospective and prospective
- ⚠️ Discrimination only: no calibration, no clinician-in-loop contour or plan acceptability data reported
- ⚠️ Dice of 81-84% leaves room for clinically meaningful target edits; no per-site breakdown in source
- ⚠️ Grad-CAM and transcriptomic findings exploratory; abstract-only source

**Sources:**
- 📄 **RADIANT: A radiotherapy foundation model for treatment planning and outcome prediction across cancers** — Zhen Zhang. *ESMO Congress 2026* (2026-10). [article](https://cslide.ctimeetingtech.com/esmo2026/attendee/confcal/presentation/list)
  > ESMO Congress 2026 abstract 170O · Proffered paper: AI and digital health · 2026-10-24 · presented by Zhen Zhang (Hangzhou, China)
  > Background: Radiotherapy generates structured planning CT, target/OAR contours and 3D dose maps, yet current AI tools are usually single-task models trained in limited cohorts. We developed RADIANT, a radiotherapy foundation model for an automated workflow from target delineation and dose generation to clinical risk prediction.
  > Methods: RADIANT used a 3D Swin Transformer encoder pretrained by masked autoencoding on 119,782 radiotherapy cases from 14 centres with paired planning CT, RT structure sets and 3D dose distributions. The encoder was refined with hierarchical GTV/CTV segmentation and dose generation. Outputs were tested in a hold-out set of 22,058 cases from the 10 most prevalent tumour sites. For downstream adaptation, a frozen RADIANT encoder was integrated with a generated-dose branch and task-specific heads for 21 endpoints across lung, oesophageal, rectal and cervical cancers. Validation included retrospective and prospective cohorts from China, the United States and the Netherlands, covering pCR/response, OS, PFS, LC and radiotherapy-related toxicities. Performance was compared with state-of-the-art baseline models.
  > Results: In hold-out testing, RADIANT achieved overall Dice scores of 83.52% for GTV and 81.53% for CTV, with dose-generation errors of 0.0695 for ΔHI, 0.0706 for ΔD98, 0.0129 for ΔD2, 0.0012 for ΔDmax and 0.0371 for ΔDmean. Across independent validations, RADIANT achieved AUCs of 0.72 for pCR after oesophageal nICT, 0.84 for rectal complete response, 0.78-0.81 for radiation pneumonitis, 0.84-0.85 for rectal radiation proctitis and 0.84 for cervical radiation proctitis. Survival models yielded external C-indices of 0.73-0.79 for OS, 0.71-0.78 for PFS and 0.76-0.86 for LC/LRC. RADIANT consistently outperformed baseline models, particularly in external cohorts. Grad-CAM highlighted clinically plausible tumour and normal-tissue dose interfaces; exploratory transcriptomic analyses suggested biological differences between model-defined risk groups.
  > Conclusions: Prospective and international multi-centre validation supports RADIANT as a scalable framework for AI-assisted radiotherapy workflows and decision-making.

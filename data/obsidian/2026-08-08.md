# 2026-08-08

**BEACON-HCC: 96.6% concordance with expert allocation vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025, elevating SBRT and TARE to preferred options in early and locally advanced HCC.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> One hepatobiliary item: a 20-expert North American Delphi framework that promotes SBRT and TARE out of BCLC's systemic-first lanes. Class 1C names TARE or SBRT over TACE when selective delivery is feasible (each ORR >50%), and Class 3B leans on NRG/RTOG1112 for macrovascular invasion. Concordance is agreement with expert opinion, not outcomes.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

A consensus allocation framework, not a trial: it moves SBRT and TARE earlier in HCC, on expert agreement rather than outcome data.

### BEACON-HCC

> 96.6% concordance with external expert decisions vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025, in a 20-expert North American allocation framework elevating SBRT and TARE.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> Classes 1D and 3B sit at BCLC advanced (C), systemic-only territory; BEACON names SBRT and TARE as options there, leaning on NRG/RTOG1112 (Vp3/4 in 63%, OS trend p=0.06 vs sorafenib) and DOSISPHERE. The counterweight is Class 4A, where EBRT is explicitly not indicated outside palliation.

- 📊 Expert decisions matched BEACON in 28 of 29 cases (96.6%) vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025
- 🔍 Modified Delphi, 20 North American experts: hepatology, med onc, surgery, radiology, rad onc
- 📊 BEACON class vs BCLC 2025 and AJCC v8, with the stated treatment adaptation

  | BEACON class | BCLC 2025 | AJCC v8 | Adaptation from BCLC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Class 1A | Very early (0) or early (A) | Stage 1A or 1B | Stages aligned; framework elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 1B | Early (A) | Stage 1B | n/a |
  | Class 1C | Early (A) | Stage 2 | n/a |
  | Class 1D | Advanced (C) | Stage 2 | Macrovascular invasion may be amenable to TARE, SBRT, or resection |
  | Class 2 | Early (A) | Stage 1A or 1B | Stages aligned; framework elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 3A | Intermediate (B) or advanced (C) | Stage 3A | Staging aligned; framework elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 3B | Advanced (C) | Stage 3B or 4A | Macrovascular invasion may be amenable to local therapies in selected cases |
  | Class 4A | Intermediate (B) | Stage 3A | Extensive intrahepatic metastasis is a systemic process; systemic therapy |
  | Class 4B | Advanced (C) | Stage 4B | Stage and treatment framework well aligned |

- 📊 Class 1C: TARE or SBRT preferred over TACE when deliverable selectively, each ORR >50%
- 🔗 Class 3B leans on NRG/RTOG1112 (Vp3/4 in 63%): SBRT vs sorafenib OS trend p=0.06
- 🔗 Class 1D cites DOSISPHERE: personalized TARE dosimetry ORR 71 vs 36%, OS 26.6 vs 10.7 mo
- ⚠️ Class 4A de-lists TACE, TARE and EBRT outside palliation for symptomatic disease
- ⚠️ Framework concedes few high-quality data show one locoregional therapy superior to another
- ⚠️ Concordance is agreement with expert opinion, not outcomes; empiric validation deferred to HCC-Live Consortium

**Sources:**
- 📄 **BEACON-HCC: Best evidence and north american consensus on treatment allocation for hepatocellular carcinoma** — Singal, Amit G.; Agopian, Vatche G; Dawson, Laura A. et al.. *Hepatology* (2026-08). [doi:10.1097/hep.0000000000001834](https://doi.org/10.1097/hep.0000000000001834)
  > Background : Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality. Optimal treatment decisions are challenging, and existing treatment allocation frameworks may not fully reflect the evolving therapeutic landscape or contemporary clinical practices in North America. Methods : Using a modified Delphi process, a multidisciplinary panel of 20 North American experts in hepatology, medical oncology, surgery, radiology, and radiation oncology developed a consensus-based treatment framework for HCC, termed the BEACON-HCC system. The framework was informed by current evidence and expert opinion through iterative discussion and voting. In a pilot study using 29 real-world patient cases, we assessed concordance between external expert recommendations and BEACON-HCC recommendations and Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) 2025 treatment recommendations. Results : The BEACON-HCC treatment allocation framework diverges from prior frameworks by incorporating nuanced clinical features such as degree of intrahepatic tumor burden and vascular invasion, and adverse tumor prognostic markers, to align treatment allocation with tumor biology and therapeutic potential. Key innovations include the incorporation of emerging modalities such as external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), transarterial radioembolization (TARE), and systemic-locoregional combination therapies. In the pilot exercise, expert treatment decisions showed 96.6% concordance with BEACON-HCC recommendations and 72.4% concordance with BCLC recommendations. Conclusions : BEACON-HCC is a consensus-based framework for treatment allocation that incorporates the expanding range of therapeutic options available to patients in the North American HCC population. Expert treatment decisions showed a high concordance with the BEACON-HCC system; further validation using empiric clinical data is planned through the HCC-Live Consortium.

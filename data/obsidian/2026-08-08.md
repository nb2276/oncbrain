# 2026-08-08

**BEACON-HCC: 20-expert Delphi re-stages HCC by burden + vascular invasion, naming EBRT/SBRT and TARE first-class locoregional options rather than salvage.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Hepatobiliary carried the day, and the news is allocational, not empirical: a burden-and-Vp-based framework that routes Class 1A-3B to SBRT or TARE alongside resection/ablation, citing RTOG 1112 and DOSISPHERE for the RT/TARE rationale. Concordance 96.6% vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025 is opinion vs opinion, not an outcome.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

One consensus document, no efficacy endpoint: what moves is where SBRT sits in the algorithm, not what it delivers.

### BEACON-HCC

> 20-expert Delphi framework re-allocates HCC treatment by tumor burden and vascular invasion; 96.6% expert concordance vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The framework moves EBRT/SBRT and TARE out of the BCLC footnote and into the allocation table itself: Classes 1A, 1B, 1C, 2 and 3A are all flagged as "elevates SBRT and TARE" relative to BCLC 2025. For a rad onc, this is the citation that supports being at the HCC tumor board earlier, at unifocal ≤3 cm rather than only after TACE failure.

- 🔍 Modified Delphi, 20 North American experts across hepatology, med onc, surgery, radiology, rad onc
- 🔍 Pilot validation: 29 real-world cases scored against external expert consensus
- 📊 Concordance 96.6% (28/29 cases) BEACON-HCC vs 72.4% BCLC 2025
- 📊 BEACON class vs BCLC 2025 / AJCC v8, and what the framework changed

  | BEACON class | BCLC 2025 | AJCC v8 | Adaptation |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Class 1A | Very early (0) or early (A) | Stage 1A or 1B | Elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 1B | Early (A) | Stage 1B | Elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 1C | Early (A) | Stage 2 | Elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 1D | Advanced (C) | Stage 2 | Vp1-2 may be amenable to TARE, SBRT, resection |
  | Class 2 | Early (A) | Stage 1A or 1B | Elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 3A | Intermediate (B) or advanced (C) | Stage 3A | Elevates SBRT and TARE |
  | Class 3B | Advanced (C) | Stage 3B or 4A | Macrovascular invasion may be amenable to local therapy |
  | Class 4A | Intermediate (B) | Stage 3A | Extensive intrahepatic spread treated as systemic process |
  | Class 4B | Advanced (C) | Stage 4B | Stage and framework aligned |

- 📊 Key move for RT: EBRT/SBRT and TARE named as first-class locoregional options, not salvage
- 🔍 Panel states no high-quality data show superiority of one locoregional therapy over another
- 🔍 Class 4A (>50% burden or multifocal Vp3/4): TACE, TARE, EBRT not routinely indicated outside palliation
- 🔗 Class 3B rationale cites NRG/RTOG 1112 (Vp3/4 in 63%), SBRT vs sorafenib OS trend p=0.06
- 🔗 Class 1D rationale cites DOSISPHERE: personalized dosimetry ORR 71 vs 36%, OS 26.6 vs 10.7 mo
- ⚠️ Panel agreement was NOT uniform across classes; per-class values are in a table whose row pairing the source text does not resolve, so no class-level agreement figure is quotable here. Range spanned 11/16 to 18/18 initially
- ⚠️ Concordance is opinion measured against opinion: external experts vs a framework the same community wrote. No survival endpoint
- ⚠️ n=29 pilot cases, single scoring exercise; empiric validation via HCC-Live Consortium is planned, not done

**Sources:**
- 📄 **BEACON-HCC: Best evidence and north american consensus on treatment allocation for hepatocellular carcinoma** — Singal, Amit G.; Agopian, Vatche G; Dawson, Laura A. et al.. *Hepatology* (2026-08). [doi:10.1097/hep.0000000000001834](https://doi.org/10.1097/hep.0000000000001834)
  > Background : Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality. Optimal treatment decisions are challenging, and existing treatment allocation frameworks may not fully reflect the evolving therapeutic landscape or contemporary clinical practices in North America. Methods : Using a modified Delphi process, a multidisciplinary panel of 20 North American experts in hepatology, medical oncology, surgery, radiology, and radiation oncology developed a consensus-based treatment framework for HCC, termed the BEACON-HCC system. The framework was informed by current evidence and expert opinion through iterative discussion and voting. In a pilot study using 29 real-world patient cases, we assessed concordance between external expert recommendations and BEACON-HCC recommendations and Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) 2025 treatment recommendations. Results : The BEACON-HCC treatment allocation framework diverges from prior frameworks by incorporating nuanced clinical features such as degree of intrahepatic tumor burden and vascular invasion, and adverse tumor prognostic markers, to align treatment allocation with tumor biology and therapeutic potential. Key innovations include the incorporation of emerging modalities such as external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), transarterial radioembolization (TARE), and systemic-locoregional combination therapies. In the pilot exercise, expert treatment decisions showed 96.6% concordance with BEACON-HCC recommendations and 72.4% concordance with BCLC recommendations. Conclusions : BEACON-HCC is a consensus-based framework for treatment allocation that incorporates the expanding range of therapeutic options available to patients in the North American HCC population. Expert treatment decisions showed a high concordance with the BEACON-HCC system; further validation using empiric clinical data is planned through the HCC-Live Consortium.

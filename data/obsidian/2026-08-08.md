# 2026-08-08

**BEACON-HCC: new North American allocation framework puts SBRT and TARE first-line for solitary HCC, 96.6% vs 72.4% expert concordance vs BCLC 2025.**

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Hepatobiliary carries the day, and it is an RT-relevant framework rather than a trial: BEACON-HCC moves EBRT/SBRT out of BCLC's post-TACE-failure slot into first-line for solitary disease (Class 1A/1B/1C) and keeps a role at Class 1D. Consensus only, no prospective outcome validation.

## 🟡 Hepatobiliary

One consensus framework, no new outcome data: the question for rad onc is whether first-line SBRT survives validation against BCLC's TACE-first sequencing.

### BEACON-HCC

> New North American HCC allocation framework; 96.6% expert concordance vs 72.4% for BCLC 2025, with SBRT/TARE elevated to first-line.

> [!info] Why it matters · Radiation oncology
> The operative change for a rad onc is slot position: SBRT and EBRT move from BCLC's post-TACE-failure fallback to a first-line option for solitary HCC (Class 1A-1C) and stay on the table for Vp1-2 invasion (Class 1D), where BCLC assigns systemic therapy. That reframes the tumor-board argument for referring an ablative-candidate lesion to RT rather than defending it after TACE fails.

- 🔍 Modified Delphi, 20 North American multidisciplinary experts (incl. rad onc); voting June 2025, >20% disagreement triggered revision + repeat vote
- 📊 External validation: 18 independent experts, 29 real-world cases, blinded to the framework
- 📊 Concordance 28/29 (96.6%) with BEACON vs 21/29 (72.4%) with BCLC 2025
- 🔍 EBRT/SBRT and TARE are first-line options for solitary HCC (Class 1A/1B/1C), not BCLC's second-line-after-TACE-failure slot
- 🔍 Where BEACON re-slots RT vs BCLC 2025
  - Class 1A/1B/2: stages aligned with BCLC, but framework elevates SBRT and TARE
  - Class 1D (Vp1-2, satellite, or infiltrative margin): potential role for TARE, SBRT, and resection despite BCLC advanced (C)
  - Class 3A: staging aligned, framework elevates SBRT and TARE
  - Class 4A (>50% burden): EBRT/TARE not routinely indicated outside palliation
- 📐 Rad onc dissent is on record: for Class 1A one respondent held SBRT should be *preferred*, not just useful
- 🔗 RT evidence cited: NRG/RTOG 1112 (63% Vp3/4) trend to better OS with SBRT vs sorafenib, p=0.06; TACE+EBRT RCT beat sorafenib in locally advanced disease
- 🔗 DOSISPHERE personalized dosimetry cited for TARE: ORR 71 vs 36%, OS 26.6 vs 10.7 mo
- ⚠️ Consensus, not outcomes: no prospective validation, and the panel concedes expert consensus is not a surrogate for improved clinical outcomes
- ⚠️ Concordance is measured against expert opinion, and the framework's own authors seeded the case set (29 cases, panel-submitted)
- ⚠️ Class-level agreement was not uniform: Class 3A cleared only 11/16 (68.8%) initially before revision

**Sources:**
- 📄 **BEACON-HCC: Best evidence and north american consensus on treatment allocation for hepatocellular carcinoma** — Singal, Amit G.; Agopian, Vatche G; Dawson, Laura A. et al.. *Hepatology* (2026-08). [doi:10.1097/hep.0000000000001834](https://doi.org/10.1097/hep.0000000000001834)
  > Background : Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality. Optimal treatment decisions are challenging, and existing treatment allocation frameworks may not fully reflect the evolving therapeutic landscape or contemporary clinical practices in North America. Methods : Using a modified Delphi process, a multidisciplinary panel of 20 North American experts in hepatology, medical oncology, surgery, radiology, and radiation oncology developed a consensus-based treatment framework for HCC, termed the BEACON-HCC system. The framework was informed by current evidence and expert opinion through iterative discussion and voting. In a pilot study using 29 real-world patient cases, we assessed concordance between external expert recommendations and BEACON-HCC recommendations and Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) 2025 treatment recommendations. Results : The BEACON-HCC treatment allocation framework diverges from prior frameworks by incorporating nuanced clinical features such as degree of intrahepatic tumor burden and vascular invasion, and adverse tumor prognostic markers, to align treatment allocation with tumor biology and therapeutic potential. Key innovations include the incorporation of emerging modalities such as external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), transarterial radioembolization (TARE), and systemic-locoregional combination therapies. In the pilot exercise, expert treatment decisions showed 96.6% concordance with BEACON-HCC recommendations and 72.4% concordance with BCLC recommendations. Conclusions : BEACON-HCC is a consensus-based framework for treatment allocation that incorporates the expanding range of therapeutic options available to patients in the North American HCC population. Expert treatment decisions showed a high concordance with the BEACON-HCC system; further validation using empiric clinical data is planned through the HCC-Live Consortium.

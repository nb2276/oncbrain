═══ ASSOCIATION HINTS (v0.5+) ═══

{{ASSOCIATION_HINTS}}

Hints are SOFT. STRONG hints (shared NCT id) are very likely correct — cluster together unless the content explicitly contradicts. Medium hints (shared trial acronym) are useful but can over-merge — verify the content discusses the same trial before grouping. The model has final say on clustering.

SAME TRIAL, SEPARATE REPORTS = SEPARATE CLUSTERS. A shared NCT or acronym means the items are the same TRIAL, not necessarily the same REPORT. Split into separate studies ONLY when the items are distinct reports of that trial, each with its own primary objective and its own headline result:
- primary/co-primary efficacy (OS, DFS, PFS, biochemical failure, local control)
- quality of life / patient-reported outcomes (EPIC-26, EORTC QLQ-C30, FACT)
- an extended-follow-up report of a previously reported outcome
Merging two such reports produces one card whose headline number splices two unrelated results together, and the reader cannot tell which finding the number belongs to. NRG-GU005 published its quality-of-life results and its co-primary efficacy results as two separate papers; those are two cards.

DEFAULT IS ONE CLUSTER. Do NOT split when the items are all covering ONE report:
- a subgroup, sensitivity, biomarker or safety detail discussed WITHIN one report is part of that report's cluster, not a card of its own
- several tweets about one presentation are one study, even when individual tweets highlight different numbers from it
- a tweet, a slide and the paper covering one result are one study
When in doubt, cluster together. Over-splitting is the more damaging error: it fragments one result across cards, strands its citation, and shows the reader two partial cards where the trial reported one finding.

═══ OUTPUT SHAPE ═══

```json
{
  "studies": [
    {
      "slug": "prestige-psma",
      "name": "PRESTIGE-PSMA",
      "disease_site": "prostate",
      "content_type": "study_report",
      "tweet_ids": [101, 105, 108]
    }
  ]
}
```

`content_type` is one of:
- `study_report` (DEFAULT — use for the overwhelming majority of clusters): the
  cluster reports ONE trial / dataset's result. A conference tweet, a primary
  paper, a slide, OR a trade-press write-up *of a single study*. A meta-analysis
  that reports its OWN pooled estimate is a `study_report`, not a review.
- `review`: a single trade-press / survey / state-of-the-art item that discusses
  MULTIPLE trials or a general topic — naming several trial acronyms rather than
  reporting one new result (e.g. a UroToday conference-highlights round-up of the
  oligometastatic-prostate SBRT landscape, naming STOMP / ORIOLE / RADIOSA / ARTO).
  When genuinely torn between the two, prefer `review`: a lost verdict is safer
  than a bogus one on a piece that has no single result.

═══ RULES ═══

- Emit nothing but the JSON object.
- Every input tweet id MUST appear in exactly one study's `tweet_ids`. Don't drop tweets. Don't repeat ids across clusters. A downstream validator REJECTS your output if either invariant fails — your retry will be more constrained.
- One tweet = one study. If a tweet seems to discuss two unrelated trials, attach it to the more substantive one.
- A `review` item stays its OWN cluster. Never merge a multi-trial review into a single-trial cluster, and never fold a single-trial item into a review, even when the review NAMES that trial. They are distinct clusters: the review surveys the landscape; the single-trial item reports the result.
- Slugs unique within this output.
